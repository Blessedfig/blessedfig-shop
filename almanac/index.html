The Antigo Almanac

Antigo's year, in real numbers

This almanac holds real numbers about Antigo's year: when people come, what they do, and where money moves. Every page names its source at the bottom.

The numbers describe the country, the state, the county or the region. None of them describes a single business.

Where Antigo has no data of its own, the page says so and uses the nearest honest stand-in.

It is not financial advice; it is a good place to start asking questions. The same almanac is in the game, where its pages are hidden around town.

Tap any title to open or close its page. This month and the season you are in are marked.

The year in Antigo

Winter in Antigo

Snowmobile trails open and close by county announcement. In 2025-26 they opened Jan 1 and closed Mar 3. The county keeps more than 500 miles of trail through about ten clubs.

Winter events cluster on Saturdays from late January through February, mostly in the northern towns: vintage sled shows, polar bear jumps, ice-fishing jamborees.

Downtown in December: the Christmas Parade and chili cook-off, Wednesday night markets until mid-month, and Santa at the historical museum on weekends. Muzzleloader deer season runs into early December.

Source: Langlade County trail reports and events calendar; Antigo Chamber; Wisconsin DNR. Dates are for 2025-26 and 2026.

Spring in Antigo

March and April are the quietest months for visitors up north (see When the Northwoods fills, under Visitors).

Inland trout streams opened to harvest on Apr 4, 2026, about a month earlier than before. The general fishing opener is the first Saturday in May (May 2, 2026). ATV trails, more than 106 miles of them, open May 1.

The Antigo Off-Road National runs at the fairgrounds in late May (May 29-31, 2026), with a kickoff parade the evening before. Independent Bookstore Day is the last Saturday in April.

Source: Wisconsin DNR; Langlade County trail reports and events calendar; Champ Off-Road 2026 schedule; American Booksellers Association.

Summer in Antigo

Farmers market: Saturdays 8 am to noon, early June to early October, at Peaceful Valley Park (420 Field St).

July 4: parade at 7 pm and fireworks at 9:20 pm, ending at the fairgrounds. The Langlade County Fair runs Thursday to Sunday at the turn of July and August (July 30 - Aug 2, 2026, its 140th year).

August adds the Tater Trot (Aug 8) and national dirt-track races at the fairgrounds (Aug 21-22). Nearby: the Crandon Brush Run (mid-June) and the Hodag Country Festival in Rhinelander (mid-July).

Source: Langlade County events calendar; Antigo Chamber; Travel Wisconsin; Antigo Farmers Market. Dates are for 2026.

Fall in Antigo

Sept 12 opened archery and crossbow deer, fall turkey and grouse (2026). Gun deer season runs nine days from the Saturday before Thanksgiving (Nov 21-29, 2026).

Fall color runs about mid-September to mid-October. The county grows about 11,000 acres of potatoes a year, mostly seed.

The holiday run starts downtown: Christmas Market on Fifth Ave (Nov 14), Downtown Widows Weekend on the gun-deer opener, Small Business Saturday (Nov 28), and Wednesday night markets at 902 Fifth Ave from mid-September (Sept 16). Crandon's Labor Day races draw tens of thousands.

Source: Wisconsin DNR; UW-Madison Extension; Langlade County events calendar; Antigo Chamber and community calendar, 2026.

Weather by month

A normal July day in Antigo runs from about 57 to 78 degrees. A normal January day runs from about 4 to 21.

About 37 nights a year drop below zero, nearly all from December to March. Days reaching 90 come about twice a year.

Antigo gets about 29 inches of rain and melted snow a year, with June the wettest month (4.5 inches). Snowfall averages about 54 inches, and snow covers the ground about 116 days a year, from late November into March.

Half the time the last spring frost comes by May 14 and the first fall frost by September 30, which leaves a typical growing season of about 139 days.

Antigo's weather station has gaps in its record, so NOAA built these normals from 13 to 22 years of data inside 1991-2020 rather than the full 30. Read them as close, not exact.

Source: NOAA National Centers for Environmental Information, U.S. Climate Normals 1991-2020, monthly and annual normals for station Antigo, WI (USC00470239).

Daylight

On the longest day (June 21, 2026) the sun rises at about 5:09 am and sets at about 8:47 pm: 15 hours 38 minutes of daylight.

On the shortest day (December 21) it rises at about 7:32 am and sets at about 4:17 pm: 8 hours 45 minutes, almost seven hours less.

The earliest sunset of the year comes before the solstice, around December 11 at about 4:15 pm. The latest comes after it, around June 27 at about 8:48 pm.

The spring and fall equinoxes each give a little over 12 hours, and daylight changes fastest around them, by about 3 minutes a day.

Source: Computed for 45.14 N, 89.15 W with NOAA's published solar position equations, Central Time with daylight saving time for 2026. Times are rounded and can differ by a minute or two from other calculators.

Visitors

When the Northwoods fills

Nobody publishes monthly visitor counts for Antigo. The nearest stand-in is short-term rentals in Rhinelander, in Oneida County to the north.

Occupancy peaks in July at 65 percent and bottoms out in March and April at about 15 to 16 percent. June to August brought about 42 percent of the year's rental revenue. Nightly prices barely move ($215 to $245); the season shows in nights booked, not in price.

A 2002 study of 20 northern counties, Langlade among them, found summer brought about 52 percent of traveler spending.

Source: AirROI Rhinelander market report, Oct 2025 - Sep 2026 (35 listings), revenue share as reported Nov 2026; Davidson-Peterson Associates for the Wisconsin Dept. of Tourism, Northwoods tourism profile (2002 data).

Visitor dollars

Tourism's total economic impact in Langlade County set a record each year from 2022 to 2025.

In 2025 visitors spent $60.3 million directly. Tourism supported 439 jobs and $4.6 million in state and local taxes. That spending averages about $165,000 a day, though it is not spread evenly through the year.

Tourism's total economic impact grew 2.8 percent in 2025, below the state's 4.4 percent and close to the 2.7 percent inflation rate.

Source: Wisconsin Department of Tourism (Travel Wisconsin), economic impact of tourism, county data 2022-2025; U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, consumer price index, 2025.

The neighbors

Direct visitor spending in 2025, by county. Langlade ranked 45th of Wisconsin's 72 counties, with about 0.35 percent of the state total.

The lake counties to the north, Oneida and Vilas, each drew about five times as much. Lincoln and Shawano drew 26 to 33 percent more than Langlade.

Source: Wisconsin Department of Tourism, county economic impact data, 2025; rank computed from the 72-county table.

Seasonal homes

Share of all housing held for seasonal or recreational use, 2020 Census. Langlade had 2,684 seasonal homes, more than three times the state's share, down from 3,100 in 2010 but up from 2,158 in 2000.

Source: U.S. Census Bureau, 2000, 2010 and 2020 Census (2020 Demographic and Housing Characteristics), housing units vacant for seasonal, recreational or occasional use.

Snow years

Winter visits here ride on snow. Trails open only when the county says there is enough.

The winters of 2023-24 and 2024-25 had little snow; in 2024-25 Langlade had about one week of snowmobiling. 2025-26 was the strongest winter in three years.

Planning for a winter with almost no trail days is part of the pattern.

Source: Langlade County trail reports; WXPR regional reporting, 2024-2026.

Traffic counts

Average vehicles per day, 2025. The busiest count in the city is the downtown stretch where four highways share one road.

Counts drop by a third or more at the city's edges. Monthly or seasonal swings on these roads were not available for this page.

Source: Wisconsin Department of Transportation, annual average daily traffic (AADT) counts, 2025.

The town

The town in numbers

Antigo counted 8,100 people in the 2020 Census. Recent estimates put it at 8,002 (state, Jan 2026) and 8,060 (Census Bureau, July 2025).

Household size and home value are 2020-2024 survey estimates. About one resident in four is 65 or older.

Source: U.S. Census Bureau: 2020 Census, ACS 2020-2024, QuickFacts, July 2025 estimate; Wisconsin DOA estimate, Jan 1, 2026.

People over time

Antigo grew fast with the railroad and the sawmills: 4,424 people in 1890 and 8,451 by 1920. It peaked at 9,902 in 1950 and counted 8,100 in 2020.

The county peaked earlier, at 23,227 in 1940, and has stayed between about 19,200 and 20,700 since 1960.

The county is older now: its median age rose from 40.5 in 2000 to 49.5 in 2020, and the share 65 or older from 18.8 to 25.2 percent.

Across rural America, counties have lost roughly a quarter or more of their expected young adults each decade since the 1970s, while gaining adults aged 55 to 74.

Source: U.S. Census Bureau decennial counts, 1890-2020 (county and city tables); 2000 and 2020 Census age data; Johnson and Winkler, Migration signatures across the decades, Demographic Research 32 (2015), 1065-1080.

What households earn

Median household income, 2020-2024 survey estimates. Antigo's figure has a margin of error of about plus or minus $5,800.

Poverty rates: Antigo 19.5 percent, the county 13.5, the state 10.6.

Transfer payments such as Social Security and Medicare made up 30.9 percent of county personal income in 2024, against 19.0 percent statewide.

Source: U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey 5-year estimates, 2020-2024; U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis, county personal income, 2024.

Who works here

Jobs located in Langlade County, 2023 average: 7,268. Stores, trucking and utilities are the largest group; manufacturing is second, about one job in five.

In 2025 the county's unemployment rate averaged about 4.0 percent (state 3.2), and about 57 percent of adults were in the labor force (state 65.4).

Source: Wisconsin DWD, Langlade County Workforce Profile (QCEW 2023), rev. May 2025; NCWRPC Langlade County profile, 2026; U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, local area unemployment, 2025 annual averages.

What the town is worth

The state values all taxable property in the City of Antigo at $644 million (2026), one fifth of the county's $3.3 billion. The city holds about 71 percent of the county's commercial value.

Countywide, commercial property is 8.5 percent of value against 19.9 percent statewide; homes and forest land weigh more here.

Source: Wisconsin Department of Revenue, Statement of Equalized Values, 2026 (including TIF districts); statewide equalized values, 2026.

The Antigo flats

The farmland around Antigo is an outwash plain capped with windblown silt. Its soil, Antigo silt loam, has been Wisconsin's state soil since 1983; its official type location lies just east of town, near state historical marker 280.

The 2022 Census of Agriculture counted 333 farms (down 23 percent since 2017) selling $145.7 million (up 42 percent). Potatoes covered 10,562 acres, and the county ranked 4th in Wisconsin for vegetable and potato sales.

UW Extension calls the county first in the state for seed potatoes.

Source: USDA NASS, 2022 Census of Agriculture county profile; UW-Madison Extension; NRCS official series description, Antigo series; WGNHS Information Circular 52 (1986).

Where shoppers spend

A pull factor above 1 means a county sells more in a category than its own residents would be expected to buy; below 1 means residents buy part of it elsewhere.

In 2022 Langlade's retail pull factor was 1.41, so the county draws shoppers from beyond its borders. The strongest categories were general merchandise, building supplies, fuel and cars.

Source: Deller and Zumbrunnen, UW-Madison Extension, A Trade Area Analysis of Wisconsin Retail and Service Markets (2022 data), August 2023.

The county's half cent

Each taxable sale adds half a cent per dollar for the county, on top of the state's five cents. A $20 sale sends $1.00 to the state and $0.10 to the county.

The county received $2.35 million in 2024 and $2.50 million in 2025 (up 6.3 percent); January to November 2026 ran about 4 percent ahead.

Across 2024 and 2025, payouts ran highest in July, August and October and lowest in March, April and June. These are payout months, and the lag after a sale is not shown here, so read the shape as approximate.

Source: Wisconsin Department of Revenue, county sales tax distributions 2024-2026; index = month divided by that year's monthly average, 2024-2025.

When public money moves

The state pays its lottery and gaming credit in March, and county and municipal aid in two parts: about 15 percent in late July and 85 percent in mid-November.

In 2025 Langlade County received $710,324 in county and municipal aid and $547,388 in supplemental aid.

Source: Wisconsin Department of Revenue, local government payment schedules and state payment register for Langlade County, 2025.

Land and history

Woods and water

Langlade County covers about 888 square miles, and about 78 percent of it is forest. Forest land grew by about 44,000 acres (11 percent) from 1983 to 2015, the same rate as the state as a whole.

The county forest holds more than 130,000 acres, the seventh largest county forest in Wisconsin, open for hunting, fishing, hiking, snowmobiling and camping. The Chequamegon-Nicolet National Forest adds about 39,680 acres in the east.

The county has 843 lakes, 418 of them with public access, and 225 streams running more than 500 miles. The land rises from about 1,070 feet where the Wolf River leaves the county to about 1,900 feet in the Town of Langlade.

The Wolf River runs the full length of the county and drops 420 feet in 50 miles below Upper Post Lake, through 17 named rapids. State law allows no new dams on it here.

Source: North Central Wisconsin Regional Planning Commission, Langlade County Comprehensive Plan (2019), natural resources chapter, citing Wisconsin DNR and USDA Forest Service data; Wis. Stat. 30.25.

How Antigo began

Before settlement this was Menominee land, and Ojibwe and Potawatomi bands moved into the area between the 1500s and the 1800s. Willard Ackley, the county's first permanent European-American settler, opened a trading post in 1853.

Francis A. Deleglise, a Swiss-born surveyor and Civil War veteran of the 6th Wisconsin Infantry, platted the village. Accounts of his arrival give 1876 or 1877; his log cabin, built in May 1878, was Antigo's first house.

The cabin served as more than a home. The first Mass in Antigo was said there on May 2, 1880, and its side wing printed the county's first newspaper, the New County Republican, the same year. Saved from demolition in 1914, it now stands beside the historical society museum on Superior Street.

The county was created on March 3, 1879 as New County and renamed for Charles de Langlade on February 20, 1880. A 1908 railroad guide to place names traces Antigo to an Ojibwe phrase, nee-quee-antigo-sebi, meaning evergreen, or water running under evergreens.

Source: National Register of Historic Places nomination, Antigo Public Library and Deleglise Cabin (listed Dec 18, 1978); Newberry Library, Atlas of Historical County Boundaries; Chicago and North Western Railway, A History of the Origin of the Place Names (1908); Wikipedia articles on Antigo and Francis A. Deleglise.

Books in Antigo

Antigo had books to borrow almost from the start. By 1879 Louis Mendlik ran a rental library in his book and stationery store on Superior Street. In 1897 a circle of the Woman's Christian Temperance Union opened a free reading room, and in 1900 it gave its 750 books to the city to found a public library.

A Carnegie grant of $12,500, later raised to $15,000, paid for a library building on Superior Street. About 1,500 people came on opening day, November 7, 1905. The library moved out in 1997, and the building is now the historical society museum.

In 2024 the Antigo Public Library and its two branches served the whole county (19,354 people): 102,153 checkouts, 22,863 of them digital, and 52,653 visits. That is about 5.3 checkouts and 2.7 visits per resident, against 8.0 and 3.7 statewide.

Visits fell by more than half in 2020 and have climbed every year since, though not yet back to 2018. Program attendance has passed its old level: 9,090 people came to 832 programs in 2024, against 5,529 in 2018.

Source: National Register of Historic Places nomination, Antigo Public Library and Deleglise Cabin (1978); Wisconsin Department of Public Instruction, public library service data 2018-2024 (Langlade County totals; 2024 library-level and statewide totals); Wikipedia, Antigo Public Library and Deleglise Cabin.
