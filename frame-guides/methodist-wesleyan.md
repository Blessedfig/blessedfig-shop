# Methodist / Wesleyan

## Summary

*Draft from general knowledge; verify against the listed sources.*

**Core beliefs:** God created the world; salvation is by grace through faith in Christ, offered to all (prevenient grace); believers can grow toward holiness and perfect love (sanctification). Christians hope for resurrection and eternal life. Authority rests on Scripture as primary, with tradition, reason and experience (the "Wesleyan Quadrilateral").

**What it values:** Holiness of heart and life; works of piety (prayer, Scripture, sacraments) and works of mercy; social holiness and service; class meetings and accountability; hymn singing.

**What it forbids or discourages:** John Wesley's General Rules: do no harm, do good, attend upon the ordinances of God. Holiness churches traditionally discourage alcohol, gambling and worldly amusements; occult practice; sexual sin.

**Family, sex and identity:** Women are ordained across most Wesleyan bodies. The United Methodist Church removed its restrictions on LGBTQ clergy and same-sex weddings in 2024; Global Methodist, Free Methodist, Wesleyan and Nazarene churches hold to marriage between a man and a woman. Identity is marked by baptism, confirmation or membership vows, small groups and hymns of Charles Wesley.

**Stories, fiction and art:** Media are judged by whether they help or hinder growth in holiness and love; holiness churches have been more cautious about film and entertainment. See [Teaching on art, media and depiction](#teaching-on-art-media-and-depiction).

**Range within the tradition:** Progressive United Methodist to traditional Global Methodist and holiness churches (Nazarene, Wesleyan, Free Methodist).

**Own sources:** The Bible; John Wesley's Sermons and Notes on the New Testament; General Rules of the United Societies; UMC Book of Discipline and Social Principles; Global Methodist Book of Doctrines and Discipline; Church of the Nazarene Manual.

## Teaching on art, media and depiction

Wesley's General Rules ask members to avoid "diversions as cannot be used in the name of the Lord Jesus" and books that do not "tend to the knowledge or love of God." Holiness churches keep this caution; the United Methodist Church is much more open today.
