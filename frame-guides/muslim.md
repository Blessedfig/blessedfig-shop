# Muslim

## Summary

*Draft from general knowledge; verify against the listed sources.*

**Core beliefs:** There is one God (Allah), Creator of all, without partner (tawhid). Humans are created to worship God and act as stewards on earth; after death comes the Day of Judgment, then paradise or hell. Authority rests on the Qur'an as God's word, the Sunnah (example) of the Prophet Muhammad in hadith, and scholarly interpretation (fiqh) through the schools of law; Shia Muslims also follow the Imams and their marja'.

**What it values:** The Five Pillars (declaration of faith, five daily prayers, zakat, Ramadan fasting, Hajj); taqwa (God-consciousness), honesty, modesty, kindness to parents, generosity, patience and justice.

**What it forbids or discourages:** Shirk (associating partners with God), sorcery (sihr), alcohol and intoxicants, pork, gambling, sex outside marriage, immodest images, lying, backbiting and mockery of the sacred. Many scholars discourage music and depictions of living beings, to different degrees; depicting prophets is widely forbidden.

**Family, sex and identity:** Marriage (nikah) between a man and a woman is strongly encouraged; sex belongs within marriage; modest dress for men and women (hijab for women as most scholars teach); distinct complementary roles. Identity is marked by prayer, halal food, Ramadan and the two Eids, Arabic recitation and modest dress.

**Stories, fiction and art:** Storytelling with a moral purpose is valued (the Qur'an calls Joseph's story "the best of stories"). Fiction is judged by its content: magic shown as real power, prophets depicted, or immorality glamorized are common concerns. See [Teaching on art, media and depiction](#teaching-on-art-media-and-depiction).

**Range within the tradition:** Sunni and Shia; the four Sunni schools; Salafi to Sufi; observance and views on music, images and animation vary widely.

**Own sources:** The Qur'an; Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim; the four Sunni madhhabs and Ja'fari fiqh; Islamic Society of North America; Fiqh Council of North America; Al-Azhar.

**Range within the tradition:** Sunni, Shia and diverse cultural practices; observance levels vary.

**Own sources:** Qur'an and Hadith; fatwa councils (e.g. Fiqh Council of North America); Muslim parenting and media guides.

| Death, afterlife and suicide | Afterlife and judgment; suicide forbidden. |
| Lying and deception | Lying forbidden, with narrow exceptions (reconciliation, war). |

## Teaching on art, media and depiction

Scholars differ on images of living beings; depicting prophets (including Jesus and Moses as prophets), God or angels is widely forbidden. Music is debated. "Lowering the gaze" (Qur'an 24:30-31) shapes views on immodest content, and some read 31:6 ("idle talk") as caution about entertainment.
