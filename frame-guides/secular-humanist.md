# Secular humanist

## Summary

*Draft from general knowledge; verify against the listed sources.*

**Core beliefs:** The universe is natural and knowable through science; humans are the product of evolution. There is no supernatural creator or afterlife, so meaning is made in this life. Authority rests on reason, evidence and shared human experience, not revelation.

**What it values:** Critical thinking and inquiry; compassion and empathy; human rights and dignity; freedom of conscience; democracy; science education; personal responsibility and living ethically without religion.

**What it forbids or discourages:** No formal prohibitions; harm, cruelty, dishonesty, coercion and dogmatism are rejected on ethical grounds. Pseudoscience presented as fact is a concern.

**Family, sex and identity:** Equality of the sexes; consenting relationships, including same-sex marriage, are affirmed; children are raised to think for themselves. Identity is marked loosely: humanist celebrations (naming ceremonies, weddings, memorials), Darwin Day, local humanist communities.

**Stories, fiction and art:** Fiction, including myths, gods and magic, is welcomed as imaginative and cultural; concern is with stories that present superstition as truth or celebrate cruelty. See [Teaching on art, media and depiction](#teaching-on-art-media-and-depiction).

**Range within the tradition:** From organized humanists to loosely non-religious families; some emphasize critique of religion, others coexist with religious relatives.

**Own sources:** Humanist Manifesto I-III (American Humanist Association); Amsterdam Declaration (Humanists International, 2022); Council for Secular Humanism, A Secular Humanist Declaration (1980); Humanists UK.

## Teaching on art, media and depiction

Free inquiry and opposition to censorship are valued, with critical thinking about what is shown. Concern centers on cruelty, exploitation and pseudoscience presented as fact.
