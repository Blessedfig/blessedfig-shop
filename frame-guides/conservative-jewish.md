# Conservative Jewish

## Summary

*Draft from general knowledge; verify against the listed sources.*

**Core beliefs:** One God created the world and entered into covenant with the Jewish people; Torah is understood as divinely inspired and transmitted through a human, historical process. Halakha is binding and develops over time through rabbinic interpretation. Beliefs about the afterlife (olam haba, resurrection) are affirmed in the liturgy and interpreted variously. Authority rests with Torah, Talmud and codes, interpreted by the Rabbinical Assembly's Committee on Jewish Law and Standards (CJLS) and the local rabbi (mara d'atra).

**What it values:** Torah study, Shabbat, kashrut, prayer, tikkun olam (repairing the world), tzedakah, Hebrew and Jewish peoplehood, Israel, lifelong learning.

**What it forbids or discourages:** Idolatry and sorcery; Shabbat desecration; non-kosher food; harmful and vulgar speech (lashon hara, nivul peh); degrading sexual content.

**Family, sex and identity:** Marriage is kiddushin, a sacred covenant; since 2006 the CJLS permits same-sex marriage and LGBTQ rabbis, and men and women are egalitarian in ritual in most congregations. Identity is marked by brit milah, bar/bat mitzvah, Shabbat and holidays, kashrut and day schools or Hebrew school, Camp Ramah and USY.

**Stories, fiction and art:** Fiction and the arts are broadly welcomed and judged by Jewish ethical values on speech, modesty and dignity. See [Teaching on art, media and depiction](#teaching-on-art-media-and-depiction).

**Range within the tradition:** Observance varies widely among members; some congregations are more traditional (non-egalitarian), others closer to Reform.

**Own sources:** Emet Ve-Emunah: Statement of Principles of Conservative Judaism (1988); CJLS responsa; Rabbinical Assembly; United Synagogue of Conservative Judaism; Etz Hayim Torah commentary; Siddur Lev Shalem.

**Range within the tradition:** Conservative (Masorti) movement: halakha seen as binding and evolving; the Rabbinical Assembly's Committee on Jewish Law and Standards (CJLS) issues rulings.

**Own sources:** Torah and Talmud; CJLS responsa; "Emet Ve-Emunah: Statement of Principles of Conservative Judaism" (1988).

## Teaching on art, media and depiction

No formal media rules. Values such as modesty, kind speech and Jewish dignity are applied by families; antisemitic portrayals are a concern.
