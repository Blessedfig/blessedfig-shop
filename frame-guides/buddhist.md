# Buddhist

## Summary

*Draft from general knowledge; verify against the listed sources.*

**Core beliefs:** The Buddha taught the Four Noble Truths: life involves suffering (dukkha), suffering arises from craving, it can end (nirvana), and the Noble Eightfold Path leads there. There is no creator God in most schools; beings are reborn according to karma until liberation. Authority rests on the Buddha's teachings (Dharma), the scriptures of each school and the community of monks and teachers (Sangha): the Three Jewels.

**What it values:** Compassion (karuna), loving-kindness (metta), wisdom, mindfulness and meditation, generosity, non-attachment and right livelihood.

**What it forbids or discourages:** The Five Precepts: no killing or harming living beings, no stealing, no sexual misconduct, no false speech, no intoxicants that cloud the mind. Media that stir hatred, craving or cruelty are discouraged.

**Family, sex and identity:** Marriage is a civil and family matter rather than a sacrament; sexual misconduct (harmful or exploitative sex) is to be avoided; views on same-sex relationships vary by teacher and culture. Identity is marked by taking refuge in the Three Jewels, temple visits, festivals such as Vesak, offerings and meditation.

**Stories, fiction and art:** The Jataka tales and other teaching stories use fiction freely; images of the Buddha and deities are devotional aids. Violence is judged by the intention and karma it shows; gods and spirits are common in Buddhist cosmology. See [Teaching on art, media and depiction](#teaching-on-art-media-and-depiction).

**Range within the tradition:** Theravada, Mahayana (including Zen and Pure Land) and Vajrayana (Tibetan); monastic and lay practice; Asian heritage and Western convert communities.

**Own sources:** Pali Canon (Tipitaka), including the Dhammapada; Mahayana sutras (Lotus, Heart); Jataka tales; teachings of the Dalai Lama and other school leaders; Buddhist Churches of America.

**Range within the tradition:** Theravada, Mahayana, Vajrayana and Japanese schools; lay practice varies.

**Own sources:** The Five Precepts; Dhammapada; teachings of the various schools; Buddhist parenting resources.

## Teaching on art, media and depiction

Right speech and mindfulness of what feeds the mind guide choices. On observance days lay people often take the eight precepts, which include abstaining from shows and entertainment. Monastics avoid entertainment.
