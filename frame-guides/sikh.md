# Sikh

## Summary

*Draft from general knowledge; verify against the listed sources.*

**Core beliefs:** There is one God (Ik Onkar), the formless Creator, present in all creation. Humans pass through cycles of birth and death by karma; the goal is liberation (mukti) through God's grace by remembering God's Name (Naam) and overcoming ego (haumai). Authority rests on the Guru Granth Sahib, the eternal living Guru, and the Khalsa Panth; the Akal Takht issues the Sikh Rehat Maryada (code of conduct).

**What it values:** Naam japna (remembering God), kirat karni (honest work) and vand chakna (sharing); seva (selfless service); langar (free community kitchen); equality of all people; courage to defend the oppressed.

**What it forbids or discourages:** For initiated Sikhs, the four kurehits: cutting hair, eating ritually slaughtered (halal/kutha) meat, adultery, and tobacco. Alcohol and drugs, superstition, idol worship and caste discrimination are rejected.

**Family, sex and identity:** Marriage (Anand Karaj) is a spiritual union; equality of men and women; modesty. Identity is marked by the Five Ks for the initiated (kesh, kara, kanga, kachera, kirpan), the turban (dastaar), the names Singh and Kaur, amrit initiation and the gurdwara.

**Stories, fiction and art:** Sakhis (stories of the Gurus) and martyrdom history are central; depicting the Gurus by actors is widely rejected, and the Akal Takht has objected to animated or acted portrayals. Disrespect to the turban, kirpan or Guru Granth Sahib is a key concern. See [Teaching on art, media and depiction](#teaching-on-art-media-and-depiction).

**Range within the tradition:** Amritdhari (initiated) and Keshdhari Sikhs to Sahajdhari and diaspora families; groups such as Namdharis differ further.

**Own sources:** Guru Granth Sahib; Dasam Granth (status debated); Sikh Rehat Maryada (Akal Takht and SGPC); Akal Takht edicts (hukamnamas); Shiromani Gurdwara Parbandhak Committee.

## Teaching on art, media and depiction

The Rehat Maryada does not address fiction. Depicting the Gurus in images or film is opposed by the SGPC. The five vices (lust, anger, greed, attachment, ego) shown as admirable are a concern.
