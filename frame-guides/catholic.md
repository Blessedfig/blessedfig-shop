# Catholic

## Summary

*Draft from general knowledge; verify against the listed sources.*

**Core beliefs:** God created the world good, out of love; humans, made in God's image, are called to share eternal life with God through Jesus Christ. Death is followed by judgment: heaven, purgatory as purification, or hell as self-chosen separation from God; the dead will rise at the end of time. Authority rests on Scripture and Sacred Tradition, interpreted by the Magisterium (the Pope and bishops in communion with him).

**What it values:** Faith, hope and charity; the seven sacraments, above all the Eucharist (Sunday Mass); prayer; the works of mercy; the dignity of every human life; the common good and care for the poor.

**What it forbids or discourages:** The Ten Commandments as taught in the Catechism: idolatry, occult practices and divination, blasphemy, murder, sexual sin and pornography, theft, lying and calumny. Media that glamorize sin or degrade persons are discouraged.

**Family, sex and identity:** Marriage is a lifelong sacramental union of one man and one woman, open to children; sex belongs within marriage; chastity and modesty are virtues for all. The Church teaches that sex is given by God and not chosen. Identity is marked by baptism, first Communion, confirmation, saints' days, the liturgical year and Friday abstinence in Lent.

**Stories, fiction and art:** The Church has long commissioned art and values beauty as a path to God. Fiction may depict evil when it serves truth and does not invite the viewer to share in it; occult practice, not its depiction in a story, is what is forbidden. See [Teaching on art, media and depiction](#teaching-on-art-media-and-depiction).

**Range within the tradition:** Traditionalist, mainstream and progressive Catholics differ on liturgy, media strictness and emphasis on social versus moral teaching.

**Own sources:** Catechism of the Catholic Church; the Bible (New American Bible in US use); Second Vatican Council documents (Inter Mirifica, Gaudium et Spes); Pontifical Council for Social Communications, Aetatis Novae; John Paul II, Letter to Artists (1999); USCCB.

**Range within the tradition:** Practice ranges from traditionalist to progressive parishes; the Catechism is the shared reference.

**Own sources:** Catechism of the Catholic Church (CCC); papal documents; USCCB movie and media reviews; Pontifical Council for Social Communications, "Ethics in Communications" (2000).

## Teaching on art, media and depiction

Vatican II's Inter Mirifica (1963), Communio et Progressio (1971) and "Ethics in Communications" (2000) hold that media should serve truth, dignity and the common good. Depicting evil is acceptable when it serves understanding and is not made attractive (Inter Mirifica 7). John Paul II's Letter to Artists (1999) praises art that seeks beauty. CCC 2493-2499 and 2525 address media and permissiveness.
