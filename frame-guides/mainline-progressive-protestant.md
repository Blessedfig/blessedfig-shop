# Mainline progressive Protestant

## Summary

*Draft from general knowledge; verify against the listed sources.*

**Core beliefs:** God created the world and loves it; Jesus Christ reveals God and reconciles humanity; Christians live in hope of resurrection and God's coming reign of justice and peace. Many hold a wider hope for salvation. Authority rests on Scripture read in its historical context, alongside tradition, reason and experience, and decided through denominational assemblies.

**What it values:** Love of God and neighbor; justice, peace and care for creation; inclusion and hospitality; worship and sacraments; service, education and ecumenical cooperation.

**What it forbids or discourages:** Discrimination, exploitation and abuse; violence glorified; harmful stereotypes; dishonesty. Few rules about entertainment itself.

**Family, sex and identity:** Women and LGBTQ people ordained in most of these churches; same-sex marriage affirmed (ELCA, Episcopal, PC(USA), UCC, UMC since 2024); emphasis on faithful, consensual, loving relationships. Identity is marked by baptism, confirmation, the church year and social-justice work.

**Stories, fiction and art:** Fiction and the arts are valued as ways to explore faith and human experience; content concerns focus on cruelty, bigotry and exploitation rather than magic or other religions. See [Teaching on art, media and depiction](#teaching-on-art-media-and-depiction).

**Range within the tradition:** Congregations and members within each denomination range from moderate to strongly progressive.

**Own sources:** The Bible (often NRSV); ELCA social statements; PC(USA) Book of Confessions (including the Confession of Belhar); UCC Statement of Faith; The United Methodist Book of Discipline and Social Principles; Episcopal General Convention resolutions.

**Range within the tradition:** ELCA, Episcopal Church, PC(USA), UCC, UMC and Disciples of Christ in their progressive expressions.

**Own sources:** Denominational social statements (UCC, ELCA, PC(USA)); progressive Christian educators and reviewers.

## Teaching on art, media and depiction

No formal media rules. Works are engaged critically, with attention to representation, justice and how the vulnerable are treated.

Grade (S to F) = how well a series' message fits this tradition's teaching, as this tradition would judge it. It is not lowered by content intensity alone. Depiction concern = how much this tradition's teaching on depiction would raise about what is shown: Minor = little; Discuss = worth talking through; Significant = many families would hold off or read together. Note the internal range when a series falls on a dividing line.
