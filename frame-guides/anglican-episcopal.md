# Anglican / Episcopal

## Summary

*Draft from general knowledge; verify against the listed sources.*

**Core beliefs:** God the Holy Trinity created the world; salvation is through Jesus Christ; Christians hope for resurrection and eternal life. Authority rests on Scripture, interpreted with tradition and reason (the "three-legged stool"), and is expressed in the creeds and the Book of Common Prayer ("the law of prayer is the law of belief").

**What it values:** Common worship and the sacraments of baptism and Eucharist; prayer; the via media (middle way); service and justice; hospitality.

**What it forbids or discourages:** The Ten Commandments and the baptismal promises: renouncing evil, respecting the dignity of every human being; occult practice and sexual sin as defined in each province.

**Family, sex and identity:** Marriage is a lifelong covenant; the Episcopal Church allows same-sex marriage (2015), while ACNA and most of the Global South provinces hold to marriage between a man and a woman. Identity is marked by baptism, confirmation, the liturgical year and the Prayer Book.

**Stories, fiction and art:** A strong tradition of literature, music and art (including C.S. Lewis as an Anglican writer); fiction is judged by its moral vision. See [Teaching on art, media and depiction](#teaching-on-art-media-and-depiction).

**Range within the tradition:** Anglo-Catholic, broad church and evangelical wings; progressive Episcopal Church to conservative ACNA and GAFCON provinces.

**Own sources:** The Bible; Book of Common Prayer (1662; Episcopal 1979; ACNA 2019); Thirty-Nine Articles; Lambeth Conference resolutions; Episcopal Church General Convention; Catechism (An Outline of the Faith).

**Range within the tradition:** Episcopal Church (progressive) to ACNA and global Anglicans (conservative).

**Own sources:** Book of Common Prayer; Thirty-Nine Articles; Lambeth resolutions; ACNA catechism "To Be a Christian."

## Teaching on art, media and depiction

A strong literary tradition (C.S. Lewis, Dorothy Sayers) sees imagination as a gift. Works are judged by how they frame good and evil rather than by what they show. Practice ranges from progressive to conservative.

Grade (S to F) = how well a series' message fits this tradition's teaching, as this tradition would judge it. It is not lowered by content intensity alone. Depiction concern = how much this tradition's teaching on depiction would raise about what is shown: Minor = little; Discuss = worth talking through; Significant = many families would hold off or read together. Note the internal range when a series falls on a dividing line.
