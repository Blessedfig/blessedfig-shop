# Hindu

## Summary

*Draft from general knowledge; verify against the listed sources.*

**Core beliefs:** The eternal reality (Brahman) underlies all; the universe passes through cycles of creation and dissolution. The soul (atman) is reborn according to karma until liberation (moksha). Life pursues four aims: dharma (duty), artha (prosperity), kama (pleasure) and moksha. Authority rests on the Vedas and Upanishads (shruti), the epics, Puranas and law texts (smriti), and living teachers (gurus) and sampradayas (lineages).

**What it values:** Dharma suited to one's stage and role; devotion (bhakti) to God in many forms; ahimsa (non-harm); truthfulness; respect for parents, elders and teachers; puja, festivals and pilgrimage; yoga and meditation.

**What it forbids or discourages:** Harm to living beings (many are vegetarian; beef is widely avoided); intoxicants for many; disrespect to deities, scriptures and elders; adharma such as deceit and cruelty.

**Family, sex and identity:** Marriage is a sacred samskara; family and lineage are central; modesty is valued, with wide regional variation. Identity is marked by samskaras (naming, sacred thread, marriage), festivals such as Diwali and Holi, home shrines, temple and dietary practice.

**Stories, fiction and art:** The Ramayana, Mahabharata and Puranas are rich in battles, gods, demons and magic, so these are familiar in stories. Concern is mainly disrespectful portrayal of deities and sacred symbols. See [Teaching on art, media and depiction](#teaching-on-art-media-and-depiction).

**Range within the tradition:** Vaishnava, Shaiva, Shakta and Smarta traditions; diaspora and Indian practice; household customs vary by region and caste community.

**Own sources:** The Vedas and Upanishads; Bhagavad Gita; Ramayana and Mahabharata; Manusmriti and other Dharmashastras (applied variously); Hindu American Foundation; teachings of the sampradayas (e.g. ISKCON, BAPS, Chinmaya Mission).

**Range within the tradition:** Highly diverse: Vaishnava, Shaiva, Shakta, Smarta traditions; many regional practices.

**Own sources:** Bhagavad Gita; Dharmashastra; ISKCON and other sampradaya teachings; Hindu American Foundation resources.

## Teaching on art, media and depiction

Classical aesthetics (rasa theory, Natya Shastra) values art that evokes emotion. Deities are freely depicted, but mockery or misuse of them is objected to. Some apply the Gita's sattvic, rajasic and tamasic qualities (chapter 17) to media.
