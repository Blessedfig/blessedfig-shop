# Unitarian Universalist

## Summary

*Draft from general knowledge; verify against the listed sources.*

**Core beliefs:** No required creed; members draw on many sources, including direct experience, world religions, Jewish and Christian teaching, humanism, science and earth-centered traditions. Views on God and afterlife range from theist to atheist; historically, Universalists taught that all are saved. Authority rests with individual conscience and the democratic congregation.

**What it values:** As revised in the UUA bylaws (2024), love at the center, with the shared values of interdependence, equity, transformation, pluralism, generosity and justice; earlier the Seven Principles, including the inherent worth and dignity of every person and a free and responsible search for truth and meaning.

**What it forbids or discourages:** No religious prohibitions; oppression, cruelty and discrimination are opposed on moral grounds.

**Family, sex and identity:** LGBTQ people fully welcomed (Welcoming Congregation program); same-sex marriage celebrated; Our Whole Lives comprehensive sexuality education for children and youth. Identity is marked by congregational life, the flaming chalice symbol, child dedications, coming-of-age programs and social action.

**Stories, fiction and art:** Stories from many religions and cultures are taught; fiction, myth and magic are welcomed, with concern for cruelty, bigotry and exclusion. See [Teaching on art, media and depiction](#teaching-on-art-media-and-depiction).

**Range within the tradition:** Humanist, Christian, pagan, Buddhist and Jewish UUs; members differ on the 2024 revision of the Principles.

**Own sources:** UUA Bylaws Article II (Principles and Purposes, as revised 2024); Seven Principles and Six Sources; Singing the Living Tradition; Our Whole Lives curriculum; uua.org.

**Range within the tradition:** Unitarian Universalist Association congregations; members include humanists, theists, pagans, Buddhists and others.

**Own sources:** UUA Principles and Sources (Seven Principles, proposed Eighth Principle); 2024 revision of UUA Article II (Love with six values: interdependence, pluralism, justice, transformation, generosity, equity); Our Whole Lives (OWL) sexuality curriculum.

## Teaching on art, media and depiction

No formal media rules. The principles (inherent worth and dignity, justice, free search for truth) are applied, with concern about stereotypes and attention to age-appropriateness.

Grade (S to F) = how well a series' message fits this tradition's teaching, as this tradition would judge it. It is not lowered by content intensity alone. Depiction concern = how much this tradition's teaching on depiction would raise about what is shown: Minor = little; Discuss = worth talking through; Significant = many families would hold off or read together.
