# Reform Jewish

## Summary

*Draft from general knowledge; verify against the listed sources.*

**Core beliefs:** One God, in covenant with the Jewish people; Torah is a foundational, evolving, human response to the divine, studied with modern scholarship. Ethical monotheism and the prophetic call to justice are central. Beliefs about the afterlife vary; the 1999 platform speaks of hope for redemption and a messianic age rather than a personal Messiah. Authority is personal autonomy informed by tradition, study and community, with guidance from the Central Conference of American Rabbis (CCAR).

**What it values:** Tikkun olam (social justice), Torah study, prayer, Shabbat and holidays as chosen practice, tzedakah, inclusion, Israel and Jewish peoplehood.

**What it forbids or discourages:** Few ritual prohibitions are treated as binding; ethical commandments are: no cruelty, deceit, exploitation or harmful speech. Antisemitic and hateful content is a key concern.

**Family, sex and identity:** Full gender equality; LGBTQ people fully included and same-sex marriage celebrated; interfaith families welcomed and patrilineal descent recognized (1983). Identity is marked by naming and brit milah, bar/bat mitzvah, confirmation, Shabbat, holidays and congregational life.

**Stories, fiction and art:** Fiction and the arts are embraced; content is judged by ethical values rather than ritual law. See [Teaching on art, media and depiction](#teaching-on-art-media-and-depiction).

**Range within the tradition:** Some members are more ritually observant, others mainly cultural; views on God range from traditional theism to naturalism.

**Own sources:** CCAR platforms (Pittsburgh 1885, Columbus 1937, San Francisco 1976, Pittsburgh 1999 Statement of Principles); CCAR responsa; Union for Reform Judaism; The Torah: A Modern Commentary; Mishkan T'filah.

**Range within the tradition:** Union for Reform Judaism and Central Conference of American Rabbis; personal autonomy informed by tradition.

**Own sources:** Pittsburgh Platform (1885); Columbus Platform (1937); "A Statement of Principles for Reform Judaism" (Pittsburgh, 1999); CCAR and URJ resolutions.

## Teaching on art, media and depiction

No formal media rules. Informed autonomy, tikkun olam and attention to antisemitism and stereotypes guide choices.

Grade (S to F) = how well a series' message fits this tradition's teaching, as this tradition would judge it. It is not lowered by content intensity alone. Depiction concern = how much this tradition's teaching on depiction would raise about what is shown: Minor = little; Discuss = worth talking through; Significant = many families would hold off or read together.
