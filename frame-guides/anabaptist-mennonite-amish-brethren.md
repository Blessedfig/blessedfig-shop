# Anabaptist (Mennonite, Amish, Brethren)

## Summary

*Draft from general knowledge; verify against the listed sources.*

**Core beliefs:** God created the world; salvation comes by grace through faith in Jesus Christ, shown in a life of discipleship (Nachfolge). Believers look to eternal life with God and the resurrection. Authority rests on the Bible, especially the Sermon on the Mount, read together by the gathered church; Old Order groups also follow the community's Ordnung (unwritten rules).

**What it values:** Following Jesus in daily life; adult (believer's) baptism; nonresistance and peacemaking; humility (Gelassenheit, yielding to God); simplicity; mutual aid; service; community discipline.

**What it forbids or discourages:** Violence, military service and revenge; swearing oaths; pride and worldliness; for Old Order groups, much modern technology and outside media; sexual immorality; occult practice.

**Family, sex and identity:** Marriage is one man and one woman for life; large, close families in conservative groups; plain dress and head coverings for women in Amish and conservative Mennonite communities; distinct gender roles in conservative groups. Identity is marked by adult baptism, plain dress, Pennsylvania German among the Amish, separation from the world and service work (Mennonite Central Committee).

**Stories, fiction and art:** Conservative communities read mainly Scripture, the Martyrs Mirror and wholesome stories and avoid TV and film; more modern Mennonites engage with literature and art. Violence shown as heroic conflicts with nonresistance. See [Teaching on art, media and depiction](#teaching-on-art-media-and-depiction).

**Range within the tradition:** Old Order Amish and conservative Mennonites (strict separation) to Mennonite Church USA and Church of the Brethren (engaged, more progressive).

**Own sources:** The Bible; Schleitheim Confession (1527); Dordrecht Confession (1632); Martyrs Mirror; Confession of Faith in a Mennonite Perspective (1995); Mennonite Church USA.

## Teaching on art, media and depiction

Separation from the world (Schleitheim Confession, Article 4) leads Old Order groups to avoid TV and most popular media. Mennonite Church USA is much more open. Violence shown as heroic is the central concern across the range.
