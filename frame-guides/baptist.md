# Baptist

## Summary

*Draft from general knowledge; verify against the listed sources.*

**Core beliefs:** God created all things; humanity fell into sin and is saved by grace through faith in Jesus Christ alone. Believers will live with God forever in heaven; those who reject Christ face eternal separation in hell. The Bible is the sole and sufficient authority; each local congregation is self-governing, and every believer may read and interpret Scripture (soul competency).

**What it values:** Personal conversion and believer's baptism by immersion; evangelism and missions; Bible reading and prayer; church membership; religious liberty; holy living.

**What it forbids or discourages:** Sexual immorality and pornography, drunkenness (many Baptists abstain from alcohol), occult practice, blasphemy and profanity, and media that glorify sin.

**Family, sex and identity:** Marriage is one man and one woman for life; sex belongs within marriage; the Baptist Faith and Message 2000 teaches complementary roles of husband and wife and limits the office of pastor to men. Identity is marked by a testimony of faith, believer's baptism, Sunday worship, Sunday school and church community.

**Stories, fiction and art:** Stories and media are judged by whether they honor God and do not lead into sin; fantasy is accepted by many, while some avoid magic and occult themes. See [Teaching on art, media and depiction](#teaching-on-art-media-and-depiction).

**Range within the tradition:** Southern Baptist, Independent Fundamental (stricter, often King James only), American Baptist (more progressive) and Reformed Baptist; each congregation sets its own practice.

**Own sources:** The Bible; Baptist Faith and Message 2000; Southern Baptist Convention resolutions; Ethics and Religious Liberty Commission; London Baptist Confession of 1689 (Reformed Baptists).

**Range within the tradition:** Wide range: Southern Baptist, Independent Fundamental, American Baptist (more progressive), Reformed Baptist. Each congregation is self-governing.

**Own sources:** Baptist Faith and Message 2000 (SBC); 1689 London Baptist Confession (Reformed Baptists); SBC resolutions on alcohol, entertainment and media; Focus on the Family / Plugged In (evangelical media guide used widely by Baptists).

| Topic | Usual position |
|---|---|
| Alcohol | Many Baptists are teetotal (SBC resolutions urge abstinence, e.g. 2006); others drink in moderation. Alcohol shown positively is often a concern. |
| Tobacco and drugs | Drugs and drunkenness condemned; tobacco discouraged. |
| Magic, occult and spirits | Witchcraft and occult forbidden (Deuteronomy 18:10-12); fantasy magic is debated, and many families are cautious, especially with spells, summoning and spirits. |
| Other religions and gods shown | Exclusive claims of Christ (John 14:6); other religions' gods shown as real or good can be a concern. |
| Violence, war and revenge | Just war generally accepted; revenge rejected (Romans 12:19); graphic violence a concern. |
| Sexuality, nudity and modesty | Strong emphasis on purity and modesty; sexual content and fan service often a major concern. |
| Same-sex relationships and gender identity | Marriage is one man and one woman (BF&M 2000, Article XVIII); same-sex romance and gender transition usually a significant concern. |
| Gender roles and family | Complementarian roles in home and church (BF&M 2000). |
| Language and blasphemy | Profanity and taking God's name in vain a common concern (Ephesians 4:29; Exodus 20:7). |
| Authority, parents and rebellion | Obedience to parents (Ephesians 6:1); respect for authority. |
| Death, afterlife and suicide | Salvation by faith; heaven and hell real; suicide treated with grief and pastoral care. |
| Lying and deception | Lying sinful (Proverbs 12:22); deceptive heroes worth discussing. |

## Teaching on art, media and depiction

Philippians 4:8 ("whatever is true... pure... lovely") is the common test. SBC resolutions have criticized entertainment content (for example the 1997 Disney boycott). Soul liberty means each family decides, and Plugged In is widely used.

Grade (S to F) = how well a series' message fits this tradition's teaching, as this tradition would judge it. It is not lowered by content intensity alone. Depiction concern = how much this tradition's teaching on depiction would raise about what is shown: Minor = little; Discuss = worth talking through; Significant = many families would hold off or read together.
