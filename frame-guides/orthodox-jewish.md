# Orthodox Jewish

## Summary

*Draft from general knowledge; verify against the listed sources.*

**Core beliefs:** One God created the world and gave the Torah, written and oral, to Israel at Sinai. Orthodox Judaism centers on halakha (Jewish law) as binding and divinely given; the purpose of life is to serve God through the mitzvot (commandments), Torah study and refining one's character. Belief in reward and punishment, the coming of the Messiah, the world to come (olam haba) and resurrection of the dead (Maimonides' Thirteen Principles). Authority rests with the Torah, the Talmud, the codes and recognized rabbinic decisors (poskim). The focus is inward: on living the community's own law and learning tradition rather than on outside culture.

**What it values:** Torah study (talmud Torah) as a supreme value; Shabbat and holiday observance; kashrut; prayer; acts of loving-kindness (chesed) and charity (tzedakah); honoring parents; guarding one's speech.

**What it forbids or discourages:** Idolatry, sorcery and divination; desecrating Shabbat; forbidden foods; lashon hara (harmful speech) and nivul peh (vulgar speech); immodest images and sexual content; time-wasting entertainment that displaces Torah study (bittul Torah).

**Family, sex and identity:** Marriage between a man and a woman is a central mitzvah; family purity laws govern marital relations; men and women have distinct ritual roles; tzniut (modesty) in dress and conduct. Identity is marked by brit milah, bar/bat mitzvah, kippah and tzitzit for men, covered hair for married women in most communities, kosher home and the Jewish calendar.

**Stories, fiction and art:** Stories in the tradition are mainly Torah, midrash and tales of the sages; outside fiction is judged by halakhic concerns about speech, modesty and idolatry. See [Teaching on art, media and depiction](#teaching-on-art-media-and-depiction).

**Range within the tradition:** Modern Orthodox families engage with secular education and media selectively; Haredi (Hasidic and Litvish) communities restrict them much more; each follows its own posek.

**Own sources:** Torah and Talmud; Maimonides' Mishneh Torah and Thirteen Principles; Shulchan Aruch with Mishnah Berurah; Chafetz Chaim (laws of speech); Orthodox Union; Agudath Israel of America.

**Range within the tradition:** Modern Orthodox to Haredi (Hasidic and Litvish); communities follow their own rabbinic authorities (poskim). Many Haredi communities are strongly inward-focused.

**Own sources:** Torah and Talmud; Shulchan Aruch and Mishnah Berurah; rulings of recognized poskim; Orthodox Union and Agudath Israel guidance.

| Topic | Usual position |
|---|---|
| Alcohol | Wine is part of ritual (Kiddush, Purim); drunkenness otherwise discouraged. |
| Tobacco and drugs | Drug abuse prohibited (guarding health, Deuteronomy 4:15); many poskim now prohibit smoking. |
| Magic, occult and spirits | Sorcery and necromancy forbidden (Deuteronomy 18:10-11); Haredi families often avoid fantasy magic, Modern Orthodox families are more accepting. |
| Other religions and gods shown | Idolatry (avodah zarah) shown approvingly is a concern; antisemitic tropes a key concern. |
| Violence, war and revenge | Self-defense permitted; revenge forbidden (Leviticus 19:18). |
| Sexuality, nudity and modesty | Strict modesty (tzniut); sexual content and immodest images a significant concern. |
| Same-sex relationships and gender identity | Same-sex acts forbidden (Leviticus 18:22); same-sex romance usually a significant concern. |
| Gender roles and family | Distinct roles for men and women in ritual and family. |
| Language and blasphemy | Vulgar speech (nivul peh) and harmful speech (lashon hara) avoided; misuse of God's name serious. |
| Authority, parents and rebellion | Honor parents (Exodus 20:12); respect for rabbinic authority. |
| Death, afterlife and suicide | World to come and resurrection; suicide traditionally grave, with compassionate rulings in practice. |
| Lying and deception | "Keep far from falsehood" (Exodus 23:7). |

## Teaching on art, media and depiction

Lashon hara, nivul peh and tzniut govern what is watched and read. Many Haredi communities restrict secular media and the internet to protect the home and community; Modern Orthodox families are selective but engaged. Torah study is valued above entertainment.
