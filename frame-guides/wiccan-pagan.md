# Wiccan / Pagan

## Summary

*Draft from general knowledge; verify against the listed sources.*

**Core beliefs:** The divine is present in nature, often honored as a Goddess and God or many deities. Life follows the seasonal Wheel of the Year; many Wiccans believe in reincarnation and a restful otherworld (the Summerland). There is no central scripture or authority; covens, traditions and individual experience guide practice.

**What it values:** Reverence for nature; personal responsibility; balance; the eight sabbats and moon rituals (esbats); magic understood as focusing will and energy; freedom of practice.

**What it forbids or discourages:** The Wiccan Rede: "An it harm none, do what ye will"; many also hold the Threefold Law (what you send out returns). Harmful or manipulative magic is rejected.

**Family, sex and identity:** Sexuality is sacred and positive among consenting adults; most traditions are LGBTQ-affirming; handfasting as marriage. Identity is marked by initiation or self-dedication, the pentacle, sabbats and wiccanings (child blessings).

**Stories, fiction and art:** Myth, magic and other gods are welcome in stories; concern is with portrayals of witches and pagans as evil or Satanic. See [Teaching on art, media and depiction](#teaching-on-art-media-and-depiction).

**Range within the tradition:** Gardnerian and Alexandrian Wicca, eclectic Wicca, Druidry, Heathenry and other reconstructionist paths; no central authority.

**Own sources:** The Wiccan Rede; Charge of the Goddess (Doreen Valiente); Gerald Gardner, Witchcraft Today; Scott Cunningham, Wicca: A Guide for the Solitary Practitioner; Covenant of the Goddess; The Troth (Heathenry).

## Teaching on art, media and depiction

No central rule. The Rede ("harm none") guides choices, and the main concern is accurate, respectful portrayals of witches and pagans.
