# Conservative / traditional

## Summary

*Draft from general knowledge; verify against the listed sources.*

**Core beliefs:** This is a values frame rather than a religion: families of any faith or none who see inherited institutions (family, faith, nation, community) as carrying tested wisdom. Views on creation and afterlife come from the family's own faith. Authority rests on tradition, the family, religious communities, constitutional order and lived experience, with change made carefully.

**What it values:** Duty, honor, loyalty, self-reliance, hard work, patriotism, respect for parents and elders, courage, faith communities and personal responsibility.

**What it forbids or discourages:** Disrespect for parents and legitimate authority; glamorized crime, drug use and promiscuity; vulgarity; mockery of faith and country; content seen as pushing political messages onto children.

**Family, sex and identity:** The married two-parent family is the foundation of society; sexual restraint and modesty; many see sex differences as natural; parents as the primary authority over children's upbringing. Identity is marked by family, faith, national holidays, military and community service.

**Stories, fiction and art:** Classic stories of heroism, sacrifice and good defeating evil are prized; violence in a just cause is acceptable; concern is with moral relativism, nihilism and gratuitous content. See [Teaching on art, media and depiction](#teaching-on-art-media-and-depiction).

**Range within the tradition:** Religious and secular conservatives; libertarian-leaning to communitarian; families differ on fantasy, magic and violence.

**Own sources:** No single body. Common references: Edmund Burke, Reflections on the Revolution in France; Russell Kirk, The Conservative Mind; the US Declaration of Independence and Constitution; William Bennett, The Book of Virtues; Parents Television and Media Council.

**Range within the tradition:** Families of any or no faith who value tradition, duty, patriotism and family structure.

**Own sources:** Plugged In; Common Sense Media; Heritage and family-policy publications; classical education resources.

## Teaching on art, media and depiction

The classical ideal of "the good, the true and the beautiful" guides choices, with concern about media that undermines family, faith, duty or national tradition.

Grade (S to F) = how well a series' message fits this tradition's teaching, as this tradition would judge it. It is not lowered by content intensity alone. Depiction concern = how much this tradition's teaching on depiction would raise about what is shown: Minor = little; Discuss = worth talking through; Significant = many families would hold off or read together.
