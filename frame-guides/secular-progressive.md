# Secular progressive

## Summary

*Draft from general knowledge; verify against the listed sources.*

**Core beliefs:** This is a values frame rather than a religion: families who are generally non-religious and see society as improvable through inclusion, equality and collective action. Views on origins come from science; the afterlife is usually not part of the frame. Authority rests on human rights, evidence and the experience of affected communities.

**What it values:** Equality and inclusion; diversity; empathy; social and environmental justice; consent; mental health; critical thinking about power and representation.

**What it forbids or discourages:** No formal prohibitions; bigotry, racism, sexism, homophobia and transphobia, bullying, exploitation and harmful stereotypes are the main concerns in media.

**Family, sex and identity:** Many family forms are valued equally; LGBTQ identities affirmed; gender roles seen as flexible; emphasis on consent and body autonomy. Identity is marked by civic engagement, community and advocacy rather than ritual.

**Stories, fiction and art:** Fiction, myth and magic are welcomed; stories are judged by representation, who is centered, and how violence, gender and race are portrayed. See [Teaching on art, media and depiction](#teaching-on-art-media-and-depiction).

**Range within the tradition:** From liberal to left; families differ on how much to emphasize critique of older works versus enjoying them in context.

**Own sources:** No single body. Common references: Universal Declaration of Human Rights; UN Convention on the Rights of the Child; Common Sense Media reviews; GLAAD and similar advocacy organizations' media guides.

**Range within the tradition:** Non-religious families who prioritize equality, inclusion and social justice.

**Own sources:** Common Sense Media; American Academy of Pediatrics media guidance; diversity and representation reviews.

## Teaching on art, media and depiction

No religious rules. Judgments focus on representation, consent, stereotypes and age-appropriateness (Common Sense Media, AAP guidance).

Grade (S to F) = how well a series' message fits this tradition's teaching, as this tradition would judge it. It is not lowered by content intensity alone. Depiction concern = how much this tradition's teaching on depiction would raise about what is shown: Minor = little; Discuss = worth talking through; Significant = many families would hold off or read together.
