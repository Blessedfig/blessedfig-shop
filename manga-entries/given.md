**Premise.** Ritsuka Uenoyama, a 16-year-old high-school guitarist, is bored with school, his basketball club and even music. He finds classmate Mafuyu Sato, also 16, sleeping in his napping spot holding a guitar with rusted strings, and fixes it (code 1). When Mafuyu sings, Uenoyama is overwhelmed. Mafuyu joins his band with two older musicians: bassist Haruki Nakayama (22, a graduate student) and drummer Akihiko Kaji (20, a music student) (ages per Wikipedia). The guitar belonged to Mafuyu's childhood friend and first boyfriend, Yuki Yoshida (16), who died by suicide while drunk, about six months before the story begins, after a small fight with Mafuyu. Mafuyu found him (fan wiki, codes 9 and 11; Wikipedia). At the band's first live show (codes 10-11, vol. 2), Mafuyu sings his grief, and afterward Uenoyama kisses him backstage. The band takes the name Given. Vols. 3-5 follow Haruki's love for Akihiko, who is bound up with his ex and roommate, violinist Ugetsu Murata. The arc ends with the band's festival performance and Akihiko's confession to Haruki (code 28). Vols. 6-9 ("Hiragi Mix") follow Yuki's old bandmates Hiragi Kashima and Shizusumi Yagi, their band syh's major debut with Uenoyama filling in on guitar, and Mafuyu's uncertainty about going professional. Vol. 9 ends at the characters' high-school graduation (age 18 per the fan wiki). The sequel 10th Mix picks up ten years later, with Mafuyu and Ritsuka as professional musicians and still together (SuBLime 10th Mix description).

## Life message

**A good life is lived by facing loss honestly and turning it into something you can give others. Love and friendship carry a person through grief, and you cannot hold another person by force.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What do you do with a love that ended in death? Mafuyu's first boyfriend, Yuki, killed himself after a small fight. It ended with Mafuyu asking, "Would you die for me, then?" (code 11; TV Tropes). Yuki, who never drank, got drunk that day, and Mafuyu found him (code 9). Mafuyu carries Yuki's guitar but cannot play it, and cannot say what happened. The series asks whether a person can grieve without being swallowed, and whether he is allowed to love again.

**The answer the story gives.** Grief has to be given a form before it can be carried. Mafuyu cannot talk about Yuki, but he can sing. At the band's first live show, after his strings snap and Uenoyama restrings them, he sings the whole story (codes 9-11). He wanted someone to understand "even a little" how hard it was (fan wiki, code 10). The song is a gift to the listeners and a release for him, and the band's name, Given, echoes this. Uenoyama kisses him backstage, and the story treats this second love as a continuation of healing, not a betrayal of the first. Healing, as one reviewer of the last film puts it, "does not mean forgetting but learning how to carry memory into the future" (Bubbleblabber). Ten years on, the epilogue shows Mafuyu and Uenoyama as working musicians, still together (SuBLime 10th Mix).

**Characters as arguments.** Mafuyu argues that pain expressed honestly can become something good. Uenoyama argues that passion lost to boredom can come back through another person. Akihiko argues, and then disproves, the idea that enough devotion can hold someone. His years of giving to Ugetsu leave both of them miserable (TV Tropes "All Take and No Give"). When he forces himself on Haruki (code 20), he stops, apologizes and leaves. He then spends months on his violin before confessing, because he wanted to become someone worthy of Haruki (code 28; Wikipedia). Ugetsu argues that love should not be a cage, and in the end he helps Mafuyu move forward (vol. 9). Haruki argues for patience and is finally loved back. Yuki's friends Hiragi and Shizusumi argue that grief is shared: Yuki was not only Mafuyu's loss. Hiragi's guilt, and Shizusumi's question whether Hiragi also loved Yuki, drive their arc (SuBLime vol. 7).

**Symbols and motifs.** Yuki's guitar: a keepsake that must be restrung and played (code 1; TV Tropes "Tragic Keepsake"). Strings as hearts that tear and can be restrung (Uenoyama's thoughts, code 10). The skylight or ceiling hatch in Mafuyu's opening dream (code 1). Yuki's demo recording, which becomes a finished song (Wikipedia). Winter in the names: Mafuyu means "true winter" (fan wiki), and Yuki can be read as "snow" (from general knowledge; to verify). Haruki's long hair, grown "like an offering or a prayer," then cut as renunciation (code 12).

**Traditions it echoes.** The Japanese boys'-love genre, which the series both uses (beautiful leads, older-younger pairings) and widens (a mainstream TV slot, grief as the core). Art as catharsis, an idea as old as Aristotle. Buddhist readers may see impermanence and the release of attachment. Christian readers may see lament, the Psalms' practice of singing grief to someone. These are readings, not the story's claims.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Existential and humanist meaning-making are affirmed. Progressive views of same-sex love are assumed and, in vol. 6, defended against hurtful remarks. Hedonism (Akihiko's casual sex) is shown as a symptom of unhappiness. Religious and traditional views are not voiced.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-2 (codes 1-11): Mafuyu joins the band, the first live show, the first kiss. Vols. 3-5 (codes 12-28): Haruki, Akihiko and Ugetsu, the forced advance and its fallout, the festival performance and Akihiko's confession. Vols. 6-9 (codes 29-51, "Hiragi Mix"): Hiragi and Shizusumi, syh's debut, Mafuyu's doubt about going pro, Ugetsu's push, and graduation. 10th Mix: ten years later. The tone moves from raw grief to warmth and nostalgia.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Mafuyu could not speak about Yuki but could sing about him. Why might art say what words cannot?
2. Mafuyu blames himself for one sentence he said. How does the story treat that guilt, and what would you tell a friend who felt the same?
3. Akihiko gives everything to Ugetsu and it does not work. What is the difference between love and dependence?
4. Akihiko forces himself on Haruki and later works on himself before they become a couple. Does the story handle that well? What should happen after someone causes harm?
5. Your family's tradition has its own teaching about love, sex, grief and suicide. Where does this story agree with it, and where does it not?
