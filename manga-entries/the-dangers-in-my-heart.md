**Premise.** Kyotaro Ichikawa is a gloomy second-year middle school student (about 13-14) at the bottom of his class's social ladder. He reads a murder encyclopedia, carries a box cutter as a "murder totem," and imagines killing his classmates, starting with Anna Yamada, the tall class idol who works as a magazine model (Wikipedia; ANN Preview Guide; THEM Anime). Anna turns out to be odd, hungry and friendly, and the two become friends. The violent fantasies fade after the first volume (ANN manga review). Plot facts for the later volumes, as labels: mutual confession by ch. 113 and dating from vol. 9 (TV Tropes; Seven Seas vol. 9); vol. 10 a study camp; vol. 11 Anna's fame grows and Kyotaro plans his future; vol. 12 Kyotaro pauses dates to study for entrance exams; vol. 13 New Year with Anna's extended family (Seven Seas; Japanese Wikipedia, ch. 175). The story ends at their middle school graduation (Anime Hunch; Anime Corner).

## Life message

**A good life opens up when a person stops sneering at the world from a distance and lets others in. Seeing the good in other people, and learning to be kind and honest about one's own feelings, is shown as the way out of self-hatred and loneliness.**

## Creator and publisher context

- Norio Sakurai previously created *Mitsudomoe* (Wikipedia; ANN, as cited in Wikipedia).
- Sakurai chose the word "karte" (medical chart) for chapters to reflect Kyotaro's improvement (Wikipedia), which frames the series as a record of healing.
- Animate Times interview, reported by Anime Hunch (opened): Sakurai decided to end at Kyotaro and Anna's middle school graduation "when the story reached its middle stage and Yamada and Ichikawa began to have feelings for each other"; the ending followed the original outline as a "soft landing"; "I am relieved I could end it properly"; serialization was at times "physically exhausting." Editor Yuko Fukuda also took part. The report says Sakurai had never written emotional dialogue before and struggled with Kyotaro's monologues to the end.
- End-of-series comment (animotaku.fr, opened): Sakurai thanked readers, said the series grew through its encounters "much as Kyotaro does," and hoped it would be a small source of light for readers.
- Real Sound Book interview (July 27, 2025, opened, 2 pages): Sakurai wanted to weave in relatable middle school moments, wanted "a story with dreams" in which ordinary small kindnesses add up, and aimed for a romcom that "moves the heart" rather than only making readers swoon; said the relationship was developing faster than first planned; praised the anime for heightening scenes; urged people who do not normally read romcoms to try it. No comment on sexual humor or content limits.
- Comicspace interviews (2019, 2020), cited by Japanese Wikipedia (not opened): the title expresses "a feeling inside me that I can't tell is love or something else"; the moment each fell in love is left deliberately unclear; the frank dirty jokes aim at realism about middle school boys; Anna was partly modeled on former idol Eri Kamei; the idea of a boy narrator came from a *Rororo!* chapter about seeing girls through a boy's eyes; the series began when a former editor returned and Sakurai brought him the storyboards.
- Sakurai has given character ages and data on X (Japanese Wikipedia). No statements on religion or politics were found; gender and private life are not stated in any source opened (use "Sakurai," no pronoun). **Status: Needs more info on the creators.**
- Publisher: Akita Shoten (Weekly Shonen Champion, Champion Cross). English: Seven Seas Entertainment, rated 13+. Anime: Shin-Ei Animation; film by Toei and Avex Pictures; North American license Sentai Filmworks / HIDIVE (Wikipedia).
- No creator controversy reported by reputable outlets was found.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can a person who has walled himself off with contempt learn to love? Kyotaro begins the series certain that he is "messed up in the head," carrying a box cutter and a murder encyclopedia, and treating his classmates as targets (THEM Anime; ANN Preview Guide). The question is whether that self-image is true.

**The answer the story gives.** The story answers that it was never true. ANN's manga reviewer says "He doesn't really want to hurt anyone"; the fantasies are a coping mechanism for being on the outskirts. Once Anna treats him as worth knowing, the dark pose fades after the first volume. The rest of the series asks a harder second question: once you are loved, how do you live up to it? In vols. 9-12 Kyotaro worries about his future, his entrance exams and whether he will harm Anna's career (ANN; Seven Seas). The answer again is honest talk and care for the other person.

**Characters as arguments.** Kyotaro argues that edginess is a shell over loneliness. Anna argues that openness and attention can draw a person out; she chooses the outsider and is the more forward of the two (ANN). Adachi argues that rivalry need not end friendship (Wikipedia). The boys who play "stupid sexual games" early (UK Anime Network) show the crude peer culture Kyotaro sets himself against, at least in his own head. Anna's parents, strict but loving, stand for the protective adult world.

**Symbols and motifs.** The chapter name "karte," a medical chart, frames each chapter as a step in recovery (Wikipedia). The box cutter and the murder encyclopedia mark the old self. Anna's constant snacking marks her unguarded warmth. Height (Anna "towering over Kyotaro," THEM Anime) reverses the usual romance picture. The graduation album that comes with the last volume closes the chart (animotaku.fr).

**Traditions it echoes.** The Japanese school romantic comedy, especially the "slow-burn" type compared with *Teasing Master Takagi-san* (THEM Anime) and *Rascal Does Not Dream* (ANN), and the chūnibyō comedy of a boy who imagines himself dangerous. Readers in many traditions will also recognize the old pattern of a heart changed from hatred to love by being loved first.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Secular humanism and virtue ethics frame the arc: growth comes from empathy, courage and honesty, with no religious frame. Nihilism and misanthropy appear only to be outgrown. Feminism is explored: Anna has agency and a career, but critics flag the early monologues. Hedonism is present as puberty comedy, held in check by care and boundaries.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1 is the darkest and crudest, which is where reviewers who quit stopped (UK Anime Network gave vol. 1 a 6 and would not continue; ANN's Eisenbeis and Dupree hesitated over the anime). Vols. 2-8 are a slow courtship (anime seasons 1 and 2). Vols. 9-12 move past the confession into the problems of a first relationship, which ANN calls the part that makes it "one of the best manga romcoms in living memory." Vols. 13-14 cover New Year with Anna's family, exams and graduation. Sakurai said in 2025 the relationship was moving faster than planned and that the aim was something that "moves the heart," not only sweetness (Real Sound).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why do you think Kyotaro imagined hurting his classmates at the start? What was he protecting himself from?
2. What did Anna see in Kyotaro that others missed? Has someone seen something good in you before you saw it yourself?
3. How do the boys in the class talk about girls? Does the story agree with them? Does it change?
4. What does respecting another person's boundaries look like among friends and classmates?
5. Is 14 too young to date? What does your family think, and why?
