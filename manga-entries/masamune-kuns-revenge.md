**Premise.** As a chubby, rich child, Masamune Hayase was friends with Aki Adagaki, a wealthy girl living in a mansion. Then a girl he took to be Aki rejected him in front of his bullies and named him "Piggy" (tonsoku, "pig's foot"). With his grandfather's help he spent eight years dieting and training, took his grandfather's surname, Makabe, and came back as a handsome, top-scoring transfer student at Aki's high school. Aki is now known as the "Cruel Princess" for humiliating every boy who confesses to her. His "Dead or Love" plan is to make her fall for him and then dump her. Aki's maid and classmate Yoshino Koiwai recognizes him and offers to help. Rivals follow: Neko Fujinomiya, a sickly heiress who knew him as a child, and Kanetsugu Gaso, a plump "fiancé" who claims to be the childhood Masamune and is later shown to be a girl in disguise. Then come a school trip to Paris and a French mafia family. In vol. 8 Masamune learns that Yoshino, out of jealousy, disguised herself as Aki and gave him the cruel nickname (ch. 38). Aki never knew of it and had loved him. The two date (vol. 9), feelings shift among Masamune, Aki and Yoshino (vol. 10), and the main story ends with Aki and Masamune reconciled with a kiss (ch. 49). The engagement sequel follows them at university, in a long-distance relationship (English Wikipedia; fan wiki; Seven Seas volume blurbs).

## Life message

**Revenge built on how you look and on a story you never checked leads you astray. Honesty about the past, owning up to wrongs and real affection matter more than appearances.** The story still treats self-improvement through diet and training as something to admire.

| Evolutionary biology | Status and mate choice: Masamune believes looks decide romance and remakes his body to win the most desirable girl. Anime Feminist faults the show for presenting that belief without enough critique. Female rivalry runs the plot: Yoshino's childhood jealousy of Aki (ch. 38), Neko and Kanetsugu as competing suitors. Wealth and lineage matter: arranged "fiancés" and a poor family's plan to marry into money (Kanetsugu, vols. 5-6). |
| Pragmatic | The revenge plan fails because it rests on a misunderstanding. Masamune never checked who rejected him, and the "Pig's foot" incident turns out to be Yoshino's doing (ch. 38). The story rewards dropping a scheme once its premise is shown to be false. |

| Socio-cultural | A satire of shojo romance tropes: Masamune learned his "kind boy" lines from his sister's shojo manga (fan wiki). ## Winners, losers and sympathy

The schemers do not win by scheming: the revenge plan, the fake fiancé and Yoshino's old lie all come apart. The ones who end well are those who tell the truth and choose to care for someone.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can revenge heal a wound? Masamune spends eight years rebuilding his body and his name to pay back the girl who called him "Piggy." The title promises a revenge story; the series asks whether getting even is what he actually wants.

**The answer the story gives.** No, because the wound was misread. In vol. 8 he learns that Aki never rejected him. Her maid Yoshino, jealous of their friendship, disguised herself as Aki and spoke the words "I can't really come to like you, Piggy" (ch. 38, whose title quotes the line). Aki had loved him and was heartbroken when he vanished. The plan, named "Dead or Love," ends in the last chapter of the main story, which takes that name (ch. 49), with love chosen over revenge.

**Characters as arguments.** Masamune argues that worth is earned through looks and effort. He is narcissistic out of fear of becoming the bullied fat boy again (fan wiki). Aki argues that contempt protects the heart; Manga Bookshelf reads her arrogance as "an affectation" much like Masamune's own cool act. Yoshino is the hidden argument: a servant who felt unseen, whose one act of envy shaped two lives, and who spends the series trying to repair it. Neko argues for steady devotion, though the Japanese Wikipedia notes her late-story actions are never explained. Kanetsugu shows deception driven by poverty and a sick sister.

**Symbols and motifs.** Weight and food: the chubby child, Masamune's calorie counting, Aki's eating, Yoshino's sweets. Names and nicknames ("Piggy," "Cruel Princess," "Prince of Stooges") mark how labels wound. Masks and disguises recur: Yoshino as Aki, Kanetsugu as "Masamune," Masamune's whole new persona. The Snow White play at the culture festival (ch. 23-28) stages the question of who the real prince is. Aki cuts her hair after ch. 48 (fan wiki), a sign of change.

**Traditions it echoes.** The revenge romance and "the taming of the haughty" (Gaffney hopes the series subverts the latter). The ugly-duckling makeover. The shojo misunderstanding romance, which Masamune literally learned from his sister's shojo manga (fan wiki). The scriptural and philosophical warnings against vengeance (Leviticus 19:18, Romans 12:19, Dhammapada 5) are parallels, not sources.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Virtue ethics comes off best: discipline is rewarded and envy and pride are exposed. Expressive self-making is admired in its effort and questioned in its motive. Feminist critics read the anime as endorsing the idea that women choose only by looks and treat men cruelly; Bamboo Dong of ANN complained that men are shown as "hapless victims of the heartless women." Sympathetic readers answer that the reveal turns this around: the "heartless" girl was never heartless. Faith readers find a familiar shape of sin, confession and forgiveness, set beside fan service most of them reject.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-4: the revenge plan, rivals and the beach trip, mostly comedy. Volumes 5-7: the false fiancé, the culture festival and Paris. Volumes 8-10: the truth about the past, dating, and the three-way emotional crisis. After School (v11) gives each side character an epilogue; engagement (v12-13) moves the couple to university.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Masamune never checked who really rejected him. How might the story have changed if he had asked?
2. Is it good that Masamune got fit? Is it good why he did it?
3. Yoshino did one cruel thing out of jealousy as a child. What did it take for her to make it right?
4. Why do the characters give each other insulting nicknames, and how do the nicknames shape them?
5. The book shows teen characters in fan-service scenes. How does your family think about pictures like that in stories about high-schoolers?
