# Folkways and cultural clusters (draft spec, 2026-11-22)

A cultural layer separate from religion and politics. It describes the everyday values a family inherits from where and how it grew up (honor, order, kin, freedom), not ancestry. Nothing here is based on genes or ethnicity: anyone can hold any cluster's values, and families can ignore this layer entirely.

## Why
David Hackett Fischer's *Albion's Seed* (1989) shows four British migrant cultures carrying distinct "folkways" (ideas of liberty, order, family, violence, faith) into America, where they still shape regional values. Colin Woodard's *American Nations* (2011) extends this to 11 North American regional cultures. Worldwide, the Inglehart-Welzel cultural map (World Values Survey) and Hofstede's dimensions group countries into value clusters. Manga often celebrate values that sit closer to one cluster than another (honor and personal combat, duty to the group, hierarchy, self-expression), so parents can see whether a series' values match their household's.

## The six value dimensions (each 0 to 10, 5 = balanced; named "0 end vs 10 end")
Every series and every cluster is placed on the same six dimensions:
1. **Dignity vs honor:** 0 = worth is inherent and disputes go to rules or courts; 10 = worth must be defended personally, insult demands answer.
2. **Order vs freedom:** 0 = strong rules, discipline, everyone in their place; 10 = individuals left alone to do as they choose.
3. **Civic vs kin:** 0 = loyalty to everyone in the community equally, rules apply to all; 10 = family and clan first.
4. **Equality vs hierarchy:** 0 = flat, everyone equal, consensus; 10 = rank, elders and leaders deserve deference.
5. **Tradition vs secular-rational:** 0 = custom, religion and inherited ways guide life; 10 = reason, science and choice guide life (Inglehart-Welzel axis 1).
6. **Survival vs self-expression:** 0 = security, hard work and thrift come first; 10 = self-expression, tolerance and quality of life come first (Inglehart-Welzel axis 2).

## Clusters

### Albion's Seed folkways (British-American roots)
| Folkway | Origin | Idea of liberty | Typical values |
|---|---|---|---|
| Puritan | East Anglia to Massachusetts | Ordered liberty: freedom within a covenanted community | Education, self-discipline, community rules, moral reform |
| Cavalier | South-west England to Virginia | Hierarchical liberty: freedom as the privilege of rank | Honor, hospitality, gentility, rank, Anglican order |
| Quaker | North Midlands to the Delaware Valley | Reciprocal liberty: freedom for all, including others' conscience | Equality, pacifism, plainness, tolerance, consensus |
| Borderer | Anglo-Scottish border and Ulster to Appalachia | Natural liberty: freedom from interference | Personal honor, clan loyalty, self-defense, distrust of authority |

### North American regional cultures (Woodard, *American Nations*)
Yankeedom; New Netherland; The Midlands; Tidewater; Greater Appalachia; Deep South; El Norte; The Left Coast; The Far West; New France; First Nation. (Each mapped to the six dimensions in the cluster table the site uses.)

### World cultural zones (Inglehart-Welzel map)
Protestant Europe; Catholic Europe; English-speaking; Orthodox Europe; Confucian (East Asia, including Japan, the source culture of manga); Latin America; South and Southeast Asia; Middle East and North Africa; Sub-Saharan Africa.

Positions for each cluster on the six dimensions come from the cited works (Fischer's chapters on each folkway's ways of order, power and freedom; Woodard's regional profiles; World Values Survey wave data for the world zones). Draft values are marked "to verify" until checked.

## Can parents identify their own cluster? (the quiz)
Most people can't name a folkway, but they recognize their own values. Four ways in, all optional (heritage picker below):
1. **Quiz (10 questions, about 2 minutes)** suggests the closest clusters; the parent can accept, adjust or ignore.
2. **Pick by region** (US region, or world zone) if they already know.
3. **Skip** the layer entirely.

Draft questions (each answered on a 5-point scale from "strongly agree" to "strongly disagree"; each maps to one or two dimensions):
1. If someone insults my family in public, it should be answered, not ignored. (Honor)
2. Children should be taught to settle fights through teachers or rules, not on their own. (Dignity)
3. A good community has clear rules that everyone is expected to follow. (Order)
4. People should be left alone to live as they choose as long as they harm no one. (Freedom)
5. I would bend a rule to help a family member before I would for a stranger. (Kin)
6. Elders and leaders deserve respect because of their position, not only because they earned it. (Hierarchy)
7. Decisions in a group work best when everyone has an equal say. (Equality)
8. Our family's traditions and faith should guide how my children live. (Tradition)
9. Hard work and financial security matter more than self-expression. (Survival)
10. Being free to be yourself, and tolerant of others who differ, matters most. (Self-expression)

Scoring: average the answers into the six dimensions, then show the nearest clusters by distance, with a plain sentence on what that cluster values. The quiz stores nothing unless the parent saves it.

- Is self-identified cultural heritage, not ancestry testing, and is worded "Which cultural heritage does your family identify with? (optional, choose any)".
- Only pre-suggests the clusters below as a starting point, with the sentence: "This is a common starting point for families with this heritage, not a description of yours. Adjust it, or take the quiz."
- Can be changed freely; the quiz result overrides it; several heritages can be chosen.
- Never claims values are inherited biologically or fixed by group.

## Neutrality rules
- Values only. Self-identified cultural heritage may pre-suggest a starting cluster (see Heritage picker), but never as genetics, never as a label on the family, and always overridable.
- No cluster is presented as better. Each is described in the words of its own historians and members.
- Name the sources; mark draft cluster positions "to verify".
- Japanese cultural norms are described as context, not judged.
