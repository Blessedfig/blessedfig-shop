Field guide: what it tastes like and how to eat it

37 wild berries, nuts, greens and mushrooms found on Langlade County's public land, with photos, flavor, kitchen ideas, safe preparation and when each is ready around Antigo.

Foraging homeCounty mapPlacesField guideHarvest guideBookstore
This guide tells you what, where and when, not how to identify. Learn each plant in person from someone who knows it, and never eat a wild mushroom unless an experienced identifier has checked it in person. Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222.

Berries and fruit 14

Wild blueberries

Vaccinium angustifolium, V. myrtilloides
Smaller and more intense than store blueberries: sweet, tangy and a little spicy.
Good raw Jul 10–Aug 3

Wild red raspberries

Rubus idaeus
Bright, floral and tart-sweet, more aromatic than any store raspberry.
Good raw Jul 13–Jul 30

Blackberries

Rubus allegheniensis
Deep, winey sweetness with a tart edge and crunchy seeds.
Good raw Jul 18–Aug 17

Wild strawberries

Fragaria virginiana
Tiny but the most intense strawberry you will ever taste: perfumed, sweet and a little tart.
Good raw Jun 9–Jul 20

Juneberries (serviceberry)

Amelanchier spp.
Sweet and juicy, like a mild blueberry crossed with a cherry, with a hint of almond from the soft seeds.
Good raw Jun 24–Jul 18

Chokecherries

Prunus virginiana
Raw they are sharply astringent and pucker your mouth (hence the name).
Cook first Jul 28–Aug 21

Pin cherries

Prunus pensylvanica
Very tart, bright sour cherry, with thin flesh around a big pit.
Cook first Jul 13–Aug 1

Highbush cranberries

Viburnum opulus var. americanum
Sharp and sour like cranberry, with a musky note some people love and some do not.
Cook first Aug 6–Sep 29

Nannyberries

Viburnum lentago
Sweet and raisin- or date-like with a prune note, best when soft and dark after a frost.
Good raw Aug 24–Oct 28

Bog cranberries

Vaccinium oxycoccos, V. macrocarpon
Tart and crisp.
Cook first Sep 1–Oct 9

Wild grapes

Vitis riparia
Very tart, intensely grapey, with thick skins and big seeds.
Raw or cooked, with care Aug 5–Sep 4

Wintergreen berries

Gaultheria procumbens
Classic minty wintergreen.
Good raw Aug 6–Oct 25

Elderberries

Sambucus canadensis
Tart, earthy and dark-fruity.
Cook first Aug 5–Aug 30

Gooseberries and currants

Ribes spp.
Gooseberries are tart when green and sweet when purple-ripe.
Good raw Jul 12–Aug 12

Nuts 1

Hazelnuts

Corylus cornuta, C. americana
Sweet and buttery, smaller than store filberts.
Good raw Jul 30–Sep 12

Greens and shoots 5

Wild leeks (ramps)

Allium tricoccum
Garlic and onion at once, sweeter and greener than either.
Raw or cooked, with care Apr 15–May 8

Ostrich fern fiddleheads

Matteuccia struthiopteris
Grassy and green, somewhere between asparagus, green beans and spinach, with a nutty, slightly bitter finish.
Cook first Apr 24–May 9

Stinging and wood nettles

Urtica dioica, Laportea canadensis
Deep green and earthy, like a richer spinach with a cucumber note.
Cook first May 17–Jun 15

Watercress

Nasturtium officinale
Peppery and mustardy, crisp and juicy.
Cook first Mar 31–May 29

Cattail shoots

Typha spp.
Young shoot cores taste like a mild cucumber or zucchini.
Raw or cooked, with care May 15–Jun 15

Mushrooms 17

Morels

Morchella spp.
Earthy, nutty and meaty, a little smoky.
Cook first May 8–Jun 2

Chanterelles

Cantharellus spp.
Fruity, apricot-like aroma with a mild peppery taste and a chewy-tender bite.
Cook first Jul 23–Sep 13

Black trumpets

Craterellus spp.
Rich, smoky and earthy, almost truffle-like; thin and delicate.
Cook first Jul 19–Sep 16

King bolete

Boletus edulis group
Nutty, meaty and deeply savory.
Cook first Jul 17–Sep 7

Oyster mushrooms

Pleurotus spp.
Mild, savory and slightly sweet, sometimes with a faint anise smell; velvety when cooked.
Cook first Jun 13–Sep 15

Hedgehog mushrooms

Hydnum spp.
Sweet and nutty with a hint of pepper; firm and holds its shape when cooked.
Cook first Jul 28–Aug 17

Lobster mushrooms

Hypomyces lactifluorum
Mild, slightly sweet and seafood-like, very firm and almost crunchy.
Cook first Jul 21–Sep 7

Lion's mane and bear's head

Hericium spp.
Delicate and sweet, with a texture and taste often compared to crab or lobster.
Cook first Aug 19–Sep 26

Matsutake

Tricholoma magnivelare
A distinctive spicy, cinnamon-and-pine aroma; firm and dense.
Cook first Sep 12–Oct 29

Hen of the woods (maitake)

Grifola frondosa
Earthy, peppery, rich and meaty, with crisp edges when roasted.
Cook first Aug 31–Sep 18

Chicken of the woods

Laetiporus cincinnatus, L. sulphureus
Lemony and meaty, with a texture like chicken breast when young.
Cook first Jun 17–Sep 7

Giant puffball

Calvatia gigantea
Mild, earthy and tofu-like; it takes on whatever you cook it with.
Cook first Aug 20–Oct 11

Resinous polypore

Ischnoderma resinosum
Meaty and mild, with a somewhat beefy quality; the young flesh is soft and juicy.
Cook first Sep 5–Oct 23

Hemlock reishi

Ganoderma tsugae
Strongly bitter as a tea, often softened with honey.
Tea, not food Jun 3–Sep 30

Chaga

Inonotus obliquus
As a tea: earthy and mild, with a faint vanilla note and slight bitterness.
Tea, not food

Turkey tail

Trametes versicolor
As a tea: mild, earthy and slightly woody.
Tea, not food

Dryad's saddle (pheasant back)

Cerioporus squamosus
Fresh and mild, with a smell of cucumber or watermelon rind.
Cook first May 17–Sep 28
