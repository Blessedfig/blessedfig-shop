Stinging and wood nettles

Urtica dioica, Laportea canadensis

Flavor

Deep green and earthy, like a richer spinach with a cucumber note. Wood nettle is milder and more tender.

In the kitchen

  • Nettle soup
  • Blanched for pesto, saag, pie or pasta filling
  • Dried for tea

Preparing it

Wear gloves. Pick the top few inches of young plants before they flower. Blanching for a minute, cooking or drying takes the sting away completely.

Keeping it: Blanch, squeeze and freeze in balls, or dry for tea.

Be careful

  • Older plants get tough and gritty; pick young.
  • Not allowed on DNR (state) land.

Where

Floodplain forests, stream banks and moist ravines (Thayer 2006).
