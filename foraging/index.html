The climate you're foraging in

Antigo sits on a high, flat plain at about 1,500 feet, ringed by glacial moraine hills. Cold air drains into its low spots, and the soil stays cold well into May. That makes Langlade's season shorter and later than southern Wisconsin's, and it means soil temperature, not the calendar, sets the timing of spring greens and morels.

Frost dates are medians of the complete years in NOAA's Antigo record, 1991–2025. Soil temperature is from our model fitted to the Wisconet weather station at the Antigo airport. South-facing slopes run a few days ahead of these dates; cedar swamps, north slopes and frost-pocket basins run a week or more behind. The map works this out for each place.

Where it grows: Langlade's landscapes

Most of what you'll find follows the ground underfoot. Langlade's glaciers left five kinds of country, and each feeds a different set of plants and fungi.

Maple hills (moraines)

Stony loam hills under sugar maple, basswood, hemlock and yellow birch, along the Ice Age Trail and in the northern county forest. Ramps in spring; lion's mane, oysters and hen of the woods on old trees; ostrich fern where streams cut through.

Sandy plains (outwash)

Dry, acid sand under red and jack pine and oak. Blueberries, wintergreen, juneberries and hazelnuts; king boletes and matsutake under the pines.

Young forest and cuts

Aspen and birch regrowing after logging, plus road and trail edges. The berry country: raspberries, blackberries, pin cherries and chokecherries peak a few years after a cut.

Swamps and bogs

Cedar, spruce and tamarack swamps and open sphagnum bogs. Bog cranberries in fall; highbush cranberry and nannyberry along swamp edges. Go with a partner; footing is unreliable.

River bottoms

The Wolf, Eau Claire, Prairie, Evergreen and Lily rivers. Ostrich fern fiddleheads, nettles, wild grape and elderberry; morels around dying elm and ash; dryad's saddle and oysters on floodplain trees.

Spring ponds and cold streams

Langlade's many spring-fed ponds and trout streams. Watercress in cold, clean spring water, one of the few greens allowed on DNR land.

Hunting and fishing

The same public land is open to hunting and fishing, and fall foraging overlaps the busiest hunting seasons. Langlade's public forests hold white-tailed deer, black bear, ruffed grouse, woodcock, wild turkey and snowshoe hare, and its spring-fed streams and lakes hold trout and warm-water fish. Seasons, bag limits and licenses are set every year by the Wisconsin DNR, so check the current rules before you go:

  • Wisconsin DNR hunting regulations and seasons
  • Wisconsin DNR fishing regulations
  • County forest land, DNR land, the national forest and Managed Forest Law "open" private land all allow hunting and fishing under state rules. Some county parks and campgrounds are closed to hunting.
Foragers in hunting season: wear blaze orange from September through December, especially during the November gun deer season, and stay off the woods at dawn and dusk when hunters are most active.

The rules in one minute
