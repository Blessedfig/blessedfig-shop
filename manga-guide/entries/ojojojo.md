**Premise.** Haru Jigokumeguri, heiress to a huge business group, is rich, famous and friendless: her haughty, bragging manner has left her alone at school after school (Seven Seas; MangaUpdates). At her newest school she sits next to Tsurezure Kawayanagi, a quiet oddball in old-fashioned clothes who does not react to her at all, and the two misfits become friends (Take Shobo vol. 1). Classmate Akane becomes her first girl friend, the elderly butler Jiiya points out her flaws, and Chris Portman, an English transfer student, is one of the marriage candidates her father has lined up, though her father wants her happy and lets her refuse (ANN). Haru and Tsurezure are dating by the end of the first volume and holding hands by the end of the second (Manga Bookshelf). In vols. 3-4, Haru's younger sister Aki returns from abroad and tries to tear her down; the sisters reconcile, Akane and Chris come to an understanding, and Tsurezure's past surfaces: he tries to run off to live in the woods until Haru follows him by helicopter (Penguin Random House; Take Shobo vol. 3; Manga Bookshelf). Gaffney says everyone ends happily.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Why can a girl who has everything not make a single friend?

**The answer the story gives.** Because pride is armor. Haru brags to protect herself; only when she meets someone who does not care about her status does she learn to be honest (ANN; Take Shobo vol. 1).

**Characters as arguments.** Haru argues, at first, that status earns respect, and learns otherwise. Tsurezure shows that indifference to status can be a gift, though his own hidden hurt nearly drives him away. Akane shows plain friendship; Jiiya the honest servant who tells the truth; Aki the jealousy that grows in a distant family, and how it can heal.

**Symbols and motifs.** Tsurezure's old-fashioned clothes; the butler; a helicopter chase to the woods; Haru's cooking.

**Traditions it echoes.** The ojou-sama comedy and the four-panel school gag strip; for faith readers, the proverb that pride goes before a fall.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Community and belonging are affirmed; wealth as a source of worth is gently critiqued.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1: the meeting and friendship; vol. 2: dating, Akane and Chris, longer chapters; vol. 3: sister Aki's return; vol. 4: Tsurezure's past, the final drama and an epilogue.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why did Haru brag so much?
2. What did Tsurezure do that nobody else had done for Haru?
3. Why do you think Aki tried to tear her sister down?
4. Haru's father lets her refuse the marriage candidates. What do you think of that?
5. Who at your school might be lonely behind a tough face?
