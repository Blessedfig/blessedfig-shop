**Premise.** In 2005 a class of high school students is on a school-trip bus when the driver collapses and the bus plunges off a bridge (fan wiki, ch. 1; Mangapedia). They wake in a classroom of an abandoned mountain school with a black sphere, Gantz, and two university students in black suits, Fujimoto and Abiko, veterans of three years of the game, who explain the rules: put on the suits, hunt the "aliens" Gantz names, earn points; leave the area and the bomb in your head explodes; reach 100 points and you can go free, get a stronger weapon or revive someone who died in the game (Wikipedia; Mangapedia). At a night zoo (ch. 1-6), the teacher, the guide and many students ignore the warnings and are killed, and the boy Naiki, a shy, bullied childhood friend of the lead, dies to the Beast Alien (fan wiki; Mangapedia). Five girls survive: Kei Kurona, a competitive track athlete (15 per the fan wiki), who resolves to earn 100 points to revive Naiki; Yoshiko Miyazaki, shy, an aspiring manga artist who turns out to be a fine shot; Kimiko Ikegami, basketball captain and model; Morishita, a secret underground idol who has stirred up online attacks through a school message board; and Kaji, withdrawn, whose father abuses her mother (Mangapedia). Back in daily life (vol. 2), the girls are split into separate classes, face a stalker, bullies and an abusive father, and are tempted to use the suits for revenge (Dark Horse blurb; ANN). In the aquarium mission against a mermaid-like alien (vol. 3), Fujimoto and Abiko die, Kei's strategy brings the alien down, Ikegami uses her 100 points to revive a fallen participant, and the survivors become the guides for new players (Wikipedia; Mangapedia).

**In a cruel and arbitrary world, survival and meaning come from working together, protecting each other and refusing to give up on the people you love, rather than from rivalry or revenge.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** If you were given a second life by a power that cares nothing for you, what would you use it for? Gantz tells the dead students that how they use their new lives is up to it (Dark Horse blurb), and the series asks whether the girls can still choose what they fight for.

**The answer the story gives.** Fight for each other. Kei fights to bring Naiki back; Ikegami spends her 100 points on reviving someone else; the survivors end as guides for the next group (Wikipedia). Revenge is offered as a temptation (vol. 2) rather than as the answer.

**Characters as arguments.** Kei argues for determination and loyalty; Ikegami, for competition that turns into generosity; Morishita, for selfishness that the game wears down; Kaji, for hatred born of abuse; Fujimoto and Abiko, for experience spent on protecting others. The teacher who leads students away from the rules is the case against trusting ordinary authority in an extraordinary world.

**Symbols and motifs.** The black sphere and its radio-exercise jingle (Mangapedia); the abandoned school as a classroom of the dead; the zoo and the aquarium, places of looking, turned into hunting grounds.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Gantz death game, which Kei's name and look deliberately echo (fan wiki); school-trip disaster stories. For faith readers, the mentors' deaths echo "laying down one's life for friends"; the god-like sphere is a dark parody of providence.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Existential choice and teamwork are affirmed; nihilism and might-makes-right are the world the girls must survive, not the story's verdict. Critics argue that the art undercuts the story's respect for its heroines (ANN).

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1 (ch. 1-6, Oku's script): the crash and the zoo massacre. Vol. 2 (ch. 7-12, Ohsaki's script): daily life, training, the girls' troubles and revenge temptations. Vol. 3 (ch. 13-18): the aquarium mission, the mentors' deaths and the ending.

**Discussion questions.**
1. The teacher led students away from the zoo and died. Why didn't he listen to the veterans?
2. Kei wants 100 points to bring Naiki back. What would you use them for?
3. The girls are tempted to use the suits for revenge. Would revenge have helped them?
4. Critics say the art treats the girls' bodies as something to look at. Do you agree, and does it change the story?
5. Why do Fujimoto and Abiko help newcomers instead of just saving themselves?
