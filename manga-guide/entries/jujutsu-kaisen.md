**Premise.** Yuji Itadori, a high-school student with unusual strength, swallows a rotting finger of Ryomen Sukuna, the King of Curses, to save his classmate Megumi Fushiguro, and becomes Sukuna's host (ch. 1). Curses are spirits born from humans' negative emotions; sorcerers, governed by a secretive, clan-ruled Jujutsu Society, fight them. Satoru Gojo, the strongest sorcerer, has Yuji's execution delayed until he can eat all twenty fingers, so that Sukuna dies with him, and enrolls him at Tokyo Jujutsu High with Megumi and Nobara Kugisaki. Yuji's dying grandfather told him to help people and to die surrounded by others (ch. 1). Curses led by Mahito, and a body-stealing ancient sorcerer, Kenjaku, wearing the corpse of Gojo's best friend Suguru Geto, seal Gojo during a Halloween massacre in Shibuya (ch. 79-136), where Sukuna, briefly in control of Yuji's body, kills thousands (ch. 118-119), and Nanami dies (ch. 120). Kenjaku launches the Culling Games, a battle royale (ch. 137-221). Sukuna takes Megumi's body (ch. 212), Gojo is freed but killed by Sukuna (ch. 236), and the remaining sorcerers, students and allies finally defeat Sukuna; Megumi is saved, Choso dies protecting Yuji (ch. 259), and Yuji resolves to keep helping people as one part of a larger whole (ch. 271) (Wikipedia; fan wiki).

## Life message

**A good life is spent using whatever strength you have to help others, so that no one, including you, dies alone; you do not have to be the strongest, only one part of something larger.**

| Meta lens | How it reads the series |
|---|---|
| Evolutionary biology | Curses grow from fear and hatred, so the monsters are humanity's own emotions. Bloodlines and clans decide who has power (the Gojo, Zen'in and Kamo clans; Wikipedia), and the story shows the cruelty of breeding for strength (Maki and Mai's father, ch. 148-149). |
| Pragmatic | Fights are won by rules, trade-offs and binding vows: a technique gets stronger if you reveal it or accept a limit. Nanami treats sorcery as a job and an adult duty (ch. 19, 29). Akutami's magic system, modeled on Hunter x Hunter, avoids emotional shortcuts (Wikipedia). |
| Religious | Curses, exorcism, shrines, a Buddhist monk's remains as a prison (ch. 90) and lore that the immortal Tengen spread Japanese Buddhism and founded jujutsu (ch. 74) make up the setting; a cult that worships Tengen applauds a murdered girl (ch. 76), and Geto later runs a cult for money (ch. 78). Plugged In notes reviewers have called the series a Buddhist-influenced parable of suffering and rebirth. No faith is practiced as an answer. |
| Socio-cultural | A critique of hidebound institutions: elders who would execute a boy for convenience (ch. 10, 137), clans that despise women and the weak (ch. 40-41, 148-150), a legal system that convicts the innocent (Higuruma, ch. 159-165). Akutami has said that no one in the story holds the ultimate truth (ComicBook.com, 2023). |

Outlook: Bittersweet — Sukuna is defeated and Megumi saved, the survivors rebuild, and Yuji finds his place, but Gojo, Nanami, Choso and many others die along the way (ch. 236, 120, 259, 271).
Hope source: P — protecting others: Yuji's purpose, from his grandfather's last words (ch. 1) to the ending, is to save people so they do not die alone; friends and allies (F) are the close second, since the final victory is a team effort (ch. 253-268).

**Where traditions disagree.** Nearly every frame credits the self-sacrifice, the protection of the weak and the defeat of a cruelty-for-fun villain. The split is the magic: Orthodox, Pentecostal, Jehovah's Witness, Adventist, Muslim and Historically Black Protestant readers take spirits and sorcery as spiritually real and see a hero who fights curses with curses as a problem, while Catholic, Anglican, Lutheran and secular readers judge it as fantasy. Progressive readers add the second-half treatment of women. Secular humanist, Atheist and Agnostic readings are the warmest, because the story's hope is human effort and its author says no one holds the final truth.

## Creator and publisher context

- Gege Akutami: born in Iwate Prefecture (February 26, 1992), moved to Sendai in fifth grade; began drawing manga by imitating a friend; assistant on Kiss x Death (2014); one-shots from 2014; the 2017 four-chapter series that became Jujutsu Kaisen 0 (Wikipedia, Gege Akutami).
- On-record intent: influences include Neon Genesis Evangelion for the mythology, horror and found-footage films, and Yoshihiro Togashi's Hunter x Hunter for the magic system; Akutami planned the ending and main stages early, leaving the path between them "fairly free" (Wikipedia). In an interview reported by ComicBook.com (2023), Akutami said no one in the story holds the ultimate truth: "If no one is really right then no one is wrong either."
- The series paused in June 2021 for the author's health and resumed that August (Wikipedia).
- No statements on religion or politics were found. **Status: Needs more info on the creator** (the original interview behind the ComicBook.com report was not opened).
- Publisher: Shueisha (Weekly Shōnen Jump). VIZ Media publishes it in English, rated T+.
- No controversies involving the creator were found.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What makes a death "proper," and what makes a life worth it? Yuji's grandfather tells him to help people and die surrounded by others (ch. 1). Nanami warns that sorcerers rarely get a good death (ch. 31). The series spends 271 chapters testing whether helping others is worth the cost when it costs everything.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, but not as a lone hero. After Shibuya, Yuji wants to die (ch. 120) and only gets up because others need him (ch. 126-133). In the end, no single fighter defeats Sukuna; a relay of students, teachers and allies does (ch. 253-268). Yuji ends content to be one part of a larger whole (ch. 271).

**Characters as arguments.** Sukuna argues that strength and pleasure are all that matter, and dies alone. Mahito argues that souls are mere shapes and nothing has meaning (ch. 30). Geto argues that protecting his own kind justifies killing others (ch. 77-78). Nanami argues for quiet adult duty (ch. 19). Gojo argues for the strongest as protector, and his death questions whether being the strongest made him lonely (ch. 236). Higuruma argues that human justice fails, and finds his own guilt (ch. 165, 245).

**Symbols and motifs.** Sukuna's fingers; hand signs; domain expansions as minds made into worlds; binding vows; the cog; Gojo's blindfold; the empty airport after death (ch. 236).

**Traditions it echoes.** Japanese yōkai and exorcism stories (GeGeGe no Kitarō, per ANN's Silverman via Wikipedia), Bleach and Hunter x Hunter, and Evangelion's mythic framing. Christian readers read the Higuruma trial as human justice needing grace (Beneath the Tangles); Buddhist readers can see suffering born of craving and anger.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Might makes right and nihilism are the villains' creeds and lose; secular humanism and communitarianism carry the hope. Tradition is mostly corrupt. Occult elements are the world's physics, not a practice the story recommends. Feminist readers credit Nobara and Maki but note the second half sidelines women.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-8 (ch. 1-70) are school-and-mission horror with humor. Volumes 9-16 (ch. 71-142) bring Gojo's past and the Shibuya massacre, the darkest stretch. Volumes 17-24 (ch. 143-217) are the Culling Games. Volumes 25-30 (ch. 218-271) are the long final battle and a short epilogue.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Yuji's grandfather asked him to die surrounded by people. What do you think he meant?
2. After Shibuya, Yuji wants to give up. What gets him back on his feet?
3. Geto starts as a hero and becomes a mass murderer. Where did his reasoning go wrong?
4. Higuruma realizes the problem was not only the system but himself. When have you blamed a system for something you also did?
5. Is it better to be the strongest, like Gojo, or one part of a team, like Yuji at the end?
