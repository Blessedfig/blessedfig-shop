**Premise.** Ryuto Kashima, a gloomy second-year high school boy whose hobby is watching a livestreamer, loses a game with friends and must confess to Runa Shirakawa, the most popular gyaru (a flashy, fashion-forward girl) at the top of the school pecking order. She says yes simply because she is single, and takes him to her room that same day, assuming sex is part of dating; he declines and says he wants to wait until she truly wants it too. Runa, who has had many boyfriends and fears the "two-month barrier" after which they have all left, slowly finds out what being cared for feels like. Complications come from Maria Kurose, Runa's fraternal twin, raised apart after their parents divorced, who is also the girl Ryuto once confessed to; from Runa's wish to reunite her parents; and from their friends' romances (Nikoru and the older Sekiya; Akari and Ryuto's friend Ijichi) (Wikipedia; J-Novel Club; ANN review; Anime Corner). In the light novel the couple plan their first time near the end of their second year, it does not happen, and after a three-year time skip they wait, move in together and marry in the final volume (J-Novel Club blurbs, vols. 5-10); whether the manga will follow is not yet known.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Does a person's past decide what they are worth, and what does it mean to love someone who has been taught that love has a price? Runa believes boys expect sex quickly and leave if they do not get it (ANN preview guide). Ryuto has no experience at all. The series asks what each of them has to unlearn.

**The answer the story gives.** Accept the person, not the rumor, and do not treat intimacy as a duty. Ryuto refuses Runa's first-day offer because he wants her to want it, not to pay for his company; the novels' reviewer calls the theme plainly anti-shaming (A Case Suitable for Treatment). The light novel takes the answer further: the couple wait through high school, a long separation and a time skip, and end in marriage (J-Novel Club).

**Characters as arguments.** Runa argues that experience is not the same as being loved. Ryuto argues for patience and attention, though reviewers note he also snoops and sometimes treats Runa as someone to rescue (Anime Feminist). Maria, the twin who stayed "good," argues the case of envy and judgment, and must be reconciled. Sekiya, who chased girls and failed his exams, is the cautionary friend who gets a second chance.

**Symbols and motifs.** The "two-month barrier" Runa fears; the moon ring and matching earrings (anime ep. 12); the twins' divided family; the confession made as a dare that becomes real.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Japanese school romantic comedy; the gyaru-and-otaku pairing; Ai Yazawa's Neighborhood Story (ANN's Silverman). Some faith readers will hear echoes of Jesus' refusal to condemn the woman judged by her neighbors, while noting the story never calls sex outside marriage wrong.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Feminism's critique of the double standard is the explicit message; Moore and Anime Feminist turn feminist critique back on the story for implying sex only belongs inside love. Classical virtue (patience, fidelity) wins the girl. Traditional courtship returns in the light novel's last volumes.

**Shifts across the series.** The anime and the early manga cover the first months: the confession, the Enoshima trip, Maria's schemes, the school festival and Christmas (light novel v1-3). The novels then move to the couple's planned first time (v5), a three-year time skip into college and work (v6), and cohabitation and marriage (v9-10). How far the 11 manga volumes go is not confirmed in sources opened.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Ryuto says no on the first day. Why does that make Runa trust him more?
2. Rumors say Runa is easy. Who benefits from rumors like that, and who is hurt?
3. Ryuto looks through Runa's drawer. Is that harmless? What would you say to him?
4. Runa thought sex was a duty to keep a boyfriend. Where might she have learned that?
5. Maria and Runa grew up apart. What does it take to rebuild trust in a family?
