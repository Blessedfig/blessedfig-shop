**Premise.** Ryousuke grows up in Hokkaido knowing he is different in how he talks, walks and what he likes; as a small boy he hides his love of Sailor Moon because it is a "girls' show" (Bunshun essay #3). In elementary and middle school he is mocked as a "girly boy," hit by classmates, and stood in front of the class by teachers (BL Library). He falls for his best friend Tsukasa, an unrequited first love, and later lives with a roommate he loves who talks constantly about his girlfriend (BL Library; Otaku USA). Moving to Tokyo, he tries dating sites, finds a gay community, comes out, and faces his mother's angry reaction; a serious relationship ends because his partner is controlling and jealous (Otaku USA; Comic Book Yeti). He finally meets another man, also named Ryousuke, whom he marries; the husband gets little page time (Otaku USA). In real life the couple submitted a marriage registration at Edogawa Ward in September 2015 and held a wedding at Tsukiji Hongwanji temple in Tokyo on October 10, 2016, with his parents attending (Bunshun essays). Whether the manga shows the wedding is not confirmed.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What does it cost a child to hide who he is, and what changes when he stops? Ryousuke learns early that a boy who likes Sailor Moon must hide it (Bunshun #3), and school teaches him the price of not hiding.

**The answer the story gives.** Honesty, patience and the right people. Crushes fail, a roommate never knows, a partner turns possessive, a mother is angry, yet each step leads toward a man who loves him and a marriage. The book's answer is that time and perseverance heal, as its blurb says.

**Characters as arguments.** Ryousuke argues for living openly, and the book lets him show his own faults (BL Library). The teachers argue, by their actions, that fitting in comes first, and the book rejects them. His mother stands for fear of how the world will treat her son; the real-life ending suggests fear can give way to love. The jealous partner shows that being accepted is not the same as being loved well.

**Symbols and motifs.** The hidden Sailor Moon wand; two men with the same name; a suit worn to the ward office; a temple wedding.

**Traditions it echoes.** The coming-out memoir and Japanese essay manga; for readers of faith, debates about the dignity of every person and the meaning of marriage.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism and progressivism are affirmed throughout. Traditional views of marriage appear only through family disapproval and are not argued, so religious families will need to supply their own side of that conversation.

**Shifts across the series.** The book moves from childhood bullying (likely ch. 1-2) through school crushes and young adulthood in Tokyo (likely ch. 3-5) to the jealous partner and meeting his husband (likely ch. 6-7); order from reviews, chapter numbers not confirmed.

**Discussion questions.**
1. What did the teachers do wrong, and what could a classmate have done instead?
