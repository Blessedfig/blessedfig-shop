**Premise.** About 80 years after Negima!, Tota Konoe, 14, lives in a mountain village with his guardian and teacher Yukihime, who is secretly Evangeline, a 700-year-old vampire mage. When a bounty hunter mortally wounds him, Tota learns that Yukihime made him an immortal vampire; his body will no longer age (ch. 1-2). He leaves for Shin-Tokyo and its space-elevator tower with Kuromaru, a sword-fighting immortal hunter from a people who are born genderless and choose a gender at 16 (ch. 3-16), and joins UQ Holder, Yukihime's secret society of immortals that protects other immortals, social outcasts and ordinary people (ch. 7-17). Arcs include a serial-killer and zombie outbreak at an academy city (ch. 43-62), a martial-arts tournament (ch. 64-85), the revelation that Tota is a clone made from Negi's and Asuna's DNA (vol. 7), and a long war against Ialda Baoth, the "Mage of the Beginning," who has possessed Negi (ch. 126-192). After a decades-long time skip, Tota reunites with his companions, the final battle is won, and the story ends with a harem ending and an alternate-reality epilogue (Wikipedia; MyAnimeList forum; Manga-News).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** If you could never die, what would your life be for? Tota is made immortal at 14 and loses the chance to grow up; Yukihime has lived 700 years and is tired. The series asks whether endless life is a gift or a curse.

**The answer the story gives.** Immortality is worth it only if it is spent for others. UQ Holder's purpose is to protect immortals, outcasts and ordinary people, and Tota's great act is to give up decades to save the Earth (ch. 183). The arc words about moving forward and courage (TV Tropes) are the story's stated answer.

**Characters as arguments.** Tota argues for forward motion and protecting friends, though ANN finds him a plain straight man for the supporting cast. Yukihime argues that even an exhausted life can be renewed by love. Dana argues that death can be a mercy (ANN vol. 9). Fate argues that saving the world justifies any means. Sayoko, a bullied girl turned vengeful ghost, is the dark mirror of the outcasts UQ Holder protects (ch. 43-56). Ialda Baoth argues for a single perfect world without freedom, and loses.

**Symbols and motifs.** The tower into space as the dream of growing up; time resets and save points (Kirie); the clone who must make his own name; the hot-spring inn as home.

**Traditions it echoes.** The vampire tale (the bitten child who cannot age), the Japanese yokai story, the shonen tournament, and Gnostic imagery of a false creator (from general knowledge; to verify). Readers of Christian, Jewish and Muslim traditions will hear the old warning that endless earthly life is not the same as eternal life; Buddhist readers will see craving for permanence.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Transhumanism is explored with mixed feelings: immortality brings loneliness and loss. Communitarianism is affirmed. Magic is the world's ordinary fabric. Expressive individualism shows in Kuromaru's choice of gender. The harem ending treats multiple partners as a reward.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-6 (ch. 1-62) are adventure with little harem comedy (Suitable for Treatment). Volumes 7-13 (ch. 63-132) add the tournament, the clone reveal and growing romance and fan service (ANN vol. 12). Volumes 14-25 (ch. 133-180) are the war against Ialda Baoth and a long time skip. Volumes 26-28 (ch. 181-192) bring the reunions, sexual content and the final battle.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Tota can never grow older. What would you miss if you stopped aging at 14?
2. Why does UQ Holder protect outcasts? Who are the outcasts in your school or town?
3. Dana says death might sometimes be kinder than living forever. Do you agree?
4. Sayoko and Santa were bullied. What could have changed their story?
5. The story ends with Tota and many partners. How does that compare with what you believe about love and commitment?
