**Premise.** Yūya Matsuda, a first-year high school student, has a strange balcony: objects drift onto it on the wind, including out-of-place artifacts, a living puppy and a photo album for a calendar year that does not exist. On the morning of Thursday, September 25, he finds a scrap of newspaper dated October 2. A violent delinquent in his year, Akira Kaneshiro, has been tormenting a quiet, slight classmate, Naoto Wakatsuki, with group beatings, and rumors that Kaneshiro has assaulted women keep everyone silent; Yūya has looked away like everyone else. That night Naoto, carrying a blood-stained baseball bat, lets Yūya understand that he has killed Kaneshiro. The newspaper scrap says a boy was killed in their city on September 25 and that a classmate would admit to the killing and take his own life in custody by October 2. Out of guilt for his silence, Yūya decides to save Naoto's life: he hides him in his room, and when they are discovered the two run away together, while Yūya learns that Naoto and the crime are not what they first seemed. Classmate Nozomi Honjō (who is curious about the balcony), Kaneshiro's crony Takagi, and Yūya's older sister Kozue, married to a police officer, also figure in the story (Japanese Wikipedia; MyAnimeList synopsis; AniList publisher blurb; Delcourt). The publisher promises a bittersweet ending; its details could not be confirmed.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What do you owe a classmate everyone is hurting, and what happens when you have already failed him? Yūya has watched Naoto be beaten and done nothing. When Naoto appears with a bloody bat, Yūya is offered a second chance at the worst possible moment.

**The answer the story gives.** Act now, even late. Yūya resolves to save Naoto's life and runs with him, trying to change the ending a newspaper has already printed (Japanese Wikipedia). The reviews suggest the answer is not a triumphant rescue: the mystery is "surprisingly anti-climactic" (MAL review) and the ending bittersweet. How much Yūya can change is the key point to verify.

**Characters as arguments.** Naoto argues that the world will not help anyone, so you must act alone to survive. Yūya argues, by his actions, that one person can choose to help after all. Kaneshiro is the case against rule by fear. Nozomi, a top student curious about the balcony, stands for reason and inquiry. The silent class is the argument the story condemns.

**Symbols and motifs.** The balcony where lost things drift in from other worlds; the newspaper from the future; the blood-stained bat; and the goat of the title. Capricorn is Naoto's sign, and the Japanese Wikipedia links the title to the scapegoat of Leviticus 16, the goat sent away bearing others' sins, which suggests the mystery turns on who carries the blame. A MAL reviewer also names "scapegoats" as a theme.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Japanese youth mystery of Otsuichi's "White" mode (sad and gentle rather than gory, per Wikipedia's account of his styles), the fugitive road story, and the time-loop warning (a message from the future that the hero tries to defy). The bystander theme echoes the Good Samaritan and Leviticus 19:16 for religious readers.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Existentialism is explored: choice and responsibility under a written fate. Might makes right is shown and condemned through Kaneshiro. Duty ethics is tested: loyalty versus the law, truth versus protection. No religious lens is present.

**Shifts across the series.** One volume of 7 chapters. The opening sets up the balcony, the bullying and the newspaper; the middle is the hiding and the flight; the last chapters reveal the truth and reach October 2 (structure from synopses; chapter placement to verify).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why did no one in Yūya's class stop Kaneshiro? What would have made it easier to speak up?
2. Yūya decides to help Naoto only after the killing. Is late help still worth giving?
3. Was hiding Naoto loyalty or a mistake? What else could Yūya have done?
4. The title points to a scapegoat. Who in the story carries blame for others?
5. If someone you know is thinking about hurting themselves, who can you tell? (Share a crisis line with your child.)
