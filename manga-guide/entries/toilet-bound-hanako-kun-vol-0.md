**Premise.** **Pilot chapters (ch. 1-3).** At Kamome Academy the rumor says Hanako-san of the third stall grants wishes. Nene Yashiro summons him and finds a boy who calls himself the third Hanako. Her first boyfriend cheated on her and mocked her "radish" ankles, so she wishes him cursed to death. Hanako agrees and heads for the roof with a kitchen knife; Nene runs after him, realizes she still cares about the boy and never really wanted him dead, and tackles her ex instead. When he calls her disgusting she beats him up in a slapstick scene, and Hanako tells her she can come to him whenever she is in trouble. Kou, a young exorcist, watches from another roof and vows to exorcise the "monster" (ch. 1, fan wiki). In ch. 2 Kou fights Hanako, Nene ends up between them, a different set of School Mysteries appears and Kou falls off a roof more than once (Yen Press blurb; Anime UK News). In ch. 3 Nene insists on a wish to see the spirits around her despite Hanako's warning, and the small rabbit-like Mokke cause trouble (Anime UK News; fan wiki cast list). **"Dear My Living Dead" (one-shot, just under half the book).** In a world overrun by the "living dead" (corpses raised by giant plant-like Mothers), a Church takes children born with a necromancer's seal at age five and uses them to fight. Its leader publicly executes parents who hide marked children. Two young necromancers, Cult (14, then 15) and Lilia (15), fight under their mentor Claid. Lilia's seal is fading; horrified by the executions, she begs Cult to run away with her. Cult stays loyal to the Church, shoots Lilia dead, binds her body to himself as his living-dead familiar and rewrites her memory so she believes she died in battle; she ends grateful, and the two vow to stay together forever (fan wiki: Lilia, Cult, Church Leader pages). Anime UK News calls it much darker than expected.

## Life message

**Wishing harm on someone who hurt you does not heal the hurt; what a lonely person needs is someone who listens. Love that overrides another person's will is its own kind of curse.**

| Evolutionary biology | A heartbreak story: Nene is rejected for her looks (ch. 1) and her first instinct is revenge against the rival male's choice; the one-shot turns pair-bonding into possession, as Cult keeps his partner by killing and binding her. Kin loyalty shows in the parents who hide their marked child from the Church and die for it. |
| Pragmatic | Curses do not work the way Nene imagined: Hanako's knife passes through people just as he does (ch. 1), and the real fix is talking it out. In the one-shot, Claid and Cult decide that obeying the Church is the only workable way to survive; the story shows the cost. |
| Religious | The pilots use Japanese school ghost lore (Hanako-san of the toilet, the Seven Mysteries, an onmyoji-style exorcist; ch. 2's Japanese title is "Shonen Onmyoji"). It is not identified with any real church. Faith readers will weigh wish-granting spirits and necromancy, and a religious authority drawn as a villain. |
| Socio-cultural | Teen dating, body-shaming ("radish ankles," a long-running Nene joke), and a girl's anger at a cheating boyfriend (ch. 1). The one-shot is a dark fable about institutions that take children and demand obedience. |

Outlook: Bittersweet — the pilot chapters end warmly, with Nene finding a strange new friend in Hanako, but the one-shot ends in a killing presented as a tragic, possessive love (fan wiki; Anime UK News).
Hope source: F — the way through is friendship: Nene does not need a curse, she needs someone to talk to, and Hanako offers to be that person (ch. 1; Anime UK News).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What do we really want when someone hurts us? And what happens when love will not let the other person go?

**The answer the story gives.** In the pilots, revenge turns out to be a disguise: Nene asks for a death curse but discovers she wanted someone to hear her, and a ghost who cannot touch the living becomes her friend (ch. 1). In the one-shot, Cult's love refuses to let Lilia leave, and he keeps her by taking her life and her memory. The story does not say he was right; it lets the horror sit under the sweetness of the last page.

**Characters as arguments.** Nene argues for honest feeling over performed anger. Hanako argues that a wish is a bargain and the wisher should know what she is asking. Kou argues that every spirit is a monster, and keeps falling off roofs. Lilia argues for conscience and freedom; Claid for quiet obedience; the Church Leader for safety bought with fear; Cult for loyalty that becomes possession.

**Symbols and motifs.** The third stall and the knock (a ritual that opens a door to another world). Hanako's kitchen knife, which passes through people (ch. 1): harm that cannot reach its target. The necromancer's seal that fades with age (childhood power that ends). Lilia's wooden body and the thorn through her eye: a living person turned into a possession.

**Traditions it echoes.** Japanese school ghost stories (gakko no kaidan), the yokai comedy, and Western gothic tales of the undead beloved. The one-shot recalls stories in which a religious order raises children as weapons.

**How each lens is portrayed.** The occult is the setting, explored without endorsement or condemnation. Authoritarian control is condemned through the Church. Virtue (mastering anger) is affirmed in Nene. Duty is shown as a trap in Cult and Claid.

**Shifts across the series.** The book moves from light comedy (pilots, ch. 1-3, 2014) to dark tragedy (the one-shot, 2013, placed last). The main series later deepens Hanako with a tragic backstory that the pilot does not have (Anime UK News).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Nene asked for a curse but did not really want one. Why do people sometimes say they want revenge when they want something else?
2. Is beating up her ex any better than cursing him? Why does the story play it as a joke?
3. Hanako warns Nene before granting a wish. What wishes in real life come with a price?
4. In the one-shot, Claid thinks the Church is wrong but obeys. When is obedience the wrong choice?
5. Cult says he loves Lilia. Can you love someone and still take away their choices?
