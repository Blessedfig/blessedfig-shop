**Premise.** At the end of Citrus, Yuzu Aihara, a fashionable, outspoken gyaru, and Mei Aihara, the reserved student council president and granddaughter of the school's chairman, became a couple and exchanged rings: they are stepsisters through their parents' marriage, not related by blood, and they share a room (Wikipedia). Citrus+ follows them after the proposal. Mei moves back home and the two agree to take things slowly, as if starting over (OtakuAuthor, vol. 1); Yuzu studies hard so as not to hold Mei back, and Mei plans to become the first chairwoman of Aihara Academy. Sayaka, a first-year on the student council who finds it hard to believe two girls can love each other, hears they are dating and tries to remove Mei as president and restore the old rules (vol. 2). Their friend Harumi deals with Matsuri, a younger girl who has stopped going to class and tries to kiss her (vol. 3). Mei plans a birthday present (vol. 4); Yuzu does well in mock college exams but quarrels with junior-high friends who think she has changed (vol. 5); Mei's plans to rebuild the academy bring in Arata, son of the chair of an all-boys' school and Mei's childhood friend, which unsettles Yuzu (vol. 6); the academy's expansion and an acquisition continue in vol. 7 (Seven Seas blurbs via Apple Books, Penguin Australia, Penguin NZ and Penguin Random House).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What happens after the confession? Most romance stories end when the couple gets together. Citrus+ begins there: Yuzu has proposed, Mei has said yes, and they have to find out what a relationship is made of when the drama is over.

**The answer the story gives.** Love is made of ordinary choices: saying the words even when it is hard (vol. 2), studying so you can stand beside your partner (vol. 1, 5), remembering a birthday (vol. 4), trusting when someone from the past appears (vol. 6). The story affirms the couple fully and asks only that they keep talking.

**Characters as arguments.** Yuzu argues for openness and effort. Mei argues for duty and reserve, and slowly learns that love needs expression. Sayaka argues that rules matter and that girls cannot really love girls; the story lets her be heard and then answers her. Harumi argues for friendship that reaches out even when it is awkward. Matsuri, the troublemaker of the first series, becomes a lonely kid who needs someone to check on her.

**Symbols and motifs.** The rings; diary-date chapter titles that make the story a calendar of an ordinary year; the school uniform rules Yuzu bends; the academy Mei means to rebuild as a symbol of a future the two will share.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Japanese yuri tradition of schoolgirl romance, the after-the-wedding domestic comedy, and the coming-of-age story of finding one's path after school. Readers from traditions that hold marriage to be between a man and a woman will read the same story as an argument they do not accept.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism and progressivism are affirmed; traditionalism is present as the school's old order, which Mei means to reform from inside rather than tear down. Communitarianism is warm: friends and parents are meddling but loving.

**Shifts across the series.** The first series (2012-2018) was a melodrama built partly on coercive advances; Citrus+ (2018-) is slower and gentler. Vols. 1-2 focus on the couple and the school's reaction; vols. 3-5 widen to friends (Harumi and Matsuri; old junior-high friends); vols. 6-7 turn to Mei's plans for the academy and an outside rival for her attention.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Mei finds it hard to say "I love you." Why might showing love be harder than feeling it?
2. Sayaka doubts that two girls can love each other. How does the story answer her, and how would your family answer her?
3. Yuzu studies hard so she will not be a burden. Is that a good reason to work hard?
4. The first series was criticized for scenes where one girl forced herself on the other. Why does consent matter in a love story?
5. What do you think a relationship needs after the big confession?
