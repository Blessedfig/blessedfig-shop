**Premise.** Junpei Takeda, a high-school student bullied by the delinquent Shouta Kido and defended by his childhood friend Noriko Tatsumiya, is summoned with them to another world by a "god" who does it on a whim for entertainment. Everyone else gets heroic powers; Junpei's stats are worse than a farmer's, though he can take skills from enemies he kills. After months of struggle, Noriko and Kido trick him into the Sacrificial Labyrinth, a dungeon no one has escaped (ch. 1-2). He survives by cunning, eats the flesh of an undead king and gains its poison body, meets a few allies (Gossan, an old adventurer, and Mia, a girl who is killed by Noriko, ch. 15-17), and climbs toward revenge. Later chapters reveal that Noriko acted under Kido's control and abuse; Junpei fights and kills her (ch. 26-31). At the end the dungeon grants a wish: Junpei cannot revive Mia, so he asks that Gossan and his family reach safety, returns to Earth, and keeps his powers to kill people he judges evil (ch. 33) (AniList; MangaUpdates; MyAnimeList; Isekai fan wiki; fan forums).

**In a cruel world where even the gods toy with people, the betrayed survive by becoming stronger and paying their enemies back; trust is dangerous, and the strong decide who deserves to die.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What does a betrayed person owe the world? Junpei is bullied, then betrayed by the one friend he trusted, then thrown into a dungeon to die. The series asks whether there is any reason left to trust or forgive.

**The answer the story gives.** No: get strong and pay them back. Junpei survives by cunning, eats a monster to take its power, and climbs toward revenge. Allies who trust him die (Mia, ch. 17). The betrayer, Noriko, turns out to have been coerced and abused by the bully, but Junpei kills her anyway (ch. 27-31). The bully himself is never confronted, and the final pages show Junpei back on Earth, still killing those he judges evil (ch. 33).

**Characters as arguments.** Junpei argues that power is the only protection. Noriko argues, too late, that she betrayed him to survive, and her regret is the story's only turn toward mercy, which fans call a retcon. Kido stands for the bully who takes what he wants. The "god" stands for a universe that is cruel for fun. Gossan and Mia show that kindness exists, and that it is fragile.

**Symbols and motifs.** The Sacrificial Labyrinth as a world without exit. The poison body: Junpei becomes untouchable and unable to be touched. Stats screens that rank human worth. The final wish: Junpei cannot bring back the dead, so he saves the living.

**Traditions it echoes.** Revenge tragedy (The Count of Monte Cristo), the dark isekai wave of the 2010s (fans name Arifureta, Shield Hero and Redo of Healer), and the bullied boy's power fantasy. Religious readers will hear the warnings against vengeance in many scriptures; secular readers may ask whether Junpei becomes what he hated.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Might makes right is affirmed. Nihilism shapes the world but is not the hero's final word: he still wishes for others' safety. Pragmatism wins every fight.

**Shifts across the series.** Volume 1 (about ch. 1-8): summoning, betrayal, first survival and the cannibal village. Volume 2 (about ch. 9-17): the dungeon, new allies, Mia's death. Volume 3 (about ch. 18-25): Gossan's revenge on the Minotaur, torture and Noriko's backstory. Volume 4 (about ch. 26-33): the battle with Noriko and the rushed ending.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Junpei was bullied and betrayed. What would justice for him have looked like?
2. Noriko was coerced and abused by Kido. Should that change how we judge her betrayal?
3. Why is Kido, the abuser, never punished, while Noriko is?
4. At the end Junpei uses his wish to save Gossan's family. What does that say about him?
5. What does it do to a person to make revenge the purpose of life?
