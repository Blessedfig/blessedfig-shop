**Premise.** Kae Serinuma, a heavyset second-year in her high school's history club, is a fujoshi: she loves boys'-love stories and secretly imagines her classmates as couples. When her favorite anime character, Shion of *Mirage Saga*, is killed off, she shuts herself in her room for a week, barely eats, and comes out dramatically thin. Suddenly four boys want to date her: classmates Yusuke Igarashi (soccer) and Nozomu Nanashima (a "bad boy" who looks like Shion), first-year Hayato Shinomiya, and third-year Asuma Mutsumi, the history club president, who liked her before she changed. Kae, who would rather see the boys with each other, accepts outings with all four. Shima Nishina, a first-year girl who is a fellow fujoshi and doujinshi artist, joins the rivals (vol. 3). Later arcs bring a Valentine's chocolate binge that returns Kae to her old weight and shows who likes her for herself, Mutsumi's older brother Kazuma (a student teacher) pursuing her (vol. 5), a hot-spring trip (vol. 7), confessions (vol. 9), and a childhood friend turned voice actor, Takeru Mitsuboshi, who tricks and abducts her (vols. 10-11). Kae ends up dating Mutsumi (vol. 13); the manga's ending, after a time skip, has them married (fan wiki; AniList). The anime instead ends with Kae choosing her fictional Shion (ANN) (Wikipedia; Penguin Random House; fan wiki).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Do people love you, or the way you look? Kae changes nothing about herself but her weight, and suddenly four boys want her. The comedy asks what they actually want, and what she does.

**The answer the story gives.** Love that lasts sees the person. Mutsumi recognized Kae at once because she was still kind (fan wiki); when she regains the weight, some suitors scheme to slim her down while others do not care (Kotaku; fan wiki). Kae herself never changes what she loves, and in the end she chooses the one who liked her before. Anime Feminist's objection stands, though: the premise rewards a week without food and uses "fat Kae" as a punchline.

**Characters as arguments.** Kae argues for joyful, unapologetic passion. Igarashi argues that attraction can grow into real regard. Shima argues that love need not follow expected lines, and that a fellow fan can understand Kae best. Kazuma shows charm abusing a position of trust and is played as a joke. Takeru, who wants Kae to stay as she was, shows possessive love and is stopped by her friends (vol. 11).

**Symbols and motifs.** Shion's death and possible return; the "prince beside a prince" fantasy; cosplay and costumes (identity as something you put on); Kae's eyes shrinking to their old shape whenever fandom takes over.

**Traditions it echoes.** Otome games and reverse-harem shōjo, which it parodies; the makeover story, which it half-mocks; and the older moral that the heart matters more than appearance, which several faiths state outright.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism wins: Kae is never asked to stop being a fujoshi. Communitarianism grows as rivals become friends. Postmodern parody keeps winking at romance conventions. Feminist readings split between the welcome female gaze and the fat jokes.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-4: the makeover, the four suitors, Shima, the Valentine's weight regain. Vols. 5-9: Kazuma, the hot-spring trip, confessions. Vols. 10-12: Takeru and the abduction. Vols. 13-14: Kae dates Mutsumi, chooses him over her devotion to Shion, and the series closes with them married after a time skip (fan wiki; to verify).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why did the boys only notice Kae after she lost weight? Which of them changed their minds about why they liked her?
2. Kae stopped eating because she was sad. What would you say to a friend who did that?
3. Is it fair that Kae imagines her classmates as couples without asking them? Where is the line between a hobby and treating people as characters?
4. What should happen when a teacher flirts with students, as Kazuma does?
5. Kae never gives up what she loves. Is there something you love that others think is strange?
