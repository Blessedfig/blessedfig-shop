**Premise.** Shun Takahata, 16, is bored with his life. One morning his teacher's head bursts and a Daruma doll emerges and starts a lethal game of Daruma-san ga Koronda (Red Light, Green Light): anyone it sees move dies, and only one student may press the button on its back (ch. 1). Shun is the only survivor of his class. Survivors from schools across Japan move through more children's games turned deadly: a giant beckoning cat (Maneki-neko) that must be belled while it eats players dressed as mice (ch. 2-4); kokeshi dolls playing Kagome Kagome in a hospital (ch. 6-8); a 100-jump rope game (ch. 8-9); a tug of war against impaling statues (ch. 10-11); and a party in Urashima Tarō's undersea castle that ends in a lottery of death (ch. 12-13) (fan wiki). Shun is joined by the composed Ichika Akimoto and by Takeru Amaya, a violent student who enjoys the slaughter and kills the other survivors of the cat game with a sword (ch. 4). Five survivors, the "God's Children," return to a changed world; three months later the god behind the games, Kamimaro, appears and summons them to a deadly sports day (Undokai): a true-or-false race chased by man-eating nutcrackers (ch. 15-17) and Zetsubou Taoshi (ch. 18-20) (Kodansha vol. 4; fan wiki). In the last chapter Shun and Amaya try to kill Kamimaro with the game's own bombs, are wounded by them and are cast out, near death, for trying to cheat God (ch. 21, Wikipedia). The Second Series saves them and reveals Kamimaro as a human given god-powers by a being from outside the world (Wikipedia).

## Life message

**Being alive is worth fighting for: courage, quick thinking and trust in others carry you through a world ruled by cruel, arbitrary powers, and the boredom you felt before was a failure to see what life is worth.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What is a life worth when it can be taken for no reason? Shun begins bored, wishing for something to happen. The games give him that wish in the cruelest form and ask whether his life was empty or whether he simply never saw its worth.

**The answer the story gives.** Life is worth fighting for, and it is best fought for together. Shun survives not because he is strongest but because he notices things (the cat's itch, the kokeshi voices) and keeps others calm. Yet the series refuses comfort: four of his group die by lottery (ch. 13), and when he tries to strike back at the god he is cast out (ch. 21).

**Characters as arguments.** Shun argues for wit and solidarity. Amaya argues that strength and destruction are the only honest response to such a world, and he survives too, which keeps the story from tidy moral bookkeeping. Ichika's calm hides a thrill in danger, a warning about how violence can attract. Kamimaro, a naked and childish god, argues that power needs no reason.

**Symbols and motifs.** The daruma, a good-luck doll, turned executioner. The counting rhyme of the title, used to choose who is 'it.' The cube that appears over the city. Urashima Tarō's treasure box and the castle party that ends in death: pleasure followed by loss, as in the folktale.

**Traditions it echoes.** Battle Royale and the death-game genre; the folktale of Urashima Tarō; the book of Job and Ecclesiastes for readers who ask why the innocent suffer (a comparison, not a source). Its god resembles the capricious gods of myth more than the God of the Abrahamic faiths, and the sequel makes him a human given power.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Absurdism and nihilism are explored, not endorsed: the story mourns its dead. Might makes right is critiqued in Kamimaro but left uneasy in Amaya. Pragmatism and cooperation are rewarded. Existentialism is the arc of Shun himself.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-3 (ch. 1-13) are the first wave of games, from the classroom to the undersea castle. Volumes 4-5 (ch. 14-21) jump three months ahead to a changed world, introduce Kamimaro and the deadly sports day, and end with the failed attack on him. Readers who want an ending must go on to the 21-volume Second Series, which is more violent in scale and not covered by this entry.
