**Premise.** In a European-style fantasy land, mages (magic users who look human) must hide, because the Bureau of Magic Surveillance arrests them and sends them to detention camps. Nona Ewin, the daughter of a mage mother, Lancia, and a human father the Bureau killed before she was born, uses her stitching magic to mend her best friend Lovi's broken glasses. The Bureau notices, interrogates Lovi and her parents in the street, and when Lovi, threatened with her parents' deaths, names Nona, executes all three on the spot as "collective responsibility"; Nona is shot in the arm, and Lancia's comb-blade magic cuts down soldiers and wrecks the town as the two escape by broom; a portal-gun prototype then shoots Lancia from the sky after she pushes Nona clear (ch. 1; fan wiki; Japanese Wikipedia). Nona washes up at Bureau Base 30, where she meets Miramira, a mage forced to work there whose brother was sent to the Uvral camp after taking the blame for her escape plan; Nona ties up a guard, threatens his eye with a needle and breaks them both out (ch. 2; AniTrendz; fan wiki). The two head for Zuwi City to find the mage resistance group Quovelta, which raids camps to free prisoners; on the way they work with delivery men Raloud, a young mage searching for his origins, and Belator Tetom, once the Bureau's most famous mage-catcher, whose wife and daughter were killed when a mage destroyed his town (fan wiki; Da Vinci Web). Nona's mother's fate is unclear in sources: Wikipedia says they are separated, Da Vinci Web says she was taken, the fan wiki says she was shot.

## Life message

**Hunting people for what they are is wrong, and the answer to it is to protect and free one another rather than to take revenge.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What happens to friendship when the law makes it a crime? Nona's single kindness, mending a friend's glasses, sets off a chain that ends with her friend's family shot in the street and her mother taken from the sky.

**The answer the story gives.** So far: protect each other, escape, and do not become what hunts you. AniTrendz notes Nona is rough with her captors but wants only to find her mother and live in peace; Da Vinci Web calls it a story of regaining hope, not revenge.

**Characters as arguments.** Nona argues that hiding is not living. Miramira argues that submission might keep you safe, and her brother's transfer proves it does not (AniTrendz). Lovi argues for friendship across the line and pays for it. The Bureau argues for safety through fear. Belator, who lost his family to a mage and became the Bureau's top hunter before quitting, argues that the fear has real roots (fan wiki).

**Symbols and motifs.** Needle and thread: Nona's magic mends and binds. Glasses: seeing clearly. Combs, wheels, anchors: each mage's magic is an everyday object. Shackles and magic tools that watch prisoners.

**Traditions it echoes.** The European witch hunts; dystopian stories of informers and camps; shonen fantasy about hunted heroes. For Jewish, Anabaptist, Latter-day Saint, Bahá'í and Black church readers, histories of persecution and deliverance.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Authoritarianism is the target; individual rights and solidarity are the answer. The occult imagery is a fantasy device; the story treats mages as a people, not a practice.

**Shifts across the series.** Ch. 1-2: massacre and prison break. Ch. 3-4: the Bureau's plan revealed (ComicBook.com). Later volumes: the road to Zuwi, the delivery men Raloud and Belator (vol. 3 cover: Raloud), the painter Kurt, and Quovelta's Anemone (from ch. 44). Details past ch. 4 are thin in sources.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Lovi named Nona only when her parents were threatened. Was she a traitor?
2. Why does the Bureau punish whole families? What does that do to a town?
3. Miramira thought obeying would keep her safe. What changed her mind?
4. Belator hunted mages after one destroyed his town. Can you understand him without agreeing with him?
5. Nona is angry but does not seek revenge. Why might that matter for how the story ends?
