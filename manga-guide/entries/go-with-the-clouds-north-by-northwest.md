**Premise.** Kei Miyama, 17, part Japanese, lives in Iceland with his grandfather, a gruff man with a mysterious bond with birds, and works as a private detective (Otaku USA; Manga Bookshelf). He has three secrets: he can talk to cars and other machines (especially his moody old car), he goes to pieces around pretty girls, and he is a detective (Kodansha blurb). Early cases include a lost dog and a woman looking for a man she fell for at first sight. Then Kei cannot reach his younger brother Michitaka in Japan; he flies home to find the aunt and uncle who raised Michitaka dead, and a Japanese policeman insists Michitaka killed them. Michitaka turns up in Iceland, sweet and beautiful, swearing he is innocent, and Kei believes him (v1; Otaku USA; The Manga Critic). Volume 2 is a road trip with Kei's childhood friend Kiyoshi; volume 3 brings a runaway teen at a hostel and a woman hounded by a caller, who turns out to be Michitaka, revealed to kill with a power that cuts people apart inside their skin (Manga Mavericks). Volume 4 deepens Michitaka's past as a manipulator, and Kei grows close to Lilja, a sharp-tongued Icelandic girl. In volume 5 Michitaka reappears, suspected of three murders; in volume 6 the police take him away; in volume 7 Kei returns to Japan to search for a brother he believes is still alive and realizes he never really knew him (Kodansha blurbs).

## Life message

**Love for family must not blind you to the truth: patient, honest attention to people and places, the way Kei listens to his car and to Iceland itself, is how you find out what is real, even when the truth is about someone you love.**

| Pragmatic | Detective work by close observation, patience and Kei's odd gift; cases are solved by noticing details, though Kei's assumptions are sometimes wrong (the runaway teen, v3). |
| Religious | No religion shapes the story. Churches appear among the Icelandic scenery (Manga Bookshelf), and quiet gifts (talking to machines, birds) give the world a mild magical realism. | Socio-cultural | A loving portrait of Iceland: Irie drove around the country before the series (Manga Bookshelf) and reviewers call it a travelogue (Manga Mavericks). One arc contrasts Kei's assumptions with Iceland's open attitudes toward gay teens (v3, Manga Mavericks). |

Outlook: Bittersweet — the land, friendships and a slow romance give real warmth, while the brother Kei loves is revealed as a killer and arrested; the English edition ends (v7) with Kei searching for him and doubting what he knew.
Hope source: J — the healing comes from small everyday joys: road trips, landscapes, a friend's visit, a song when Kei is sick (v4-5); family and friends (F) are the close second, though family is also where the danger lies.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What do you do when the person you love most may be a monster? Kei, a born observer, refuses for volumes to see what is in front of him.

**The answer the story gives.** Truth wins, slowly. The evidence piles up (v3-5), the police take Michitaka (v6), and Kei admits he did not know his brother (v7). Love is not abandoned, but it is no longer blind.

**Characters as arguments.** Kei argues for loyalty and careful attention, and learns where loyalty fails. Michitaka argues for being adored no matter what, and shows where that ends. Lilja argues for honest care. The grandfather stands for quiet wisdom; the Japanese investigator for the law's cold but correct suspicion.

**Symbols and motifs.** The car that talks back; north-by-northwest roads and clouds; birds around the grandfather; Michitaka's two faces, drawn glassy-eyed or soft (Manga Mavericks); Iceland's waterfalls, craters and snow.

**Traditions it echoes.** The road story and the travelogue; the detective with a gift; Japanese iyashikei healing manga. Its title echoes Hitchcock's North by Northwest (from general knowledge; to verify).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Virtue ethics is tested, not preached: loyalty without prudence fails. Pragmatism shows in the detective work. Love of nature is everywhere. One arc affirms a gay teen; the story otherwise avoids politics.

**Shifts across the series.** Volume 1 sets up cases and the murder mystery; volume 2 is a road trip with Kiyoshi; volume 3 reveals Michitaka's power and guilt; volumes 4-5 deepen the romance with Lilja and the brother's pathology; volume 6 brings his arrest; volume 7 moves to Japan. Volume-only chapters from 2026 were not checked.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Kei is a great detective but cannot see the truth about his brother. Why?
2. Michitaka is sweet to some people and cruel to others. How can you tell what someone is really like?
3. Kei guessed wrong about why Jon ran away. What did he assume, and why?
4. Some scenes show Kei and Lilja grabbing each other as a joke. Is that okay? How would you feel?
5. What place makes you feel the way Iceland makes Kei feel?
