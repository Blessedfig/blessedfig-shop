**Premise.** Mikako Koda, 16, dreams of becoming a fashion designer and sells her own label, Happy Berry. She and her lifelong neighbor Tsutomu Yamaguchi enter Yazawa Arts Academy in Tokyo, where Tsutomu becomes popular for looking like a rock singer and starts dating Mariko Nakasu ("Nice Body-ko"), the school's most admired girl, and Mikako realizes she is jealous (VIZ; ANN). With friends Risa (a punk from Hokkaido who wants to design children's clothes), P-chan (a shy doll maker) and the boys Jiro, Yuusuke and later Ayumi, she founds Akindo, a club that sells crafts at flea markets. Mikako and Tsutomu become a couple; Mikako's divorced parents, a manga-artist mother and a photographer father, slowly reconcile; Tsutomu finds his calling as a photographer under her father. Mikako wins the school fashion show and a scholarship to study in London (ch. 26), struggles over leaving Tsutomu (ch. 27-29), and faces her mother's health scare (VIZ calls it a serious diagnosis) and the news that her mother is pregnant (ch. 29-30, fan wiki; to verify). She goes to London (fan wiki; VIZ vol. 4). An epilogue set about ten years later shows the friends grown, married and with children, setting up the sequel Paradise Kiss (fan wiki; Wikipedia).

## Creator and publisher context

- Ai Yazawa (born March 7, 1967, Amagasaki) debuted in Ribon in 1985 at 17; she enrolled at the fashion school Osaka Mode Gakuen to train as a stylist and left when Ribon began serializing her work; breakthrough with I'm No Angel (1991-1995), then Neighborhood Story (1995), Last Quarter (1998), Paradise Kiss (1999, Zipper) and Nana (2000, Cookie; Shogakukan Manga Award 2003) (Wikipedia, Ai Yazawa).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can you love someone and still go your own way? Mikako has organized her whole life around Tsutomu since the day he found her hiding after her parents' divorce (ch. 29). The series asks whether that love is a home or a cage.

**The answer the story gives.** Love lets the other person grow. Tsutomu refuses to tell Mikako not to go (ch. 28), Risa calls her reluctance dependence, Seiji tells her that choosing either way takes courage, and Mariko warns that needing someone too much is a burden. Mikako goes to London (fan wiki), and the epilogue shows that the bond survived.

**Characters as arguments.** Mikako argues for passion and craft. Tsutomu argues for gentle loyalty, and finds his own vocation only when he stops living in her shadow. Mariko argues against being valued for your body: she refuses relationships that are only about sex, and Tsutomu's refusal of her is what makes her love him (ANN; fan wiki). Risa is the counterweight: she turns down London to stay with Takeshi, and the story does not judge her. Ruriko and Hirohiko show that adults also get second chances.

**Symbols and motifs.** Happy Berry, Mikako's label, as her self. Hairstyles that change when she cannot face something (ch. 28). The snow rabbit that melted the day her parents divorced (ch. 29). The playground where Tsutomu always finds her (ch. 1, 29). The flea market as a place where young people sell what they make.

**Traditions it echoes.** The shojo coming-of-age story and the artist's novel of vocation. Its view that love must not become possession echoes many traditions' warnings about attachment, and its restored marriage echoes reconciliation stories.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism wins: Mikako's dream is honored above comfort. Community is the safety net that makes the dream possible. Feminist readers find a heroine whose ambition is never mocked and a 'sexy' rival given an inner life. Traditional readers find a marriage repaired and families formed, alongside teen romance and cohabitation treated as normal.

**Shifts across the series.** English vol. 1 (about ch. 1-9): jealousy, Mariko, Akindo. Vol. 2 (about ch. 10-17): the couple forms; Mikako's father returns. Vol. 3 (about ch. 18-26): summer villa, Mariko and Yuusuke's breakup, the school festival and fashion show. Vol. 4 (about ch. 27-33): the London decision, Ruriko's pregnancy, departure and epilogue.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Tsutomu would not tell Mikako to stay. Was that love?
2. Risa chose to stay with her boyfriend instead of studying in London. Is her choice as good as Mikako's?
3. How does Mariko's nickname affect her? What changes how others see her?
4. Mikako's parents divorced and later remarried. What do you think made the second time possible?
5. What is the difference between depending on someone and loving them?
