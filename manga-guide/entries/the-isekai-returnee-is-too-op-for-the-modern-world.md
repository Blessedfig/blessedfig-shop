**Premise.** Daiki Morishita, a high school boy who had been bullied (MangaUpdates), was summoned to another world, became its hero and defeated the Demon Lord. Back in Japan, he hides his power and enjoys an ordinary life, until he learns that modern Japan has a hidden world of apparitions (yōkai) and people with special powers, "a supernatural school-battle story" (publisher synopsis). His power draws attention: he ends up helping the long-haired Kaguya Abeno raise her "stats" and is targeted by the blonde Lera Sakaguchi of a mysterious organization (vol. 2). He rescues Kaguya and Lera from the nine-tailed fox Kyūbi, "the strongest curse god," and overpowers it (vol. 3). Volume 4 returns to his past as a hero, when he reached out to a girl who had given up on the world and was about to end her life. In volumes 5-6, Vatican paladins loyal to Lera (the Guardians) appear; a fellow isekai returnee, the Vatican paladin Linford, whose cheat skill reflects every attack, plans to burn Daiki's city; Daiki loses the holy sword Excalibur, regains his strength through the Guardians' support and defeats Linford with the relic Longinus. His younger sister Maria returns from studying abroad strangely cold, and a new assassin arrives (BookWalker blurbs, vols. 2-6).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What would a hero who already won do when he comes home? Daiki wants a quiet life, but strength draws trouble, and the people around him need protecting.

**The answer the story gives.** From the blurbs: power is for protecting. Volume 4 states the ideal through a girl who had stopped believing in heroes who help the weak and defeat the wicked; Daiki becomes that hero for her. The story does not question power itself.

**Characters as arguments.** Daiki argues that the strong should protect. Kaguya argues for growing stronger by training. Lera and the Guardians argue that loyalty to what is right outranks loyalty to an institution. Linford is the dark mirror, a fellow returnee whose power and zeal turn destructive.

**Symbols and motifs.** Hidden strength; stats and MP; Excalibur lost and Longinus gained (vol. 6); the curse god defeated.

**Traditions it echoes.** The isekai-returnee and "overpowered hero" genres; Japanese yōkai lore and the nine-tailed fox; Arthurian and Christian relic legends used as game items (from general knowledge; to verify).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Strength rules is affirmed but tied to protection. Folk religion is a setting rather than a belief system. Fan service is endorsed as comedy.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-2: return, school and the hidden world; vol. 3: the curse god; vol. 4: the hero's past and a rescued girl; vols. 5-6: Vatican paladins and Linford; vol. 6 onward: his sister and a new assassin.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Daiki hides his power at first. Why would someone hide a strength?
2. The girl in vol. 4 had stopped believing heroes exist. What changed her mind?
3. Are there problems that strength cannot solve? Does the story show any?
4. How are the women in the story written? Would you want to be written that way?
5. Holy relics become weapons in the story. How do you feel about sacred things used that way?
