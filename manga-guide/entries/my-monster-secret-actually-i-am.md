**Premise.** Asahi Kuromine, a high school second-year, is so bad at hiding things that his nickname means 'leaky sieve.' When he goes to confess to his crush, Youko Shiragami, he sees her unfold bat wings: she is half vampire, allowed at a human school only if no one finds out. Asahi swears to keep her secret. He soon finds his school full of secrets: the class president Nagisa is a tiny alien piloting a human-sized body; Shiho is a werewolf who switches between a tough boy (Shirou) and a self-proclaimed pervert girl when she sees a moon; the principal Akane is a thousand-year-old demon in a child's body; the student council president is a fallen angel; and a granddaughter from the future, Rin, arrives (Wikipedia; fan wiki). Asahi and Youko confess and become a couple (ch. 85-86) and go on a first date (vol. 12). Late arcs send Asahi into the past to learn why Youko's father distrusts humans (vol. 20), and a new principal, Shirayuki, tries to separate humans and non-humans (vol. 21). In the final volume Youko reveals to her classmates that she is a vampire to save Shirayuki, Asahi proposes, and they graduate (vol. 22; fan wiki).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What does it mean to keep a secret if you cannot lie? Asahi, who cannot hide anything, becomes the keeper of everyone's biggest secret, and the series asks whether honesty and loyalty can coexist.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes: honesty is not telling everything, but never betraying a trust. Asahi grows from a nervous liar-by-accident into someone others confide in (ANN vol. 1; fan wiki). In the end the secret is no longer needed: Youko tells the truth herself and is accepted (vol. 22).

**Characters as arguments.** Youko argues for living openly; Genjirou argues that humans will reject non-humans, from his own painful past (vol. 20). Shirayuki argues that separation prevents suffering; the story rescues her rather than defeats her. Shiho argues for shameless enjoyment, and the story mostly laughs with her. Akane, a demon, argues for coexistence.

**Symbols and motifs.** Wings unfurled in an empty classroom (ch. 1); the 'leaky sieve' nickname; nosebleeds as the comic sign of temptation; the moon that changes Shirou into Shiho; time travelers carrying the consequences of today's choices.

**Traditions it echoes.** Monster-girl and harem comedies (ANN contrasts it with Prison School and Monster Musume); the folklore of vampires, werewolves and yokai; stories of minorities 'passing' to be accepted.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Duty ethics wins in Asahi's honesty; communitarian coexistence wins over separation. Hedonism is played for laughs through Shiho, without a verdict.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-4 introduce the secret-keepers; vols. 5-11 build the romance to the confession (ch. 85-86); vols. 12-19 pair off the supporting cast; vols. 20-22 turn serious with the past, Shirayuki and the graduation.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Asahi can't lie, but he keeps secrets. Is that the same as being honest?
2. Why did Youko have to hide who she was? What changed so she could tell the truth?
3. Shirayuki thought keeping humans and non-humans apart would stop suffering. What do you think?
4. Some jokes in this series involve touching people without asking. How would those scenes feel to the person being touched?
5. Who in your life could you trust with a secret, and why?
