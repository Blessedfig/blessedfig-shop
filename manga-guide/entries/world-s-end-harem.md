**Premise.** In 2040 Reito Mizuhara, a medical researcher with a rare illness, is cryogenically frozen. He wakes five years later to find that the MK (Man-Killer) virus has killed 99.9% of the world's men. He is one of five immune men in Japan, and the women-led United World government expects each of them to father children with as many women as possible. Reito refuses: he will search for a cure and for his childhood sweetheart Erisa Tachibana, who vanished three years earlier, and he will not sleep with women he does not love (Wikipedia; TV Tropes). The other men take different paths. Kyōji Hino indulges fully; Shōta Doi, a high school student who was bullied before his freeze, is placed in an all-girls school staged around him by his handler Karen Kamiya and grows predatory; Zen Kinebuchi is held captive by a cult (TV Tropes). The virus proves to be a man-made bioweapon; Reito's attendant Mira is revealed to be Erisa's clone; Hino is killed at the end of Part 1; antiviral proteins from Reito's blood point toward a cure. After World expands the conflict among the council, the Izanami rebels and a terrorist group, Maria's Womb of a Virgin, while Shōta and Karen scheme to take over Japan (Wikipedia; TV Tropes). The final chapters are not described in sources opened.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** If the species depends on you, do you still own your body and your love? The United World says no; Reito says yes.

**The answer the story gives.** Through Reito, that love and consent cannot be ordered: he refuses for years, finds a cure through science, and gives himself only to Erisa (and Mira, with Erisa's blessing) (TV Tropes). Through everyone else, the story lingers on what he refuses, and lets Shōta, who embraces coercion, rise. The two answers sit side by side.

**Characters as arguments.** Reito argues for fidelity. Hino argues for pleasure and dies. Shōta argues that the bullied may become the bully, and the story rewards him. Erisa argues for the species over the self. The Sages argue for a world without men, and are cartoon villains.

**Symbols and motifs.** Cryosleep (waking into a changed world); the clone who replaces a loved one; the cure carried in the faithful man's blood; names from Japan's creation myth given to rebels who repeat the abuses they fight.

**Traditions it echoes.** "Last man on Earth" fiction (Y: The Last Man; ANN's Eisenbeis makes the grim-realist comparison) crossed with harem fantasy. The faithful hero recalls Joseph resisting Potiphar's wife for many religious readers.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Authoritarianism is condemned; liberalism's consent is the hero's line; hedonism is both condemned in Hino and sold to the reader; feminism appears only as a villainous caricature.

**Shifts across the series.** V1-2 (ch. 1-14): Reito's awakening and refusal. V2-5 (ch. 13-34): Shōta's school (the most troubling content). V6-8 (ch. 35-55): the conspiracy, cult and Izanami. V9-12 (ch. 56-85): the virus's origin, Erisa's past and the end of Part 1. V13-18 (After World): politics, terrorism and the end of the world crisis; final chapters not confirmed.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Reito keeps saying no. Why does the story make that so hard?
2. Is the story criticizing the world it shows, or enjoying it?
3. Shōta was badly bullied. Does that excuse what he becomes?
4. Erisa says the species needs Reito to have many partners. How would you answer her?
5. Why might a parent decide some books are not for their family at all?
