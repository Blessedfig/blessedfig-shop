**Premise.** Misuzu Moritani, a shy high-school girl, can stop time for three minutes a day. She uses the power to look up the skirt of Haruka Murakami, the most popular girl in class, and discovers that Murakami is immune and knows what she did. Moritani apologizes and agrees to do what Murakami wants, which turns into secret dates in frozen time (Seven Seas; CBR; Wikipedia). Murakami tells Moritani that a teacher, Yamaguchi, is harassing her and asks for help, then admits she made it up to get exam answers, though she says he does touch her sometimes; classmates gossip and someone writes a slur about her on the board (TV Tropes). The two grow close in stolen moments: Murakami puts Moritani's hand on her chest, Moritani kisses her in the sickbay and they start dating, and during a test Murakami undresses on her desk in frozen time and tells Moritani to do as she likes (Wikipedia; chapter 6 is titled "I Want You to Watch Me," Goodreads). Moritani makes a new friend, Yukari Kobayashi, and uses her power to protect her from mockery. To prove her sincerity, Moritani shows that she bought the same underwear as Murakami, Murakami reciprocates in front of classmates, and the two run off together (Wikipedia; ch. 11 title, Goodreads). Moritani's power begins to fade. Murakami confesses that she has always played roles to be liked and fears being disliked; Moritani outs their relationship to the class, chases her, and tells her she loves the real her. Months later Moritani's power is gone, she is more outgoing, and the two are still together (Wikipedia; final chapter "I Love You," Goodreads).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can two people really know each other when one hides from everyone and the other shows everyone a different face? And can love grow from a beginning that was a wrong?

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, if both stop hiding. Moritani's power fades as she lets people in (TV Tropes), and Murakami is loved once she admits she has been performing (Wikipedia). The story treats Moritani's apology as enough to move past the first scene; critics such as Okazu do not.

**Characters as arguments.** Moritani argues that it is safer to watch than to be seen. Murakami argues that you must be whatever others want. Kobayashi is the plain, kind friend who shows another way. Yamaguchi-sensei and the gossiping class show adults and peers who fail the girls.

**Symbols and motifs.** Stopped time: a private world where no one can judge, and a place where boundaries are broken. Murakami's immunity: the one person who wants to enter Moritani's space (TV Tropes). The flipbook of roles. Matching underwear as a clumsy pledge of loyalty.

**Traditions it echoes.** The yuri school romance, the time-stop fantasy, and the coming-of-age story about masks and true selves.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism and existential authenticity are affirmed. Sexual curiosity is explored and mostly played as romantic. Progressive acceptance of the couple is assumed rather than argued.

**Shifts across the series.** The first half is built on frozen-time teasing and secrets; the second half turns to Murakami's loneliness, Moritani's fading power and a rushed but sincere resolution in the last two chapters (AniList review).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Moritani's first use of her power on Murakami was wrong. What made it wrong, and is an apology enough?
2. Why does Murakami act differently with everyone? Have you ever felt you had to do that?
3. The teacher who touches Murakami is never stopped. What should have happened?
4. Why might Moritani's power fade as she makes friends?
5. What is the difference between being private and hiding?
