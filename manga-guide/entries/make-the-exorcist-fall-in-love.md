**Premise.** In 2018 a 12-year-old boy raised by the Church in a monastery in northern Italy, beaten by his father and then by his trainer Abbot Nicholas, goes on his first mission with the exorcist Dante. Asmodeus, the Demon Lord of Lust, turns a town's women on him; to resist, he obeys Matthew 5:29 literally and tears out his right eye, then banishes her (ch. 1). Dante tells him he is a slave, not a believer, and that he should fall in love. Four years later the boy, now 16 and the world's strongest exorcist (his miracles are Bible verses made real), is sent to Japan to guard Imuri Atsuki, an 18-year-old painter whose "Gallery of Evil" shows demons in love. Satan has invited her to Gehenna. Imuri is secretly a succubus descended from Lilith, sent by Satan to make the boy fall in love and so fall from grace. The two live together while the Church's exorcists (the nuns Leah and Barbara, the influencer Mikhail) fight the Lords of the Seven Deadly Sins and a sabbath of witches. The boy's trauma, suppressed desire and resentment of the Church surface; when Imuri shields him from a Church attack (ch. 82-83) he turns on his colleagues and goes to Gehenna to save her. He leaves the Church, takes his real name, Joshua El Angelus Adams, and he and Imuri become a couple (ch. 99-100). The current arc is a war launched by the witch Baba Yaga, with Satan possessing Dante (ch. 102-129) (Wikipedia; fan wiki).

## Life message

**Faith that is beaten into a child and obeyed out of fear is not love; a person must be free to heal, to feel and to choose their own path, and love freely given can redeem even someone sent to deceive.**

A MyAnimeList reviewer calls it a deconstruction of several kinds of abuse; Screen Rant says it shows how people have twisted religion into something sinister. Sexual violence against a boy is treated as violence, which reviewers call unusual. |

Outlook: Bittersweet — the boy escapes the Church, heals enough to love and is loved in return (ch. 99-100), but at the cost of his friends, a child's death (ch. 75-76), a world war of witches still under way and chapters titled "Tragedy" (ch. 106, 123-124).
Hope source: L — love between two is the way through: Dante tells the abused boy to fall in love (ch. 1), and the story's healing comes through Imuri and the boy's choice of each other (ch. 99-100); forgiveness and redemption (R) are close behind, as he forgives her deception and she seeks to atone.

**Where traditions disagree.** Almost every frame condemns what the story condemns: child abuse, sexual assault and religion forced through fear. The split is over the cure. Catholic, Orthodox, evangelical, Pentecostal, Adventist, Witness and Muslim readings see a named Church cast as the villain and a succubus sent by Satan cast as the healer, with a 17-year-old's sexual relationship as the happy turn, and grade it D or F. Lutheran, Methodist and Anglican readings find a real critique of law without grace and grade it C. Mainline, secular, Agnostic and Satanist readings welcome its focus on consent, bodily autonomy and freedom of conscience (B or A), while still flagging the graphic sexual content involving minors.

## Winners, losers and sympathy

Those who love freely and protect the vulnerable (the boy, Imuri, Leah, Barbara) survive and grow, while those who control, abuse or use people (Abbot Nicholas, Marco, Asmodeus, Satan, the purifying priests) are defeated, exposed or left behind; the story is unfinished.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Is love a curse or a blessing for a boy taught that love and desire are sin? Real Sound names this as the series' main theme. The boy was raised to be a weapon, believes his suffering will buy Heaven, and treats his own feelings as enemies. Dante's advice in chapter 1, to fall in love, is both the cure and the trap that Satan has set.

**The answer the story gives.** Love freely chosen is a blessing; love used as a weapon (Asmodeus) or obedience enforced by fear (the abbot) is a curse. The boy heals when he can name his anger at those who hurt him (Part 5), when someone is hurt for his sake (ch. 83), and when he decides for himself what to believe and whom to love (ch. 97-100).

**Characters as arguments.** The boy argues that faithful obedience is not the same as faith. Imuri argues that even a deceiver can be changed by love, though she is honest that she would manipulate him to keep him. Asmodeus argues that desire is natural, then proves that she does not respect consent. Belphegor argues that the world is too cruel to keep; his son's death at the hands of priests (ch. 75-76) is his evidence. Marco argues for the institution and drives away its best member. Dante argues for love and pays for it.

**Symbols and motifs.** The torn-out eye (Matthew 5:29) and the false eye that bleeds at injustice. Baking and sweets: the boy's true self. Imuri's paintings of demons in love. The bracelet that measures his stress. Snail plush toys from Belphegor (an AniList reviewer notes the snail as a sloth symbol). The Sleeping Beauty kiss (ch. 3, 99). His hidden name, given back by Imuri (ch. 100).

**Traditions it echoes.** Milton's Paradise Lost and the fallen angels (a MyAnimeList reviewer's comparison); medieval demonology (Asmodeus, Mammon, Leviathan, Beelzebub, Belphegor, Lucifer); Lilith legends; Russian folklore (Baba Yaga, Vasilisa); Dante and Virgil (the names Dante and Vergilius). Screen Rant compares it with Blue Exorcist and Witch Watch. Christian readers may hear Paul on law and grace and Jesus on the letter and the spirit.

**How each lens is portrayed.** The Christian lens is explored seriously and argued with: God is real and Scripture has power, but the visible Church is an abuser. Duty ethics is the main target. Liberalism's consent and Expressive individualism's self-chosen path are the answer. Nihilism is offered by a sympathetic demon and refused. Pagan/occult content is mostly villainous (witches) but humanized.

**Shifts across the series.** Part 1 (ch. 1-9) sets up the romance and the trauma. Parts 2-3 (ch. 10-35) are demon-lord battles, sacrifice and the boy's near death. Part 4 (ch. 36-55) is the school arc and the second fight with Asmodeus, the heaviest sexual content. Part 5 (ch. 56-83) is the turn: resentment, despair, Belphegor and the Church's attack. Part 6 (ch. 84-101) is Gehenna, Lucifer and the new life with Imuri. Part 7 (ch. 102-) is a world war with the witches, unfinished.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Dante tells the boy he is a slave, not a believer. What is the difference?
2. The boy tore out his eye because he read one verse literally. How should hard verses be read, and who helps us read them?
3. Asmodeus tells the boy he is free to resist. Why is that not real consent?
4. Imuri lied to the boy from the start. Does her love make that right? How does he forgive her?
5. The boy leaves the Church but not God. Can someone keep faith and leave an institution?
