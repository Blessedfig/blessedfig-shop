**Premise.** Hajime Nagumo, 17, an otaku bullied because the popular Kaori is kind to him, is summoned with his class and teacher Aiko to the world of Tortus, where the Holy Church says the god Ehit needs heroes to fight the demon race (ch. 1). His classmates get strong classes; he gets Synergist, a craftsman's transmutation skill (ch. 2). In the Great Orcus Labyrinth a classmate, Daisuke Hiyama, knocks him into the abyss with a spell (ch. 3-4). Alone at the bottom, Hajime loses his left arm to a bear monster and survives by eating monster flesh, which changes his hair, body and personality (ch. 5-6). He builds guns and frees Yue, a vampire princess sealed for 300 years who looks like a 12-year-old (ch. 7-8; fan wiki), and they become lovers. At the labyrinth's end they learn that Ehit is a cruel being posing as each race's god, keeping humans, demons and beastmen at war for his amusement (ch. ~14; Wikipedia). Hajime sets out to clear the Great Labyrinths, gain their ancient magic and go home, gathering a harem: Shea, a rabbit-girl (ch. 15), Tio, a masochistic dragon princess (ch. 32), and later his classmate Kaori (ch. 48). He adopts Myu, a small mer-child rescued from slave traders, who calls him Papa (ch. 40-42). He saves his old class (ch. 43-48), defeats Ehit's Apostle Noint (ch. 75-79), survives the betrayal of classmates Eri and Hiyama, who kill Kaori (ch. 80-84), restores her soul into Noint's body (ch. 82-85), and frees beastmen from the Empire's slave caravans (ch. 87-88).

## Life message

**In a world rigged by a cruel god and false friends, you survive by making yourself strong and staying fiercely loyal to the few you choose, though care for others can grow back.**

| Meta lens | How it reads the series |
|---|---|
| Evolutionary biology | A weak male made strong by eating his predators; a growing harem drawn to the strongest male; protective fatherhood toward a child (Myu, ch. 42). Rivals (Hiyama, Kouki) are humiliated or killed. |
| Pragmatic | Hajime wins by engineering: guns, grenades, vehicles and artifacts from a "commonplace" craftsman's skill. The story treats killing enemies as practical necessity; even the knight Meld says his failure was not teaching the students to kill (ch. 47). |
| Religious | The world's god is the final villain: Ehit poses as the god of every race to keep them at war, and his Holy Church summons the students, persecutes heretics and kidnaps Aiko (ch. 1, 65-69). The ancient "Liberators" who rebelled against the gods are the heroes' guides. Faith readers will see a false god rather than God, but organized religion is shown only as manipulation. |
| Socio-cultural | An isekai power fantasy built on bullying and revenge; Gaffney calls it wish fulfillment of the highest order. Race prejudice and slavery against beastmen are opposed (ch. 15-19, 87-88); women are defined by devotion to Hajime. |

Outlook: Hopeful — Hajime keeps winning, protects his chosen family, restores Kaori (ch. 82-85) and is on track to beat Ehit and go home, though his path leaves many enemies dead and his heart hardened.
Hope source: E — the way through is effort and growing stronger: Hajime survives the abyss by forging himself and his weapons, and every problem is solved by more power; protecting his own people (P) is the close second.

**Where traditions disagree.** Nearly every tradition welcomes Hajime's care for Myu and the freeing of slaves, and nearly every one is troubled by the sexualized child-looking Yue and the approval of executions. The sharpest divide is over religion: Christian, Jewish, Muslim and Bahá'í frames see the false god Ehit as a fair target but object that faith is shown only as fraud, while Satanist, Atheist and secular frames welcome the rebellion against a god. Peace churches and Adventists, Witnesses and Pentecostals reject a story whose answer is strength and killing (F); Hindu, Shinto, Wiccan and Scientologist frames read a warrior's tale with less concern (C).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What happens to a kind person who is betrayed and left to die? Hajime was gentle and self-sacrificing before the fall (fan wiki). The abyss asks whether he can survive without becoming a monster.

**The answer the story gives.** Survive by becoming strong, and trust only those who prove themselves. Hajime eats monsters, builds weapons and decides that his own people are the only ones who matter. The story rewards this at every turn, while letting Aiko, Myu and Kaori pull him back toward care for others.

**Characters as arguments.** Hajime argues for strength and chosen loyalty. Kouki argues for mercy and principle, and the story makes him self-deceived (ch. 47). Hiyama and Shimizu are what resentment becomes when it has power, and both die. Aiko argues that a teacher must care for every student and is the one voice Hajime heeds. Ehit is power with no love at all, the dark mirror of Hajime.

**Symbols and motifs.** The abyss; the lost arm and the prosthetic; eating monsters to become one; guns as a commoner's craft beating chosen heroes' magic; the slave collar Hajime puts on Shea to protect her (ch. 48); Myu calling him Papa.

**Traditions it echoes.** The isekai revenge fantasy, the gunslinger western, and Gnostic stories in which the world's god is a false ruler (my own framing). It echoes the Count of Monte Cristo's descent and return, without his final turn to mercy.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Might makes right and pragmatism are affirmed; duty ethics and pacifism are critiqued through Kouki; organized religion is critiqued through Ehit's church; communitarian loyalty is affirmed inside the party.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-2 (ch. 0-14): betrayal and the abyss, the darkest and goriest. Volumes 3-7 (ch. 15-44): the road, Shea, Tio, Ur and Myu, with comedy and fan service. Volumes 8-12 (ch. 45-69): reunion with the class, Kaori, the volcano and sea labyrinths, the church. Volumes 13-17 (ch. 70-94): war in the capital, the Apostle, the classmates' betrayal, Kaori's death and rebirth, the Empire and the Sea of Trees.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Hajime was kind before he fell. What changed him, and was it all bad?
2. Kouki said killing a defeated enemy was wrong. Hajime said Kouki was lying to himself. Who do you agree with, and why?
3. Why do you think the story made the world's god the villain?
4. Yue looks like a child but is 300 years old. Why might readers be uncomfortable with how she is shown?
5. What does Myu bring out in Hajime?
