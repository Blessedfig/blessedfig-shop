**Premise.** Natori Midori, a 25-year-old temp office worker who gives everything to her job (Okazu), is let go after three years (Wikipedia; Okazu says for piling up too much overtime). Adrift, she opens the mobile visual novel she plays, Fairy Tale of Jewels, and is summoned into it by its cruel villainess, Lapis Tenebrae, a noble at the game's magic academy who is about 15 or 16 (Okazu) and engaged to the crown prince, who kills her in the game's plot. Cornered, Natori pretends that her knowledge of the plot is psychic foresight, uses it to save Lapis, and is made her private secretary. She sets out to rewrite the story so that Lapis escapes her fated death (Wikipedia; Seven Seas). Vol. 2: Natori tries to keep the heroine Diana apart from Gulan, but Diana is challenged to a duel and Gulan trains her. Vol. 3: Natori has told Lapis how she feels; Prince Grandis gives up his claim to the throne; a fight makes Diana look like the villain. Vol. 4: Diana announces she will compete with Lapis for Natori, who has eyes only for Lapis; a sports tournament. Vol. 5: Diana's team wins the Magicrosse Tournament; Lapis offers Diana the prize, Natori, but Diana declines; Natori finds Lapis crying and reaffirms her devotion, and Lapis answers coldly. Vol. 6: a flashback to Lapis and her adopted younger sister Aurora, whose peaceful life is destroyed by anti-aristocracy forces (Penguin Random House synopses).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What is a person worth when the company that used her throws her away? Natori gave three years to a job that dropped her. In a game world she finds someone who values exactly what she is good at.

**The answer the story gives.** Purpose comes from serving someone you believe in, and devotion can change a fate written in advance. Natori's foresight, competence and loyalty keep Lapis alive, and her love grows out of that service (Wikipedia; PRH).

**Characters as arguments.** Natori argues for devoted service. Lapis, the "villainess," is the argument that labels lie: her coldness has a history (vol. 6). Diana, the original heroine, is ambition and rivalry, and she chooses to step back (vol. 5). The prince who gives up the throne (vol. 3) shows that roles can be refused.

**Symbols and motifs.** The game script as fate; the psychic "gift" that is really knowledge; the secretary's schedule as a tool of rescue.

**Traditions it echoes.** The villainess isekai (a reader who rewrites a doomed character's story), the office-worker comedy, and yuri romance. Readers of faith may hear echoes of the servant who is faithful in little things (Luke 16:10).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Pragmatism is celebrated; progressivism appears as girls' love without comment; the labor critique is light. No religious lens is present.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1 (ch. 1-5) sets up the summoning and the secretary job; vols. 2-3 (ch. 6-17) rewrite the game's romances and bring Natori's confession; vols. 4-5 (ch. 18 onward) turn to the rivalry with Diana and Lapis's guarded feelings; vol. 6 turns to Lapis's past (PRH).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Natori was let go after working very hard. What makes a job worth giving your best to?
2. Natori pretends to be psychic to save Lapis. Was the lie right? What else could she have done?
3. Everyone calls Lapis a villain. How does Natori see her differently?
4. Natori is an adult and Lapis is about 15. How should adults behave with teenagers they work for or care for?
5. Diana steps back from the rivalry. Why might she have done that?
