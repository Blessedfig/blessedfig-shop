**Premise.** Yoshida, a 26-year-old office worker at an IT company, is turned down by his boss Airi Gotou, whom he has loved for five years. Walking home drunk, he finds Sayu Ogiwara, a 17-year-old high school girl from Asahikawa, Hokkaido, sitting under a streetlight; she offers sex in exchange for a place to stay. He refuses but lets her sleep at his apartment (ch. 1). She tells him she ran away six months earlier and has survived by staying with men who expected sex (ch. 1-2). Yoshida lets her stay rent-free in exchange for housework, buys her clothes, a futon and a phone, and treats her like a younger sister (ch. 2; The Outer Haven). Sayu takes a convenience-store job and befriends Asami, a classmate-age coworker (vol. 3). Yaguchi, a man she once stayed with, turns up at the store, blackmails her and attempts to rape her at the apartment; Yoshida arrives and throws him out (ch. 18-23, vols. 4-5). Gotou admits she lied when she rejected Yoshida, and his coworker Mishima and his high school ex-girlfriend Kanda complicate his love life (vols. 2, 6). Sayu's older brother Issa comes to take her home (vol. 7). She reveals her past: her only friend Yuuko was bullied and took her own life by jumping from the school roof, and her mother, who had long been cold to her, blamed Sayu (vols. 9-10). Yoshida goes with her to Hokkaido and confronts her mother, who begins to reconcile with her daughter (vols. 12-13). Sayu tells Yoshida she loves him, and they part to walk their own paths (vol. 13, One Peace Books). In the anime and the light novel, Sayu returns to the streetlight two years later, after high school, and asks to stay again (Epicstream); in the light novel side story she confesses as a university student and is turned down in favor of Gotou (Wikipedia).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What does a good adult owe a young person who offers herself in exchange for safety? Sayu has learned that her body is her only currency. Yoshida's answer to her first offer, a flat refusal, sets the whole story in motion.

**The answer the story gives.** Protection without conditions, and then the harder part: helping her stop running. The Outer Haven calls Yoshida the first true father figure Sayu ever had. But the story also lets others question him. Beneath the Tangles points to the scene where Yaguchi accuses him of playing hero, and Yoshida admits to himself that keeping her is not entirely selfless. The story's final answer is that care means letting her go home (vols. 11-13).

**Characters as arguments.** Yoshida argues that an adult's duty does not depend on what a child offers. Yaguchi is the counter-argument, a man who saw a runaway as an opportunity; his later small decencies (keeping his promise, hiding Sayu from Issa) complicate him without excusing him. Gotou argues that kindness sometimes means telling hard truths: she forces Sayu to face her situation (vol. 2). Asami argues for friendship between equals. Sayu's mother argues, by her coldness and blame, how a child is driven away; Yuuko's death shows what cruelty among peers can do.

**Symbols and motifs.** The beard: Yoshida shaves it when Sayu arrives, and she teases him about it at the end. The streetlight where they meet, and where, in the anime and light novel, she returns. Sayu's school uniform, the only clothes she ran away in, worn again on the day she goes home (Epicstream).

**Traditions it echoes.** The prodigal's return; the guardian-and-ward story (Daddy-Long-Legs, Léon; from general knowledge); Joseph's refusal of temptation for some Christian readers. It also belongs to a Japanese light-novel wave of adult-and-teen premises, which reviewers set it beside (Koikimo, per Anime Feminist).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Duty and virtue are celebrated in Yoshida; hedonism is critiqued in Yaguchi; communitarian help is the means of healing; the romance between Sayu and Yoshida is left open, which critics see as the series' compromise.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-3 (ch. 1-16, approx.) set up the household, the office love triangle and Asami. Vols. 4-5 (ch. 17-25) are the Yaguchi arc. Vols. 6-8 (ch. 26-40) bring Kanda, Issa and the summer festival. Vols. 9-10 (ch. 41-50) reveal Sayu's past and Yuuko's death. Vols. 11-13 (ch. 51-67) are the decision to go home, the trip to Hokkaido and the parting.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why does Yoshida refuse Sayu, and why does it surprise her?
2. Was letting her stay the right choice? What else could he have done?
3. How did the adults in Sayu's life fail her, and who helped?
4. What does Yuuko's story say about bullying and about blame?
5. The story ends with Sayu saying she loves Yoshida. How should an adult respond to a teen's feelings like that?
