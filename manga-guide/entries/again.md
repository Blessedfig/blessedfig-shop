**Premise.** Kin'ichiro Imamura, a blond, brow-less boy everyone takes for a delinquent, reaches graduation at Kaho South High in Saitama with no friends and no club. Visiting the empty room of the school's defunct ouendan (a traditional, usually all-male cheering squad), he startles classmate Akira "Aki" Fujieda, who falls down the stairs; he falls after her, and both wake up on their first day of high school three years earlier (ch. 1-2). Imamura joins the ouendan, whose only member is its stubborn captain Yoshiko Usami, and sets out to save it from the cheerleading squad's captain Tamaki Abe, while Aki finds her perfect high school life will not repeat itself (Wikipedia; The Manga Critic). He rounds up the old members (the drummer Okuma, the "master of groveling," vice-captain Okamoto and the karate-minded Suga) (vol. 2), briefly returns to the future and learns Usami dropped out and lived a lonely life (vol. 4), comes back determined to change her fate (vol. 5), cheers the baseball team to victory over a rival school (vol. 6), turns down a seduction ploy by Abe's junior Reo Shibata (vols. 6-7), helps the death-obsessed drama-club girl Hanataka stage "I Want to Die: The Musical" (vols. 8-10), and competes to become the next captain (vol. 11) (PRH blurbs; chapter titles). The last volume jumps "Nine Years Later" (ch. 130) and ends with "Screw a Do-Over!" (ch. 136) (Wikipedia chapter list).

## Life message

**You do not need a do-over to change your life: stop standing apart, throw yourself into cheering for other people, and your life and theirs change for the better.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** If you could live your high school years again, would you? Imamura wasted his three years standing apart because others misjudged his looks and he let them. The question is not whether time travel is possible but why it took a fall down the stairs to make him try.

**The answer the story gives.** Commit to others now. Cheering, shouting for someone else's success, is the cure for a life of watching from the side. Even foreknowledge does not save Aki, whose easy popularity does not repeat. The last chapter's title, "Screw a Do-Over!" (ch. 136), suggests the story ends by rejecting the fantasy of replaying life (content to verify).

**Characters as arguments.** Imamura argues for engagement over regret. Usami argues for tradition, and is shown as both admirable and part of the problem; her original fate (vol. 4) is a warning about rigidity and isolation. Aki argues that popularity is luck as much as personality (The Manga Critic). Abe argues for winning by scheming and loses; Reo shows that someone sent to manipulate can choose sincerity. Hanataka, obsessed with death, is given a stage and a future.

**Symbols and motifs.** The stairs and the fall as the door through time; the school anthem; the gakuran; the drum; the baseball game against a rival school.

**Traditions it echoes.** Second-chance stories such as It's a Wonderful Life and Groundhog Day; Japanese "do-over" manga (Erased, orange, The Manga Critic); the Christian and Jewish idea of repentance as turning; the Confucian ideal of finding one's place in a group.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarianism wins; traditionalism is defended but asked to reform; feminism appears as a critique of the members' sexism, told with comedy; existential regret is answered with action.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-3 (ch. 1-30): time slip and rebuilding the squad. Vols. 4-6 (ch. 31-64): the glimpse of Usami's future, the misunderstanding, the big baseball game. Vols. 7-10 (ch. 65-111): Reo's romance plot and the drama club's I Want to Die musical. Vols. 11-12 (ch. 112-136): the captain contest, a time skip of nine years and the ending (to verify).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why did people decide Imamura was a delinquent? Have you ever been judged by your looks, or judged someone that way?
2. Aki knew the future but was lonelier the second time. Why?
3. What made the ouendan fail the first time, and what did Imamura change?
4. Hanataka's musical is about wanting to die. How did the group help her? Who could you talk to if you felt that way?
5. If you cannot go back in time, what is one thing you could start doing differently this year?
