**Premise.** Kii Kogami, 16, a bored first-year high school student who keeps his head down (he ignores the bullying of his childhood friend Tsukune Fukumoto), wishes something would happen. A small girl in a gothic lolita dress walks into school chanting a single word, explodes a teacher's head and massacres his class; the dead rise as a chanting horde (ch. 1). Kii flees with Tsukune, her former bully Kaede Sayano, the older student Yoruka Hanzawa and the medical student Ren Kushiro, and the "magical girls" overrun the world. In a mall they meet Rintarō Akuta, a deranged policeman (ch. 5); a parasite magical girl takes over a little girl, Miu (v2). Time slips back to 2002 show that Tsukune, whose sadistic second personality Asuka set up her own bullying, has a family link to the magical girls (v3). Ren and Yoruka die in a hospital siege; told that killing Tsukune would save the world, Kii shoots her (v4-6). The timeline resets; the magical girls turn out to be weapons engineered in 2030 by the company Wahre Liebe, and the witches descend from a human's pact with a demon (v7-11). In the final season the villain Himeji tries to become a god, a demon is released, Akuta dies fighting it, and Kii gives his life so Tsukune can build a new world in which the magical girls never came; the dead live again, and some remember (v12-16, ch. 63) (Wikipedia; fan wiki; TV Tropes; Dread Central; The Otaku Author).

## Life message

**Wishing for excitement to escape a dull life can bring horror; what finally saves the world is love and the willingness to give your life for others.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What happens when a bored boy gets his wish? Kii wanted something to happen; the first chapter gives him a massacre. The series asks whether a person who has avoided every risk can learn to act for someone else.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, but at the highest price. Kii's arc runs from ignoring Tsukune's bullying (v1) to shooting her on another's word (v6) to giving his own life so she can make a better world (v16). The ending rewards sacrifice, not survival.

**Characters as arguments.** Kii argues that apathy is a kind of guilt. Akuta argues the opposite of decency at first: a man who sees the apocalypse as license (ch. 5). The story stops him and then, troublingly for many readers, keeps him as comic relief before making him a hero. Tsukune, with her hidden cruel self Asuka, argues that the victim and the villain can live in one person. Himeji argues for power without limit and is destroyed.

**Symbols and motifs.** The chant of a single word by the dead; the gothic doll-like magical girls (a genre's cuteness turned to horror); the wands humans learn to use; the time machine and the many timelines; the casket of light; the world remade from a sacrifice.

**Traditions it echoes.** Zombie survival horror (Thompson compares it to Highschool of the Dead), the dark magical-girl turn after Madoka Magica (from general knowledge; to verify), and the Faust story of power bought from a devil. Christian readers may hear an echo of the one who lays down his life for his friends, though the series does not frame it that way.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Might makes right and nihilism are on display and finally rejected. Occult elements are plot machinery. Transhumanism (engineered and fused bodies) is a tool for both sides. Utilitarian sacrifice of one for many is tried and regretted.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-6 (ch. 1-24) are school, mall and hospital survival horror with the strongest gore and the sexual threats. Volumes 7-8 (ch. 25-31.5) reset the timeline. Volumes 9-11 (ch. 32-43) move to 2030 with a new cast and a demon. Volumes 12-16 (ch. 44-63) are the final battle and the remade world.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Kii ignored Tsukune's bullying because he did not want trouble. What does the story say about bystanders?
2. Akuta threatens two girls when we first meet him, and later becomes a hero. Should the story have kept him in the group? Why or why not?
3. Kii shoots Tsukune because someone says it will save the world. Was that ever right?
4. Is a world remade without the magical girls a happy ending, if no one remembers what happened?
5. Why do you think some readers enjoy horror this extreme? What does it do to the reader?
