**Premise.** Akame and her younger sister Kurome are sold by their parents to the Empire. In a survival test, a hundred children are left in a forest full of Danger Beasts; few come out. The assassin-trainer Gozuki, one of the Empire's Four Rakshasa Demons, separates the sisters so they will not depend on each other: Akame joins his Elite Seven, children he raises as their "father" and arms with Shingu weapons (her sword Kiriichimonji makes wounds that cannot heal), while Kurome goes to a lesser squad kept strong with experimental drugs. Akame's first mission is to kill Martha, a village friend who is a rebel spy (vol. 1). The Revolutionary Army hires assassins of its own, the Tengu Mercenaries and the Oarburgh clan; Cornelia is killed by Taeko, an Oarburgh assassin who had become her secret friend, and Akame kills Taeko (ch. 10-12); Gozuki kills Babara (ch. 13). In a tomb raid (vols. 3-4) the sisters are reunited and two of the Seven die. The Oarburgh leader Merraid ("Mera") captures Akame, Kurome and Natala (vols. 5-6), shows Akame the suffering of the Empire's people under Prime Minister Honest, and reveals Kurome's drug dependency; vols. 6-7 include sexual content involving Akame, a minor (see Content scores), and Akame kills Mera (ch. 41-42). After a revenge group strikes and Green dies (vols. 8-9), Akame meets the rebel general Najenda (ch. 51). In vol. 10 she sees Gozuki's true colors, kills him and the loyal Tsukushi, defects, and joins the new unit Night Raid (ch. 55-60) (Yen Press blurbs and fan wiki; Wikipedia).

**Children raised to kill for a regime are victims of its lies; seeing the people's suffering with your own eyes and choosing your own side is the way out, even at the cost of the family you were given.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can a child who was taught that killing makes people happy learn to see for herself? Akame is bought, trained and loved by the man who makes her a weapon. The prequel asks what it takes to break a belief that was built into you before you could choose.

**The answer the story gives.** Seeing with your own eyes. Akame's doubts start when friends die, but the turn comes when her captor takes her through the Empire and she sees the people's suffering (vols. 6-7), then when she sees Gozuki's true colors (vol. 10). The story does not offer peace as the way out: she defects to a rebel assassin unit, Night Raid, so her freedom is a change of sides as well as of heart.

**Characters as arguments.** Akame argues that conscience can survive indoctrination. Kurome is the darker case: the same upbringing plus drugs leaves her no exit. Gozuki argues that harsh love makes strong children, and dies admitting he loved them. Tsukushi argues for loyalty above friendship and dies by it. Mera argues that the rebels and the Empire are alike and that pleasure is the only truth; her tour helps Akame see, though the story makes her a predatory villain. Green argues for quiet survival.

**Symbols and motifs.** Swords whose wounds never heal; the sisters' separation and reunion in a tomb; candy-like pills; the word "father"; the forest of the first test, where only a few come out.

**Traditions it echoes.** Child-soldier stories, the assassin-school genre, and Japanese fiction about obedience to an emperor; for Jewish and Christian readers, the Exodus picture of people bought and sold who walk free.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Authoritarianism and the cult of strength are critiqued through their victims; utilitarian "greater happiness" talk is exposed on the Empire's side but accepted on the rebels'; hedonism is the Oarburgh temptation and is defeated. Faith lenses are absent.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-2 (ch. 1-13): training and first missions. Vols. 3-4 (ch. 14-25): the tomb raid and the sisters' reunion. Vols. 5-7 (ch. 26-42): the Oarburgh arc, Akame's captivity, the truth about the Empire and the sexual content. Vols. 8-10 (ch. 43-60): revenge groups, Najenda, Gozuki's death and the founding of Night Raid.

**Discussion questions.**
1. The children were told every killing made someone happy. Why did they believe it?
2. Gozuki seems to love the children he trains to kill. Can someone love you and still use you?
3. What did Akame have to see before she changed her mind?
4. Akame leaves the Empire but keeps killing for the rebels. Is that freedom?
5. Why does Kurome stay when Akame goes?
