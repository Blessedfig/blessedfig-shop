**Premise.** Haruyoshi Kugano, the strongest onmyōji (exorcist-sorcerer) of a Heian-style Japan, is feared by the imperial court, which takes his students hostage and sends his favorite former student to kill him (Wikipedia; TV Tropes). Dying, he casts a rebirth spell and wakes in a European-style fantasy world as Seika Lamprogue, the illegitimate son of a family of magic scholars. He cannot use the local magic, but keeps his talismans, curses and the yōkai he sealed in his past life, and resolves to stay hidden and live a happier life. He brings his only friend, the family's slave girl Yifa, to the Lodonea Magic Academy, where he secretly protects Amyu, the prophesied Hero whom both humans and demons want dead (v1-3); exposes the Merchants' Association's slave soldiers Mabel and Kyle in a deadly tournament (v3-4); frees Yifa from a prince who tries to buy her (v4-5); and meets the Holy Princess Fiona, who sees him as the destined Demon King (v6). After killing a demon strike team (v7), he breaks Amyu out of the imperial prison and the four leave the empire to become adventurers in the free city of Rakana (v7-10) (Wikipedia; J-Novel Club).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What should a person do with great power after the people he trusted betrayed him? Haruyoshi was the strongest in his world and was killed for it (Wikipedia; TV Tropes). Reborn, Seika decides to hide his strength and live quietly, but his curiosity and compassion keep dragging him into other people's troubles (Wikipedia).

**The answer the story gives.** Keep power hidden until those you love are threatened, then use all of it. Seika will not sell Yifa (v4-5), will not let Mabel kill her brother or die for a lie (v4), and will burn through a prison to free Amyu (v7). The story also accepts his ruthlessness: a spy eaten alive, witnesses killed, a monster provoked into attacking his own family's town (v1-3; TV Tropes).

**Characters as arguments.** Yifa stands for loyalty freely given; she is offered a better life and chooses Seika (v5). Mabel and Kyle show what happens when people are made into tools. Fiona argues for politics as a long game, and turns out to be playing for the same side. Cecilio stands for entitled privilege and is shamed into decency (v5).

**Symbols and motifs.** Paper talismans and hidden servants; the Hero and the Demon King, with the twist that Seika may be the Demon King himself (Wikipedia); fog and disguise; the free city as the end of hiding.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Abe no Seimei legend and onmyōdō tales; the isekai reincarnation genre; the hidden master who pretends to be ordinary.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Pagan/occult is affirmed as the hero's craft; pragmatism is admired; might makes right is explored, since power is good in good hands; authoritarian courts are critiqued.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-7 (ch. 1-30, the anime's range) are the academy years; volumes 8-10 are adventuring in Rakana and Keltz (J-Novel Club); the Demon King thread continues.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Seika was betrayed by his own student. How does that change the way he treats people in his new life?
2. Yifa is a slave in Seika's family. Why do you think the story never makes a big issue of freeing her?
3. Seika lets his yōkai eat a spy. Was that justice, or something else?
4. Mabel and Kyle were raised as weapons. Who is to blame for what they did?
5. Is it wise to hide your strengths? When should you show them?
