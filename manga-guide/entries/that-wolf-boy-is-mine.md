**Premise.** Komugi Kusunoki, a high-school girl, was shunned by the girls at her Tokyo school after she defended a bullied girl who had become too friendly with the "school prince" (ANN, vol. 1). Her parents divorced eight years earlier; when her mother is transferred to Fukuoka for a year, Komugi chooses to live with the father she has barely seen, in rural Hokkaido, hoping for a quiet fresh start and planning to avoid boys (ANN, vol. 1). At Maruyama High she sits next to Yu Ogami, one of four popular boys the girls call the school's idols, who tells her she smells good. When she finds him asleep under a tree, he turns into a wolf. All four boys are ayakashi (Japanese folklore spirits) who can take human form: Yu a wolf (half human, raised after his human mother left him in the woods), Rin Fushimi a fox who says he hates humans, Aoshi Awaji a mischievous tanuki and Senri Miyama a two-tailed cat (nekomata) (ANN; Soliloquy in Blue; Otaku USA). Yu tries to erase her memory by hypnosis and fails, and Komugi promises to keep the secret (Soliloquy in Blue, vols. 1-2). She falls for Yu, who has vowed never to fall in love; Rin also comes to care for her, making a love triangle (vols. 2-3). In vol. 4 Yata, a three-legged crow spirit, hypnotizes Komugi to erase her memories of the boys, believing Yu is better off without her; the boys push back, the couple's feelings win, and Yu learns his mother loved him and left for reasons he was too young to understand. Komugi and Yu end together; a closing story tells how Senri, a stray kitten taken in by a sick girl, gave his life to Yata in exchange for a human form so he could help her (ANN, vol. 4).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What does it take to be trusted with someone's real self? Komugi, burned by a clique for defending one girl, wants only to be left alone; Yu, abandoned as a child, has vowed never to love. The series asks whether two people who fear being hurt can risk letting each other in.

**The answer the story gives.** Trust is earned by keeping faith, not by force. Yu's hypnosis fails on Komugi (ch. 1-2), and Yata's later memory erasure cannot undo what she and Yu feel (vol. 4). What lasts is a promise kept and a choice to stay.

**Characters as arguments.** Komugi argues for quiet courage: she defended a bullied girl and pays for it, then does the same for the boys. Yu argues from fear that love ends in abandonment, and learns that his mother's leaving was not rejection (vol. 4). Rin argues that humans cannot be trusted and is disproved by Komugi. Yata argues that people can be protected by controlling what they know; the story resists him. Senri, who gave his life so he could help a sick girl, is the image of love that gives itself away.

**Symbols and motifs.** Scent (Yu knows Komugi by smell), the wolf asleep under a tree, the secret itself, memory taken and returned, and the move from a crowded city to open Hokkaido.

**Traditions it echoes.** The shoujo "secret supernatural boyfriend" story; Japanese animal-bride and fox-spirit tales; the Yatagarasu of Japanese myth (from general knowledge; to verify). For Christian readers, Senri's story echoes laying down one's life for another.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarianism and acceptance are affirmed; the folklore is atmosphere rather than doctrine; control of others' minds is shown as wrong.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-2: arrival, the secret, Komugi's crush and Rin's interest. Vol. 3: the love triangle tightens and ends on a cliffhanger. Vol. 4: Yata's memory erasure, the truth about Yu's mother, the couple's resolution and Senri's story (ANN).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Komugi lost friends in Tokyo for defending someone. Was it worth it? What would you have done?
2. Yu tried to erase Komugi's memory. Is it ever right to change what someone knows "for their own good"?
3. Why did Yu vow never to fall in love, and what changed his mind?
4. Senri gave up his life as a cat to help a sick girl. What does that tell you about love?
5. Komugi moved to live with a father she hardly knew. What helps families heal?
