**Premise.** On her first date with her crush Masaya Aoyama, at an endangered-species exhibit, 13-year-old Ichigo Momomiya is caught in an earthquake and a strange light that fuses her DNA with that of the endangered Iriomote wildcat (ch. 1). Ryou Shirogane and Keiichirou Akasaka of the secret Mew Project tell her she can now transform into Mew Ichigo to fight Chimera Animas, alien parasites that turn animals into monsters, and send her to find the four other girls hit by the light: rich ballerina Minto (blue lorikeet), shy, bullied Retasu (finless porpoise), the acrobat Bu-Ling (golden lion tamarin) and the model Zakuro (gray wolf) (vols. 1-2). The team works undercover as waitresses at Cafe Mew Mew. Three aliens, Quiche, Pie and Tart, want to reclaim the Earth from the humans who are destroying it; Quiche falls for Ichigo and pursues her. The girls gather Mew Aqua, a pure-water substance that cleans pollution (vol. 4-6). When Ichigo is overexcited she turns into a black cat and needs a kiss to turn back (vol. 3-5). The Blue Knight who protects her proves to be Masaya, who in turn is a false form of Deep Blue, the alien leader (vol. 6). In the finale Masaya regains control and sacrifices himself, the Mew Aqua heals Tokyo, Ichigo gives her life to revive him and his kiss revives her, and the aliens go home with Mew Aqua to restore their planet (vol. 7) (Wikipedia; fan wiki).

## Creator and publisher context

- Mia Ikumi (1979-2022) created and drew the series; she won the 24th Nakayoshi Newcomer award at 18, spent a year designing Tokyo Mew Mew from an earlier pitch, Tokyo Black Cat Girl (reprinted in vol. 4), and drew the 2020 Re-Turn sequel shortly before her death (Wikipedia).
- Reiko Yoshida (story) is a prolific anime screenwriter (Wikipedia). Kodansha hired her as scenario writer; Yoshida and two editors set each volume's plot and Ikumi adapted it, which differs from most manga (Wikipedia, Production).
- Ikumi said she had reservations when the editors reshaped her lead for a five-heroine team, since the character was designed for a more dramatic series (Wikipedia). No statements on religion or politics were found.
- Publisher: Kodansha (Nakayoshi). English: Tokyopop (2003-2005), then Kodansha Comics (2011-2012), rated 13+ on the current page. No controversies involving the creators were found; critics objected to a KKK-like robe on a villain in the sequel a la Mode (Wikipedia), which is outside this entry.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Who will protect the Earth, and what will it cost them? Ichigo is an ordinary 13-year-old with a crush, suddenly made into a weapon against aliens who believe humans have forfeited the planet.

**The answer the story gives.** Ordinary girls, working together, and finally love that gives itself away. The last volume turns the fight into a series of sacrifices: Quiche dies for Ichigo, Masaya dies to destroy Deep Blue, Ichigo dies to save Masaya, and the healing power that follows restores Tokyo, the dead and even the aliens' world (vol. 7).

**Characters as arguments.** Ichigo argues for courage that grows out of love. Masaya, the boy who loves nature, turns out to be the vessel of the enemy, and shows that the same love can defeat it. Quiche argues for desire that will not take no for an answer, and the story both rejects it (vol. 6) and lets him redeem himself (vol. 7). Deep Blue is the case for saving the planet by wiping out people; the ending answers him by healing both.

**Symbols and motifs.** Endangered animals as hidden strength. Pure water (Mew Aqua) against pollution. The bell Masaya ties on Ichigo (vol. 2). The kiss that turns a cat back into a girl. The mock wedding (vol. 7).

**Traditions it echoes.** The magical-girl team of Sailor Moon; the fairy-tale kiss that breaks a spell; environmental anime of its era; for Christian readers, love that lays down its life and is raised.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Environmentalism is stated outright. Teamwork and duty are honored. Genetic experiments on children are treated as simply heroic, which some secular readers may question.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-2: gathering the team. Vols. 3-5: the cat curse, Mew Aqua and the Blue Knight. Vols. 6-7: Quiche's obsession, the Deep Blue reveal and the sacrificial finale.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why do the aliens think humans do not deserve the Earth? Are they partly right?
2. Quiche says he loves Ichigo. How is his love different from Masaya's?
3. Which Mew Mew's animal is most endangered in real life, and why?
4. Why does Ichigo stop the Blue Knight from killing Quiche?
5. What does each character give up in the last battle?
