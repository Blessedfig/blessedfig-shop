**Premise.** In QQ Sweeper, sixteen-year-old Fumi Nishioka, homeless and without memories, is found sleeping in a hidden room of her school by Kyutaro Horikita, a second-year student with a talent for cleaning. Kyutaro's family are "Sweepers" who enter people's Mind Vaults to clean out "bugs" bred by uncontrolled negative emotion; they take Fumi in, and she turns out to carry the "Queen," a rare power to control minds that can work for good or evil (Wikipedia). Queen's Quality follows Fumi's training and the struggle between a White Queen and a Black Queen inside her (v1-5); the old Byakko clan's holy ground and the truth about Kyutaro's parents and his lost childhood friend Fuyu (v6-9); Fumi and Kyutaro becoming a couple (v10); the snake sealed inside Kyutaro and the rival clans Seiryu and Suzaku (v11-18); and the revelation that the Suzaku leader Yanagi is Fumi's father and that her mother Kaede was turned into a monster (v19-21). In the finale the clans unite to protect the city, and Fumi faces her father's attempt to drive her into despair (v22-25) (VIZ blurbs).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What do you do with the darkness inside you? Fumi carries a power that could control others, and two Queens inside her, one white and one black (v3). Kyutaro carries a snake that wants to devour (v10-12). The story asks whether the answer is to kill what is dangerous in yourself, or to know it, master it and live with it.

**The answer the story gives.** Not destruction but acceptance with love. Fumi is told to kill the Black Queen (v5) and instead learns its truth and gains power; she must choose whether her earlier self, Fuyu, should return (v9); later Kyutaro strikes a bargain with his snake instead of dying to kill it (v11). But Why Tho praises how Fumi stops fearing her power and how the couple act as partners.

**Characters as arguments.** Kyutaro argues for protecting the one you love at any cost, and pays it (v15, v18). Ataru argues for revenge on a world that took everything from him, and is saved instead (v5, v14). Kaede, Fumi's mother, shows where revenge leads: consumed by the White Queen's power and turned into a monster (v20-21). Yanagi, the father, is the will to use people, even family, as tools.

**Symbols and motifs.** Cleaning and sweeping as healing; bugs as uncontrolled negative feelings; the Mind Vault as the self; snakes as appetite and power; the Casket as the hidden heart of the White Queen's story; white and black as two halves of one person.

**Traditions it echoes.** Japanese purification practice (from general knowledge; to verify), Chinese four-beast cosmology, the psychological idea of integrating the shadow self, and shojo romances where love steadies a dangerous power (Motomi's own Dengeki Daisy, per VIZ's recommendations).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Spiritual practice (Pagan/occult) is affirmed as healing; found family is affirmed; domination is critiqued. Buddhist and Taoist images (craving, hell, balance of light and dark) appear as imagery more than doctrine.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-5: Fumi's two Queens and the bug handlers. Volumes 6-9: the Byakko holy ground and the past. Volume 10: the romance pause. Volumes 11-18: the snakes and the clan wars. Volumes 19-25: Fumi's parents and the final battle.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Fumi is told to destroy the Black Queen inside her. Why does she choose another way?
2. The "bugs" grow from feelings people cannot control. What helps you when your feelings get too big?
3. The Horikitas take in a homeless girl. What makes a family?
4. Kyutaro lets his snake eat his wishes to get stronger. Was that wise?
5. Kaede was consumed by revenge. How is Fumi different from her mother?
