**Premise.** Tousuke Rokudou is a timid first-year at Amori High, a school full of delinquents, where he and his friends "Colonel" and "Manager" are bullied daily and vent in a toilet stall. His late grandfather sends him a priest's robe and a family scroll from the Heian period said to ward off demons and evil spirits. The ritual leaves a pentagram mark on his forehead, but the spell does something else in modern times: any "bad girl" who meets his eyes falls in love with him (Wikipedia). The first is Ranna Himawari, a legendary sukeban (delinquent girl boss) with a wooden sword, feared as a monster; she is gentle only around him. Rokudou at first hopes to use her for a peaceful school life, but when Ranna keeps her promise not to fight even while being beaten, he decides he must show his own courage (Japanese Wikipedia). He stands up to his bully Iinuma and earns his friendship, and one by one he helps a series of bad girls change: the tiny school boss Sayuri, the vigilante biker Azami, the loan shark Minami, the kung-fu fighter Ling Lan and the clairvoyant Sachiyo, adopted daughter of a yakuza-linked money-lender. Arcs pit Amori High against the Onishima Alliance gang (which wants Ranna), a rival school's attackers, and Sakura Finance's executives, who kidnap Sachiyo. In the final arc the spell breaks, Ranna reverts to her violent self and disappears; Rokudou and all his allies search for her, he confesses as his true self, and she chooses to stay with him (ciatr; Japanese Wikipedia).

## Life message

**Real strength is refusing to run and standing by your friends; even the "worst" people can change, and love only counts when it is freely chosen.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Is love real if magic caused it? Rokudou never asked for his spell, and he refuses to use it to take revenge or keep the girls around him (Akiyama, via Japanese Wikipedia). But every bad girl who loves him starts loving him because of a mark on his forehead. The series also asks a second question: what makes a weak person strong?

**The answer the story gives.** Courage and keeping your word make a person strong; and love is real when it survives the spell's end. Rokudou's turning point is watching Ranna take a beating rather than break her promise to him; he decides he must match her courage (Japanese Wikipedia). In the last arc the spell breaks, Ranna returns to her old self and leaves, and Rokudou, as himself, asks her to come back. She chooses him (ciatr).

**Characters as arguments.** Rokudou argues that the weak can be brave. Ranna argues that even a "monster" can belong somewhere. Iinuma argues that a bully can become a friend. Douji, who joined a gang because he was afraid, argues that fear makes people gather strength; he learns his followers stay for him, not for power. Minami argues for greed and gives it up. Sachiko, a crime boss who wants her daughter to have a normal life, shows a parent's love inside a criminal world. Tateshima fakes reform to return to crime and is shown mercy anyway.

**Symbols and motifs.** The exorcism scroll that summons "bad" women instead of driving out demons. The pentagram on Rokudou's forehead. Prayer beads that calm an evil heart and that Ranna fears. Flower names for the girls (sunflower, lily, thistle) and oni names for the gang. Momotarō, the peach boy who tamed demons with companions, behind Rokudou's own name.

**Traditions it echoes.** The yankī manga of the 1980s-90s and the Japanese "oni-taming" tale (Momotarō); the harem comedy, turned on its head because the hero is the weakest; Buddhist temple discipline as a path out of violence. For religious readers it echoes stories of conversion and repentance, though here the change begins with a charm rather than grace.

**How each lens is portrayed.** The honor code is affirmed, with its violence trimmed toward protection. Might makes right is shown and then subordinated to loyalty. The occult and Buddhist imagery works as folklore. The feminist critique (ANN) targets the spell's control; the series' own answer is the free choice at the end.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-4 (roughly ch. 1-35) introduce the spell, Ranna, Iinuma, Sayuri and Azami. Volumes 5-9 (to ch. 73, the anime's end) are the Onishima Alliance war. Volumes 9-12 bring Minami, the beads and Ling Lan's temple. Volumes 13-18 cover White Day, the Kuromusamu raids and the Amori-Harizo alliance. Volumes 19-25 are the Sakura Finance arc and Sachiyo's kidnapping. Volume 26 is the spell's end and Ranna's choice (arc order from Japanese Wikipedia; chapter ranges estimated).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Rokudou could have used Ranna to get revenge. Why didn't he?
2. Is love caused by a spell really love? What changes when the spell breaks?
3. Ranna kept her promise not to fight even while being hurt. Was that brave, or should she have defended herself?
4. Which bad girl changed the most, and what helped her?
5. What is the difference between strength used to protect and strength used to rule?
