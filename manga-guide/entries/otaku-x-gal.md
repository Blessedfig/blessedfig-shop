**Premise.** Koichi Otakura was teased for his nerdy hobbies in middle school, so he plans to blend into the background in high school. Yua Narumi, the popular gyaru (fashion-forward "gal") who sits behind him, finds out he can paint plastic models and draw, and begs him to fix the fake nails she botched; he stays up all night to make them. Hair styling and makeup follow. Her best friend Riko Himeno thinks otaku are creepy until Otakura helps with Yua's plan to give Riko, who is being bullied, a makeover as "armor" (vol. 1). Later volumes add shopping, a pool trip where his hidden muscular build draws attention, karaoke, study sessions, a class trip with the gyaru twins Shion and Kanon Murata, his birthday and the school festival, for which he sews costumes (vols. 2-3). Yua clearly has a crush; Otakura does not notice (Square Enix; The Outerhaven; Anime UK News; Siliconera).

## Life message

**Skills and kindness can cross any social line: an otaku and a gyaru find their hobbies are closer than they thought, and friends who respect each other's passions make each other braver.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Do the labels that sort a school (otaku, gal, cringe, popular) say anything true about people?

**The answer the story gives.** No: the skills that make Otakura an otaku are the same ones Yua needs for her nails and hair, and Riko's contempt turns into friendship once she sees what he does for her (vol. 1).

**Characters as arguments.** Otakura argues for quiet competence and kindness. Yua argues for openness: she credits his work in public (Siliconera). Riko argues for the clique view and changes it. The bullies are the cost of the clique view taken to its end.

**Symbols and motifs.** Fake nails painted like a model kit; the makeover as "armor" (vol. 1); a hidden physique under plain clothes (vol. 2); costumes for the festival (vol. 3).

**Traditions it echoes.** The opposites-attract school rom-com, close kin to My Dress-Up Darling (several reviewers). The idea that a craft is a gift to others echoes many religious and civic traditions.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism is affirmed but mild; friendship carries it. Consumer fashion is enjoyed without critique.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1: nails, hair, Riko's makeover. Vol. 2: shopping, pool, karaoke, studying; the love triangle starts. Vol. 3: class trip, summer, birthday, festival; more admirers. Later volumes not covered.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Which of Otakura's skills turned out to be useful in a way he did not expect?
2. Riko changed her mind about otaku. What changed it?
3. Is it ever right to hide a hobby at school?
4. How did Yua help Riko with the bullying? Would that work in real life?
5. What is the difference between caring about how you look and caring too much?
