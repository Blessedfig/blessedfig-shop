**Premise.** Sakura Adachi and Hougetsu Shimamura, first-year high school girls who both skip class, meet on the second floor of the school gym and start a lazy routine of ping-pong and talk. The withdrawn Adachi realizes she wants to be the most important person in Shimamura's life and dreams of kissing her; the easygoing but emotionally distant Shimamura treats her as a friend. Their friends Hino and Nagafuji, Shimamura's little sister, and Yashiro, a small girl who claims to be an alien from the future, drift through the story. Adachi plans a Christmas date (v2) and Valentine's chocolate (v3); in their second year she panics when Shimamura makes new friends (v4) and when she sees Shimamura at a summer festival with an old friend, Tarumi (v5); while Shimamura visits her grandparents she notices her own feelings changing (v6). Under festival fireworks Adachi confesses and Shimamura says yes, and vol. 7 follows them as a new couple (Yen Press; Wikipedia; Japanese Wikipedia).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can someone who keeps everyone at arm's length let one person in, and what does it cost to ask? Adachi wants to be the first person Shimamura thinks of; Shimamura is not sure she wants to be that close to anyone (But Why Tho).

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, slowly. Small steps (walking home together, bowling, karaoke, a Christmas plan, Valentine's chocolate) build toward a confession that is accepted (Yen Press v2-7).

**Characters as arguments.** Adachi argues that wholehearted feeling is worth the risk, even when it turns into jealousy; Shimamura argues for distance as protection and then lets it go; Tarumi stands for the friendships we drift away from; Yashiro, the cheerful "alien," stands for wonder that needs no explanation.

**Symbols and motifs.** The gym's second floor as a private world; ping-pong as a back-and-forth; fireworks for the confession; Shimamura's inner images of sinking into water when she thinks about relationships (Anime Feminist, anime).

**Traditions it echoes.** The yuri genre (Bloom Into You is the usual comparison, CBR via Wikipedia); the slow-burn school romance.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism and a progressive view of romance are affirmed without argument; there are no religious or political voices.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-3 (first year) build the friendship; vols. 4-6 (second year) bring jealousy and Shimamura's own change; vol. 7 begins the relationship. Japanese vols. 8-9 continue it (not covered by sources opened).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Adachi gets jealous when Shimamura spends time with other friends. When is jealousy a warning sign?
2. Why do you think Shimamura keeps people at a distance?
3. The two skip class a lot and nothing happens. Is that realistic? What would really happen?
4. What helps someone shy take a risk with a friend?
5. What does it mean to be someone's "first thought"?
