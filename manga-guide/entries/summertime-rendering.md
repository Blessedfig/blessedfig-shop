**Premise.** Shinpei Ajiro, 17, orphaned as a child and raised on the small island of Hitogashima (modeled on Tomogashima, Wakayama) by the Kofune family, returns from Tokyo on July 22 for the funeral of his childhood friend and adoptive sister Ushio, who drowned saving a girl. There are strangle marks on her neck. Ushio's younger sister Mio tells him that the sisters met Ushio's double days before: a "Shadow," the island legend of doppelgangers that copy people, kill them and take their place. Shinpei is killed, and wakes again on the ferry: each time he dies, he loops back in time, though each loop starts later and his enemies begin to remember. With Mio, his friend Sou, the novelist Hizuru Minakata (who carries her murdered twin brother's Shadow inside her), an old hunter, Nezu, and a Shadow copy of Ushio who chooses to fight for him, he uncovers the truth: the island's worshipped god, Hiruko, and its vessel Haine are the mother of all Shadows, guarded for centuries by the clinic family and the shrine priest Karikiri (really Shide, the first Shadow), who plan a mass-murder ritual at the summer festival. In the final loop Shadow Ushio sends Hiruko back in time to be erased; in the new timeline Ushio, Shinpei's parents and Hizuru's brother are alive, and Haine is reborn as an ordinary child (Wikipedia; TV Tropes; reviews).

**Grief and fear can be faced with a clear head and loyal friends; love and sacrifice, not a hidden god, are what save the people you care about, and the dead you lost may be restored.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** If you could relive the worst days of your life, could you save the people you lost, and what would it cost you? Shinpei comes home too late to say goodbye to Ushio. The loops give him a chance to undo that, but only by dying again and again.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, with clear thinking, help and sacrifice. Shinpei's habit of stepping back to see the whole board, his friends' loyalty and Shadow Ushio's choice to give up her own existence break the loop. The dead are restored in a new timeline (TV Tropes). The cost falls on those willing to pay it.

**Characters as arguments.** Shinpei argues for reason and persistence. Shadow Ushio argues that what you choose matters more than what you were made to be. Hizuru argues for justice for the dead. Haine argues for the survival of her own kind at humanity's expense; Shide argues for destruction. The Hishigata clinic shows how institutions protect a lie. Mio shows a love that is not returned and goes on anyway.

**Symbols and motifs.** The ferry where every loop begins. The swimsuit Shadow Ushio cannot take off, a sign that she is a copy of one moment. Shadows as the dark copy of each person. The summer festival. The whale form of Hiruko (TV Tropes). Shinpei's blue eye, the mark of his power (Wikipedia).

**Traditions it echoes.** Time-loop stories (Groundhog Day, Re:Zero), the doppelganger and body-snatcher tradition, Lovecraftian cosmic horror (ANN), Japanese island and summer ghost stories, and the Kojiki's Hiruko (from general knowledge; to verify). For Christian readers, the false god that devours its worshippers echoes the prophets' attacks on idols.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Worship of the island god is shown as deadly deception. Virtue, community and pragmatic reason are rewarded. Existential questions about copies and persons are explored with sympathy. Hidden institutional control is condemned.

**Shifts across the series.** Omnibus 1 (about ch. 1-21): the funeral, the first Shadows, the first loops, the heaviest fan service. Omnibus 2-3: allies gathered, the tunnels under the island, the god, a sacrifice rite and a lost battle (ANN vol. 3). Omnibus 4-6 (to ch. 139): the priest revealed, the festival massacre, the final loop and the new timeline. Omnibus chapter ranges are estimates.
