**Premise.** Chieri Ōmine, a second-year high school girl, has grown to eight meters tall for reasons no one understands. After the first stir, her town quietly accepts her, and with help from a government "Giant Girl Support Bureau" she lives alone in a custom prefab house and transfers to a school that can accommodate her (Japanese Wikipedia). In class she meets Kotaki Yumeji ("Yume-chan"), her childhood friend from elementary school, who is appointed her "helper" simply because he knew her as a child; he wants her to enjoy a normal teenage youth despite everything her size makes hard (Japanese Wikipedia). Chieri, free-spirited and fond of cute things (she keeps wearing miniskirts), has loved him since childhood, while he is slow to notice (Mangapedia). Her friends Hina Sasaki and Tsukino Yanase help with her hair and makeup; her older sister Ringo drops in to check on her (v2) and later, drunk, accuses Yumeji of seeing four girls at once (v4). Summer brings swimsuits, festivals and the beach (v2-3), and Nanami Neiro, an older student who became a giant before Chieri, arrives as a rival, starting a love triangle (v3) (Seven Seas synopses; Mangapedia). A Giant Girl Café and a Support Bureau official complicate the school festival (v4). How the romance resolves is not described in any source opened (to verify).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What does it take to treat someone as normal when nothing about her life is normal? Chieri is eight meters tall. She cannot sit at a desk, ride a train or wear a store-bought uniform. Yumeji's job is to help her have the youth any other girl would.

**The answer the story gives.** Patient, everyday kindness. The town's curiosity fades into acceptance (Japanese Wikipedia), friends help with her hair and makeup, and Yumeji keeps showing up. The romance grows out of that care. How the story ends is not confirmed in sources opened.

**Characters as arguments.** Chieri argues for confidence: she keeps her style and her feelings whatever her size. Yumeji argues for faithful service. Neiro, a giant girl who came first, argues for pride and rivalry. Ringo, the sister, argues for family worry. The Support Bureau stands for public help with strings attached.

**Symbols and motifs.** Height as difference itself. The custom prefab house: a home built around one person. The helper's armband-role (osewa-gakari). The summer beach, where size matters least.

**Traditions it echoes.** Gulliver's Travels and the giant of folk tales; the shōnen romantic comedy; for religious readers, 1 Samuel 16:7 (the heart over appearance) and the many teachings on welcoming the one who is different. The fan-service tradition of Jump+ romantic comedies sits uneasily beside that message.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarian inclusion is affirmed. Expressive individualism shows in Chieri's style. Hedonism is the book's own temptation: the body as spectacle, which some readers enjoy and others call fetish bait.

**Shifts across the series.** Volume 1 sets up the helper role; volume 2 brings Ringo and summer swimsuits; volume 3 introduces Neiro and the love triangle; volume 4 covers the café, autumn and the festival (Seven Seas synopses). Volumes 5-6 (ch. 42-63, about) are not described in sources opened; a reviewer says ch. 50 reverses the relationship's progress.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Yumeji was made Chieri's helper only because he knew her as a child. Why does he keep helping?
2. How does the school change from staring at Chieri to accepting her?
3. Some jokes in the book are about Chieri's body. Are they fair to her?
4. What would you need if you suddenly became very different from everyone else?
5. Neiro was a giant first. What might she feel when Chieri arrives?
