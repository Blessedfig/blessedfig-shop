**Premise.** Hana Nonomura, 16, a blunt high school sprinter who has always come second to her beautiful older sister Yukari, 23, is sent in Yukari's place to an arranged marriage meeting (omiai) with Takane Saibara, 26, the arrogant heir of the Takaba conglomerate, where Hana's father works. Takane is rude, so Hana throws her wig in his face and walks out. Takane, who knew from the start she was a stand-in, is fascinated that she does not want his money, and keeps the "arrangement" going: Ferrari pickups, luxury restaurants, roses and trips meant to make her give in to his lifestyle. Through constant bickering each tries to make the other fall first (Wikipedia; MangaUpdates). Supporting threads include Hana's childhood friend Okamon, who confesses to her (ch. 51, Wikipedia); Takane's playboy friend Nicola and Hana's friend Mizuki; Takane's transgender former classmate Rino; his grandfather Soten, who tricks Takane into living with the Nonomura family; and his cousin Yakumo, who plots to hurt Hana and expose the arrangement (Wikipedia). By vol. 16 Hana is a second-year student thinking about her future (The Fandom Post); in vol. 17 Takane meets Hana's grandparents in Kyushu and must save the engagement when her grandfather objects (Simon & Schuster); in vol. 18 the two register their marriage and settle into a home together (NetGalley; AIPT). A bonus epilogue is set seven years later (Wikipedia).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can love be bought, and can it be fair between two people who are not equal? Takane has money, age, height, education and power over Hana's father. Hana has only her pride and her tongue. The series asks whether the second can balance the first.

**The answer the story gives.** Love grows only when both stop performing. Takane's gifts fail; Hana's mockery is a defense. They come together when he chooses a personal gift instead of an expensive one (vol. 16) and when he wins over her family honestly (vol. 17). The story ends in marriage (vol. 18) and a glimpse of seven years later.

**Characters as arguments.** Takane argues that status earns love and learns it does not. Hana argues for self-respect and learns that it can coexist with tenderness. Yukari is the case of beauty as currency, and is passed over. Okamon is the equal-aged alternative, and loses. Rino shows Takane's best side in the past: he told her to be herself. Yakumo is envy turned to scheming.

**Symbols and motifs.** The wig thrown at the first meeting (the end of pretending). The Ferrari and the roses (status). The title's "flower on a high peak": each is out of the other's reach. Hana as a sprinter, always running ahead.

**Traditions it echoes.** Pride and Prejudice (a proud rich man humbled by a sharp-tongued woman of lower status), the shojo bickering romance, and the Japanese omiai comedy. Religious readers may hear Proverbs on pride and riches; Buddhist readers, the loosening of attachment to status.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Consumerism loses. Virtue, in the old sense of character formed by practice, wins. Tradition is kept but softened: the arranged meeting becomes a love match that still seeks the families' blessing. Feminist critics welcome Hana's voice but question the frame, and the series' own answer to the age gap is to name it often and keep the romance chaste.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-5 are the bickering game and the omiai secret. Middle volumes add living with the Nonomura family, rivals (Yukari, Rino, Okamon) and company intrigue. Volumes 16-18 turn to Hana's future, the families' approval and the marriage, with a seven-year epilogue in the limited edition.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Takane tries to win Hana with money. Why does that fail, and what finally works?
2. Hana is 16 and Takane is 26. The characters say this is a problem. Do you agree, and what would make it fair?
3. Hana always came second to her sister. How does that shape how she treats Takane?
4. Okamon is Hana's age and has known her all her life. Why does the story not choose him?
5. How would you want someone to show they value you?
