**Premise.** Emi Asahina, 24, is out of work and unsure what to do with her life. Her friend Mayumi drags her to a Tokyo nightclub and then leaves with a man; a stranger gets grabby with Emi, and Kei (Keiko Yoinoma), a confident 30-year-old DJ who works a day job at an interior design firm, steps in (Wikipedia; ComicBookBin; ANN). They talk all night, Emi goes home with Kei, and their relationship becomes physical, though volume 1 leaves this mostly to the reader's imagination (Wikipedia; Soliloquy in Blue). Kei pulls Emi into her crew as a VJ (the person who mixes live visuals to the music) (ch. 1-5). In volume 2 (ch. 6-10) the crew plans a rave in an abandoned building and adds a new member, Midori; Emi has not told Kei that she still shares an apartment with a boyfriend she has only half broken up with, and Kei pulls back when she learns it (ComicBookBin; Okazu; Comic Natalie headline cited by Wikipedia). Emi tells the boyfriend through a closed door that she is not coming back (The OASG); she and Kei agree that being together makes them happiest, and the volume ends with a sex scene that reviewers call non-explicit (Okazu; ANN). In volume 3 (ch. 11-16 and a final chapter) the rave succeeds despite a petty crime that threatens it; then Kei leaves to deal with family trouble in her hometown, and Emi goes back to her parents' home and looks for work. Reviewers call the ending realistic rather than epic and not wholly happy (ComicBookBin; Okazu and its comments).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What gets a person unstuck? Emi is 24, out of work, living with a boyfriend she does not love and hiding in a corner of a club she does not even like. The story asks whether one chance meeting can change that, and what it costs.

**The answer the story gives.** Change comes from saying yes to something new and then doing the work. Kei's kindness opens the door, but Emi grows by learning to be a VJ, by taking part in the hard planning of a rave and finally by being honest about her past (ANN; Okazu). Love is part of it, but the story does not make love the whole answer: Kei leaves in volume 3 and Emi must stand on her own (Okazu).

**Characters as arguments.** Emi argues for trying, even when anxious. Kei argues for generosity and for living by what you love, while holding a day job with responsibility (ANN, Silverman). The boyfriend stands for the easy, stuck life. Kei's crew argues that creative work is better done together.

**Symbols and motifs.** Mixing two turntables: Kei tells Emi that if the sound only comes from one side, it is not really mixing, which later applies to their relationship (Soliloquy in Blue). The key to Kei's apartment and the gift of records. The abandoned building turned into a rave.

**Traditions it echoes.** Reviewers compare it to Nana (ANN Spring 2017 guide). It belongs to adult yuri and to the coming-of-age story set late, at 24, in the gap between school and a career.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism and a quiet progressivism are affirmed: the relationship is never questioned inside the story. Communitarian values come through the crew. Faith traditions find no religion, and most weigh the sexual relationship and nightlife against the real goods of honesty, work and kindness.

**Shifts across the series.** Volume 1 (ch. 1-5): the meeting and Emi's first VJ night. Volume 2 (ch. 6-10): the event plan, the hidden boyfriend, a crisis of trust and a sex scene. Volume 3 (ch. 11-16, final chapter, bonus): the rave, a petty crime, Kei's departure and a realistic ending, then a bonus story that returns to their first meeting (ComicBookBin).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why do you think Emi hid her boyfriend from Kei? What did hiding it cost her?
2. Kei says mixing only works if both sides give something. How is that true of friendships too?
3. What helped Emi more: falling in love, or learning to do a job well?
4. Emi goes home with someone she has just met. What risks does the story skip over?
5. The story ends without a big happy finale. Did that feel true or disappointing?
