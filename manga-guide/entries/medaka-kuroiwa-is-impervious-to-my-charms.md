**Premise.** Mona Kawai, a second-year at a Tokyo high school who moved from Osaka a year earlier, is the school's self-declared "queen bee": every boy adores her. The exception is the glaring new transfer student, Medaka Kuroiwa. Determined to win him over, she tries more and more daring tricks, and in trying, falls for him herself (Wikipedia). The reader soon learns why he glares: Medaka is training to become a Buddhist monk and inherit his family's countryside temple, has been told not to grow close to girls, and transferred to a co-ed school to train his mind against desire; he steadies himself by muttering four-character phrases, chanting mantras and dousing himself with cold water (Japanese Wikipedia; anime ep. 1, 12 via Wikipedia). His friends tell him that his vow is no excuse for being unkind, and he softens (ep. 4). Rivals and allies multiply: Tsubomi, who adores Mona and pushes her toward Medaka; Asahi, a first-year basketball star who falls for him at first sight; Mona's childhood friend Tomo, who later falls for him too; and Anri, a rich girl who finds his precepts cramped and urges him to let something change him (Wikipedia; Japanese Wikipedia). Mona admits to herself that she is in love at an amusement park (ch. about 40-44; anime ep. 11). Later arcs include a beauty pageant (about ch. 55-65; reader comments), Christmas, when Medaka honestly tells Mona about his precept (Japanese Wikipedia), Valentine's Day and a move to the third year. The series is ongoing and the romance unresolved in sources opened.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can someone who has always been loved learn to love? Mona has collected admiration all her life; Medaka, the one boy who refuses her, forces her to discover what she actually feels.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, by giving up the performance. Mona realizes she has been showing Medaka her real self all along, and that is what he likes (ep. 11, about ch. 40-44). Medaka learns that his vow does not excuse unkindness (ep. 4). Whether he can keep both his vow and his heart is still open.

**Characters as arguments.** Mona argues that charm is power, until it is not. Medaka argues for discipline, and lives it decently. Tsubomi argues for loving someone by helping them. Asahi argues for honest directness. Tomo argues for knowing yourself. Anri argues that rules imposed by others should yield to your own will (Japanese Wikipedia).

**Symbols and motifs.** Medaka's glare (actually resistance); cold water; the "Bridge of Love" at the school festival (ep. 3-4); the phone lock screen (ep. 7-8); the Ferris wheel reflection where Mona sees her own face (ep. 11).

**Traditions it echoes.** The "teasing" romantic comedy (Anime UK News compares Nagatoro and Takagi-san), the harem comedy, and older stories of a monk tempted by love. Buddhist readers may recall teachings on desire and restraint; Christian, Jewish and Muslim readers may recall teachings on guarding the eyes and the heart.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Buddhist practice is the comic engine, treated with mild respect; individualism (being yourself) is affirmed; duty to a vow is tested and not yet resolved; consumer status (looks, costumes, pageants) is played for fun.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1-5 (ch. 1-44): Mona's campaign, the school festival, Asahi and Tomo arrive, and Mona admits her love; the heaviest fan service is early (Beneath the Tangles; Anime UK News). Around ch. 55-65: the beauty pageant, which readers say turned the series toward a harem (MangaUpdates comments). Later: Christmas, Valentine's Day, Anri, and the third school year (Japanese Wikipedia).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Mona wanted Medaka to like her because he did not. Is that love? When did it become love?
2. Medaka's friends told him his vow was no excuse to be unkind. Can you keep a rule and still be kind?
3. Is it fair to keep testing someone who has told you about a promise they made?
4. Why do you think the story uses so many embarrassing situations? What would it be like without them?
5. Anri thinks Medaka's rules box him in. What rules in your life protect you, and which ones box you in?
