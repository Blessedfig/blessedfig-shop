**Premise.** Tatsuhiko Hirayama, a Tokyo university student, wanted to be an animator after a film director praised his work in an elementary-school class, but quit when middle-school classmates mocked his drawings. He switched to bass in a band with his friend Kensuke; after about four years it broke up without success, and he became bitter about anyone's dreams (Mangapedia; Wikipedia). At a drinking party he sneers that dreams are pointless, and a drunk young woman tells him off in the street, roughly: act as if you have nothing to lose and do what you want (ch. 1). He animates again, a short video goes viral, and the anonymous indie musician he has long admired, Nico, asks him to make her music video; at their meeting he recognizes her as the woman from that night (ch. 1-4). When a major label signs Nico, Tatsuhiko steps back so as not to hold her back; they promise to meet again at the top. He finds mentors in two student creators at his university and help from his old bandmates, and finishes a video for Nico's song (vol. 2-3). Nico's debut is delayed; a songwriting contest reunites her with a pop star, MIYU, her childhood best friend, whom she abandoned when she quit music. Nico reveals she is the daughter of a famous singer, Nagisa, and blames herself for her mother's retirement; MIYU forgives her (vol. 3-4). A label rival leaks her parentage to the press, and with support from Tatsuhiko, MIYU and her mother she comes through (vol. 5). In the last volume his video is screened at an anime festival and the two become a couple (VIZ vol. 6 blurb; MyAnimeList reviews) (Mangapedia; Wikipedia).

## Life message

**Dreams are worth chasing again after failure and ridicule; mocking other people's dreams is the real waste, and two people who believe in each other can both start over.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What do you do with a dream that has already failed you twice? Tatsuhiko gave up animation when he was mocked and music when his band broke up, and decided dreams themselves were foolish.

**The answer the story gives.** Try again, and help someone else try. A stranger's drunken scolding (ch. 1) starts him animating; his video brings him to the very woman who scolded him, and their work together carries both forward. The story says that sneering at dreams is a form of self-protection that costs more than failure.

**Characters as arguments.** Tatsuhiko argues that hurt can harden into contempt, and that it can soften again. Nico argues for wholehearted effort, but she too has run from music out of guilt over her mother. MIYU argues that a friendship broken by silence can be mended by honest anger and forgiveness. The label rival who leaks Nico's parentage stands for using people for advantage, and fails.

**Symbols and motifs.** Beat and motion: Nico is the beat (music), Tatsuhiko the motion (animation), and the title is their collaboration (Wikipedia). The music video as a shared work. The song "I want to see the sea" (海がみたい, Mangapedia). A festival screen in the final volume.

**Traditions it echoes.** The artist's coming-of-age story (compare Blue Period and Bakuman), the Japanese "second chance" youth drama, and the old idea, found in religious teaching about talents as well as secular self-help, that a gift buried out of fear is wasted.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism is the creed, tempered by friendship and mutual help. Nihilism is the hero's starting point and is refuted. The music business is shown with its gatekeeping and gossip. Drinking is part of the leads' social world and is linked to Nico's pain, but sources do not show it judged.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1 (ch. 1-4): the meeting and the first viral video. Vol. 2 (ch. 5-12): the label offer, Tatsuhiko stepping back, mentors and old bandmates. Vols. 3-4 (ch. 13-27): MIYU, the hidden family past and reconciliation. Vol. 5 (ch. 28-35): the leak and public backlash. Vol. 6 (ch. 36-48): the festival screening and the couple, with an ending several reviewers found rushed.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Tatsuhiko stopped drawing because classmates laughed. Have words ever stopped you from doing something you loved?
2. Nico tells him off while drunk. Why do her words work on him anyway?
3. Why does Tatsuhiko step back from Nico's music video when she signs with a label? Was he right?
4. Nico and MIYU fight before they make up. When is anger part of forgiveness?
5. Nico drinks a lot. How does the story connect her drinking to what she is carrying?
