**Premise.** Chinatsu was raised by his father to be a professional killer alongside his older brother, Fuyuki. On his first job he cannot pull the trigger, and Fuyuki dies protecting him. In the alley afterward a blind boy, Kasumi, takes Chinatsu's trembling hand (MyAnimeList synopsis; MangaUpdates blurb). Five years later Chinatsu still freezes on jobs. He catches a thief who has snatched a bag from Kasumi, now a young man, and returns it; Kasumi invites him to his family's traditional inn, where his disability has left him isolated and his mother worries about appearances (ANN, Silverman). Kasumi believes he is a burden on his family (MyAnimeList). The two lonely men become friends and then lovers, while Chinatsu hides that he is a hitman and that Kasumi witnessed the day his brother died. Reviews list gang activity, kidnapping and extortion in the later chapters and describe a happy ending; MangaUpdates tags an eloping couple and a time skip in the epilogue (to verify).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can a person who was made into a weapon still be loved, and can he love back? Chinatsu was raised to kill and failed at it at the cost of his brother's life. Kasumi lives in literal darkness and is treated as a burden. The book asks what it means to "touch" someone in the dark.

**The answer the story gives.** Being seen without being judged is what lets a person change. Kasumi's first act, taking a stranger's trembling hand, comes before any knowledge of who he is; the second meeting repeats it. Reviewers describe the ending as hopeful: the two men find solace together and leave the old life behind (MyAnimeList; Boys Love Library).

**Characters as arguments.** Chinatsu argues that guilt can paralyze but also mark a conscience: his failure to kill is his best trait. Kasumi argues for unconditional kindness, and his blindness lets him see past appearances that his own family cares about. The father stands for using people as tools; Fuyuki stands for self-giving love. The hidden witness plot asks whether love can survive the truth.

**Symbols and motifs.** Night and darkness (the Japanese title is "touching your night"); light that Kasumi can still perceive; a silhouette against a sunset, the first time he "sees" Chinatsu (Boys Love Library); hands, from the alley to the end; the inn as a beautiful cage.

**Traditions it echoes.** The BL redemption romance; the noir hitman who wants out; the classic story of a blind character who sees more truly than the sighted. Christian readers may hear echoes of confession and forgiveness, Buddhist readers the end of suffering through compassion; most traditional faiths will also weigh the sexual relationship the story celebrates.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Progressive and humanist lenses are affirmed: inclusion, empathy, and healing by human love. Existentialism's theme of choosing oneself is affirmed. Nonviolence appears as the hero's conscience. No religious lens is present.

**Shifts across the series.** Chapter 1 sets up the failed job and the alley; the middle chapters build the friendship at the inn and the romance; later chapters bring gang trouble, kidnapping and extortion; the final chapter has a time skip to the couple's future (MangaUpdates). The extras, booklet and 2024 one-shot follow the couple afterward.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Chinatsu cannot pull the trigger. Is that a weakness or a strength?
2. Why does Kasumi's family keep him at the inn? What would you want if you were Kasumi?
3. Chinatsu hides the truth for a long time. When should a person tell someone about his past?
4. What does the title mean: what is "the abyss," and who touches it?
5. Your family's views on the relationship at the center of the story: how would you talk about them?
