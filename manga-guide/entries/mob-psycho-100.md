**Premise.** Shigeo Kageyama, called "Mob", is a quiet 14-year-old middle schooler with immense psychic power. He keeps his emotions in check because he fears what his power could do. When his emotions reach "100%", the power breaks loose. He works for Arataka Reigen, a con man who poses as a spirit medium, pays Mob very little and uses his power, but also gives him real advice about life. Mob wants to be ordinary: he joins the Body Improvement Club to build himself up without his powers and hopes to impress Tsubomi, a girl from his childhood. Supernatural threats keep finding him: evil spirits, a smile cult, a rival esper, his own brother's jealousy, the esper terror group Claw, the evil spirit Keiji Mogami, a religion that grows up around a giant tree, and finally his own suppressed self, "???".

## Life message

**A good life is not defined by talent or power. You become a good person by how you treat others, by honest effort and by accepting all of yourself, including the feelings you would rather hide.**

## Creator and publisher context

- **ONE** (pen name) wrote and drew the series; he also created One-Punch Man. Per Wikipedia's Shigeo Kageyama article (opened), ONE created Mob "as a contrast to the strong character Saitama" (Weekly Shonen Jump interview, Viz), based Mob on himself as a student who barely voiced his wish to become a manga artist (CBR, 2020), and said the series began with Mob's kindness and connections, with psychic powers added later to support the slice-of-life story; he pointed to Dimple changing after meeting Mob as proof that Mob's kindness drives the story (Crunchyroll, 2016). He asked Bones to keep Mob's character faithful to the manga. The underlying interviews were not opened. No public statements on religion or politics were found, and his private life is not public. **Status: Needs more info on the creators.**
- In January 2019 a fan asked ONE to tweet in English; he replied "I can't speak English :(" and the exchange went viral (Comicbook.com, opened). No other controversy involving the creator was found.
- **Publisher:** Shogakukan (Ura Sunday and MangaONE), part of the Hitotsubashi Group (from general knowledge). Dark Horse Comics publishes the English edition; Dark Horse is owned by Embracer Group (from general knowledge; to verify). Anime by Bones; Crunchyroll holds streaming and dub rights (Wikipedia).
- **Anime production note:** Season 3's English dub replaced Mob's voice actor, Kyle McCarley, and many fans dislike the recast (TV Tropes; the reasons were widely reported as a union dispute, from general knowledge; to verify).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** If you had the power to do anything, what would make you a good person? Mob could flatten a city. He wants to be popular with a girl, to build muscle and to be ordinary. The series asks whether worth comes from what you can do or from how you live.

**The answer the story gives.** Worth does not come from power. Reigen tells Mob early that psychic power is "a dangerous weapon" (ch. 14) and that it is fine to run from bad situations (ch. 46). Mob keeps a rule against using his power on people. He trains with the Body Improvement Club, where he is weak and slow, because that kind of growth is his own. The Mary Sue sums up the finale: "Your talents aren't what define you; how you treat others and navigate the world are far more important." The story adds a second answer near the end. Suppressing feelings is not the same as goodness. Mob's bottled emotions become ???, a self that wants to be seen. He becomes whole by accepting it, not by locking it away (ch. 99-100).

**Characters as arguments.** Teru argues that espers are the main characters of the world; Mob's victory turns him into a humbler ally (ch. 10-19). Ritsu argues from envy: he wants the gift his brother has, and when he gets power, he misuses it (ch. 20-33). Claw argues that the strong should rule the world (ch. 34-50, 74-91). Mogami argues that people are wicked at heart. His own story is tragic: he killed with curses for money to treat his mother, and lost her anyway (ch. 62-65). He traps Mob in a false life of bullying to prove his point (ch. 63-67). Mob's answer, given later, is faith in people, and Mogami spares his host before vanishing (ch. 87). Reigen argues for practical wisdom without powers. He is a liar, but he cares about Mob, and his confession is what pulls Mob back (ch. 99-100).

**Symbols and motifs.** The percentage meter tracks bottled emotion rising toward 100%. "???" stands for the unknown, unaccepted self. Salt, the fake exorcist's tool, stands for Reigen's bluff. Smiling masks and forced laughter in the (LOL) cult (ch. 5-9) stand for forced happiness. The Divine Tree, a giant broccoli in the Japanese text, mocks belief that grows around a spectacle (ch. 91/92-97).

**Traditions it echoes.** Virtue ethics on habit and self-control. Buddhist ideas on ego and attachment. Japanese urban legends and exorcist stories. Shonen battle conventions, which it parodies (TCJ: "He is never in any danger; he is the danger"). A Christian site hears echoes of humility, the divided self and the "dishonest steward" (Beneath the Tangles).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Might makes right and authoritarian groups are clearly defeated. Secular humanist and virtue themes are shown as true. Organized religion appears mostly as manipulation, while spirits are simply part of the world. Pragmatism is embodied by a flawed mentor and endorsed with reservations. Expressive individualism appears in the finale, balanced by restraint and care for others.

**Shifts across the series.** Early chapters (ch. 1-19) are comedy with exorcism jobs and a parody cult. The middle (ch. 20-67) darkens with Ritsu's jealousy, Claw's kidnappings and the Mogami arc, the series' psychological low point. The Separate Ways arc (ch. 68-73) turns on Reigen being exposed. The long Claw war (ch. 74-91) and the Divine Tree (ch. 91/92-97) scale up to city-wide threats. The last chapters (ch. 98-101) turn inward to Mob's own emotions, his confession and a quiet epilogue.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Mob could solve most problems with his power, but often chooses not to. When is it right not to use a strength you have?
2. Reigen lies to Mob for years but also helps him. Was he a good mentor? What does his confession change?
3. Mogami tries to prove that people are cruel. What would you say back to him?
4. Mob's buried feelings turn into ???. What is the difference between controlling your feelings and hiding them?
5. Several groups in the story want to worship someone or make everyone smile. Why might the author make these groups villains?
