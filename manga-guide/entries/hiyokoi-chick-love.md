**Premise.** Hiyori Nishiyama is 15 and only 140 cm tall. Badly injured in an accident the day before high school began, she spent a long time in hospital and then stayed away from school for a year (MyAnimeList; Wikipedia says the accident stopped her bones growing). When she finally comes to class, a year late and terrified of being teased, her self-introduction is interrupted by Yuushin Hirose, a 190-cm, cheerful, popular boy who sits next to her. After an accident shows her underwear to the class, Yuushin comforts her, and she slowly falls for him (Wikipedia; the anime adapts ch. 1-2). With her best friend since kindergarten, Ritsuka, who coaxed her back to school, and classmates such as Natsuki, Hiyori learns to make friends. She confesses at a fireworks festival; Yuushin, interrupted at the time, confesses in turn over summer break and asks her out (Wikipedia). In the second year a short, prickly classmate, Kou Nitobe, grows fond of Hiyori and confesses to her (end of ch. 37), and a returnee from the United States, Reina, admits a crush on Yuushin (Wikipedia). A later arc involves Akiho, connected to Yuushin's former classmate Kisaki, who wants to die and tries to make Hiyori break up with Yuushin; one reviewer says she dies by suicide (two MyAnimeList user reviews; MangaUpdates Attempted Suicide tag; chapter not found). The ending has a time skip with the main couple starting their chosen careers and the side characters paired off happily (MyAnimeList reviews).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** How does someone who has been left behind catch up? Hiyori arrives a year late, smaller than everyone, sure she will be teased. The series asks what it takes for a frightened person to belong.

**The answer the story gives.** Other people's patience, and her own small acts of courage. Yuushin's easy friendliness, Ritsuka's loyalty and a class that finds Hiyori cute rather than strange give her room; she has to take the steps herself, up to confessing at a festival (Wikipedia).

**Characters as arguments.** Hiyori argues that courage can be learned. Yuushin argues for warmth that includes everyone. Ritsuka argues for loyalty, even to the point of hitting a boy who mocks her friend. Kisaki and Reina, rivals who let go with good grace, argue that love lost need not turn bitter. Akiho, as reviewers describe her, is the dark counterpoint: someone whose loneliness becomes despair.

**Symbols and motifs.** Height: the 50-cm gap between Hiyori and Yuushin, and his crouching to meet her. The chick of the title, small and new. Fireworks festivals, where confessions happen. The teacher's after-school diary, a comic mirror of romance gone wrong.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Ribon school romance; the ugly-duckling or late-bloomer story; the friend who brings the shy one into the circle.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Community and friendship are celebrated; self-expression is the goal of Hiyori's growth. No political, religious or philosophical argument is made.

**Shifts across the series.** Early volumes: first year, friendships, Valentine's Day and the fireworks confession; the couple start dating fairly early (MyAnimeList review). Middle volumes: second year, Kou and Reina, and the side romances. Late volumes: the Akiho storyline and a time-skip ending.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Hiyori was afraid of being teased. What did her classmates do that helped?
2. Ritsuka hit a boy who insulted her friend. Was that right?
3. Kisaki and Reina both liked Yuushin. How did they handle it?
4. Akiho felt she wanted to die. Who could she have talked to, and who can you talk to?
5. What small brave thing could you do this week?
