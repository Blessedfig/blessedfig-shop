**Premise.** Takara Itozaki, 33, a real estate agent in Tokyo who grew up in Kumamoto, is dumped by the boyfriend she had lived with for two years: he says she never gets jealous, so he cannot tell if she loves him. She realizes she has never been upset by a breakup. Then a beautiful woman walks into the agency: Ema Natsume, 33, an executive at a popular café chain, the classmate who transferred away in the second year of middle school and whom Takara once tried to kiss (Cocohana; MangaUpdates). Ema is married to Hario, a kind, wealthy man who encourages the friendship (ANN). Takara slowly admits she has loved Ema for twenty years and that she had told herself it was "just a phase" (ANN). She meets Hario and feels guilt and jealousy (v3); Ema looks for an apartment to live apart from her husband, with Takara's help (v3, Okazu); Ema's cruel mother reappears and Takara proposes they run away (v4); after an overnight escape Ema cuts off her mother, and the two confess their love to each other (v5, Okazu; v6 blurb). Volumes 6-7 turn on the fact that someone must be hurt: Takara leaves the choice to Ema, between the life she has with her husband and something else (Maruzen Junkudo blurbs for Japanese vols. 6-7).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What do you owe to a love you denied for twenty years, and what do you owe to a good man who did nothing wrong? Takara's ex-boyfriend leaves because she never gets jealous; Ema's return shows her why.

**The answer the story gives.** So far, that honesty with oneself is necessary but not sufficient. Takara admits her love (v5-6) and Ema returns it, but the blurbs insist there is no happy ending while Ema is married. In vol. 7 Takara leaves the decision to Ema, wanting only her happiness. The series has not yet given its final answer.

**Characters as arguments.** Takara argues for truthfulness and for a love that puts the other first. Ema argues for the safety she chose to escape a cruel home (ANN's Silverman reads her early marriage that way). Hario argues, without words, for kindness and fidelity: his goodness is what keeps the story from being an easy romance. Ema's mother is the case against control dressed as family duty.

**Symbols and motifs.** The title, cold and soft: Ema's cool, soft hands (VIZ vol. 1 blurb). Real estate: Takara finds other people homes and helps Ema look for one of her own (v3). Recreating a summer from twenty years before (v3, Okazu). Running away overnight (v4-5).

**Traditions it echoes.** Josei adult romance and the modern adult yuri of Even Though We're Adults, to which ANN and Okazu compare it; also the older Class S tradition of intense girls' friendships, which the story reframes as real love rather than a phase. Religious readers will hear the long tradition that adultery begins in the heart; the story does not dismiss it.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism is affirmed as honesty but not as license: the cost to Hario is kept in view. Progressivism's challenge to the "phase" idea is affirmed. Traditional marriage is respected through Hario, while abusive family authority is rejected.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-2: reunion, denial, the lesbian bar. Vol. 3: meeting Hario; Ema seeks to live apart. Vol. 4: the mother's visit and the plan to run away. Vol. 5: the escape, cutting off the mother, the confession. Vols. 6-7 (Japanese only so far): mutual love and the question of what Ema will choose.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why did Takara believe her feelings for Ema were "just a phase"? Who taught her that?
2. Hario is kind. How does that change the way you read the story?
3. Takara wants Ema to be happy above all. Is that the same as wanting Ema for herself?
4. When is it right to cut off a parent, as Ema does?
5. Is there a choice here where no one gets hurt?
