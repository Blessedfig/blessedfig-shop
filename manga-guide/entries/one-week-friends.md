**Premise.** Second-year high schooler Yuki Hase wants to befriend Kaori Fujimiya, a classmate who keeps everyone at a distance. Eating lunch with her on the school roof, he learns why: every Monday she loses all memory of the friends she made, though she remembers her family and people who are not friends. Yuki promises to become her friend again every week and suggests a diary; she slowly holds on to more of him (vol. 1-3). His blunt best friend Shogo Kiryu and forgetful classmate Saki Yamagishi join them, and Saki becomes Kaori's first girl friend (Wikipedia; fan wiki). In the second term Hajime Kujo, Kaori's elementary-school friend, transfers in and calls her a traitor; she loses her memory of Yuki again (vol. 3-4). The story then uncovers the cause: as a child Kaori was close to Hajime, a girl friend turned the others against her over it, and that shock together with a car accident on the day she was rushing to keep a promise to meet Hajime set off the memory loss (Japanese Wikipedia; fan wiki). Yuki steps back to let Hajime help her and grows cold and unsure of himself (vol. 5-6, Yen Press). Kaori remembers everything (ch. 36), makes peace with Hajime (ch. 37), and Yuki asks to be friends again (ch. 38). They do not become a couple by the end (MyAnimeList review); the sequel describes Kaori as fully recovered, with Yuki hoping for more than friendship after high school (MangaDex).

## Life message

**Faithful, patient friendship is worth offering again and again, even when it is forgotten, and being honest about the past is what lets a person heal.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Is a friendship real if one of the two cannot remember it? Kaori has decided it is safer to have no friends than to lose them every week. Yuki decides that a week of friendship is worth having, even if he must start over.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, and faithfulness can heal. The diary (ch. 1-3) turns memory into a shared practice; friends hold what Kaori cannot. But the deeper cure is honesty about the past: Kaori's memory returns once the childhood hurt and the broken promise are faced (ch. 36) and forgiven (ch. 37).

**Characters as arguments.** Yuki argues for patient love, then has to learn that love can also mean stepping back (vol. 6) and that he must value himself. Kaori argues for self-protection and learns courage. Shogo argues for blunt truth; Saki for learning to stand on her own. Hajime shows how a hurt left unspoken turns to resentment.

**Symbols and motifs.** Mondays as resets; the diary; the school roof as a private refuge; a park promise missed because of an accident; the chapter titles built on the word "friend" (Wikipedia chapter list); ch. 26's math title about a sequence approaching zero, beside Kaori's skill in math.

**Traditions it echoes.** Japanese school-life and "healing" (iyashikei) stories; amnesia romances such as 50 First Dates (from general knowledge; to verify). Religious readers hear echoes of steadfast love and of forgiveness healing what was broken.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Virtue and loyalty are celebrated quietly; promise-keeping is honored and a broken promise is forgiven; community (friends, mother, teacher) carries Kaori. No worldview is attacked.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-3 (ch. 0-14) are light, four-panel-heavy and hopeful, ending in summer. Volumes 4-5 (ch. 15-26) bring Hajime and the past. Volumes 6-7 (ch. 27-38) turn to story pages, Yuki's doubt and the resolution (Japanese Wikipedia; Yen Press).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why did Kaori decide it was safer not to have friends? Have you ever felt that way?
2. Yuki keeps asking to be friends after Kaori says no. When is persistence kind, and when should someone stop?
3. How did the gossip and the old friend group hurt Kaori?
4. Why does Kaori start to remember once she faces what happened with Hajime?
5. Who is someone in your school who might need a friend every week?
