**Premise.** On the day her best friend moves away, shy, romance-manga-loving Yuna Ichihara lends train fare to a stranger, Akari Yamamoto, and the girls discover they live in the same apartment building as they start high school (ch. 1). Yuna is a dreamer who has only loved a prince in a picture book; Akari is a realist who already has a boyfriend (he breaks up with her early, ch. 3; TV Tropes). Yuna falls for Rio, a handsome boy who dates any cute girl who asks and who turns out to be Akari's stepbrother through their parents' recent remarriage; Rio secretly loves Akari, whom he met before the marriage (Wikipedia). Akari falls for Yuna's easygoing childhood friend Kazuomi Inui, who is slow to understand love. Yuna confesses to Rio and is turned down, but keeps loving him and grows bolder; the two confess to each other at the school festival (vol. 7; But Why Tho). Akari is pressed by her ex-boyfriend Ryosuke, struggles with her parents' distrust of her and Rio, and is held back by Kazuomi's loyalty to Rio (vols. 7-11; VIZ). Kazuomi tells his parents he wants to make films (vol. 11). When the Yamamotos are transferred abroad, Akari chooses to go to America and Kazuomi follows later; Rio stays in Japan, and he and Yuna are a couple in college at the end (vol. 12; VIZ; Wikipedia).

## Life message

**Love grows when you are honest with yourself and considerate of others: a shy dreamer finds courage, a guarded realist learns to trust, and real friendship survives rejection, jealousy and family pressure.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Is love something that happens to you or something you make happen? Yuna believes in fate and a picture-book prince; Akari believes you choose and pursue a good match (The Graphic Library). The series puts both girls, and two boys who also misunderstand love, through four years of trial to see what each view is missing.

**The answer the story gives.** Both and neither. Yuna's dream gives her the courage to keep loving after rejection, but she has to learn to speak and act, not wait (vol. 7). Akari's realism protects her, but her ex calls her empty (vol. 7) and she has to risk being hurt. What wins is honesty, with yourself first and then with others.

**Characters as arguments.** Rio is the case against loving for looks: he starts as a boy who dates any cute girl and ends as one who chooses Yuna for who she is. Kazuomi is the case for sincerity, and also for loyalty: he turns Akari down out of consideration for Rio (Wikipedia) before he is free to love her. Akari's mother argues for caution and is half wrong about her children but right to care. Ryosuke, the ex, represents love as possession.

**Symbols and motifs.** The bracelet Akari leaves as a pledge on the first page, and the borrowed fare she repays: trust kept. The prince in the picture book. The title, from a schoolgirl acne-fortune rhyme about who loves whom (Wikipedia). Kazuomi's films: he lends Akari (500) Days of Summer (TV Tropes), a story about mistaking an idea of love for a person.

**Traditions it echoes.** The shojo school romance of Sakisaka's own Strobe Edge and Ao Haru Ride; the double love triangle of classic romantic comedy. For readers of faith, its picture of love as patient and truthful echoes 1 Corinthians 13, though the story itself has no religious frame.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism is affirmed in its gentle form: say what you feel. Communitarianism appears as friendship that comes before winning. Pragmatism is tested and softened.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-3 set up the four and the step-sibling secret. Vols. 4-7 bring Yuna's rejection, Rio's change and the festival confessions. Vols. 8-11 turn to Akari and Kazuomi, her ex and family problems, and Kazuomi's career. Vol. 12 is the transfer abroad and the choices about distance.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Yuna confessed and was turned down, then tried again. Was that wise? When should you keep trying, and when should you stop?
2. Rio began by dating girls for their looks. What changed him?
3. Kazuomi turned Akari down for a friend's sake. Was that kind, or unfair to her?
4. Akari's mother did not trust her alone with Rio. How should parents handle a situation like that?
5. Akari chose to go abroad even though she would be apart from Kazuomi. What would you have chosen?
