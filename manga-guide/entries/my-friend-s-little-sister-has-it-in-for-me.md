**Premise.** Akiteru Ōboshi, a second-year high school student, lives by extreme efficiency: no girlfriend, one friend (Ozuma Kohinata). Secretly he is producer and director of the 5th Floor Alliance, a small team making a phone game: Ozuma is its genius programmer, the novelist Makigai Namako writes its scenario, and the illustrator "Murasaki Shikibu" is really their homeroom teacher, Sumire Kageishi (25). Ozuma's little sister Iroha, a first-year who is a polite honor student at school, spends her free time in Akiteru's apartment pestering him, and secretly voices every character in the game; her mother, who has no patience for entertainment, does not know. To win the whole team jobs at his uncle Makoto's entertainment company, Akiteru agrees to be the fake boyfriend of his cousin Mashiro Tsukinomori, who transfers to his class, is icy to him in public, and is secretly Makigai Namako. Other girls with prickly personalities join the circle (Japanese Wikipedia; English Wikipedia; Manga UP!).

## Creator and publisher context

- Ghost Mikawa (original story): debuted after a silver prize in the 18th Dengeki Novel Prize; studied business management, worked in market research, and writes light novels, manga scripts and game scenarios; best known before this for a series with MF Bunko J (interview).
- On-record intent (Light Novel News Online, 2019): he felt the market wanted obedient heroines and wanted to challenge that with an "annoying-cute" heroine who causes the hero stress; he chose "friend's little sister" because it keeps a junior close to the hero while adding a little of the opposite sex's tension that a real sister lacks; the title came first, in a meeting with his editor; besides the heroine he wanted to depict "comrades," people too sharp-edged to fit in, who chase a dream together. He says Iroha teases only Akiteru because she has fully opened her heart to him.
- Mikawa cites Hollywood films (The Butterfly Effect, Equilibrium, Cars, Marvel) and light novels such as Shakugan no Shana as models for his storytelling (interview).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Is a life without friends and distractions really better? Akiteru has thrown out romance and most friendships as inefficient, yet his apartment is full of people he cannot turn away. The series asks whether efficiency is wisdom or a wall.

**The answer the story gives.** Friendship wins. Akiteru's efficiency turns out to be a talent for seeing what others need; he gathers people whose gifts are overlooked and makes a team (Japanese Wikipedia). Each heroine shows her true self only to him: Iroha her loud self, Mashiro her shy self behind coldness. Mikawa frames teasing as a sign of trust (interview).

**Characters as arguments.** Akiteru argues for efficiency and learns it serves people. Iroha argues for following a dream against a parent's wishes. Mashiro argues that shyness can hide care. Ozuma argues for loyal friendship. Sumire argues, comically, that talent and adulthood do not mean having one's life together. Iroha's mother stands for duty over dreams.

**Symbols and motifs.** The spare key: trust given and taken. The bed Iroha occupies. The game the team builds. The fake relationship as a contract.

**Traditions it echoes.** The teasing-kohai comedy (Teasing Master Takagi-san, Uzaki-chan Wants to Hang Out!) and the light-novel harem (from general knowledge; to verify). For religious readers, questions of honoring parents, honesty and modesty; for secular readers, a story of creative young people supporting one another.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism and communitarianism are both affirmed: you become yourself among friends. Pragmatism is explored and softened. Traditionalism appears only as an obstacle.

**Shifts across the series.** The anime covers manga ch. 1-19 (v1-4): the teasing, the fake-dating deal and the team. Later volumes follow the novels' growing love triangle and the characters' family conflicts; how far the manga has reached was not confirmed in sources opened.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Akiteru thinks friendships are inefficient. What does he gain from them anyway?
2. Iroha is polite at school and loud with Akiteru. Which is the real Iroha?
3. Is it right for Iroha to hide her voice-acting work from her mother? What else could she do?
4. Is a fake relationship as a business deal fair to everyone involved?
5. Where is the line between teasing a friend and making them uncomfortable?
