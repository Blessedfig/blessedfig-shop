**Premise.** In the autumn of their first year of high school, the popular, airheaded and casually flirtatious Hayato Kuga is seated next to Yuki Amano, a cool, bookish girl who almost never speaks or smiles and writes fantasy web novels in her spare time. Kuga sets out to get past her wall and make her laugh, and Amano, annoyed at first, slowly opens up. Neither has ever had a first crush, and neither notices what is happening (AniList, from the Coolmic blurb; MyAnimeList; BookLive). Around them are Kaneko, a bespectacled otaku who delights in their "light-novel developments," and Natsume Sato, a curvy classmate who wants Kuga for herself (BookLive). Kuga dotes on his younger sister as a running gag (MangaUpdates; MyAnimeList). Amano writes about her friendship with Kuga in her stories (MyAnimeList). In the final volume Kuga feels jealous when he sees Amano with another boy, Sato pursues him hard, and a study session puts him in an awkward spot with Amano (National Diet Library, vol. 3).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** How do two people who have never had a crush notice that they have one?

**The answer the story gives.** Slowly, and mostly through other people's eyes: Kaneko sees a light novel in their every moment, Sato's pursuit and a rival boy stir jealousy (vol. 3), and Amano works out her feelings in the stories she writes (MyAnimeList).

**Characters as arguments.** Kuga argues for cheerful friendliness; Amano for quiet independence. Neither is corrected; each draws the other halfway.

**Symbols and motifs.** The seat change; Amano's web novels as a mirror of her real life; the wall Kuga senses around her (Coolmic blurb via AniList).

**Traditions it echoes.** The yonkoma school romance and the seatmate comedy; for this guide, a small picture of fiction as a way to understand one's own feelings.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism is gentle; community is a friendly class. No other worldview is engaged.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-2 are slice-of-life seatmate gags; vol. 3 adds jealousy, a rival's pursuit and the finale (NDL). MangaUpdates voters tag it as cut short (4 votes), which is unconfirmed.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Kuga keeps trying to make Amano smile. When is that kind, and when could it be too much?
2. Amano writes stories about her own life. Why might writing help someone understand their feelings?
3. Why do you think neither of them notices they have a crush?
