**Premise.** Koyuki Hikawa, a short first-year high school student, keeps everyone at arm's length; classmates call her the "Ice Queen." Her wall comes from middle school: her friend Manatsu turned the basketball club against her, and her brief relationship with Tsubasa, who mocked her tastes and pushed past her discomfort, ended badly (Wikipedia; ANN, episode 11). Her only friend is Miki, a popular girl who hides her rowdy real self behind an "idol" image. Two boys break in: Minato, a sociable "fixer" who thinks he can unlock anyone, and his tall, gentle friend Yota, who has loved Miki since middle school. Over two school years the four become friends; Koyuki falls for Minato, who, believing she does not care for him, dates the manipulative first-year Momoka to move on, sickens under the strain and breaks up with her (anime episodes 16-22, Wikipedia). Minato's habit of reading the room is traced to watching his older brother Kaito fight with their strict father, who drove Kaito from home (Japanese Wikipedia). The series ends with two couples: Koyuki and Minato, and Miki and Yota (Wikipedia).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Is it safer to keep people out? Koyuki built her wall after a friend turned others against her and a boyfriend belittled what she loved. The series asks what the wall protects and what it costs, and it asks the same of the cheerful characters: Miki's idol mask and Minato's easy friendliness are walls too.

**The answer the story gives.** Walls melt through honest words and patient friends. Every major turning point is a conversation: an apology (episode 5), a confession, a confrontation that clears the air (Koyuki and Tsubasa, episode 18), a friend saying what she sees (Tsukiko). The story also insists that caring for others does not mean hiding yourself: Minato makes himself sick dating someone he does not love because he cannot say what he feels.

**Characters as arguments.** Koyuki argues that self-protection is understandable but lonely. Minato argues, at first, that he can fix anyone; the story answers that his "fixing" comes from fear of being left out himself (Japanese Wikipedia). Miki argues for pleasing others, then learns to be herself. Yota argues for patient, honest kindness, and is rewarded. Momoka argues that love goes to whoever moves first; she loses, but is allowed to grow. Tsubasa and Manatsu show how teens hurt each other through status and pressure, and the story lets Tsubasa own his part.

**Symbols and motifs.** The rampart of ice itself, and the final chapter "Melting Ice" (ch. 117); Minato's image of keys and locks ("A Locksmith," ch. 7); color and heat ("Heat and Color," "Heat Conduction," ch. 54-55); the full-color vertical scroll, which ANN reviewers note frames Koyuki's point of view.

**Traditions it echoes.** Shōjo school romance and the ensemble friendship drama; ANN's Caitlin Moore compares its love polygon to American teen dramas. Its focus on emotional honesty fits contemporary talk of healthy relationships and mental health, while its patience and repair echo older ideals of friendship and humility.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Community is the cure, and being your real self is part of it. Virtues of honesty and courage are rewarded. Group harmony is valued but not at the cost of truth. There is no religious content.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-3 (ch. 1-33): the four meet, misunderstandings, Miki's mask. Volumes 4-6 (ch. 34-57): second year, Koyuki's past with Manatsu and Tsubasa, Momoka arrives. Volumes 7-8 (ch. 58-74): sports festival, Yota's confession and Miki's refusal (anime season 1 ends about ch. 71). Volumes 9-11 (ch. 75-97): Minato dates Momoka, sickens and breaks up; Kaito returns. Volumes 12-14 (ch. 98-117): recovery, the school trip (ch. 103-105) and two couples (from chapter titles and Wikipedia; to verify).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why did Koyuki build her wall? Was it wrong to protect herself?
2. Minato wanted to "unlock" people. What was he really afraid of?
3. Miki acts like an idol at school. When is it kind to adjust yourself for others, and when does it become a lie?
4. Yota was turned down and stayed a good friend. What does that show about him?
5. Momoka never apologizes to Koyuki. Can someone change without saying sorry?
