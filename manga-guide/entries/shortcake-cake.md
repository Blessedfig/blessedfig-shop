**Premise.** Ten Serizawa, a calm, blunt first-year from a mountain village, faces a two-hour bus ride to high school. After secretly staying overnight at her friend Ageha's boardinghouse, she moves in (vol. 1). Its residents include Chiaki Kasadera, kind and studious, and Riku Mizuhara, a handsome boy who rudely turns down admirers until Ten scolds him and he falls for her. A house rule bars residents from having someone of the opposite sex in their rooms; a girl who fell in love has just moved out because of it (Wikipedia). The story follows a slow love triangle (Riku and Chiaki both love Ten) and, from vol. 8, Riku's hidden family pain: he was left as a baby in a teacher's classroom and adopted, his adoptive parents died in a car accident, his grandfather favored him over his brother Rei, and Rei drove him from the house (Manga Bookshelf, vols. 9-10). Ten and Riku become a couple (vol. 7), Riku meets his birth mother and chooses his adoptive family (vol. 11), and the series ends with the friends' futures and bonus epilogues (vol. 12).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What makes a place, or a group of people, home? Ten moves into a boardinghouse for convenience and finds a family; Riku has lived in other people's houses all his life and fears he belongs nowhere.

**The answer the story gives.** Home is where people stay and speak honestly. Ten wins Riku not by drama but by patience and plain words (vol. 7). Riku is healed when Chiaki and Ten refuse to let him disappear (vol. 10), and when Rei admits his wrong and asks him back (vol. 11). Riku chooses the family that raised him over his birth mother.

**Characters as arguments.** Ten argues for honesty: she says what she feels and asks what others feel. Riku argues the cost of hiding pain behind a smile. Chiaki argues for love that wants the other's good, even when it means helping a rival (vols. 3, 10). Rei argues, and then disproves, the idea that love must be earned by being the favorite.

**Symbols and motifs.** The long commute and the shortcut of living together. The boardinghouse rule as a line between friendship and romance. Rain (vol. 3, Manga Bookshelf). The title: a shortcake, plain and sweet, like Ten.

**Traditions it echoes.** Shojo boardinghouse romances and the Biblical and folk theme of the favored son and the jealous brother (Joseph, Jacob and Esau) for faith readers (from general knowledge; to verify).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Community and found family are affirmed. Confucian family order is respected but corrected: favoritism causes harm, and repentance restores order. Individual honesty is praised.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-6: the boardinghouse, the triangle, fake dating, two kisses. Vols. 7-8: Ten and Riku become a couple. Vols. 9-11: Riku's past, grief and reconciliation. Vol. 12: endings and epilogues.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Ten moved to save time and found friends. What makes a place feel like home?
2. Riku smiled to hide his pain. Why is it hard to tell people we are hurting?
3. Chiaki helped Ten and Riku even though he loved Ten. Was that wise or kind or both?
4. Rei was jealous of his grandfather's favor. How did that harm everyone, and how was it fixed?
5. Riku and Chiaki each kissed Ten without asking. How should someone show feelings respectfully?
