**Premise.** Yuuto, a high school boy, has loved his classmate Akira from afar, but she dated his older brother Kai, who saved her after she was pushed down a flight of stairs (Anime UK News; Siliconera). Kai broke up with her because she was obsessive and clingy, and she cannot let go. Yuuto makes a deal: Akira may sit in his bedroom, next to Kai's, to listen to his brother, and in return she "pays" Yuuto with small affections such as holding hands, head pats or letting him touch her cheek (ANN Manga Guide). She asks to bug Kai's room (he refuses), stands outside the brothers' house in the rain until 4 a.m., and keeps a secret room of photos (Anime UK News). Kai starts dating Shion, whom he thinks may be the one; Shion bonds with Yuuto over a tokusatsu (special-effects hero) show; Akira fixates on Shion and tries to remake herself in her likeness (The Outerhaven; Anime UK News). Kai discovers the arrangement and acts to protect his brother. Akira's backstory: she was named after the father who died before she was born, and her mother tried to shape her into his image (Anime UK News; ANN warns of child abuse). When everything collapses, Akira tries to kill herself by jumping from a bridge and Yuuto stops her (Anime UK News). A time-skip epilogue shows the characters years later (ANN, Orsini).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** If you love someone who is using you, and you know it, what is that love worth, and what are you willing to call it?

**The answer the story gives.** It is not love, on either side. Akira's clinging to Kai is shown as a wound from childhood, not romance; Yuuto's willingness to be used damages his brother and himself. The breaking point is the bridge: Yuuto saves Akira's life (Anime UK News), and the time-skip epilogue shows people who have grown after facing what happened (Siliconera; ANN).

**Characters as arguments.** Akira argues, by her ruin, against making another person your whole identity; raised to be someone else (her dead father's image), she keeps trying to become someone else (Kai's ideal, then Shion). Yuuto argues for devotion and shows its danger when it lacks honesty. Kai argues for boundaries and protecting family, though not always wisely (Siliconera). Shion is ordinary health in a sick triangle.

**Symbols and motifs.** The shared bedroom wall between the brothers; the "payments" of touch; the secret photo room; the rain outside the house; the bridge.

**Traditions it echoes.** Japanese yandere fiction, and reviewers compare its discomfort to Shūzō Oshimi's psychological manga (ANN, Cormack); the cautionary-romance tradition.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Using people as means is clearly wrong (duty ethics); brotherly loyalty is the good to be restored; remaking yourself for someone else is critiqued. Buddhist readers will recognize clinging as the source of suffering, though the story uses no religious language.

**Shifts across the series.** Volume 1 (ch. 1-15) is dark comedy: the deal, the stalking gags, the class rep's unnoticed crush. Volume 2 (ch. 16-30) turns serious: Shion, the discovery of the arrangement, the photo room, the bridge and the epilogue (estimated from reviews).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why does Yuuto agree to Akira's deal even though he knows she is using him?
2. What does Akira's mother have to do with how Akira loves?
3. When is protecting someone right, and when does it become controlling?
4. What should you do if a friend talks about hurting themselves? (Talk to a trusted adult; in the US, call or text 988.)
5. The title calls Akira "trash." Is that fair? What does the ending suggest?
