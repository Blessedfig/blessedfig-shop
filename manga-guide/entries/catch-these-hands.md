**Premise.** Ayako Takebe, once the top delinquent at her high school (Anime UK News gives her old title as "The Black Shadow of Tenjinyama"), is in her early twenties and adrift: her old friends are marrying and having children, and she cannot hold a steady job (Wikipedia; Okazu vol. 1; ANN). Shopping for an outfit for a friend's wedding, she runs into her old rival Kirara Soramori, the "Bloody Cardigan," now a nervous shop clerk. Soramori admits a long crush and proposes one more fight: if she wins, they date. Takebe loses and treats dating as another challenge (vol. 1). Volume 2 shows their high-school gang rivalry, Soramori's former girlfriend and her friend Maria, a woman in a stable relationship with another woman; Takebe's attempts to become a "new person" fall apart and the two stop speaking. In volume 3 Takebe talks with Maria, trains, and challenges Soramori to a fight in the park where they first met; the brawl clears the air and they agree to keep dating (Okazu vol. 3). Volume 4 ends the series with an honest conversation: Takebe says she does not really understand romantic or physical attraction but is open to trying, Soramori admits her fear of being "too much," and they agree that their feelings differ but they can still be together; they first hold hands near the end (Okazu vol. 4; Manga Bookshelf; The Ace Couple).

## Life message

**You do not have to follow the usual adult script to grow up: being honest about who you are, and accepting the other person as they are, can turn an old rivalry into a real partnership.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What does it mean to grow up if you do not want the life your friends have? Takebe was the toughest girl in school; at twenty-something she has no job, no plan and no idea how to be an adult. The series asks whether "growing up" means fitting a script, or finding out who you are.

**The answer the story gives.** Be honest, and let others be who they are. Takebe tries to become a polished new person and fails (vol. 2); she goes back to where she started, settles things the only way she knows (vol. 3), and then does the harder thing: she talks (vol. 4). Okazu sums up the lesson as people imagining others think worse of them than they do.

**Characters as arguments.** Takebe argues that life is a contest you must win; she learns that a relationship is not one. Soramori argues for total devotion and learns that she also needs a life of her own (vol. 4). Maria argues, by example, that a settled queer adulthood is possible (WWAC). Takebe's married friends are not villains; they simply show the path that does not fit her.

**Symbols and motifs.** The fist: the title's "catch my fist" turns a punch into an embrace. Soramori's cardigan, her old gang name. The park where they first fought (vol. 3). The Onstegram photos: performing a relationship for likes versus enjoying it (vol. 1). Held hands at the end (vol. 4).

**Traditions it echoes.** The yankii manga and the rivals-to-lovers romance; the yuri tradition of slow-burn relationships between women; and, in its last chapter, recent stories that show asexual experience without a label (The Ace Couple).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism wins: the characters are told they are fine as they are. Progressive and feminist readings find a hopeful picture of queer adult life. Pragmatism supplies the method (honest talk). Traditional readers will see a story that treats marriage and children as pressure and makes a same-sex couple the happy ending, though without any sexual content.

**Shifts across the series.** Volume 1 is pure comedy (the bet, the dates, Onstegram). Volume 2 turns to backstory and a falling-out. Volume 3 is the reconciliation brawl. Volume 4 is the quiet, honest ending, where the ace reading appears.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Takebe feels behind because her friends are getting married and having children. Is there a "right age" for those things?
2. Why does Takebe treat dating as a fight to win? What changes when she stops?
3. The two make up by fighting in the park. What else could they have done?
4. In the end they agree that they feel differently but want to stay together. Can a relationship work like that?
5. Soramori worries she is "too much." Have you ever felt that way? What helped?
