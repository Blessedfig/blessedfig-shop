**Premise.** Roland, an assassin trained for killing since childhood, slips away from his hero party and defeats the demon lord alone. He does not kill her: he puts a collar on the demon lord, Rila, that seals her power and turns her into a cat who can take human form, and leaves a body double behind. Offered any reward by the king, he remembers a teammate's wish and asks for an ordinary life. He takes a job at the Adventurers Guild in the town of Lahti, where his "worthless" skill, a faint presence that makes him easy to overlook, hides his deadly abilities. He soon becomes the guild's best employee, quietly solves the town's problems (a monster-tamer's wolves that massacred a village, vol. 2), commands the king's escort for a royal marriage (vol. 4), and has affairs with several women while Rila, as his cat, objects (Yen Press; Anime UK News; ANN; MangaUpdates).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What does a man trained only to kill do when the war is over? Roland has never wanted anything but the next target; when the king offers him anything, he borrows a dead teammate's wish for an ordinary life (MangaUpdates description).

**The answer the story gives.** An ordinary life turns out to be work done well, a town kept safe and a steady stream of companionship. The series does not test the wish much: Roland's skill solves every problem, and the women around him fall for him (ANN; Anime UK News).

**Characters as arguments.** Roland is competence without ambition. Rila, the defeated demon lord, is mercy (he spares her) and also control (he collars her). The guild women are admiration. The villains are those who prey on villages and get no mercy.

**Symbols and motifs.** The "worthless" skill of being overlooked, which is really the perfect assassin's gift; the collar; the guild counter as the place of normal life.

**Traditions it echoes.** The retired gunfighter or hitman who settles in a town and is pulled back into trouble; the Japanese overpowered-hero and harem genres.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Pragmatism and expressive individualism are affirmed: the hero chooses his life and does it well. Hedonism is endorsed through sex as reward. Might makes right is never questioned. Feminist readers find the women reduced to admirers (Anime UK News).

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-2: settling in at the guild and the village massacre. Vol. 3: the best employee with admiring coworkers. Vol. 4: the royal marriage escort. Vols. 5-6: a magic class, a vampire, a company trip. Vol. 7: a girl from Roland's childhood and a return to his past (Yen Press synopses).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Roland asked for an ordinary life. What does ordinary mean to you?
2. He spares the demon lord but keeps her in a collar. Is that mercy?
3. How does the story treat the women who like Roland? Would they say the same?
4. Can someone who was trained to kill become a different person? What would it take?
5. The publisher rates this Older Teen. Do you agree? Why?
