**Premise.** Every thousand years the gods of every pantheon meet to decide humanity's fate, and this time they vote for extinction. The valkyrie Brunhilde, who secretly despises the gods, proposes Ragnarok instead: thirteen one-on-one fights between gods and chosen humans, with humanity spared if it wins seven. Each human champion (the Einherjar) fights through a valkyrie who becomes a weapon (Völundr) and dies with them if they fall. The human side is a roll call of history and legend: Lü Bu, Adam, the swordsman Sasaki Kojiro, Jack the Ripper, the sumo wrestler Raiden Tameemon, Buddha (who defects from the gods' side), Qin Shi Huang, Nikola Tesla, Leonidas, Okita Soji, Simo Häyhä and Sakata Kintoki. Their opponents include Thor, Zeus, Poseidon, Heracles, Shiva, Zerofuku and the demon Hajun, Hades, Beelzebub, Apollo, Susano'o, Loki and Odin. Each fight intercuts flashbacks that explain why the fighter fights. By the twelfth round humanity leads six wins to five (Kojiro, Jack, Buddha, Qin, Okita and Simo against Thor, Zeus, Shiva, Beelzebub and Apollo), and a conspiracy has surfaced: Odin plans to sacrifice humankind to revive the primordial gods, among them Satan (Wikipedia, character list; the running score is my own count from it, to verify).

## Life message

**Human beings have worth the gods cannot take away: ordinary and flawed people prove it by refusing to bow, fighting to the end for those they love, and earning even their enemies' respect.**

| Meta lens | How it reads the series |
|---|---|
| Evolutionary biology | A tournament of champions where strength, skill and endurance decide survival; kin loyalty drives many fighters (Adam fights for his children, Qin and Leonidas for their peoples). |
| Pragmatic | Fights turn on adaptation and cunning: Adam copies any technique, Kojiro learns from every defeat, Jack wins by deception (ch. 20-29), Tesla brings science to the arena (ch. 71-72). |
| Religious | Gods from many religions are characters, mostly drawn as proud, vain or cruel, with exceptions (Buddha, Heracles, Zerofuku redeemed). The Abrahamic God is absent; Adam and Eve's story is retold with Eve framed by the Serpent (ch. 10); Jesus appears with Confucius and Socrates as one of "Four Sages" cheering Buddha (manga only); Satan is a primordial god sealed away (Wikipedia). Plugged In: Christians will not accept its theology. Hindu groups objected to Shiva's portrayal (Wikipedia). |
| Socio-cultural | A Japanese seinen battle manga built on world mythology and historical celebrity, in the tradition of death-tournament stories; the human heroes are mostly famous men from Japan, China and Europe. |

Outlook: Bittersweet — humanity leads and its dignity is vindicated, but most fighters on both sides die and, with them, their valkyries; the tournament and Odin's plot are unresolved.
Hope source: P — the way through is protecting others: Adam fights for his descendants, Qin for his people, and each human champion fights for those behind them; humanity's own courage (E) is the close second.

**Where traditions disagree.** Every frame welcomes the courage and the love for family. The divide is the gods. Buddhist and Shinto readers see their figures treated with more respect than most, but still as fighters.

## Creator and publisher context

- Shinya Umemura and Takumi Fukui write the story; Azychika draws it (Wikipedia; AniList). Sabukaru describes Azychika as a group of artists rather than one person (to verify). Umemura also wrote Chiruran, a Shinsengumi manga, which may explain Okita Soji's prominence (my own reading).
- On the anime's announcement Umemura thanked readers for their support (Coamix, 2020). No creator statement on the religious content or the gods' portrayal was found. **Status: Needs more info on the creators.**
- The anime's director Masao Ōkubo says the story has an emotional side that goes beyond the fighting and that viewers root for the imperfect humans (ANN interview, 2023).
- Publisher: Coamix (Monthly Comic Zenon), formerly with Tokuma Shoten. VIZ Media publishes it under VIZ Signature, rated T+.
- Controversy: in October 2020 Rajan Zed of the Universal Society of Hinduism urged Coamix not to trivialize Shiva and other Hindu gods; after complaints from Indian Americans, Netflix cut Shiva from the trailer and removed the anime in India (Wikipedia).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Does humanity deserve to exist, and who has the right to decide? The gods have judged seven million years of human history and found it wanting. Brunhilde's answer is to make the gods look humanity in the face, one champion at a time.

**The answer the story gives.** Humanity's worth is shown, not argued: in Adam's love for his children (ch. 7-12), Kojiro's humility (ch. 13-19), Raiden's gratitude (ch. 41 is titled "Thank You"), Qin's care for his people and Buddha's freedom. Even in defeat, humans earn the gods' respect. The story's answer is a humanist one: worth belongs to people as they are, flaws and all.

**Characters as arguments.** Zeus argues for divine judgment and is answered by Adam, who copies the gods' own moves because he was made in their image. Poseidon argues for contempt and is cut down by a man who never won a duel in life. Jack the Ripper is the hard case: a murderer as humanity's champion, defeating Heracles, a god who loved humans (ch. 20-30). Buddha argues that one should choose one's side freely and heal enemies rather than hate them (ch. 42-53). Beelzebub, cursed to destroy whatever he loves, argues that some gods suffer too (ch. 67-75). Odin's secret plan turns the tournament into a cover for something worse.

**Symbols and motifs.** Valkyries becoming weapons (bonds that cost a life); the forbidden fruit eaten out of love; the arena crowd of history's famous dead; the score, human versus god, kept like a sports league.

**Traditions it echoes.** The death-tournament manga, the Norse Ragnarok and the Greek Gigantomachy, Promethean defiance of the gods, and the Japanese habit of using deities as characters. For faith readers it raises the question of where human worth comes from: from a Creator, as Genesis teaches, or from human courage alone.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Secular humanism and virtue ethics are celebrated. Pagan and Buddhist material is the setting, with Buddha the most sympathetic religious figure. Christian material is reworked rather than attacked, but the God of the Bible is absent and Jesus is reduced to a sage. Hindu material became a real-world controversy.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-3 (ch. 1-12): the setup, Lü Bu and Adam, two human defeats. Volumes 4-8 (ch. 13-30): Kojiro and Jack win. Volumes 8-13 (ch. 31-53): Raiden and Shiva, then Buddha's defection. Volumes 14-19 (ch. 54-75): Qin Shi Huang, then Tesla and Beelzebub, and the reveal of Odin's plan. Volumes 19 onward (ch. 76-): Leonidas, Okita, Simo Häyhä and Kintoki (chapter ranges after ch. 82 not in sources opened).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Brunhilde says humanity deserves another chance. What would you show the gods to prove it?
2. Jack the Ripper wins for humanity. Should he have been chosen? What does that say about the story?
3. Adam eats the fruit so he can follow Eve out of the garden. How is that different from Genesis 3?
4. Buddha switches sides. Was that loyalty, disloyalty, or something else?
5. How would you feel if a god from your own faith appeared as a fighter in a manga?
