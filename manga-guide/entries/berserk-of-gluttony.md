**Premise.** In a kingdom where everyone is born with a skill and rank follows skill, Fate Graphite is a despised gatekeeper for the noble Vlerick family, whose holy-knight children beat and cheat him. His skill, Gluttony, seems useless and leaves him always starving, until he kills a thief and finds that Gluttony devours a victim's soul, stats and skills (ch. 1-2). A talking cursed sword, Greed, becomes his partner. Gluttony now must be fed souls regularly or it will drive him berserk, so Fate hunts monsters at night in a skull mask while serving by day in the house of Roxy Hart, the one holy knight who judged him by his work rather than his skill (ch. 4 onward). He rescues a kidnapped orphan (ch. 3), takes revenge on his tormentor Hado Vlerick by killing him (ch. 10-11), and follows Roxy, sent on a likely-fatal mission, to the frontier of Galia (ch. 11-30), where he meets other bearers of the seven deadly-sin skills: Myne (Wrath), thousands of years old, and Eris (Lust), later revealed as the queen. After defeating a corrupt knight possessed by Envy and a dragon to save Roxy, at the cost of his arm and almost his mind (ch. 26-30), Fate returns to the capital, where later arcs bring Rafale Vlerick's plan to overthrow the skill hierarchy by force (v8), a body swap with Roxy (v9), the dead returning (v10), the mystery of Fate's own father (v11-12) and the ruins of ancient Galia (v13-15) (Wikipedia; fan wiki).

## Life message

**The despised can rise and protect the people they love, but power fed by killing has a terrible cost and must be held in check by love and purpose; cruelty that comes from rank is the real evil.**

Outlook: Bittersweet — the outcast rises and saves the woman he serves, and the cruelest nobles fall, but every gain comes through killing and Fate is nearly consumed by his own power; the series is ongoing.
Hope source: P — protecting others is the way through: Fate kills to survive, but what keeps him human is his resolve to protect Roxy and the forsaken (ch. 26-30); effort and growing stronger (E) is close behind.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What happens when a person who has been starved of everything is suddenly given a power that grows by killing? Fate has been beaten, cheated and despised because his inborn skill looked useless. Gluttony answers his hunger, but each soul it eats makes it hungrier.

**The answer the story gives.** Power is neither good nor evil in itself; what matters is what it serves. Fate's revenge on Hado (ch. 10-11) is shown as earned, but the story keeps the cost in view: the red eye, the self-loathing after a kill in front of Roxy (ch. 25), the dream of drowning in his victims' blood, and finally his choice to give up his power rather than lose himself (ch. 30). What keeps him human is love and the will to protect.

**Characters as arguments.** The Vlerick siblings argue that birth gives the right to rule and to abuse; they are destroyed or disgraced. Roxy argues that effort and character matter more than skill; she is the story's model of good authority. Rafale argues that the hierarchy must be torn down by force; he goes mad (v8-10). Eris argues for sacrificing one innocent to save the many; Fate refuses and she relents. Myne, cursed never to die, shows where endless power and grief can lead.

**Symbols and motifs.** Hunger and the red eye; the skull mask; the talking sword Greed and his growing forms; the seven sin-weapons; the Philosopher's Stone; the 'Domain of E' beyond human limits.

**Traditions it echoes.** The revenge and rise-of-the-underdog fantasy, Japanese web-novel level-up stories, and the medieval list of seven deadly sins (used as a power system, not a moral teaching). Its picture of a craving that can never be filled echoes, for some readers, religious teaching on the passions or on tanha.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Might-makes-right is the world's rule and the hero's means, but it is not simply endorsed: the strongest characters are the most at risk of losing themselves. The anti-caste critique is plain (ANN), though its answer is one strong man rather than new rules. Bushido-style loyalty and a knight's duty to protect the weak are the moral anchors.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-3 (ch. 1-15) are abuse, awakening and revenge. Volumes 4-6 (ch. 16-30), adapted in the anime, are the frontier: Myne, Eris, Babylon, Envy and the dragon. Volumes 7-10 (ch. 31-53) return to the capital: Rafale's revolt, the body swap, the dead returning. Volumes 11-15 (ch. 54-83) turn to the mystery of Fate's father, holy beasts and ancient Galia.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Fate's power grows when he kills. What keeps him from becoming a monster?
2. Was Fate right to kill Hado? Would the story be different if he had let him live?
3. Roxy judges people by their work, not their skill. Why is she the exception among the nobles?
4. Eris wants to sacrifice one person to save many. Why does the story reject her plan?
5. Is Fate fighting the caste system, or just climbing to the top of it?
