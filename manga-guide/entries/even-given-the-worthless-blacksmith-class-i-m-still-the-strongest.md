**Premise.** Once a year, at a church ceremony, God gives every 15-year-old a divine weapon and, if lucky, a job (class); a good pair can make one rich (K Manga; web novel ch. 1). Lerius (also spelled Relius), whose parents were killed by monsters five years earlier, has been raised by the family of his childhood friend Rin, who run an inn (web novel ch. 1). Rin receives the legendary sword Excalibur and the job of Hero; Lerius receives the Create Hammer and the Blacksmith job, thought the most useless because divine weapons outclass anything people can forge. He gives up adventuring and works at the inn, then discovers that his hammer can appraise anything it breaks and recreate it endlessly, and that he can add skills to what he makes (K Manga; Magazine Pocket). Making what he likes, he becomes connected with nobles and young women: a merchant who sells his goods, a nun-turned-swordswoman for whom he forges a sword, and a baby dragon companion, Val (web novel ch. 26, 58-60). In the web novel a kindly bishop tells him that blacksmiths are scorned because one once killed the heroes of old, and that the Pope later reshaped that history (ch. 60); the novel's last chapter has Lerius on another continent searching for Rin (ch. 112). The manga's coverage and ending are not confirmed.

## Life message

**A gift everyone calls worthless can turn out to be the greatest; honest, patient work for the people around you, not the rank you were handed, decides a person's worth.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Who decides what a person is worth: the rank handed out at a ceremony, or what the person does with it? Lerius is told by God's own oracle that he has the least useful job in the world.

**The answer the story gives.** Quiet, steady work. He keeps his job at the inn, tests his hammer, learns its rules, and makes things that fit the people who use them (web novel ch. 58-60). The "worthless" gift turns out to be the most powerful because it serves everyone else's gifts.

**Characters as arguments.** Lerius argues for humility and diligence. Rin, given the greatest gift, carries its burden; the novel ends with Lerius searching for her (ch. 112). The bishop argues that faith should protect the scorned and tell the truth even about its own leaders (ch. 60). The nun-swordswoman, assigned to church work by her own gift, argues that people should not be locked into roles (ch. 60).

**Symbols and motifs.** The hammer that destroys in order to understand and remake. The inn as home. The church's oracle stone. Excalibur beside a blacksmith's hammer.

**Traditions it echoes.** The underdog-craftsman tale (Wayland the Smith; the despised younger son), the Biblical theme of the stone the builders rejected, Luther's teaching on vocation and Sikh kirat karni; in Japanese terms, monozukuri, pride in making things (from general knowledge; to verify).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Pragmatism and virtue win; community (foster family, friends) is the reason to work. Fixed tradition is gently critiqued, and the church is shown as mixed: kind people inside, a slanted history at the top.

**Shifts across the series.** In the web novel: the ceremony and the discovery (ch. 1-5), skill-testing and first clients (ch. 6-30), a dungeon and new friends (ch. 31-57), the bishop's revelation (ch. 58-60), promotion exams (ch. 64-71), and a journey to another continent to find Rin (to ch. 112). The manga's 163 chapters likely follow this order; their ending is unconfirmed.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Lerius was told his job was the worst. How did he find out it was not?
2. Why does it matter that he makes each sword to fit its owner?
3. The bishop says the old story about blacksmiths was changed. Why might leaders change history?
4. Is it better to be given a great gift, like Rin, or to discover a hidden one?
5. What do you think Lerius owes the family that took him in?
