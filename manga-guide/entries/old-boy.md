**Premise.** One drunken night, 25-year-old Shinichi Goto, a salaryman about to marry, is abducted and locked in a small room on a hidden "Floor 7.5" of a Tokyo building, with a bed, a bathroom, a television and the same Chinese food every day. No one tells him who did it or why. For ten years he keeps his sanity by training his body and mind, even learning to box by watching television (Wikipedia; Anime UK News). Then, without explanation, he is drugged, stuffed in a trunk and dumped in a park. Calling himself Yamashita, he works construction, meets Eri, a young woman in a bar who takes him home (v1), and tracks the gyoza back to the private prison, which jails people for money (v2). His captor contacts him by phone and frames everything as a game (v3): Takaaki Kakinuma, a wealthy man who was his sixth-grade classmate (v4-6). The terms: if Goto can work out why Kakinuma hates him, Kakinuma will die; if not, Goto and Eri will (Dark Horse, v8). With his old primary-school teacher, now a crime novelist, Goto digs into memories that turn out to have been blocked by hypnosis, and learns that Eri, too, was hypnotized (v7). In the end he finds the reason, Kakinuma keeps the bargain and kills himself, and Goto is left with Eri and the fear that the game is not over (v8; TV Tropes).

## Life message

**Hatred nursed from a small wound can imprison the hater more than the hated: a man robbed of ten years keeps his humanity by refusing to kill and insisting on the truth, while the man who could not bear being pitied destroys himself.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What could make one person spend a fortune to lock another away for ten years, and what does a man owe himself when he walks free? Goto wants revenge, but more than that he wants a reason.

**The answer the story gives.** The reason is small and terrible: as children, Goto pitied Kakinuma, the only one who did, and Kakinuma could not forgive being pitied (TV Tropes; Wikipedia). The story's answer is that resentment, fed by pride, can grow into a prison that holds the hater too. Goto escapes it by refusing to become a killer (v2) and by seeking the truth; Kakinuma, who built his life around the grudge, ends it when the game is lost (v8).

**Characters as arguments.** Goto argues for endurance and restraint: he trains in his cell, throws away the gun and keeps asking why. Kakinuma argues that money and cunning can buy control over another's life, and that pride must be avenged. Eri, hypnotized into her role, becomes a person with her own resolve (v6). Kyoko Kurata, the former teacher turned crime writer, argues for imagination and reason as weapons (v7).

**Symbols and motifs.** Floor 7.5, a floor that should not exist. The television, Goto's only window for ten years. The gyoza, a taste that leads home to the prison. A song Goto will not sing (v7). Hypnosis as a hidden lock inside the mind, which the epilogue suggests may still be there.

**Traditions it echoes.** The locked-room revenge tale (The Count of Monte Cristo, from general knowledge), film noir and the Japanese hard-boiled detective story. Christian readers may hear Romans 12:19 and Hebrews 12:15 on bitterness; Jewish readers Leviticus 19:18 on grudges; Buddhist readers the Dhammapada's teaching that hatred is not ended by hatred.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Existentialism and Stoicism carry Goto; the revenge ethic is examined and found wanting; honor-to-the-death appears in Kakinuma's end, but as the fruit of pride. Hypnosis is a dark mirror of the mind's freedom. Women's agency is the weak point critics name.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-2: imprisonment, release and the search for the prison (the most action). Volumes 3-4: the phone game, the boxing match, Eri's growing role, the captor's identity. Volumes 5-6: Goto broken by a long, cruel conversation, then rebuilt by Eri and Kusama. Volumes 7-8: memory, hypnosis, the song and the final reckoning.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Goto had a gun and threw it away. Why, and what would have changed if he had kept it?
2. Why could Kakinuma not forgive being pitied?
3. Can a small childhood moment really shape a whole life? Do you remember one?
4. Eri's feelings began under hypnosis. Can a relationship that started that way become real?
5. The manga is very different from the famous film. Why might a filmmaker change so much?
