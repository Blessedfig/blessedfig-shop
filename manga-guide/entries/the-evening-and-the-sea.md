**Premise.** Tsukiko Yano, a withdrawn transfer student (half Japanese, half European, per Okazu's French-edition reviewer), finds nothing at her new school worth caring about and prefers things she would not mind leaving behind (MangaPlaza synopsis). One evening she sees her blunt, swim-loving classmate Aya Utsumi swimming in the school pool and afterward imagines fish swimming all around her (MangaUpdates); the sea imagery ties back to a childhood aquarium trip with her father (Okazu, vol. 1). A rainy day brings them together, and each is drawn to what the other is not: Aya to Tsukiko's cool distance, Tsukiko to Aya's openness (Okazu, vol. 1). Chapters rotate viewpoints, including the theater club's Maihara (vol. 1). In volume 2, their second year, Aya's words have moved the relationship forward but neither names what it is; classmate Hanano, who hides a crush on a teacher behind flirting, and actresses Maihara and Shinonome have arcs of their own, and Tsukiko dreads the end of school (Okazu, vol. 2). In volume 3 the two are about to start different universities; Aya resolves to face her fear of swimming in the ocean, and Tsukiko, understanding why she is drawn to Aya, gently ends things so Aya can move on. An epilogue with a comic double twist and a bonus chapter years later show both grown up and together again in a new setting (Okazu, vol. 3; dtf.ru).

## Creator and publisher context

- Goumoto (story and art) became a mangaka after leaving a previous job when an editor approached her; she says she works often in girls' love, grew up near the coast, finds the sea both familiar and threatening, and drew on picture books of sea creatures; some scenes were partly inspired by Dracula and The Little Mermaid; she cites Bonobono as a strong influence (Manga-Passion interview for the KAZÉ edition).
- On intent: she chose contrasting motifs to show the two leads' mutual misunderstanding in a positive light, and says sea scenes are "better suited than words to express feelings" (Manga-Passion, translated). She did not label the story as romance or friendship in the interview. **Status: Needs more info on the creator.**
- Publisher: Houbunsha (web serialization). English: MangaPlaza, digital only, rated 13+. KAZÉ's German edition was its first girls'-love title (Manga-Passion).
- No controversies involving the creator were found.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can someone who keeps the world at arm's length learn to care, and what does caring then require? Tsukiko prefers things she would not mind losing (MangaPlaza). Seeing Aya swim makes the world fill with fish for her, and she cannot look away.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, slowly, and caring may mean letting go. The girls misunderstand each other again and again (Manga-Passion), learn each other's space (Okazu, vol. 2), and at the threshold of adulthood Tsukiko releases Aya so she can grow (Okazu, vol. 3). The reunion years later suggests the bond was real.

**Characters as arguments.** Tsukiko argues for detachment and learns its limits. Aya argues for directness and learns patience. Hanano shows how flirting can hide what one really feels (vol. 2). Maihara and Shinonome show two opposite fears of being seen (vol. 2).

**Symbols and motifs.** The school pool (adolescence) and the ocean (adult life), as Okazu's reviewer reads them (vol. 2). Fish that fill the air when Tsukiko looks at Aya. Evening light. An aquarium remembered from childhood.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Japanese "Class S" tradition of intense girls' friendships (Okazu, vol. 1); The Little Mermaid, which the creator names as a partial inspiration (Manga-Passion); and, for some readers, teaching on non-attachment and on love that seeks the other's good.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism is gentle: each girl is accepted as she is. Existentialism is answered with connection. The question of labels is left open, which Agnostic and progressive readers will welcome and others may want to discuss.

**Shifts across the series.** Volume 1: meeting, the pool and the first steps. Volume 2: second year, side characters, the ocean trip. Volume 3: three years on, the parting and the epilogue (Okazu).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why did Tsukiko prefer things she could leave behind?
2. What did Aya see in Tsukiko, and what did Tsukiko see in Aya?
3. Was ending things at graduation kind, sad or both?
4. Hanano hides her real feelings behind flirting. Why do people do that?
5. Does a close friendship need a name? Talk about what your family believes about friendship and romance.
