**Premise.** Hayato Kasukabe, 18, was orphaned at three and raised by his grandmother Sachiko, who ran Café Terrace Familia in the seaside town of Miura. After a quarrel he left for a Tokyo high school; on passing the University of Tokyo exams he learns she has died and returns intending to close the café. He finds five young women living there whom she took in as family and who worked for her: Ouka (19, fashion student), Ami (17, high school karate member and prankster), Riho (20, former child actress), Shiragiku (20, chef's daughter and his childhood friend) and Akane (19, heiress and rock singer). Moved by their bond with his grandmother, he reopens the café with them. Arcs follow the women's family troubles and dreams, a debt collector, Shigehisa Fuwa, who holds a grudge against the Kasukabe family and opens a copycat café, trips (a hot-spring stay, vol. 7), and a growing contest for Hayato's affection. Akane proposes (vol. 8), and in the end Hayato marries her (Wikipedia; Japan Powered).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What makes a family? Hayato has no living relatives and a plan for an elite future. His grandmother left him a failing café and five young women who call her family.

**The answer the story gives.** Family is the people who keep faith with each other. Hayato stays, the café survives, and each woman's broken family story is answered by the one they share. The ending in marriage folds the romance into that household (Wikipedia; Japan Powered).

**Characters as arguments.** Hayato argues for practical self-reliance, then learns humility and love. Akane argues for following your own calling against family expectations. Riho, hurt by her parents' divorce, argues for being seen for herself. Ami hides old pain behind pranks (vol. 7). Fuwa, the debt collector with a grudge, is what a family feud becomes when it is never healed.

**Symbols and motifs.** The café itself; Hayato's resemblance to his grandfather, the founder; the grandmother's recipes; the copycat café as a hollow imitation of a home.

**Traditions it echoes.** The harem rom-com (Seo's earlier Fuuka; Love Hina-style shared houses), the family-business drama, and stories of chosen family.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarian found family is the moral center; tradition is honored through the grandparents; hedonistic fan service runs beside it as comedy and is never questioned. Critics differ on whether the two fit together (ANN's Dupree versus Eisenbeis and Kennedy).

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1-5 (ch. 1-43): reopening the café and each woman's introduction (anime season 1). Vol. 5-10 (ch. 44-89): the copycat café and Fuwa, the hot-spring trip, Akane's proposal (anime season 2). Vol. 11-22 (ch. 90-217): the café's first anniversary, Ami and another member starting university, a widening harem, and the ending (Manga-News; Wikipedia). Later arcs are not summarized in sources opened.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Hayato planned to sell the café. What changed his mind?
2. The five women call Sachiko family though they are not related. What makes someone family?
3. Hayato regrets his last argument with his grandmother. Is there someone you would want to make peace with?
4. One reviewer says a seduction scene would be called assault if the roles were reversed. Do you agree? Why do stories treat it as a joke?
5. Would you give up a planned future to keep a family business going?
