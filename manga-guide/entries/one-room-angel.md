**Premise.** Kouki is a convenience-store clerk in his thirties with no hobbies, no friends and no will to live; his looks and a criminal record keep him from better work. One night unruly young customers stab him, and as he passes out he sees a winged angel fall from the sky. He recovers to find the angel in his one-room apartment, with no memory and unable to fly; the angel says his wings heal only from Kouki's good feelings, and moves in. Kouki loses his job, asks his estranged mother Arisa for money, and admits that his past criminal work dragged his younger brother Tomoki into the yakuza. The angel learns that he died the same night Kouki was stabbed, by falling from his home above the store, and that only Kouki can see him. Together they open his old social media and learn he was Takashi Takashina, a student bullied after a popular classmate, "Boy A," made sexual advances toward him on a school trip, then died in an accidental fall for which Takashi was blamed; Takashi later died by suicide. Having found closure, the angel tells Kouki he loves him. They move to a snowy town where Arisa helps them; one winter day the angel says he can fly and vanishes. Kouki grieves, finds a feather, takes a job with a moving company, reconciles with Tomoki, and lives on; a short epilogue reunites him with the angel after his own death (Wikipedia; ciatr).

## Life message

**Even a life that seems empty is worth living: being loved and cared for, even briefly, can heal shame and grief and give a person the strength to go on.**

| Evolutionary biology | Two outcasts form a pair bond that restores the will to live; Kouki returns to kin (his mother, his brother) and steady work. The angel's need for Kouki's good feelings is a picture of how social animals heal through attachment. |
| Pragmatic | The angel nags Kouki into job-hunting and fixing his life; the apartment gets cleaner as he improves (the drama director's note, Wikipedia). Concrete steps (a job, asking for help, making amends) carry the healing. |

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Is a life worth living when it has nothing in it? Kouki has no hobbies, friends, lover or future, and when a knife comes he is almost relieved. The book asks what could make such a man want to wake up tomorrow.

**The answer the story gives.** Being needed, and being loved. The angel cannot fly until Kouki feels good, so Kouki must care about something; caring about the angel leads him to a job, to his mother and to his brother. When the angel leaves, the feather tells Kouki that the love was real, and he goes on living (Wikipedia).

**Characters as arguments.** Kouki argues that shame and a bad past need not be the end; he makes amends. The angel argues that even a person destroyed by others can give love; he is also a picture of what bullying costs. Boy A and the online crowd stand for the harm done by cruelty and blame. Arisa and Tomoki show that families, too, can be repaired.

**Symbols and motifs.** Wings that heal only through another's happiness; the one-room apartment growing cleaner; the feather left behind; snow and the winter flight; the night of the stabbing and the fall, joined as one moment of two deaths averted and fulfilled.

**Traditions it echoes.** The guardian-angel and "it's a wonderful life" story of a despairing man shown his worth; the Japanese idea of a spirit that lingers until its attachment is resolved; and the BL tradition of tragic love between outsiders. Some Christian readers will see grace in the gift of the angel, while noticing that the angel is a dead boy rather than a messenger of God.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Existentialism is answered warmly: meaning is chosen, and found in love. Secular humanism is the working frame: work, family and human care heal. A gentle, undogmatic afterlife (feel-good faith) closes the book. Critical readers will note the stigma Kouki faces and the online mob that hounded Takashi.

**Shifts across the series.** Early chapters are comic cohabitation (the angel's crass mouth, job-hunting); the middle turns to Kouki's family and past; the final chapters reveal Takashi's story, the confession and the parting; the extra and the epilogue close with Kouki's new life and the reunion (Wikipedia; ciatr).

**Discussion questions.**
1. What made Kouki want to live again: the angel, the work, or his family?
2. Takashi was blamed for something he did not do. How did the blame hurt him, and what could classmates have done?
3. The angel could only heal when Kouki felt good. What does that say about how people affect each other?
4. The story gives Takashi peace after death. How does your family think about suicide and about what happens after we die?
5. Some readers are uneasy that the romance is between a grown man and a teenage boy's spirit. What do you think?
