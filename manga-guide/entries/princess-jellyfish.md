**Premise.** Tsukimi Kurashita, 18, moved to Tokyo from Kagoshima hoping to become an illustrator. She loves jellyfish because her late mother took her to see them and compared their tendrils to a princess's dress, and promised to make her a wedding dress like a jellyfish. She lives in Amamizukan, an old boarding house where only otaku women live, no men are allowed, and the residents (who call themselves the "Sisterhood") avoid stylish people. Rescuing a jellyfish at a pet shop, she meets a glamorous woman who turns out to be Kuranosuke Koibuchi, the son of a powerful politician and his former mistress; he dresses as a woman to escape his family's political expectations and to feel close to the mother he was separated from (Wikipedia; Japanese Wikipedia). Kuranosuke makes over Tsukimi, and his shy older half-brother Shū falls for her. When a developer, Shōko Inari, moves to demolish Amamizukan for a hotel, Kuranosuke pushes the residents to turn Tsukimi's jellyfish dress designs into a fashion brand, "Jellyfish," to buy the building (Wikipedia; Manga Bookshelf). Inari drugs Shū, makes him believe they slept together and blackmails him (likely Japanese vol. 4); later she stages a suicide attempt and he slaps her (Japanese vols. 5-6; The Fandom Post; Japanese Wikipedia). In the later volumes Tsukimi goes to Singapore to design for a fashion executive, Kai, to save Amamizukan, and Kuranosuke goes after her (omnibus 7, Japanese vols. 13-14; Kodansha USA; TV Tropes). The brand settles on clothes "for Amars, by Amars" (omnibus 9). Tsukimi turns down Shū's proposal to focus on the brand; both brothers stay in love with her and agree to live alongside her and the Sisterhood, leaving the romance open (TV Tropes). The final chapter (ch. 84) reveals the never-seen resident Mejiro, a boys' love manga artist, to be a man who loves Chieko and is the reason men were banned (Wikipedia; Japanese Wikipedia).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Who counts as beautiful, and who gets to decide? Tsukimi and her housemates have opted out of a world that judges them by looks; a glamorous stranger drags them back in.

**The answer the story gives.** Everyone has a beauty of their own, and the way to defend it is to make something. Kuranosuke's makeovers show Tsukimi another side of herself, but the real change is that her jellyfish drawings become dresses, and then a brand made for people like her (omnibus 9). The house is saved by work, not by rescue.

**Characters as arguments.** Tsukimi argues for devotion to what you love, however odd. Kuranosuke argues for self-expression against family duty. Shū argues for propriety and duty, and is undone by his own honesty when Inari exploits it. Inari argues that looks and sex are tools for getting ahead; her scheme fails and she is left with feelings she cannot use. The Sisterhood argues that you need no one's approval, then learns that working with others is not the same as giving in.

**Symbols and motifs.** Jellyfish, drifting and lovely, as a picture of the residents; the princess dress promised by a dead mother; makeovers as masks and as revelations; the old house against glass towers.

**Traditions it echoes.** Cinderella turned inside out (the narrator calls it a 21st-century princess story, TV Tropes), the found-family comedy, and Japanese josei manga about women's careers. Christian readers may hear 1 Samuel 16:7 on outward appearance; Buddhist readers, non-attachment to looks.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism and communitarianism are both affirmed and kept in balance. Feminism is affirmed in its respect for women's interests and work, though critics note the one sexually active woman is the villain. Consumerism is examined: fashion is a tool, but the brand chooses its own people over prestige.

**Shifts across the series.** Japanese vols. 1-4 (the anime) are the meeting, the makeovers and the first fight against redevelopment, including Inari's scheme. Vols. 5-12 build the Jellyfish brand. Vols. 13-14 take Tsukimi to Singapore. Vols. 15-17 bring her home, settle the brand and close on an open romance and the Mejiro reveal (ch. 84).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why do the Sisterhood fear stylish people, and what changes their minds?
2. Kuranosuke dresses as a woman for reasons tied to his family. How do different families you know think about that?
3. Inari uses deceit to get what she wants. What does the story say about using people?
4. Tsukimi turns down a marriage proposal to focus on her work. Was that wise?
5. Is there something you love that others think is strange? How do you share it?
