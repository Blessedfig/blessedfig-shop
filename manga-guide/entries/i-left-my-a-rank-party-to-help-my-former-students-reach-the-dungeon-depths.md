**Premise.** Yuke Feldio, a 20-year-old Red Mage (support magic and alchemy), quits Thunder Pike, the A-rank party of his childhood friend Simon, after five years of being belittled as a handyman. He is quickly recruited by three of his former students: Marina (sword mage), Silk Amberwood (a dark-elf ranger) and Rain (priest and mage). They form the party Clover, livestream their dungeon runs through a flying camera artifact and rise fast in rank and popularity, later adding Nene (a cat-girl scout serving a sentence) and Jamie (the one decent member of Thunder Pike) (Wikipedia). Thunder Pike declines without Yuke. Simon tries to steal Clover's rewards on a live broadcast that also airs his party's slurs about dark elves; then Thunder Pike ambushes Clover and tries to force Yuke to sign a slave contract by threatening the girls. Both parties fall into an orc den; Jamie betrays her teammates to save Clover; Barry and Camilla die at the orcs' hands; Simon becomes undead and attacks again, and Yuke ends him (the manga's version differs from the anime's eternal-punishment ending). A noble, Besio, who wanted Rain for himself and plotted with Simon to enslave the girls, is arrested (Wikipedia; ep. 7-8 summaries; MyAnimeList thread). To shield Rain from a forced political marriage, Yuke marries her on paper; a mistranslation in a foreign land later declares all the girls his wives, and by the latest arcs Wikipedia lists five wives. In the dungeon city of Duna, Clover uncovers an otherworldly capital destroyed by a curse, Yuke learns he is the fabled Hero and is named Dungeon Count, and the party is sent abroad by the king (Wikipedia).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What is the worth of someone whose work makes others shine? And what happens when people who took him for granted lose him?

**The answer the story gives.** His worth is real and is revealed once he works with people who see it: Clover rises and Thunder Pike collapses (Wikipedia). Loyalty is rewarded and arrogance destroyed. The story also gives Yuke the whole party as wives, which is its picture of reward.

**Characters as arguments.** Yuke argues for quiet competence and preparation. Simon argues that strength and fame make a leader; Barry that might entitles him to take; Camilla that her church upbringing makes her better than others; Besio that rank entitles him to women. Jamie argues that people who went along with wrong can repent.

**Symbols and motifs.** The flying camera (fame and accountability: Simon's crimes are caught on stream). The slave collar and contract (control over others, broken by Yuke). Persephone's mark (power bought from death). Clover's leaves: each new member adds a leaf.

**Traditions it echoes.** The narou "kicked out of the party" revenge-and-rise genre, the harem fantasy, Greek myth (Persephone as a ruler of the dead) and the chosen-Hero prophecy.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Virtue and community are affirmed; might-makes-right is condemned in the villains; occult power is a tool; consumer fame is a neutral backdrop; traditional hierarchy is the world's settled order.

**Shifts across the series.** The first arc (to about vol. 7) is the rivalry with Thunder Pike; the Duna arc turns to a cursed otherworldly city and Yuke's destiny; the latest arc sends Clover abroad on the king's orders (Wikipedia; Kodansha vol. 7).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Yuke stayed five years in a party that mocked him. Why do people stay, and what finally helps them leave?
2. Jamie went along with Simon for a long time. Does her later sacrifice make up for it?
3. The villains threaten the women to control Yuke. What does the story say about the women's own choices?
4. Is the way the villains die justice or revenge?
5. What would you want a reward for your hard work to look like?
