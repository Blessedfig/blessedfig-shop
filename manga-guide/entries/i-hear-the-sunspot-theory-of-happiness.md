**Premise.** After the kiss that ended the first book, college students Taichi Sagawa and Kohei Sugihara, who has a severe hearing loss, are barely speaking. Maya Okami, a new student who is also hard of hearing, sides with Kohei and tries to keep Taichi away (ch. 1-2). Taichi, drunk after a night out with coworkers, is taken home by Kohei, and the kiss comes up awkwardly (ch. 2). Maya's uncle Sai, who founded Sign, a company that teaches sign language, sees Taichi's way with people and offers him a job (ch. 3); Taichi decides to leave college for it, and his grandfather, who raised him, agrees as long as he is not choosing out of loneliness (ch. 4). Kohei tells Maya he loves Taichi (ch. 4) but hides his hurt so Taichi can go. Taichi defends Maya from a high-school acquaintance who belittles her, and she goes home in tears (ch. 5). Six months later, after a rumor that Kohei has a girlfriend, Taichi runs into him on an errand, finally understands the hand sign Kohei once made to him ("love"), and the two confess to each other (ch. 6, series ch. 11). The extra shows their high-school years and a past meeting (fan wiki; ANN; The OuterHaven).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What does happiness require: being useful, being normal, or being understood? The title promises a theory, and the volume tests three: Kohei's (keep the people you love happy by hiding what hurts), Maya's (never let anyone see your weakness), and Taichi's (say what you think and do what you are good at).

**The answer the story gives.** Happiness comes from honest connection. Kohei's self-sacrifice leaves him alone for six months; Maya's pride leaves her in tears; Taichi's frankness lands him a job but cannot reach Kohei until he understands Kohei's language, the sign for "love" (ch. 6). ANN sees the volume's resolution in Kohei coming to see his own value.

**Characters as arguments.** Taichi argues that feeling different is lonely and that care can end it (ANN). Kohei argues for quiet sacrifice, and the story gently corrects him. Maya argues that pity is an insult, and the story agrees with her even when it shows the cost of her pretending to hear (Percy). Sai argues that if everyone learned sign language, people would be happier (fan wiki). The grandfather argues that a big choice made out of loneliness is the wrong choice (ch. 4).

**Symbols and motifs.** The hand sign for love, made early and understood late. Notes: the bond the two shared, ended when Taichi leaves school. The company called Sign. Food brought to a doorstep (ch. 2). Sunlight, the sunny spot of the series title.

**Traditions it echoes.** The BL slow-burn romance; Japanese disability narratives in the line of A Silent Voice (from general knowledge; to verify); the coming-of-age story of choosing work. Faith traditions will hear echoes of care for the deaf (Leviticus 19:14; Surah Abasa) and of honest speech.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Progressivism is affirmed throughout: inclusion is good, and so is the romance. Secular humanism and communitarianism shape the solutions (education, a company with a mission, friends). Critical theory's critique of ableism appears through Maya, but the story avoids lecturing.

**Shifts across the series.** The first book is friendship to a first kiss; Theory of Happiness (this entry) is separation and confession; Limit (3 volumes) brings the couple's first serious troubles, consent and Deaf identity; Four Seasons (ongoing) follows graduation and work (Wikipedia; the first book's entry).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Kohei hides his hurt so Taichi can take the job. Was that kind, or was it unfair to both of them?
2. Why is Maya angry when people feel sorry for her? Why is she upset when Taichi defends her?
3. Taichi's grandfather says not to choose out of loneliness. What does that mean for a big decision in your life?
4. Sai thinks everyone knowing sign language would make people happier. Do you agree?
5. What is your own "theory of happiness"?
