**Premise.** Fumi Nishioka, 16, is a cheerful homeless girl with no memory of her past who secretly sleeps in an unused room of Kurokado High's old building and dreams of a rich Prince Charming. A door only she should not be able to see pulls her into the Void, a space linking people's minds, where a delinquent classmate, Sakaguchi, is being eaten from within by "bugs" bred by uncontrolled negative emotions. Kyutaro Horikita, a gruff second-year with a gift for cleaning, rescues her: his family are Sweepers, who enter people's "mind vaults" and cleanse them with cleaning tools. The school chairman, Kyutaro's brother-in-law, takes Fumi into the family as Kyutaro's apprentice (ch. 1-5). Kyutaro sees in her his childhood love Fuyu, who vanished ten years earlier; a student, Ren Shimizugawa, calls Fumi a "cursed girl" (ch. 6-10). In the last volume, Ataru Shikata of the Bug Handlers, who use bugs to manipulate people, turns Fumi's classmate Kaori against her and stirs bullying; Fumi cleanses Kaori and overpowers Ataru with a dormant power called the Queen, which can control minds for good or ill. She chooses to stay and keep training, and the story continues in Queen's Quality (ch. 11-15) (Wikipedia; VIZ synopses; ANN; The Fandom Post; Outer Haven; Comic Book Bin).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Where do dark feelings come from, and can they be cleaned away? In QQ Sweeper, depression and self-loathing breed bugs that infest a person's mind; left alone, they destroy the person (Outer Haven, v1).

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, with skill, courage and care. Sweepers clean the mind the way they clean a room; neglect lets bugs breed, attention keeps them away. Fumi, who has every reason for despair (homeless, nameless, called cursed), chooses a cheerful outlook and becomes a cleaner of others' hearts. In the last volume she cleanses the friend who betrayed her rather than taking revenge (ANN, v3).

**Characters as arguments.** Fumi argues for optimism as a choice. Kyutaro argues for duty and steadfast love: he has carried a lost friend in his heart for ten years. Ataru argues the opposite, that people's weakness is a tool to control them, and loses. Kaori shows how envy, stirred by a manipulator, turns a friend into a bully and then a victim.

**Symbols and motifs.** The broom and the duster. Clean rooms and dirty ones. Doors into minds. Insects as the shape of bad feelings. The Queen, a power to command others, which can be white or black (Manga Bookshelf on the sequel).

**Traditions it echoes.** Shinto purification and the Buddhist picture of defilements in the mind (my own framing); Western fairy tales (the sleeping maiden, Prince Charming, Alice's door, which reviewers note); and shojo's long habit of the orphan heroine taken into a powerful household.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarian found family and duty are affirmed. The cleansing of negative energy is explored as fantasy, not doctrine. Power used to dominate others is condemned through the Bug Handlers.

**Shifts across the series.** Volume 1 (ch. 1-5) sets up the Sweepers with comedy; volume 2 (ch. 6-10) turns to Fuyu and Fumi's cursed past; volume 3 (ch. 11-15) brings the Bug Handlers, bullying and the Queen, ending as a prologue to Queen's Quality, which reviewers call darker (Manga Bookshelf; Otaku USA).

**Discussion questions.**
1. The bugs grow from negative feelings. Which feelings, and what helps keep them away in real life?
2. Fumi has no home and no memory, yet stays cheerful. Is that strength or hiding?
3. Why does Fumi cleanse Kaori instead of getting even?
4. The Queen's power can control people. When, if ever, is it right to change someone's mind for them?
5. What makes the Horikitas a family to Fumi?
