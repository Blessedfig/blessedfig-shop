**Premise.** Ichitaka Seto, 16, a second-year at high school, has loved his classmate Iori Yoshizuki since first year but cannot say so: a sixth-grade rejection left him guarded and prone to blurting rude things. Iori, a drama-club member, has just appeared in a magazine swimsuit spread and is suddenly besieged by boys, so Ichitaka fears she will see him as one more creep. Working with her on the freshman welcome committee brings them closer, but his childhood friend Itsuki Akiba returns from America, moves into his house and makes no secret of her feelings. Itsuki later leaves to study sculpture; in their third year Ichitaka meets Izumi Isozaki, a younger girl who pursues him relentlessly, and he finally confesses to Iori on Christmas Eve. They date in secret because her talent agency forbids it. After graduation Ichitaka fails his university exams and lives alone, grows close to a neighbor who looks like Iori (Aiko Aso), and is told by Iori's agency to step aside for her career; he does. Throughout, a former senior calling himself the Marionette King tries several times to assault Iori and is stopped by Ichitaka; the last fight leaves Ichitaka hospitalized until Iori's voice on the phone brings him back, and the collected edition adds their reconciliation (Wikipedia; TV Tropes; Renote).

## Creator and publisher context

- Masakazu Katsura (story and art) was discovered by Weekly Shōnen Jump editor Kazuhiko Torishima; his romance and hero series include Video Girl Ai, DNA² and I"s, and he is a lifelong fan of tokusatsu heroes, saying the hero element comes out even in his romances as the boy risks his life for the girl (Wikipedia).
- On-record intent: Katsura said that with I"s he was asked to bring shōjo manga's first-person telling of a girl's feelings into a shōnen series (Wikipedia). He says he researches fashion and mannerisms to draw girls who appeal to both male and female readers, and includes erotic elements; he is especially known for drawing female buttocks (Wikipedia, Style).
- Jason Thompson (ANN, on Video Girl Ai) notes Katsura's repeated use of a sexual assault so that the hero can rescue the heroine, in Video Girl Ai, DNA² and I"s, and calls I"s's characters more normal and its problems less tortured than Video Girl Ai's.
- VIZ's 2005 edits (stars over bare breasts) drew complaints; VIZ said it follows the creator's wishes and makes no edits without his knowledge and approval (ANN news, 2005).
- Publisher: Shueisha (Weekly Shōnen Jump). VIZ Media published the English edition. No controversies involving the creator were found beyond the edit debate. **Status: Needs more info on the creator's statements about this series.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Why can't Ichitaka just say it? I"s is built on a single, very ordinary fear: that telling someone you love them will make you one more person who wants something from them. The series asks what that fear costs, and what it takes to get past it.

**The answer the story gives.** Honesty, slowly earned, and love that protects rather than takes. Ichitaka wins Iori not by charm but by staying faithful through rivals and separation, by putting her dream ahead of his wish, and by risking himself against the man who would hurt her. ANN's reviewer complained the characters never just talk; the story's answer is that learning to talk is the whole journey.

**Characters as arguments.** Ichitaka argues for faithfulness held back by fear. Itsuki argues for saying everything, and leaves to follow her art. Izumi argues for pursuit without limits and is played for comedy. Aiko argues for love that steps aside. Jun argues for quiet loyalty and is the story's most generous friend. The Marionette King and Kida are the dark mirror: desire with no respect for the person.

**Symbols and motifs.** The swimsuit photo that makes Iori public property; the stage, where Iori learns to show feelings she hides in life; look-alike girls whose names all start with I, testing whether Ichitaka loves Iori or a type (Wikipedia); snow on Christmas Eve; the phone call that wakes him at the end.

**Traditions it echoes.** Shōjo romance told from a boy's side (Katsura's stated aim); the Japanese rom-com of missed confessions; the knight who rescues the lady. Christian and Jewish readers may hear echoes of patient love (1 Corinthians 13; Jacob waiting for Rachel), though the series has no religious frame; Buddhist readers may see right speech.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Virtue (courage, honesty, fidelity) is affirmed. Expressive individualism is affirmed in careers and in learning to speak. Hedonism lives in the art more than in the plot. Consumer culture, in the idol industry, is the quiet villain.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-7: second year, Itsuki's return, the school trip to Kyoto (v4, Wikipedia) and the first stalker plots. Volumes 8-12: summer (beach, v8), Itsuki's departure, Izumi's arrival and the Christmas Eve confession (placements likely). Volumes 13-15: after graduation, failed exams, Aiko, the agency's demand, the last attack and the reconciliation (Renote).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Ichitaka is afraid Iori will think he is "just another creep." Why does he think that, and is he right?
2. How many problems in the story would have been solved by one honest conversation?
3. Ichitaka steps aside so Iori can pursue her career. Was that love, or giving up?
4. Iori's swimsuit photos change how people treat her. How does the story feel about that, and how does the art?
5. Jun is Ichitaka's most loyal friend. What makes a good friend in this story?
