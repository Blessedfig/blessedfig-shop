**Premise.** Thirteen-year-old Rachel "Ray" Gardner wakes on floor B7 of a strange building with no memory except her name and that she came for counseling after witnessing a murder. Each floor belongs to a killer, an "angel": Isaac "Zack" Foster, a bandaged 20-year-old serial killer with a scythe (B6); her counselor Danny Dickens, obsessed with her blank blue eyes (B5); Eddie Mason, a boy who loves digging graves (B4); Cathy Ward, a sadistic former prison guard (B3); and the reverend Abraham Gray (B2), who built the whole place as an experiment to find the meaning of religious faith. After Zack kills Danny (who has faked his death), both are marked as "sacrifices." Ray and Zack make an oath sworn to God: she will help him escape with her intelligence, and once they are out he will kill her, as she wishes (vol. 1-2). They defeat Eddie (vol. 2) and Cathy (vol. 3-5); Gray puts Ray on trial as a witch in a drug-induced vision (vol. 7-8). On B1, a copy of her house, Zack learns the truth: Ray's father killed her mother, Ray shot her father and stitched their bodies together into a "perfect family"; reading a Bible later filled her with guilt and a wish to die (game plot; vol. 9-10, to verify). Danny shoots Ray; Gray kills Danny; Zack carries Ray out as the building burns and lets himself be arrested so she can be saved. Sentenced to death, he escapes and comes for Ray at the care center, and they disappear together (Wikipedia, game plot; manga vol. 11-12 to verify).

## Life message

**Two people broken by abuse find a reason to live in a promise kept to each other; the story asks what faith is, and answers that a bond of honest loyalty saves Ray where her idea of God and Gray's religious experiment do not, though it leaves that bond dark and unresolved.**

| Pragmatic | Ray solves puzzles and traps (Cathy's poison-gas room, vol. 3); Zack keeps his word because a lie would be useless to him. The alliance works because each has what the other lacks. |
| Religious | Faith is the explicit subject: a reverend designs the building to test it, the killers are called angels, Zack and Ray swear their oath to God, Zack says God does not exist (vol. 7), Ray calls Zack her God (vol. 8), and Gray declares himself God of the building (vol. 8). | Socio-cultural | Child abuse and neglect produce killers; counseling and prison are shown as places of predation (Danny, Cathy). Anime Feminist notes that the anime implies seeking counseling is untrustworthy. |

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What is faith, and what do people put their faith in? Gray builds a building of killers to find out. Ray wants to die because she believes death will save her; Zack wants out because he believes in nothing but his own word.

**The answer the story gives.** Ray's faith moves from an idea of God that only condemns her, to a person who keeps his promises. Zack says God does not exist here (vol. 7); Gray says he is God here (vol. 8); Ray chooses Zack. The story treats that choice with sympathy, but it is not a happy resolution: she still asks him to kill her at the end.

**Characters as arguments.** Danny argues for possession dressed as care; Cathy for punishment as pleasure; Eddie for owning what you bury. Each is the dark version of something Ray wants: to be seen, to be judged, to belong. Zack argues for honesty without morality. The blind old man who took Zack in (ch. 33-34) is the only picture of grace, and he is killed for his wallet.

**Symbols and motifs.** Eyes (Danny), graves (Eddie), the electric chair and the stake (Cathy, Gray), stitching (Ray's sewn bird and sewn parents), the knife Zack leaves behind, the moon in the last chapter titles.

**Traditions it echoes.** Dante's descent in reverse (an ascent through floors of sinners), the Book of Job's question of suffering, the witch trial, and Gothic horror. Christian readers may see a story about law without gospel; secular readers a story about faith misplaced in institutions.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Christian imagery is used heavily and critically; Existentialist self-making is affirmed; Zack's unbelief is voiced without punishment; promise-keeping is the one rule that holds.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1-2: B7 to B4, the pact. Vol. 3-5: Cathy's tortures. Vol. 6-8: Gray's trial and Zack's past. Vol. 9-10: Ray's past on B1. Vol. 11-12: the escape, Zack's sentence and the final meeting (to verify against the manga).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why does Ray want Zack to kill her, and what changes for her by the end?
2. Gray says he is testing faith. Is his test fair? What does faith mean to you?
3. Zack never lies. Is honesty enough to make someone good?
4. Every killer in the building was hurt as a child. Does that excuse what they do?
5. Who in the story shows real kindness, and what happens to them?
