**Premise.** Will Serfort, a sixth-year student at the Regarden Magical Academy, cannot cast even the simplest spell in a world where magic means rank. He survives on perfect written scores, credits earned by killing monsters in the school's dungeon and extraordinary sword skill. His goal is to climb the Mercedes Caulis, the tower whose five Magia Vander maintain the barrier that has protected the world from invaders from the sky for 500 years, and to keep a childhood promise to Elfaria, who grew up with him in an orphanage and became the youngest Magia Vander (Wikipedia; fan wiki). Students and a prejudiced professor, Edward, try to drive him out (ch. 1-4). He wins over rivals at the Grand Magic Festival (ch. 5-10), kills a summoned demon in a dungeon exercise where professors die (ch. 11-20), and fights off an attack on the city by the cult-like Goetia during the Terminalia festival, in which his roommate Rosti gives himself to protect him (ch. 21-28). After graduating he is recruited into the tower's Thunder Faction (ch. 29-40). Later arcs reveal that the barrier holds back the demonic Celestial Hosts and their imprisoned king, Ba'al, and that Will's power, Wis, may exist to destroy him; a war with Goetia follows (ch. 48-61) (fan wiki; Wikipedia).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Is worth something you are born with or something you prove? Regarden ranks students by magical power, and Will has none.

**The answer the story gives.** Worth is proved by effort, courage and fidelity. Will passes every test the academy sets, wins over the bullies, and rises into the tower (ch. 1-40). Later arcs complicate the answer: Will's power, Wis, may exist precisely to destroy Ba'al (fan wiki), so the talentless boy is also a chosen one.

**Characters as arguments.** Edward argues for magic supremacy and contempt for dwarves; his humbling (ch. 2) and later act of protection (Wikipedia) answer him. Sion and Julius argue that talent justifies arrogance and are turned into rivals. Wignall, another "no-talent" student, overcomes his inferiority (ch. 15-19). Goetia argues that a scornful society deserves destruction, the dark mirror of Will, who was scorned too and chose to protect it.

**Symbols and motifs.** The wand and the sword: magic and effort, finally joined when Will absorbs spells into his blade. The tower and the sunset at its top that Will and Elfaria promised to see. The barrier, a "false sky" that protects and imprisons.

**Traditions it echoes.** The magic-school story (Harry Potter, per ANN and Anime UK News), the shōnen underdog (Black Clover's director also directed the anime) and chivalric romance. For faith readers, the despised one raised up and the friend who lays down his life are familiar patterns.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Virtue ethics and duty (the promise) are affirmed; the critique of a talent-based elite is affirmed; might-makes-right is critiqued but not fully, since Will still wins by strength. Demonology supplies only villains.

**Shifts across the series.** V1-5 (ch. 1-20): academy, festival and dungeon. V6-7 (ch. 21-28): the city attack and loss. V8-12 (ch. 29-48): life in the tower and its factions. V13-15 (ch. 49-61): war with Goetia and Ba'al. V16 on (ch. 62-69): beyond Regarden, the city of Samios.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Will cannot use magic in a world built on it. What makes him valuable anyway?
2. Will saves a bully he could have left behind. Why?
3. Professor Edward looks down on dwarves and swordsmen. Where do you see that kind of prejudice?
4. Goetia's members were also scorned. Why did they choose revenge when Will did not?
