**Premise.** Tatara Tani is a first-year high school student who is average at everything (Kodansha Japan). His childhood friends are the Hanazono twins: Ranko, a flashy gyaru, and Yuriko, a popular idol forbidden by her agency to date, whom Tatara has loved for years. When Yuriko asks him to an amusement park disguised as Ranko and the outing is exposed at school, the real Ranko protects her sister's career by pretending to be Tatara's girlfriend, and a fake relationship and love triangle begin (Kodansha USA; ANN). Later volumes have Tatara managing Ranko's school band (vol. 4), the band winning a festival contest whose prize is a hot-spring trip for two that Tatara and Ranko take (vol. 6), and a burst pipe that has Tatara living with the twins for a month, during which Ranko, coached by her friend Maricchi, makes a bold move on him (vol. 7). The final volume says the twins' romance takes a major turn (Kodansha USA; Kodansha Japan).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What happens when everyone pretends: an idol pretending to be single, a sister pretending to be a girlfriend, a boy pretending not to notice?

**The answer the story gives.** Pretending protects for a while and then gets in the way. The plot moves from disguise and fake dating (vol. 1) to honest feelings (vol. 7), and the final turn rewards the twin who acted first, per database tags (to verify).

**Characters as arguments.** Yuriko is image and duty to a career; Ranko is loyalty turned into honest desire; Tatara is an ordinary boy who has to choose; Maricchi is the friend who pushes too hard (vol. 7).

**Symbols and motifs.** Twins who can swap places; the disguise; the amusement park date; the band stage; the hot spring; a house shared after a burst pipe.

**Traditions it echoes.** Twin-swap comedies and the love-triangle rom-com; critics of the idol industry's "purity" (Silverman).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive honesty is affirmed; the consumer image of idols is quietly criticized; the art itself relies on the fan service that some critics dislike.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-3: the swap and the fake relationship. Vols. 4-6: the band, the festival and the hot-spring trip. Vol. 7: living together and the resolution, with a time-skip epilogue.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Ranko pretends to date Tatara to protect Yuriko. Was it a kind lie?
2. Should idols be told not to date? Who benefits?
3. How does the art show the girls, and does it change how you see them?
4. What would have happened if Tatara had been honest from the start?
5. How do sisters stay close when they want the same thing?
