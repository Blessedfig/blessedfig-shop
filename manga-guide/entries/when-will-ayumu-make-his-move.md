**Premise.** First-year Ayumu Tanaka, a strong former kendo player, joins his school's two-person shogi club because he fell for its president, second-year Urushi Yaotome. He vows to confess only after he beats her at shogi, but as a beginner he loses every game. Urushi suspects his crush and keeps trying to make him admit it, offering a hug or a kiss as bait (anime ep. 1). Side stories follow his childhood friends Takeru and Sakurako (who playfully "hypnotizes" Takeru), Urushi's teasing best friend Maki, and Rin Kagawa, Ayumu's kendo kōhai, who joins the club (from about ch. 70), secretly loves Ayumu, and decides to help him beat Urushi instead. After training with Rin he wins a handicap game but chooses to wait until he can win fairly (anime ep. 12, about ch. 115). On Urushi's graduation day he finally beats her, and they confess at the same moment (ch. 224); the last chapter shows them dating, with Ayumu as the new club president (ch. 225) (Wikipedia; fan wiki; anmosugoi).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can you earn the right to say what you feel? Ayumu has decided he can only confess once he is good enough to beat the girl he loves at her own game. Urushi already knows, and the comedy comes from her trying to get him to break his rule.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, by patience and practice, and the waiting is part of the love. Ayumu keeps his vow even when he wins a handicap game (anime ep. 12, about ch. 115) and confesses only after a fair win on Urushi's graduation day (ch. 224). The confession is mutual: both speak at once.

**Characters as arguments.** Ayumu argues for keeping your word, however slow. Urushi argues, at first, for winning the teasing game, and slowly learns to be honest. Rin is the self-giving rival: she loves Ayumu but chooses to help him win someone else (fan wiki). Takeru and Sakurako are the shy mirror-couple who cannot say anything at all. Maki is the cheerful audience who wants them to get on with it.

**Symbols and motifs.** The shogi pawn (Ayumu's name) that can only move forward one square. The board between them as both distance and closeness. The good-luck charm Ayumu gives Urushi before her school trip, whose loss makes her realize her feelings (anime ep. 12). The shared umbrella (ep. 1).

**Traditions it echoes.** The teasing romantic comedy Yamamoto made famous with Takagi-san, and the Japanese tradition of discipline through a craft or game. For Christian and Jewish readers, the seriousness about a vow echoes "let your yes be yes" and Numbers 30; for Buddhist readers, right effort without despair.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Virtue ethics and duty ethics are both affirmed: the story rewards a person of steady character who will not cut a corner on his promise. Traditionalism is gentle and warm. Nothing is critiqued harshly; the only friction is a critic's sense that the early jokes made a hobby of embarrassing the heroine (Anime Feminist).

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-5 (ch. 1-68): the two-person club, first dates, the culture festival and New Year. Volumes 6-9 (ch. 69-121): Rin joins, the club becomes official, Urushi's school trip and the handicap win (the anime ends here, ch. 115). Volumes 10-17 (ch. 122-225): the later years, Urushi's graduation, the fair win and the confession (details of the middle volumes not confirmed in sources opened).
