**Premise.** Tasuku, 13, a junior-high student, is in love with his neighbor Shia Hoshino, three years older, a high-school film buff who is cheerful on the surface and private underneath. Every Wednesday they watch movies together, and every Wednesday he confesses and she turns him down (Seven Seas; ANN). Over a school year he tries to become mature enough for her: he studies the films she loves, from the Marvel Cinematic Universe on (vol. 2 blurb), learns kanji to read her writing (MyAnimeList review), and slowly learns what she is hiding behind her smile. By autumn the two start to understand each other and he thinks seriously about what dating means (vol. 3 blurb). In the last volume Shia plans to leave home in a year to become a screenwriter; Tasuku wants her dream to come true and does not want to lose her, and they spend Christmas and New Year together (vol. 4 blurb). Reviewers say both realize their feelings and the ending is hopeful, with an epilogue that pays off his growing taller and a final movie outing (MyAnimeList reviews). Supporting cast include Tasuku's brother Tsumugu and classmates and friends on both sides (AniList).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Is love something you win, or something you learn? Tasuku starts out believing that if he is mature enough, Shia will have to say yes. The series asks what maturity actually is.

**The answer the story gives.** Maturity is understanding, not performance. Tasuku learns her films, her writing and finally her worries, and wants her dream to come true even if it takes her away (vol. 4 blurb).

**Characters as arguments.** Tasuku argues for honesty and persistence; ANN's reviewer finds him grating at first, which is the point of his arc. Shia argues for privacy and self-protection, and has to learn to share. The supporting cast of siblings and friends shows that both are known and loved by others.

**Symbols and motifs.** Wednesday movie night as ritual; films as a language for feelings; Spider-Man and Mary Jane (vol. 2); Tasuku's height as a measure of growing up; a final movie outing in the epilogue (MyAnimeList review).

**Traditions it echoes.** The coming-of-age first-love story, and the Japanese onee-san romance, which this series turns toward character growth rather than fan service.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism (her dream) and classical virtue (his patience) are both affirmed; consumer culture is simply the shared hobby.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1 is comedy of rejection; vol. 2 summer and the MCU; vol. 3 autumn and real understanding; vol. 4 winter, her plans to leave, and the finale (Seven Seas blurbs).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Shia keeps saying no. What does Tasuku learn from that?
2. What does 'mature' mean to Tasuku at the start, and at the end?
3. Why might Shia hide her worries behind a smile?
4. Is it good to keep trying when someone says no? When should you stop?
5. What movie would you choose to share with a friend, and why?
