**Premise.** Tenichi Iemori, 16, the eldest of three, runs his family's cooking and cleaning while his father works elsewhere, and takes a part-time house-cleaning job to save for college. On the first day of his second year, Himari Yoshino, a famous model and influencer who owns the makeup brand Hima Riz, transfers into his class; that afternoon his new client's luxury apartment turns out to be hers, and filthy. She swears him to secrecy to protect her brand and soon has him making her lunches (ch. 1-7). He has had a crush since middle school on his classmate Honoka Kanai, who secretly likes him too; Kanna Aizawa, a first-year model and gravure idol from Himari's agency, falls for him after he helps her (ch. 12-16); his energetic classmate Mayu and a former idol turned assistant teacher, Nonoka Himeno (ch. 50, 53-61), round out the cast. Over one school year Himari realizes she loves him (ch. 37-38), lets him go as her employee so as not to date someone she pays (ch. 74-79), and finally steps aside; Tenichi turns down Kanna (ch. 71, 83-84) and chooses Honoka, who kisses him (ch. 95). A five-year epilogue shows Tenichi and Honoka living together after college and Himari calling him her best friend (ch. 96) (Wikipedia volume summaries).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What is a person really like behind the image, and what makes someone worth loving? Himari is a polished influencer whose apartment is a disaster; Tenichi is an unremarkable boy whose care makes other people's lives work.

**The answer the story gives.** The hidden, everyday self matters most. Himari's worth is her hard work and honesty, not her brand; Tenichi's is his attentive care, which Himari names as his love language (ch. 93). Love is won by speaking up: Honoka's letter and confession (ch. 94-95).

**Characters as arguments.** Himari argues that a public image can coexist with a real self, if someone sees both. Honoka argues for patience and sincerity, and the story rewards her. Kanna argues for boldness, and loses gracefully. Himeno, the ex-idol teacher, is the adult who helps the teens be honest. Tenichi is the case that diligence and kindness are attractive.

**Symbols and motifs.** Cleaning: restoring order to a messy life. Keys (Himari's apartment key, returned in ch. 95-96). Makeup and the "lucky love" lipstick (ch. 21-22). Bento lunches. The photo book that makes their relationship public but professional (ch. 92). The title, which joins two names that do not end up together.

**Traditions it echoes.** Shonen Jump harem romcom (Nisekoi, We Never Learn), the "secret relationship" premise, and the childhood-friend heroine (Aftermath). Its short run, single school year and limited cast set it apart (Scrmbl).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Classical virtue ethics wins: the good character gets the good ending. Consumerism is explored, not condemned. Expressive individualism is gentle: be yourself, but keep your commitments.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-3 (ch. 1-25) set up the secret job, Honoka and Kanna. Vols. 4-6 (ch. 26-52) bring summer, the festival and Himari's realization. Vols. 7-9 (ch. 53-79) add Himeno, Christmas and Kanna's confession and end Tenichi's job. Vols. 10-11 (ch. 80-96) resolve the triangle and close with a five-year epilogue.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why does Himari hide what her apartment really looks like? Is that dishonest?
2. Tenichi stops working for Himari before anything can happen between them. Why does that matter?
3. Honoka wins by writing a letter and speaking up. What does the story say about telling people how you feel?
4. Himari and Kanna both lose. How do they handle it, and what can we learn from that?
5. Some scenes focus on the girls' bodies. How do those scenes fit with the rest of the story?
