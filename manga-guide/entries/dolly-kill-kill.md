**Premise.** Iruma Ikaruga is a cheerful first-year high-school baseball player until a storm of wasps arrives whose stings rot flesh and dissolve bone, followed by the Dollies: giant, mascot-like doll creatures with alien weapons, including living "human vacuums," that slaughter humanity. He loses his best friend and the senpai he loves. Half a year later about 1% of humanity survives; Iruma, traumatized and close to suicide, drifts through abandoned supermarkets. When he saves a girl, Yuu Banira, from men about to rape her, she recruits him into the resistance group Trial and Error. A more intelligent and powerful "Pygmy Doll" destroys their captured Dolly, and Iruma's encounter with it becomes the central mystery. After two time skips, Iruma emerges as a hero with dark powers, survivors divide between wiping out the Dollies and coexisting with them, and Kyūjin, a powered fighter, aims to defeat the Dolly leader; the alien masters appear only at the very end (MAL synopsis and reviews; MangaDex description).

## Life message

**Even after losing everything, people can refuse despair and fight back together; revenge drives Iruma at first, but protecting others is what makes him a hero.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What keeps a person alive when everyone they loved is gone? Iruma survives the Dolly invasion only to wish he had not.

**The answer the story gives.** Someone else to protect, and people to fight beside. His turn begins when he saves Yuu and she brings him into Trial and Error (MAL synopsis).

**Characters as arguments.** Iruma argues first for revenge, then for protection. Yuu argues for hope. The coexistence faction argues that killing is not the only answer, against those who want the Dollies wiped out (MAL review). The would-be rapists show what people become when order collapses.

**Symbols and motifs.** Dolls and mascots turned into killers, the cute made monstrous; insects that dissolve the body; the "human vacuum" weapons that take people away.

**Traditions it echoes.** Attack on Titan and Seraph of the End, to which reviewers compare it (MAL); the alien-invasion survival story. Faith readers may hear Lamentations and Job in a story of near-total loss.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Strength rules the world, but the story values strength used to protect. Despair is the enemy within; community is the answer. The hero's dark powers are left morally open.

**Shifts across the series.** The opening chapters are survival horror; after two time skips it becomes a shonen battle manga with powers and a widening cast (MAL reviews); the final chapters, after cancellation, rush to an ending that reveals the alien masters (MAL reviews).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Iruma wanted to die after the invasion. What gave him a reason to keep going?
2. Is revenge a good reason to fight? What about protecting someone?
3. Some survivors want to live alongside the Dollies. Is that wise or foolish?
4. Why do you think the monsters are made to look cute?
5. How does a group of strangers become a family?
