**Premise.** Six months after the first series, Yokai Academy, a boarding school where monsters learn to live disguised among humans, reopens. Tsukune Aono, the human boy kept alive and strengthened by the vampire blood Moka Akashiya has given him, returns for his second year with the Newspaper Club: Moka (sweet "outer" Moka, whose rosary seals the proud, powerful "inner" Moka), the succubus Kurumu, the young witch prodigy Yukari, the snow fairy Mizore and the witch Ruby, now joined by Moka's little sister Kokoa (Wikipedia; fan wiki). Early arcs are school comedy (love potions, a dungeon of thieves, potions that turn the girls into children, ch. 1-9). In Mizore's snow village the club first meets Fairy Tale, a secret organization that wants to destroy the human world; its agent Miyabi molests Mizore (ch. 10-14). Tsukune trains to control the vampire blood inside him, and the club helps a siren, San, keep her place with the human woman who raised her (ch. 15-22). A trip to China to repair Moka's cracked rosary reveals her past: her mother Akasha gave her First Ancestor blood and sacrificed herself to seal the monster Alucard, and the rosary was made to protect her (ch. 23-37). Moka's half-sister Aqua takes her to Fairy Tale's flying fortress; the club storms it, defeats Moka's stepmother Gyokuro, and faces the Masked King, Miyabi, a clone of Alucard. Tsukune breaks his last seal and becomes a vampire to save Moka; with allies from both worlds, and with human soldiers also fighting, they destroy Alucard, and Akasha dies with him. Monsters are now known to the human world, and Tsukune vows that coexistence can still be won (ch. 38-66; Wikipedia; fan wiki).

## Life message

**Friendship and love can bridge peoples who fear each other; strength is for protecting others, and coexistence is worth fighting for even when hatred runs deep on both sides.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can people who fear and hate each other learn to live together? Yokai Academy exists to teach monsters to pass among humans; Fairy Tale wants to end humanity instead.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, but only through costly love. Akasha dies to seal Alucard; Tsukune gives up being human to save Moka; humans and monsters fight side by side at the end (fan wiki). Coexistence is unfinished: monsters are now known to the world, and the work begins.

**Characters as arguments.** Tsukune argues that difference is what lets people understand each other (his answer to Alucard, fan wiki). Aqua argues from grief: humans killed her sister, so humans must be feared; she changes. Gyokuro argues for power and is consumed by it. Marin, a human who forgives and loves a siren as her daughter (v5), is the quiet proof that it can be done.

**Symbols and motifs.** The rosary that seals Moka and connects her to her mother; the holy locks on Tsukune; the flying fortress; blood given to save a life (Moka to Tsukune, Aqua and Tsukune to Moka).

**Traditions it echoes.** Dracula (Alucard, the Belmont whip from Castlevania), yokai tales, the harem comedy turning into a shōnen tournament story, and outsider stories like X-Men (Comic Book Bin).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarian coexistence is affirmed; might-makes-right and monster supremacy are defeated; the occult is the setting; Christian symbols are props with no faith behind them.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-3 (ch. 1-12): school comedy and the snow village. Vols. 4-5 (ch. 13-22): training and the siren arc. Vols. 6-9 (ch. 23-41): the Huang family, China and Moka's past. Vols. 10-14 (ch. 42-66 and epilogue): the war with Fairy Tale and Alucard, nearly all battle.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Yokai Academy teaches monsters to hide among humans. Is hiding the same as getting along?
2. Aqua hated humans because they killed her sister. How does the story change her mind?
3. Marin forgives the man who killed her husband. Could you? Should she have to?
4. Tsukune gives up being human to save Moka. What do we give up for people we love?
5. The series has lots of fan service. How does it change how you see the girls, who are heroes in the battles?
