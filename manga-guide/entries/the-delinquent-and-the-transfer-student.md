**Premise.** In 1980s Japan, Kanzaki Riri, a cheerful new transfer student, has an odd hobby: she writes lists of cute words and wants Nagumo Atsuko, the school's toughest lone-wolf sukeban (girl-gang delinquent), to read them aloud. Atsuko plays along, thinking Riri is a harmless weirdo, but the silly game starts making her blush, and Riri too. In volume 1 the two spend their days together, hold hands when scared of ghosts, watch the stars on campus after hours, and Riri tosses Atsuko a rice ball in the middle of a fist-fight with a gang of rowdy boys, which powers her up to win (Yattatachi); Atsuko's second-in-command resents how much time Riri takes (Okazu). Volume 2 brings summer, the candy shop and a summer festival, and a misunderstanding between them; volume 3 opens with the two as girlfriends, with a first date and a school festival (Seven Seas blurbs). MangaUpdates lists a first kiss and parents who accept the relationship.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Who are we beneath our reputations? Atsuko is the school's most feared fighter; Riri wants to hear her say something cute.

**The answer the story gives.** That tenderness was there all along, and that it takes someone patient and a little silly to draw it out. The word game lets two shy people be close before they can admit why (Seven Seas vol. 1).

**Characters as arguments.** Riri argues for gentle persistence; Atsuko for loyalty and strength; the second-in-command for the old gang code, which yields (Okazu).

**Symbols and motifs.** The lists of cute words; the rice ball tossed mid-fight (vol. 1); stars watched after hours; the summer festival.

**Traditions it echoes.** The "gap moe" comedy of the tough girl with a soft side; Showa-era delinquent manga; the class-S and yuri school romance.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism and progressive acceptance are affirmed quietly; virtue (kindness, loyalty) carries the plot; force is played for laughs.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1: friendship and the word game; vol. 2: summer, a misunderstanding; vol. 3: girlfriends and firsts (Seven Seas).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why do you think Atsuko agrees to read Riri's lists?
2. Have you ever been wrong about someone because of their reputation?
3. What words make you feel cared for?
4. Atsuko wins fights for Riri. Is fighting ever the right way to help a friend?
5. How do Riri and Atsuko fix their misunderstanding?
