**Premise.** Asahi Igarashi, a bookish, glasses-wearing high school otaku who cooks and keeps house, lives in an apartment with his older sister Tsukiko, a college student three years older. Tsukiko's friend since middle school, Hinako Kakei ("Hi-chan"), a blonde gyaru college student, practically lives at their place (Mangapedia). She is beautiful, cheerful and has no sense of personal space; the official description says her closeness leaves Asahi flustered and off-balance, and the MangaUpdates blurb says she teases him with a suggestive attitude while treating him like a little brother or a pet. Asahi has liked Hinako since middle school but thinks she sees him only as a kid brother; Hinako likes him too and thinks he does not see her that way (Mangapedia; MANGA Million blurb on AniList). An outing to an aquarium turns into a chase after Tsukiko and a student from her tutoring job, Utsugi, and brings the two closer (Mangapedia). A MyAnimeList reviewer at 24 chapters describes a confession scene in which both already know their feelings (to verify). The English blurb calls it a sweet, frustratingly adorable romance between two people who are hopelessly in love with each other.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What happens when two people each think the other is out of reach? Asahi thinks Hinako sees him as a kid brother; Hinako thinks Asahi does not see her as a woman (Mangapedia).

**The answer the story gives.** Keep showing up, and eventually say it. The comedy is in the gap between how obvious the feelings are and how neither will speak.

**Characters as arguments.** Asahi argues for steady, practical care: he cooks, cleans and looks after his household. Hinako argues for affection shown through closeness and play. Tsukiko, the sister in the middle, has her own story with Utsugi.

**Symbols and motifs.** The apartment as a second home for Hinako; food Asahi makes; the aquarium outing as a step closer (Mangapedia).

**Traditions it echoes.** The Japanese romantic-comedy formula of mutual unspoken love (Takagi-san-style teasing) and the older-woman, younger-man pairing popular in seinen romance.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism is mild and playful. The question most parents will ask, whether an adult pursuing a high school student is all right, is not one the story raises in sources opened; that silence is the main thing to discuss.

**Shifts across the series.** Early chapters are setup and teasing; the aquarium arc moves them closer; a confession is reported around ch. 24 (to verify). Later chapters were not described in sources opened.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Hinako ignores Asahi's personal space. Is that flirting, or is it a problem? Why?
2. Asahi is in high school and Hinako is in college. Does that matter? Would it matter if the genders were switched?
3. Why do you think neither of them says what they feel?
4. Asahi does most of the housework. What does that say about him?
5. What should older friends of the family keep in mind around younger teens?
