**Premise.** Kenta Fukuyama, a mild office worker who loves grilling, meets Chihiro Yamaguchi on a dating app; she arrives late to their first date, they switch to a yakiniku restaurant, and his grilling puts her at ease. Both fear being rejected for who they really are. When Kenta is transferred to Hamamatsu, he tries to end things quietly, but Chihiro, a polished, slightly older career woman, confronts him at his office and proposes; they marry after "zero days" of dating (ch. 1-7, v1; OASG; gendai.media). Living apart at first, they get to know each other through weekend barbecues: their first night together (ch. 10), their first quarrel (ch. 15), Chihiro's farewell party and move (ch. 30-31), Kenta's family (ch. 54), Chihiro's grief and anger when her widowed father finds a new partner (ch. 68-70, v8), Kenta's birthday and a growing "grill team" of friends (v10), Chihiro's viral doodles and new career (v16), and quarrels over privacy (v19). Side couples include the idol Rina and her friend Mifune, and Toda and his older coworker Fushimi (v7). Each chapter ends with a simple recipe (Wikipedia chapter titles; Kodansha and Apple Books synopses).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can you marry someone you barely know and grow into love? Kenta and Chihiro skip dating entirely: a job transfer forces the question, and Chihiro proposes (v1). The series asks what love is when it starts as a decision rather than a feeling.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, if both keep showing up. Love grows through small acts: a meal cooked for a tired spouse (v2), an apology after a quarrel (ch. 15-16), honesty about fears (end of v1). The grill is the image: slow, attentive heat.

**Characters as arguments.** Kenta argues for patient, practical care. Chihiro argues that a strong, self-reliant person still needs to be known. Mifune shows how admiring someone from a distance is not the same as friendship (v16, v19). Chihiro's father, finding love again after his wife's death, tests whether grief allows new happiness (v8).

**Symbols and motifs.** The grill and the fire; recipes as acts of love; the ring (ch. 73, "The Ring's Whereabouts"); the one bed (v2); seasons marked by food.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Japanese iyashikei (healing) slice of life and the gourmet manga; arranged-marriage stories where love follows commitment; for many religious readers, the idea that love is a choice made daily.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Traditionalism and communitarianism are affirmed gently; hedonism is the pleasure of food, kept in proportion; feminism is present in Chihiro's career and proposal but is not argued.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-4 (ch. 1-34) are the long-distance newlywed period. Volumes 5-10 (ch. 35-88) widen to family, friends and side couples. Volumes 11-22 (ch. 89-196) add careers, an idol subplot and quarrels about privacy. Volumes 23-25 (to ch. 232, the end) are not yet in English and were not described in sources opened.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Chihiro proposes after only a few dates. What would you want to know about someone before marrying?
2. What small things do Kenta and Chihiro do for each other? Which matter most?
3. How do they make up after a quarrel? What could they have done differently?
4. Chihiro is angry that her father found someone new. Why, and was she right?
5. Why do you think food is such a big part of this love story?
