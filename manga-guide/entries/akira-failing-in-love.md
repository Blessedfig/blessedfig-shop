**Premise.** Akira Momose, a top student who is shy and extremely clumsy in person, moved to Tokyo as a child and has now come back to the countryside town where she grew up, with a detailed plan: win the heart of her former classmate Hajime Kugayama, the boy she has liked since grade school. She writes out scripts for every approach, and every one of them goes wrong. Hajime, a gifted but impulsive athlete, has admired her all along and is too dense to see that she likes him back (VIZ; WebSunday). Vol. 1 covers her scripted attempts and a sports day where Hajime overexerts himself to impress her and is hurt (The Outerhaven). In vol. 2 Hajime realizes his own feelings and asks his deadpan friend Rendaiji for advice, while Akira's new friends Mei Onimai, Tsumugi "Kin-chan" Kintetsu and the very tall Urara Konoura try to help; it ends with an accidental first kiss at a festival, whose aftermath fills vol. 3, along with a track-team arc and a visit to the hot-spring inn run by Akira's mother, where Hajime misses the last train home (VIZ vol. 3; The Outerhaven; k-manga). Vol. 4 brings a beach day, a hot-spring episode and Hajime's discovery of Akira's scripts; vols. 5 and 6 bring summer school, a cultural festival, a school play and a sick visit, and the Japanese blurbs say the pair's feelings come out into the open (VIZ vols. 5-6; k-manga vols. 5-6). In Japanese vol. 8 Akira secretly kisses Hajime while he is inside a mascot costume, and he saw everything (WebSunday; k-manga).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can you plan your way into someone's heart? Akira believes she can: she returns to her hometown with scripts for every encounter. The series asks what love needs instead.

**The answer the story gives.** Honesty and courage. Every script fails, usually because Hajime misreads it or because Akira freezes, yet each failure brings them closer. The Japanese blurbs describe the turn: by vol. 6 she holds his hand and stops hiding the reason, and by vol. 8 she kisses him, clumsily and in secret. The publisher's promise that the two will marry someday makes the destination certain; the comedy is all in the road.

**Characters as arguments.** Akira argues for preparation and learns its limits. Hajime argues for acting first and thinking later, and pays for it (the sports-day injury, vol. 1) before learning to act with purpose (vol. 4). Rendaiji, who cannot look girls in the eye, gives the most earnest advice. Hinako, who melts at praise, mirrors Akira's shyness and is helped by her (vols. 5-6).

**Symbols and motifs.** The script, crossed out by events. Spring jokes at the start of chapters (The Outerhaven). The family hot-spring inn as home. The mascot costume in vol. 8, a mask that lets Akira finally act.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Shonen Sunday romantic comedy (Rumiko Takahashi, who recommended vol. 3) and recent shy-heroine comedies such as Komi Can't Communicate (School Library Journal) and Teasing Master Takagi-san (whose creator recommended vol. 2).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarianism and classical virtue are affirmed lightly: a kind town and courage learned by practice. There is no politics, religion or critique of anyone.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1 is gag-driven and repetitive (ANN guide); vols. 2-4 add friends, a kiss and summer episodes; vols. 5-8 (Japanese) move toward open feelings. Later volumes have not been reviewed in English.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why do Akira's scripts keep failing?
2. Hajime gets hurt trying to impress Akira. What would have been a better way to show he cared?
3. How do Akira's friends and the adults in town help, and when do they get in the way?
4. Have you ever rehearsed something you wanted to say? What happened when you actually said it?
5. Is it funny when Akira hits Hajime? Would it be funny the other way round?
