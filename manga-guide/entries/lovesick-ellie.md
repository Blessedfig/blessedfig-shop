**Premise.** Eriko Ichimura is a first-year at Fuji High School, so unnoticed that classmates call her by the wrong name. Her one hobby is watching Akira Ohmi, the school's handsome, gentlemanly star, and posting her daydreams about him on an anonymous Twitter account, "Lovesick Ellie," under a no-boyfriend hashtag (Wikipedia; Japanese Wikipedia). She calls herself a pervert, and the tweets are "thirsty" in the publisher's word (Kodansha USA). One day she overhears the real Ohmi, childish, sharp-tongued and bossy behind his princely smile, and flees, leaving her phone; he reads the account, laughs, and the two agree to keep each other's secrets (Soliloquy in Blue; Anime UK News). He finds her odd honesty refreshing, she accepts his real self, and a secret romance begins. Around them: Sara Misaki, who sews Rococo dresses and has no interest in real boys; Reo Takagi, a delinquent-looking senior who is top of his year and has loved Sara since childhood; Yoichiro Kaname, a gamer who falls for Ellie and becomes Ohmi's rival and then friend; and their homeroom teacher Sumi Shiota, Ohmi's uncle, who raised him while his parents worked (Japanese Wikipedia). They become a couple and share a first kiss at Christmas (vols. 3-4); when Ellie's father opposes the relationship, Ohmi tells her family he means to marry her (vols. 7-8) (ciatr). An online controversy over a "high school boy contest" leads Ohmi to deny dating her (vol. 11) (Kodansha USA). In the final volume he gives up the contest final to help Ellie at the sports festival, invites her home, and the two have their first sexual experience, which reader reviews describe as implied rather than shown (vol. 12; Kodansha Japan; ciatr; BookLive). The digital-only vol. 13 jumps eight years ahead to their wedding, with Ellie, now 26, nervous on the day (Kodansha Japan).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What happens when the person you fantasize about sees the fantasies? Ellie has built a private world online in which she is bold and loved; Ohmi has built a public world in which he is perfect. Each sees the other's hidden self in the first chapter.

**The answer the story gives.** Being seen is the beginning of love. Ohmi laughs instead of recoiling, and Ellie likes the rude, childish boy better than the prince (Soliloquy in Blue). The series rewards the two for being honest with each other and with their families: Ohmi states his intentions to her father (vols. 7-8) and gives up his own contest to help her (vol. 12).

**Characters as arguments.** Ellie argues that desire and oddness are nothing to be ashamed of. Ohmi argues, at first, that a mask keeps people at a distance and is safer. Sara prefers fantasy aristocrats to real boys; Reo is the real boy who keeps showing up. Kaname, who cared only about games, learns to care about a person and then to step back. Ellie's father speaks for caution and is answered with respect, not mockery.

**Symbols and motifs.** The phone and the anonymous account (the hidden self); the princely smile (the mask); the gas station where Kaname and Ohmi work and become friends; the sports festival, where Ohmi chooses Ellie over a public prize (vol. 12); the wedding day eight years on (vol. 13).

**Traditions it echoes.** The shōjo "secret relationship with the prince" plot, turned around: the plain girl is the one with the shocking secret. The comedy of the two-faced hero echoes earlier shōjo such as Wolf Girl and Black Prince (from general knowledge; to verify).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism is celebrated: authenticity is the point. Virtue ethics is quietly present in honesty and sacrifice. Traditional readers will find the blessing and wedding congenial and the sexual comedy and teen sex not. Feminist readers will find a heroine whose desire drives the story.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-4: secrets, friendships and the first kiss. Vols. 5-8: rivals, jealousy, the father's opposition and Ohmi's declaration. Vols. 9-11: second year, Sara and Reo, an online scandal. Vol. 12: the sports festival, the first night together and the ending; vol. 13: the wedding side story (ciatr; Kodansha).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Ellie posted fantasies about a real classmate. How would you feel if someone did that about you?
2. Ohmi acts like a prince at school and like a brat with Ellie. Which is the real Ohmi?
