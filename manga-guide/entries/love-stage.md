**Premise.** Izumi Sena, 18, is the youngest son of a celebrity family: his father is a singer and agency head, his mother an actress, his brother Shogo the lead singer of the rock band Crusherz. Since freezing up in a family wedding commercial at 8, where he was dressed as a bride, he has avoided show business; he is a shy otaku in his college manga club who loves the anime Magical Girl Lala-Lulu and wants to be a manga artist, though he has little talent (Wikipedia; SuBLime). Bribed with Lala-Lulu merchandise, he agrees to a ten-years-later sequel commercial, again in a dress, opposite the popular actor Ryoma Ichijo, who has loved the "girl" from that shoot for ten years. Learning the truth, Ryoma is furious and, trying to prove to himself that Izumi is male, forces himself on the unwilling Izumi until Shogo interrupts (v1; anime ep. 3). He apologizes on his knees; they become "texting buddies," and Ryoma courts him while helping his manga ambitions. Paparazzi photos force Izumi into an entertainment debut, where he is revealed as a man (anime ep. 8). Izumi realizes he loves Ryoma and they sleep together (v3; anime ep. 10). Later volumes follow Izumi's surprising acting success, Ryoma's jealousy and a hypnosis-induced amnesia that erases his memory of Izumi (v5-6), a stalker who targets the family's manager Rei, and the couple living together (v7). It ends with Izumi a successful manga artist, a role reversal in the bedroom, and Shogo marrying Rei (TV Tropes; SuBLime).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Do we love a person or the role they play? Ryoma loved a "girl" in a bridal costume for ten years; when the costume comes off, he must decide whether what he loved was real. Izumi, the only Sena without a stage persona, must decide whether he is allowed to want something his family did not plan.

**The answer the story gives.** Love the person. Ryoma moves from fury and denial to deciding that Izumi's sex does not matter (Kotaku), and in vol. 7 both men think through what it means for a man to love a man (SuBLime). Izumi, for his part, finds his footing as an actor and finally as a manga artist on his own terms.

**Characters as arguments.** Ryoma argues for devotion, but his first act on learning the truth is to violate Izumi; the story has him apologize and later stop when Izumi is not truly willing (anime ep. 7), yet the family treats the first scene as minor (Fandom.com). Rei argues for practicality and discipline and is shown to be right about Izumi needing a plan (TV Tropes). Shogo argues for unashamed love and protectiveness, toward his brother and toward Rei. The strangers and the stalker show predation without love, and are stopped.

**Symbols and motifs.** The wedding commercial and its bouquet; the good-luck marble Ryoma gave Izumi as children, which cracks (anime eps. 1, 3); glasses as Izumi's disguise; Lala-Lulu, the magical girl who stands for Izumi's dream; the stage itself.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Cinderella story (Japanese Wikipedia calls it a Cinderella story), the gender-mistake romantic comedy, and boys' love conventions of the 2010s, including the dominant/receptive (seme/uke) pairing the ending playfully reverses. Critics place its handling of consent within a broader pattern in boys' love of assault played as romance (Fandom.com).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism and progressivism are affirmed: love and calling are personal choices. Hedonism is present but bound to faithfulness. Communitarianism appears as a protective family. Feminism is largely absent.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-3 (covered by the anime) run from the commercial and the assault through courtship, the debut and the first night together. Volumes 4-5 follow Izumi's acting success and Ryoma's jealousy. Volumes 5-6 bring the amnesia arc. Volume 7 has the couple living together, Rei's stalker, Izumi's wish to switch roles and the weddings and happy endings.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Ryoma says he loved the "girl" for ten years. Did he love Izumi or a role? What changed his mind?
2. In the first volume Ryoma forces himself on Izumi, then apologizes. Should an apology be enough? Why does the family call it minor?
3. Izumi wants to draw manga even though he is not good yet. When should you keep chasing a dream?
4. Rei pushes Izumi to have a career plan. Is he controlling or caring?
5. How does the story treat a same-sex relationship as a "scandal"? How does that compare with where you live?
