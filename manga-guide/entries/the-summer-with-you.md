**Premise.** After high school, Chiharu Saeki moves back to Tokyo from Osaka to start university and spends another summer with his boyfriend Wataru Toda. Both juggle classes and part-time jobs, keep their weekend movie dates, go to a fireworks festival (where Wataru stops to help a lost child), and continue their "pilgrimage" to film locations with a trip to a deserted island. When Wataru misses the last train and stays the night at Chiharu's, Chiharu asks one thing in return: that Wataru kiss him (Kodansha vol. 2; Goodreads blog review). In vol. 3, in their second year, Chiharu returns from study abroad, both turn twenty, and Wataru, who works at a cinema, falls asleep after one drink and has to be collected by Chiharu; his boss sees them kiss and asks if they are dating, and Chiharu realizes something he did in high school still troubles Wataru. A bonus story has them helping at a seaside inn (Kodansha vol. 3; Anime UK News).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What comes after "I love you"? The first book ended with two boys admitting their feelings; the sequel asks how a real relationship is built from shy, unequal starts.

**The answer the story gives.** Slowly, and by speaking up. Chiharu learns to wait and to ask rather than take; Wataru learns to act and to say what has hurt him. The kiss Chiharu asks Wataru to give (vol. 2) and the truth that comes out when Wataru is tipsy (vol. 3) are steps in the same lesson (Goodreads blog; Anime UK News).

**Characters as arguments.** Chiharu is the case for open desire made safe by consent. Wataru is the case for kindness that must also learn honesty. Niimi, the boss, stands for the outside world's curiosity, left open.

**Symbols and motifs.** Summer and the color blue for optimism (Anime UK News); movies and their locations as a shared language; the missed last train; a deserted island; fireworks.

**Traditions it echoes.** The slow-burn romance and the coming-of-age story; the BL tradition of gentle "pure love" stories rather than dramatic ones.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism and progressivism are affirmed quietly; the consent theme reads as liberal respect for the other's choice. No lens is argued against.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 2: first year of university, a new summer, the overnight stay and the kiss. Vol. 3: second year, twentieth birthdays, drinking, the boss's question, and facing an old hurt; a bonus seaside-inn story. A further English volume is due in 2027 (Kodansha; Anime UK News).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Chiharu asks Wataru to be the one to kiss him. Why might that matter?
