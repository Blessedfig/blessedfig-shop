**Premise.** Haruka Sakura, a lonely teenage fighter shunned all his life for his two-tone hair and mismatched eyes, transfers to Furin High in the town of Makochi because the school is said to value strength over study. He means to fight his way to the top. Instead he finds that Furin's students, known as Bofurin, have made it their duty to protect the town's shopping street from outside gangs, and the shopkeepers thank and feed them. Under their cheerful leader Hajime Umemiya, who gardens on the school roof, Sakura learns to accept kindness, make friends (Nirei, Suo, Sugishita) and fight for others (ch. 1-30). Arcs set Bofurin against the rival gang Shishitoren (ch. 5-30), the criminal outfit KEEL (ch. 31-66), the poor youth gang GRAVEL with the street's guardians Roppo Ichiza (ch. 67-87), and Noroshi, former Furin students who want to return to the days of fighting for the top (ch. 98-162), with later arcs still running (fan wiki).

## Life message

**Strength is for protecting others, not for standing above them: a loner becomes stronger when he lets a community accept him and fights for the people who need him.**

## Creator and publisher context

- Satoru Nii (story and art). He read Weekly Shonen Magazine as a child, submitted a dark fantasy (Uramare-ya) for a newcomer award at university, and serialized the badminton gender-bender series Danshi Bado-Bu ni Joshi ga Magireteru before turning, on an editor's advice, to stories about "cool boys"; Rave Master and Samurai Deeper Kyo were influences (Wikipedia, Production).
- Intent (Wikipedia; K MANGA blog interview, originally May 2021; Kodansha USA interview, about late 2024; both read through fetch summaries): he did not set out to write a delinquent manga; he wanted a feel-good story about cool guys, "boys who fight" rather than bad boys; he likens the fights to sports and to the Sentai genre, with tension followed by gratitude; Kotoha was created so a girl in this rough town would be strong. Sakura was added as an outsider protagonist; his heterochromia signals his inner conflict.
- The setting is modeled on a real Tokyo shopping district; Bofurin's green stands for plants, Shishitoren's red for a tavern lantern. Nii had almost no involvement in the anime (Wikipedia).
- No statements on religion or politics were found. **Status: Needs more info on the creators.**
- Publisher: Kodansha (Magazine Pocket, shonen). Kodansha USA publishes it in English, rated T+.
- No controversies involving the creator were found.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What is strength for? Haruka Sakura has only ever had his fists, because they were the one thing nobody could take from him. He comes to Furin to be the strongest. The series asks whether strength means standing above others, or standing in front of them.

**The answer the story gives.** In front of them. Kotoha tells him on day one that he will never reach the top alone (ch. 1). Umemiya, the strongest at Furin, spends his time gardening and knows every member's name. Sakura's growth is learning to accept thanks, to fight for a classmate (ch. 2) and to lead (class captain). Villains who think strength entitles them to rule (Natori; Noroshi) lose.

**Characters as arguments.** Sakura argues for self-reliance and learns its limits. Umemiya argues that fights are conversations and that the point is to protect; his backstory, a grieving boy who wanted to die and was saved by a Furin student (ch. 88-97), shows where that conviction comes from. Tomiyama of Shishitoren argues that strength is everything, then changes. Natori argues for domination by fear. Endo and Takiishi argue for fighting as the only thing that makes life feel real. Tsubakino argues for being true to yourself and protecting others who are mocked for it.

**Symbols and motifs.** Sakura's two-tone hair and mismatched eyes (the outsider's mark, which Nii says signals inner conflict). The wind, which gives Bofurin and the title their names. Umemiya's rooftop garden. The green of Bofurin and the red of Shishitoren. Café Pothos, where Kotoha feeds the boys.

**Traditions it echoes.** Yankee manga (Tokyo Revengers is the comparison Anime UK News makes), Sentai team heroics (Nii), and the samurai-style protector of a village. Its message parallels the old idea that the strong have duties to the weak, found in many traditions.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarianism is the heart, affirmed in every arc. Might makes right is the recurring enemy. An honor code governs fights. Expressive individualism appears in Tsubakino and is folded into community rather than set against it. Religion is absent beyond guardian names.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-4 (ch. 1-30): Sakura arrives and Bofurin settles with Shishitoren. Volumes 4-8 (ch. 31-66): KEEL, a criminal gang, raises the stakes. Volumes 9-12 (ch. 67-97): GRAVEL, Roppo Ichiza and Umemiya's past. Volumes 12-20 (ch. 98-162): the Noroshi war, the longest arc. Volumes 21-27 (ch. 163-218): summer break and a new semester. From ch. 208: the Red Chanpuru arc (fan wiki).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Kotoha tells Sakura he will never reach the top alone. Was she right?
2. Umemiya says fights can be a kind of conversation. Do you agree? What are the limits?
3. Why do Natori and Umemiya both talk about strength but mean opposite things?
4. Tsubakino was bullied for how he looked. How does Bofurin treat him?
5. Could Bofurin protect the town without fighting? What would that look like?
