**Premise.** Kanan Zebul, a demon thousands of years old and daughter of Beelzebub, the Demon Lord of Gluttony, hypnotizes a human family into thinking she is their daughter and enrolls at a Tokyo-area high school as Kanan Takakiyo, head of the discipline committee, to keep the students' souls pure and tasty. She picks Yōji Kyōgi, a second-year boy of about 17 with a vigorous soul, but when she tells him she will devour him, he takes it as a sexual proposition, confesses, and she accidentally becomes his girlfriend (ch. 1; anime ep. 1). Kanan knows nothing of romance; she thinks holding hands causes pregnancy. Running gags follow: the succubus maid Ami, who dates the homeroom teacher Yuriko and pushes the couple to kiss; the clumsy angel Jeanne, sent from Heaven to protect Yōji from demons; and Yōji's childhood friend Nadeko, who enjoys Kanan's insults. In the Demon Realm arc, Yōji must win the approval of each of Kanan's family, from her man-fearing mother Lilim to her sisters Milch and Miel and finally Beelzebub, who declares them a couple (anime ep. 8-12) (Wikipedia; fan wiki).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What happens when a predator falls in love with her prey? Kanan came to eat souls; she ends up blushing at hand-holding.

**The answer the story gives.** Love tames appetite. Beelzebub's lesson that food tastes better eaten with someone (ep. 1) is the series' gentle thesis, and the croquettes she shares with Yōji return at the end of the first arc (ep. 12).

**Characters as arguments.** Kanan argues that nature is not destiny. Yōji argues for straightforward devotion. Ami argues that desire is good fun. Jeanne, sent to protect purity, is the butt of the jokes. Beelzebub argues for fatherly protection and finally trust.

**Symbols and motifs.** Croquettes and shared meals; the soul as food; the stamp rally of family approval; the starry sky in the Demon Realm (ep. 12).

**Traditions it echoes.** Monster-girl and demon-girlfriend comedies (Urusei Yatsura and later, from general knowledge; to verify) and the tamed-predator romance. Christian readers will notice the inversion: Beelzebub is a warm father and Heaven's envoy is the fool.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Hedonism is celebrated in food and fan service. The Pagan/occult lens is setting, not teaching. The Christian lens appears only as parody. Communitarian family approval gives the romance its frame.

**Shifts across the series.** The school chapters (to about ch. 30) set up the couple, Ami, Nadeko and Jeanne. The Demon Realm arc (about ch. 31-56, the anime's end) brings in the family. Later chapters add Yōji's sister Koko (ch. 58) and crossover characters; their content was not checked.
