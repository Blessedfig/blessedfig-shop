**Premise.** Asahina Wakaba, a clumsy, shy high-school girl, is the most disliked girl in her class. After she is framed for breaking class leader Nanase Ikumi's compact mirror, the class forces her into a "punishment game": confess love to Iruma Haruto, the heavyset otaku everyone mocks, and date him as a joke (NDL, vol. 1; AniList review). Haruto turns out to be warm, cheerful and kind, with good friends and an open love of games (including adult games, per an AniList reviewer). Wakaba's grim school life brightens. She meets Haruto's middle-school friend and his mother and learns of his sad past and difficult family (vol. 2). Then his best friend Bizen Ryoichi discovers the game and confronts her; she panics, lashes out at her family and classmates and isolates herself, and Haruto learns the truth (vol. 3; the novel's vol. 2 blurb). Reader reviews describe Wakaba repressing what she did until she has a mental breakdown, and the story ending happily for the couple (MyAnimeList; AniList).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can love that starts as a lie become true? And can a person who has been crushed by a crowd learn she is worth loving?

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, but only through the truth. Wakaba falls for Haruto while still hiding the game; the hiding eats at her until it breaks her (vol. 3). Healing comes after the truth comes out.

**Characters as arguments.** Haruto argues that kindness does not depend on how others treat you. Wakaba shows how fear makes good people do cruel things. Bizen stands for loyal friendship that insists on honesty. The class shows how cruelty spreads when no one stands apart.

**Symbols and motifs.** The broken compact mirror (a false charge that starts everything, and a picture of distorted self-image); the confession that is both fake and, in the end, true; the circle in the title, a blank for what kind of boyfriend he is.

**Traditions it echoes.** The "beauty sees past the beast" romance; Japanese web-board love stories; Christian readers may hear 1 Samuel 16:7, Buddhists right speech.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Truth-telling is the turning point; virtue is shown in Haruto rather than preached; the class's group mind is criticized while friendship is praised.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1 sets up the bullying and the fake confession; vol. 2 deepens the romance and Haruto's past; vol. 3 brings exposure, breakdown and resolution.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why did Wakaba agree to the punishment game? What else could she have done?
2. What makes Haruto different from how the class sees him?
3. Why does keeping the secret hurt Wakaba more and more?
4. If you saw a classmate being set up like Wakaba, what could you do?
5. Is it ever too late to tell the truth?
