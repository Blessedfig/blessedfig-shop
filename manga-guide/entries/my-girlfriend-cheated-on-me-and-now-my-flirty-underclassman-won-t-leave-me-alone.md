**Premise.** Yūta Hasegawa, a second-year university student in a basketball circle, finds out just before Christmas that his girlfriend, Reina Aisaka, has apparently been cheating on him. On Christmas Eve he runs into Mayu Shinohara, a first-year student from his school handing out flyers in a Santa costume, and the bold, teasing underclassman starts turning up everywhere, then at his apartment (manga vol. 1). His best friend since high school, Ayaka Mino, is the other woman in his life: Mayu and Ayaka clash when they meet in his apartment (vol. 2); Mayu stays the night, showers and sleeps beside him in one bed while the blurb teases whether they will cross the line (vol. 3); he and Ayaka take a two-person hot-spring trip that tests their friendship before a flashback to how they met in high school (vol. 4); and Reina gets back in touch, then appears at a circle welcome party with words that seem to admit the cheating (vols. 5-6) (Kadokawa blurbs). In the light novels, Reina insists she never cheated and reveals what really happened (novel vol. 3); Yūta and Mayu try a "trial relationship" and her middle-school past with Ayaka comes out (vols. 4-5); Mayu challenges Ayaka to see who can get closer to him, Ayaka confesses (vols. 7-8), and Yūta makes his choice at the school festival. The after-story shows him two years into a relationship with Mayu, now a busy entertainer, while he works as a company employee (BookLive; Kadokawa).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** After someone you trusted betrays you, how do you trust again? Yūta begins the story alone at Christmas, the season Japan treats as a couples' holiday, and the series asks what real affection looks like next to the false kind.

**The answer the story gives.** Trust comes back through people who keep showing up. Mayu teases her way into his life; Ayaka has been there for years. The final novel's blurb puts the theme plainly: choosing someone is choosing a shape of happiness. Faithfulness and honesty are the standard by which everyone is judged.

**Characters as arguments.** Yūta argues for faithfulness and for growing into a better person rather than wallowing. Mayu argues that love should be bold and persistent. Ayaka argues for the steady love of long friendship, and her unreturned confession is the story's sorrow. Reina, the ex, complicates the title: she says she did not cheat and explains what happened (novel vol. 3), so the series asks readers to hear the other side before judging.

**Symbols and motifs.** The Santa costume (the gift that arrives when hope is gone). Yūta's apartment, where Mayu lets herself in. The basketball circle. The hot spring as a place where friendship is tested. The school-festival beauty contest where the choice is made (novel vol. 8).

**Traditions it echoes.** The Japanese rom-com built on a love triangle and a teasing kōhai, and the rebound-romance story. Faith readers will connect its stress on fidelity to teachings on faithfulness, while noting that its dating customs differ from theirs.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism wins: the right partner is the one your heart chooses. Virtue matters too, as Yūta works to become worthy. Hedonism is mild and comic.

**Shifts across the series.** Manga vols. 1-2 (ch. 1-12) are the meeting and the triangle; vol. 3 (ch. 13-17) the sleepover; vol. 4 (ch. 18-22.5) the hot-spring trip and high-school flashback; vols. 5-6 (ch. 23-31.5) the return of the ex. The novels then move to a trial relationship, a contest between the two women, and the final choice.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Yūta was hurt by someone he trusted. What helped him most, and what might have made things worse?
2. Mayu keeps showing up even when Yūta wants to be alone. When is that caring and when is it pushy?
3. Reina says the story everyone believes is not true. Why is it important to hear both sides?
4. Ayaka loved Yūta for years. How should a friend handle being told no?
5. What does being faithful mean before marriage?
