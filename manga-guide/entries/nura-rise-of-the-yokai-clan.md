**Premise.** Rikuo Nura is a 13-year-old middle-schooler who is one-quarter yokai: the grandson of Nurarihyon, supreme commander of the Nura Clan, a yakuza-like alliance of Japanese spirits and monsters, and the son of the half-yokai Rihan, who was murdered when Rikuo was a child. By day he is a kind, ordinary boy who does good deeds and hides his family from friends in the Kiyojuji Paranormal Patrol (including his childhood friend Kana and the young onmyoji exorcist Yura Keikain); by night he becomes a confident yokai swordsman. Four years before the story he saved his classmates from Gagoze, a child-eating yokai who wanted to be heir (ch. 1). He accepts his role as Third Head after a loyal lieutenant, Gyuki, tests him (ch. 6-17), defeats the rival Shikoku clan of Tamazuki and spares him (ch. 20-47), trains in Tono, and joins the Keikain onmyoji to stop the nine-tailed fox Hagoromo Gitsune in Kyoto (ch. 58-134), who killed his father 400 years after first fighting his grandfather. Later arcs pit him against the Hundred Tales Clan, which makes yokai from urban legends (ch. 138-184), and finally against Abe no Seimei (the Nue), Hagoromo Gitsune's son, who wants yokai to rule humans, as Rikuo unites clans across Japan (ch. 185-210) (Wikipedia; fan wiki).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can someone who belongs to two worlds choose who he is, and lead both?

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, by accepting both halves and using his power to protect. Day Rikuo wants to be a good human; night Rikuo is a yokai commander. The story first treats them as split selves, then shows Rikuo talking with his other self and deciding to leave the human side to his day form and the yokai side to his night form (Wikipedia), and finally accepting that he is both, which makes him stronger (Kyoto arc). He protects humans and yokai alike.

**Characters as arguments.** Nurarihyon argues for the freedom and charm of the old commander. Gagoze argues that yokai should prey on humans and that the strong deserve the throne; he dies in chapter 1. Gyuki argues that loyalty to the clan may require testing its heir, even by deceit. Tamazuki argues for ambition that spends its followers, and is spared on condition that he honor them (ch. 47). Hagoromo Gitsune feeds on human malice. Seimei argues for an order in which yokai rule humans, the opposite of Rikuo's coexistence.

**Symbols and motifs.** The night parade (Hyakki Yako); the sake cup of loyalty; Nenekirimaru, a blade that cuts only yokai, broken against Seimei (Wikipedia); "Fear" as the yokai's power; day and night as Rikuo's two selves; Yamabuki Otome's farewell poem, used to kill Rihan.

**Traditions it echoes.** Edo-period yokai prints and the Hyakki Yako scrolls, the onmyoji tales of Abe no Seimei, Tono Monogatari, yakuza cinema's chivalrous gangster, and the shonen heir story (from general knowledge; to verify).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarianism, bushido-like honor and Confucian filial duty are affirmed. Pagan/occult folklore is the setting, neither endorsed as belief nor mocked. Strength rules is explored and finally subordinated to mercy and service. Authoritarian order (Seimei) is critiqued.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-6 (ch. 1-51): school life, the Gyuki test and the Shikoku war. Vols. 7-16 (ch. 52-138): the Keikain, the past of Nurarihyon and Yohime, Tono training and the Kyoto war. Vols. 16-21 (ch. 135-184): urban-legend horror and the Hundred Tales Clan, including the Edo past of Rihan. Vols. 22-25 (ch. 185-210): the Gokadoin and the final war with Seimei.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Rikuo tries hard to be a "normal" human by day. Why does he finally accept his yokai side?
2. Why does Rikuo spare Tamazuki? Was that the right choice?
3. Gyuki tricks Rikuo to test him. Is it ever right to deceive someone for their own good?
4. The Hundred Tales Clan makes monsters out of rumors. How do rumors become "monsters" in real life?
5. What makes someone a leader people want to follow?
