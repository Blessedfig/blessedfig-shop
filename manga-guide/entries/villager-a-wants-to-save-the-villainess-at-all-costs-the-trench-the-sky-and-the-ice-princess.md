**Premise.** On his eighth birthday Allen, a poor boy raised by his widowed mother Katerina in the royal capital, remembers his past life as an aerospace engineer and gun enthusiast in Japan, and realizes he is living inside a smartphone otome game his sister made him play. He is not even a named character: he is "Townsperson A." In eight years the game's condemnation of the villainess, Duke Ramslet's daughter Anastasia, will spark a war that destroys the capital and kills him and his mother. Allen cleans sewers (ch. 2), learns appraisal and alchemy, graduates the free school early at 11 (ch. 4), wins a wind god's blessing by matchmaking two wyverns (ch. 5-7), builds gliders and firearms, and becomes a C-rank adventurer. At 15 he enters the Royal Academy (vol. 4), where the game's heroine Amy, another reincarnated player, chases a "harem ending" with the crown prince Karl and his friends. Allen stands in as Anastasia's proxy in a duel against five noble boys and wins with nonlethal rounds (ch. 22, vol. 5), and Anastasia's family takes him and his mother under its protection. After a summer in an elf village he proposes to her (vols. 6-7). The prince then forges the royal seal and hands Anastasia to the Esto Empire, where a cursed sword slowly breaks her mind (ch. 35-36, vols. 7-8); Allen rescues her with the wyverns and fights a war, her father's duchy declares independence, and Amy's mind-controlling "witch" blessing is broken. In the completed novel, Allen and Anastasia marry (Japanese Wikipedia; k-manga synopses).

## Life message

**Steady work, knowledge and loyalty can let a nobody rewrite a fate that looks fixed and protect the people he loves; treating other people as game pieces is the real villainy.**

| Meta lens | How it reads the series |
|---|---|
| Evolutionary biology | A son protects his mother first; a capable young male rises in status through skill and courage and wins a high-status mate. Rivals for the same young women (Karl and his friends around Amy) are humiliated or destroyed. Even the wyverns pair off only after the male proves himself in a fight (ch. 5-7). |
| Pragmatic | Allen plans like an engineer: he buys and resells goods, grinds levels in labyrinths, builds gliders, pistols and incendiaries, and uses nonlethal rounds and smoke in the duel. Reviewers praise that he earns his strength; critics find modern guns in a magic world lazy (MangaUpdates comments; MAL). |
| Religious | Gods are real in this world and hand out "blessings" (加護): the wind god blesses Allen, Anastasia becomes the "Ice Saint," and Amy's supposed "Saint of Compassion" power turns out to be a "Witch of Enchantment" blessing that lets her control minds (Japanese Wikipedia). A church and its bishop appear in later politics. Faith readers will weigh real gods and magic, a witch as the final villain, and the reported sexual content against a story of loyalty, honest work and a faithful couple. |
| Socio-cultural | A rigid class system where commoners may barely speak to nobles (NovelUpdates reviews) is challenged by merit but not overturned: Allen is finally adopted into the Ramslet name. The heroine of the otome genre is turned into the villain, a common reversal in Japanese "villainess" web fiction. |

Outlook: Hopeful — Allen saves his mother, wins the duel, rescues Anastasia and, in the finished novel, marries her, though the road runs through a kidnapping, a war and the near-destruction of Anastasia's mind.
Hope source: E — effort and growing stronger: the novel's own subtitle calls it a reversal story of "effort and pure love" (Kakuyomu), and reviewers single out that Allen earns his power (MAL); love between two (L) is the close second.

**Where traditions disagree.** Nearly every frame welcomes the core story: a poor boy's love for his mother, honest work, and loyalty to a wronged young woman. ## Creator and publisher context

- Kōtarō Isshiki (original story) writes isekai web fiction on Kakuyomu and Shōsetsuka ni Narō; his profile lists a dozen works, several completed, and notes this novel was a runner-up in the 2nd Earth Star Novel Grand Prize (Kakuyomu).
- Japanese Wikipedia reports that the author ended the Narō posting of the main story on December 31, 2021, citing continued online abuse, and kept only the epilogue there; the full text remains on Kakuyomu.
- Parum is credited with the original character designs; Sankichi Meguro draws the manga (AniList; MAL). No interviews with any of the three were found.
- Publisher: Earth Star Entertainment (Comic Earth Star web magazine; Earth Star Novel). English: emaqi (digital manga, R18+); Seven Seas Airship (light novel, from 2027).
- The author permitted a fan-written spin-off novel retelling the story from the reincarnated heroine's side (2024), in which Amy works to save Anastasia (Japanese Wikipedia).
- Neither the manga artist nor the publisher has explained the manga's added sexual content in sources opened. **Status: Needs more info on the creators.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** If you knew how the story ended, and it ended with your mother dead, could someone who is not even in the story change it? Allen is "Townsperson A," an unnamed extra, and the game's script runs toward a war that burns the capital.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, by work, knowledge and loyalty. Allen cleans sewers, studies, grinds labyrinths and builds tools for eight years so that he is ready when the condemnation comes (vols. 1-5). His success comes from preparation more than luck, and it is shared: he protects his mother, the townspeople and Anastasia.

**Characters as arguments.** Allen argues that effort beats destiny. Anastasia argues that duty can be noble, then learns to want something for herself. Amy, another reincarnated player, argues the opposite of Allen: that the other people are only characters in her game, and her "blessing" becomes literal mind control (Japanese Wikipedia). Karl argues for privilege without responsibility and loses everything.

**Symbols and motifs.** The ditch (sewer work), the sky (gliders and wyverns) and the ice princess in the manga's subtitle trace Allen's path from the bottom of the city to the sky and to Anastasia. The ring that takes a fatal blow for its wearer (vol. 9) and the sword that unlocks Anastasia's sealed mind (vol. 10) make love a protection.

**Traditions it echoes.** The isekai "villainess" genre and its reversals, the rags-to-riches fairy tale, and the knight who rescues a captive princess. Faith readers may hear echoes of honoring one's mother and the lowly raised up; the story itself credits effort and the gods' blessings.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Pragmatism and virtue are rewarded throughout. Traditional hierarchy is kept and joined, not overturned. Strength-rules thinking belongs to the villains. The gods are real but distant; the decisive acts are human ones.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-3 (ch. 1-15): childhood, work, wyverns and labyrinths. Vols. 4-6 (ch. 16-30): the academy, the heroine, the duel and the elf village; reported fan service and sex scenes begin in this stretch. Vols. 7-10 (ch. 31-50): betrayal, kidnapping, war and Anastasia's awakening. Vol. 11 (ch. 51-): the conflict with the brainwashed capital (manga ongoing).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Allen began by cleaning sewers. Why does the story start there?
2. Amy also remembers the game. What makes her choices different from Allen's?
3. Anastasia gave up her own wishes for her kingdom. Was she right?
4. Allen uses guns and nonlethal rounds. When is force the right answer?
5. Is it fair that Allen has to become a noble to marry Anastasia?
