**Premise.** Taku Kamishiro, a quiet second-year high schooler and class neat freak, plans to confess to his childhood friend Mika Hanazono, a popular, teasing girl. The night before, he steps in when a man is harassing a woman; the man warns him off or he will infect him with 'these feelings.' The next day Taku is seized by the urge to kill Mika, sees a vision of stabbing her throat with an umbrella, and tells her he hates her and wants her dead instead of confessing. With the urge come super strength, fast reflexes, near invulnerability and visions that come true only when they show him committing atrocities (Yattatachi, vol. 1). The cause is a spreading virus, ID, that turns love into bloodlust and gives the infected powers (MangaUpdates). The school is overrun at a bloody school festival (vol. 3); after a one-month time skip at ch. 9-10, Mika is missing and infected, and Taku joins Rudiment, a group of the infected led by Nave, to find her (vols. 4-5; MyAnimeList; Apple Books). They clash with CID, a rival organization with a squad of unusual killers, and with the Eguma Group, a religious cult that kidnaps and kills the infected. CID and the cult plan a massacre at a Christmas Eve event (vol. 8), and vol. 10 ends the English run with that massacre, Taku's meeting with the man behind the school attack, and his reunion with Mika (Kodansha USA; Apple Books). The Japanese -last- arc then follows Taku and Mika against his former allies in search of a cure (MangaUpdates).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What if loving someone made you want to kill them? Taku's crush becomes, overnight, a murderous compulsion. The series asks whether love is a feeling that happens to us or a choice we keep making.

**The answer the story gives.** A choice. Taku spends vol. 1 trying not to act (Yattatachi) and later learns to control and use his power (MyAnimeList). The story rewards those who hold their urges in check and condemns those who give in or exploit them.

**Characters as arguments.** Taku argues for restraint. Mika is the beloved whose safety tests it. Nave and Rudiment argue that the infected must protect each other. CID's Hayashi, who never cared about killing, argues for apathy and is changed by feeling. The Eguma Group argues for purging the infected and becomes the mass murderer.

**Symbols and motifs.** The umbrella of the first vision; the moon of the title, a symbol of love turned into a death sentence; the school festival and the Christmas Eve event, celebrations turned into massacres.

**Traditions it echoes.** Zombie and pandemic fiction (Yattatachi), superhero origin stories, and yandere romance, which it subverts. Morally it echoes the old idea of an inner evil inclination to be mastered: the yetzer hara, Cain warned that sin crouches at the door, the Buddhist account of craving turning to hatred.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Existential choice is the core; nihilism and might-makes-right are what the villains embody. Religion appears only as a murderous cult, which faith readers will notice. Hope rests on love, friendship and a cure.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-3 (to about ch. 9): the school, the first visions, the festival massacre; gore at its height. Vols. 4-7: after a time skip, Rudiment, CID and the search for Mika in a shonen-action style. Vols. 8-10: the cult and the Christmas Eve massacre, ending the English run. The Japanese -last- arc (5 volumes) continues to a cure and an ending not covered here.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Taku's feelings turn into the urge to hurt. What does he do to keep from acting on them?
2. Is love a feeling or a choice? What does the story say?
3. Why might people join a group like the Eguma Group when they are afraid?
4. How does the story treat people who are infected? Does that remind you of anything real?
5. The gore is heavy in the first volumes. What does it add, and what does it cost?
