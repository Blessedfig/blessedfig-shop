**Premise.** Ruu Kurokiya is a "kuro gyaru," a high school girl in the tanned, flashy gal style, and she is about to fail and repeat a year. Her childhood friend Tadao Tadashi, whose name is a pun on "straight, honest man," is a straight-A student and offers to tutor her. In elementary school it was Ruu who had the better grades and helped him; they were separated in middle school and meet again in high school, each secretly in love with the other (One Peace Books; MangaUpdates). Ruu uses the study sessions to try to fluster him with her looks and flirting, staking her "gyaru's pride" on it, but Tadao takes everything at face value, answers bluntly and earnestly, and is never embarrassed, so she ends up flustered instead (One Peace Books; ANN; Anime UK News). In return she teaches him pop culture such as bubble tea and ASMR (Otaku USA). Their friends Mashiro Yukiginu, who secretly draws a manga about the pair, and Ren Hamada, who secretly loves Mashiro, join in vol. 2 (Anime UK News). Publisher blurbs carry the rest: finals and a promised date (vol. 2); studying in Ruu's room until her worried mother steps in (vol. 3); a storm that keeps Ruu overnight at Tadao's house (vol. 4); and a school sleepover event where a confession finally brings them together (vol. 5) (One Peace Books; Comic Previews). MangaUpdates readers tag the ending Childhood Friends Become Lovers, and two tag the series as cut short.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can you win someone's heart by playing games with them? Ruu sets out to make Tadao blush and lose his composure, as gyaru heroines do in a popular strand of teasing romantic comedies. Tadao never does.

**The answer the story gives.** No: what wins is being known. Tadao takes Ruu at her word, praises her plainly, and keeps showing up to help her study; every tease backfires because he responds with honesty instead of embarrassment (ANN; Anime UK News). The final chapter's title says she still cannot lead him around (Kirapo), and the series ends with a confession (vol. 5, blurb).

**Characters as arguments.** Ruu argues for image and control: a look that keeps others guessing and a pride that must not lose. Tadao argues for sincerity: say what you mean and do the work. Their friends Mashiro and Ren mirror them with a quieter, hidden crush. Ruu's mother speaks for caution, stepping in when the two are alone (vol. 3).

**Symbols and motifs.** The study session as a stage for courtship; report cards and finals as the clock; the gyaru look as a mask over an earnest girl who once tutored him; the storm and the sleepover as tests (vols. 4-5).

**Traditions it echoes.** The teasing-girl romantic comedy (the "wants to tease" pattern in recent manga), the childhood-friends-to-lovers story, and the older moral comedy in which the plain-dealing hero cannot be tempted or tricked.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Virtue ethics wins quietly: honesty and patience are rewarded. Expressive individualism is explored through Ruu's style, which the story never condemns; it only shows the person under it. Hedonism appears as flirting over study and is corrected.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-2 are the classroom routine with friends added; vols. 3-4 move to Ruu's room and Tadao's house, where parents and a storm raise the stakes; vol. 5 brings the sleepover and the confession (blurbs). Two MangaUpdates readers tag it as cut short, so the ending may feel quick (to verify).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why do Ruu's teasing tricks never work on Tadao?
2. Ruu once helped Tadao with schoolwork, and now he helps her. What does that say about their friendship?
3. Ruu's mother is worried when they study alone in her room. Is she right? Why might a parent worry?
4. How does the art show Ruu, and is that the same as how Tadao sees her?
5. Is it ever kind to try to "lead someone around by the nose"?
