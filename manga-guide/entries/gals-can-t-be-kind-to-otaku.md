**Premise.** Takuya Seo, a loner high school student, secretly loves GlitterMon (Glittery Monpets), a monster anime aimed at young girls, and is afraid of being mocked for it. He sits behind two popular gyaru (fashionable "gal" girls) he sees as belonging to a different school "caste": outgoing Kotoko Ijichi and reserved Kei Amane. When his GlitterMon eraser comes up, Takuya slips and corrects them, and Amane shows she knows the show suspiciously well, claiming her little sister is the fan (ch. 1). The three become friends: Takuya draws GlitterMon with Amane, whose "little sister" is really Sayu, a neighbor girl who exposes her (v2); Ijichi, who cares for her younger brothers to help her mother, takes him to the movies and to a family beach barbecue (v2). Both girls fall for him while he stays oblivious. Later chapters (titles only) move through a school festival, a beauty contest, Christmas, Valentine's Day, a double date, "playing couple" and a one-year anniversary (MangaDex chapter index; Wikipedia; The Outerhaven).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can people from different social worlds be real friends? Takuya assumes popular gals would mock an otaku. The title's question mark is the story's answer.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, if someone stops hiding. Takuya's slip about GlitterMon (ch. 1) lets Amane's secret out too, and Ijichi turns out to have no interest in mocking anyone. The friendship rests on honesty, small loyalties (Takuya covering for Amane, v3) and families who make room (v2).

**Characters as arguments.** Takuya argues that fear of judgment keeps us lonely. Amane argues for the hidden self and slowly lets it show. Ijichi argues that kindness needs no shared hobby: she enjoys their joy (ANN, Eisenbeis). Sayu, a blunt child, argues for plain honesty and punctures Amane's cover story.

**Symbols and motifs.** The eraser; the GlitterMon merchandise; seat changes that move the triangle around (ch. 16); fireworks and an almost-confession (ch. 13).

**Traditions it echoes.** The recent wave of kind-gyaru romantic comedies, the Precure-style fandom ANN's Silverman compares it to, and the long tradition of school love triangles.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism is affirmed in its gentlest form: being honest about what you like. Consumerism is present but uncritical, since fandom is simply the language the friends share. Community wins over isolation.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-2 (ch. 1-13) set up the trio and summer. Volumes 3-4 (ch. 14-29) are the school festival. Volumes 5-8 (ch. 30-60, titles) run through birthdays, Christmas, Valentine's and White Day. Volumes 9-13 (ch. 61-101, titles) suggest the romance moves forward ("Gyaru Confession Meeting," "A Couple Date," "What is Dating," "Family Approval?!"); content not confirmed.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why did Takuya hide that he liked GlitterMon? Have you ever hidden something you liked?
2. What did Takuya assume about the gals, and what did they assume about him?
3. Ijichi doesn't care about anime but enjoys Takuya's excitement. Can friends share a joy without sharing a hobby?
4. Takuya keeps Amane's secret. When is keeping a secret loyal, and when is it a problem?
5. Is there a "caste" at your school? Who decides it?
