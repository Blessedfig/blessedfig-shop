**Premise.** Yonosuke Sakai, nicknamed Eeyuu ("hero"), is a first-year high school student who loves childhood-friend rom-com manga but is mortified that he now sees his childhood friends Shio Minamo (his next-door neighbor) and Akari Hiodoshi (a tsundere gyaru) in a sexual way, and is sure they would be horrified to know. In fact both girls like him and keep trying to turn life into a rom-com, while he dodges every chance so as not to spoil the friendship (ch. 1, v1; Kodansha blurb; Wikipedia). More childhood friends join: Runa Tsukimi, a year younger, who stays at his house and calls him big brother (v1-3); Haru Hinata, a track athlete he had thought was a boy (v4); Kozue Kogure, an older friend who has known him since birth (v7); Oriana Marigold, raised in America (v11); and finally Chika Tsuchimikado, a girl in Japanese dress who kidnaps him (v20). Along the way Shio confesses (v2), Haru confesses (v6), Shio struggles with a trauma from ten years before (v8) and runs away over her estranged father (v16), and Eeyuu confesses to Akari (v15-16) but cannot commit. During a winter study camp he ends up with all five as limited-time 'girlfriends' (v17-18), then goes on a one-day date with each (v19) and in the final volume confesses to one of them (v21; which one was not confirmed) (BookWalker volume blurbs).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can a friendship survive love? Eeyuu has read enough rom-coms to know how childhood friends are "supposed" to end up, and he is terrified that wanting it will ruin what he has. So he hides it, and the girls hide things too.

**The answer the story gives.** Hiding does not protect anyone. The blurbs track the cost: confessions half-heard, friends hurt by his indecision (v17), a harem that is happy, fun and painful at once (v18). The answer is to say what you feel and choose, which he does in the final volume (v21).

**Characters as arguments.** Eeyuu argues that romance would ruin friendship. Shio argues for going after love openly (and acts out rom-com tropes to do it). Akari argues, without meaning to, that pride and sharp words keep love locked up. Haru shows that a friendship can survive a confession (v6). Chika, the outsider, shows that the circle will close ranks to protect itself (v20).

**Symbols and motifs.** The nickname "hero" (Eeyuu), earned by rescuing the girls as children and tested by whether he can be brave now. The rom-com manga he reads. Seasonal events as stages: summer fireworks (v3), Christmas Eve (v6), cherry blossoms that bring back Shio's pain (v8).

**Traditions it echoes.** The harem rom-com and its childhood-friend type (the title parodies the genre); the love triangle; the "will they, won't they" school comedy.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarianism (the friend circle) and expressive individualism (say what you feel) pull against each other and are reconciled by honesty. Hedonism is played for laughs and is the main source of the series' fan service; postmodern parody frames it all.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-5 (ch. 1-41, the anime): the triangle with Shio and Akari, Runa and Haru join, the first confessions. Volumes 6-10: Christmas, New Year, Kozue, Shio's past, second year, the beauty pageant and track meet. Volumes 11-16: Oriana, summer, sports day, the school trip, Eeyuu's confession to Akari and Shio's family crisis. Volumes 17-21: study camp and five girlfriends, one-day dates, Chika, and the final confession.
