**Premise.** Fuuta Okeya, 14, a second-year middle school student (Seven Seas; ANN; English Wikipedia says high school), can see ghosts and hides a birthmark on his cheek. When transfer student Kouko Ishigami, who has a scar on her forehead and a ghost named East at her side, sees the mark, she declares him her enemy from past lives and attacks him with a flaming "spirit circle." Each strike sends Fuuta back into one of his earlier lives, where he and Kouko's incarnations hurt or killed each other: Fone, who tries to stop a village's human sacrifice and is beheaded; Vann, a knight who kills a "witch" on the Church's orders and is cursed; Flors, an architect building a sphinx in ancient Egypt; Houtarou, a blacksmith in feudal Japan; Lafalle, a worker in a 33rd-century tower of dying minds; Fuuko, a gender-swapped girl in an alternate 2016; and Fortuna, the far-future genius who made the spirit circles. Kouko means to destroy his soul so he can never be reborn. Fuuta, helped by the cheerful spirit girl Rune, tries instead to understand every life and end the cycle (Wikipedia; TV Tropes; Wrong Every Time; ANN).

## Life message

**Grudges carried from the past only end when people understand and forgive each other and themselves; however hard life gets, it is worth living, because something good will happen.**

| Meta lens | How it reads the series |
|---|---|
| Evolutionary biology | Kin and adoptive bonds carry each life: Vann raises an abandoned daughter, East raises Kouko, Lafalle and Lapis adopt Carol (TV Tropes; Wrong Every Time). Revenge is shown as the old reflex of a wronged animal; the story asks whether people can rise above it. |
| Pragmatic | Each life is a lesson Fuuta brings back to the present: patience with Kouko, gratitude, endurance. The cycle ends by negotiation (a bargain with the planet's will), not by winning a fight (TV Tropes). |
| Religious | Rebirth is literally true in this world: souls rise from rock to animal to human to higher beings (TV Tropes). Religious authorities often do harm: elders keep a human sacrifice for morale, a Church orders a herbalist killed as a witch. Yet priests also appear as flawed helpers (Vann's wayward priest companion). Faith readers weigh the strong forgiveness message against a reincarnation framework that differs from most Western faiths. |
| Socio-cultural | Human cruelty driven by fear, superstition and the search for meaning, not fate, keeps the cycle going (Wrong Every Time). Future lives raise science-fiction questions: banning genetic research, keeping minds in jars, "recycling" the spirits of the dead. |

Outlook: Hopeful — across tragic past lives, the present ends in reconciliation, the marks of the old grudge fade, and the spirits who helped Fuuta are reborn as humans (TV Tropes).
Hope source: R — forgiveness and redemption are the way through: Fuuta's empathy wears down Kouko's hatred, and the essays read forgiveness of the world and oneself as the core (Wrong Every Time; ComicsAlliance); Rune's promise that something good will happen adds a note of simple hope (J).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can a grudge outlive a life? Kouko remembers being killed by Fuuta's past selves, again and again, and wants his soul destroyed so it can never be reborn. Fuuta, a cheerful 14-year-old, has to decide whether he is responsible for what his past selves did, and whether anything can end a cycle that has lasted for ages.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, it can end, but only through understanding. By living each life from the inside, Fuuta learns how cruelty started (fear, obedience, superstition, grief) and learns to forgive himself as well as Kouko. Wrong Every Time sums up the series as a call to forgive the world and ourselves, and Rune's answer to Fuuta's despair is that something good will happen.

**Characters as arguments.** Fuuta argues that empathy is stronger than justice-as-revenge. Kouko argues that wrongs must be paid for, and the story takes her seriously before she lets go. Vann is the soldier who obeys an unjust order and pays with a ruined life, then is partly redeemed by raising a child. Flors shows a life that is neither tragic nor triumphant, just messy and regretful. Houtarou and Iwana fight to the death and then find peace by letting go. Lafalle and Lapis show the same tragedy leading two people to opposite choices. Fortuna, the genius who made the spirit circles, is the final argument for power over life and death, and he loses.

**Symbols and motifs.** The spirit circle itself (blue flames for Fuuta, pink for Kouko). Fuuta's birthmark and Kouko's scar, the marks of the grudge, which fade at the end. Flors' sphinx with a cat's face. The minds in glass jars in the Sleeping Tower. The final chapter, titled for Fuuta as if a new first life begins.

**Traditions it echoes.** Reincarnation stories (Osamu Tezuka's Phoenix is the obvious ancestor; from general knowledge; to verify), Buddhist teaching on grudges and rebirth, and the Western witch-hunt and sacrifice stories. Christian and Jewish readers will hear the call to forgive and to give up vengeance; Buddhist readers, the release of attachment.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Buddhist ideas and pacifism are affirmed in spirit: letting go ends suffering, and revenge only extends the wheel. Religious authority is critiqued wherever it demands blood. Utilitarian and transhumanist projects (sacrifice the many for one; master death) are shown as the villain's path.

**Shifts across the series.** Volume 1: Fone's and Vann's lives and Kouko's attacks. Volume 2: Vann's end and Flors' life; Fuuta's breakdown. Volumes 3-4: Houtarou and Iwana's feud in feudal Japan, then Lafalle and Lapis in the far future. Volumes 5-6: Fuuko's peaceful life, Fortuna, the takeover of Fuuta's body and the ending (volume placement partly from TV Tropes order; to verify).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Kouko was really wronged in her past lives. Was she right to want revenge? What changed her mind?
2. Vann killed a woman because his superiors told him to. Is following orders ever an excuse?
3. Houtarou and Iwana fought to the death but died at peace. What did they let go of?
4. Fuuta asks why we are alive, and Rune says something good will happen. Is that enough of an answer?
5. Is there an old grudge in your family, school or town? What would it take to end it?
