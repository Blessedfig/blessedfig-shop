**Age note for reviewers (not a Content warning paragraph).** The central romance is between Mafuyu (15-17 during the story, fan wiki) and her homeroom teacher Takaomi (22, fan wiki), who was her childhood neighbor. Sources describe it as a slow, subtle romance (MangaUpdates categories) that is only confirmed in the epilogue after Mafuyu has graduated, where the two are adults and he is expected to propose in three years (Manga Bookshelf, vol. 29). No source opened reports a kiss or anything sexual between them while she is a student; the teacher's "occasional bit of sexual harassment" in vols. 1-2 (Manga Report) is a gag that should be checked in print. On current evidence the content-warning threshold is not met.

**Premise.** Mafuyu Kurosaki was the bancho (delinquent gang leader) of East High in Saitama until her followers fled a police raid and she was caught and expelled. Her mother sends her to Midorigaoka Academy, a private school on a mountain that takes students expelled elsewhere, to live alone in an apartment, and warns that she will be disowned if she ever fights again (ch. 1). Mafuyu wants to become a "normal," girly high school student, but her homeroom teacher, Takaomi Saeki, turns out to be the delinquent neighbor who taught her to fight when she was little (ch. 3). Takaomi has bet the school chairman that he can double enrollment in three years, or work five years unpaid (ch. 5); the school once belonged to his grandfather, who was tricked out of it (fan wiki). He drafts Mafuyu and the blond loner Hayasaka into a new Public Morals Club (ch. 6) to clean up the school. To keep her promise, Mafuyu fights in secret as "Super Bun" in a rabbit mask or cross-dressed as a male delinquent, "Natsuo" (ch. 8). The club takes on the school bancho Okegawa (who turns out to be her pen pal, ch. 9) and then the Student Council of the chairman's son, Miyabi Hanabusa, whose members are won over one by one. In the final year Takaomi resigns after an incident (vol. 23-24), a new teacher, Mr. Maki, is tied to Mafuyu's lost childhood memories (vol. 25), and the club saves the school festival and the school's future (vol. 29). An epilogue shows the cast as adults after graduation (Wikipedia; fan wiki; VIZ; Manga Bookshelf).

## Life message

**Loyalty and friendship can turn rough kids and a failing school around; you can keep your promises and protect others without giving up who you are.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can a person change without becoming someone else? Mafuyu has promised her mother never to fight again, but she is a fighter to her bones, and her new school is full of fights. The series asks how she can keep her word and still be herself.

**The answer the story gives.** She keeps her promise in letter by hiding behind a rabbit mask and a boy's uniform, and in spirit by fighting only for others. Her strength becomes protection, and her real power turns out to be friendship: rivals become friends, and the club saves the school.

**Characters as arguments.** Mafuyu argues for loyalty and a good heart under a rough surface. Takaomi argues that results matter more than rules, and that the people you love are worth any trick; the story finds him both admirable and troubling. Hayasaka argues for facing every fight head-on. Hanabusa argues for control by charm, and loses to friendship. Runa, Kanon and Ayabe show how hurt children harden, and how friendship softens them (Wikipedia).

**Symbols and motifs.** The rabbit mask (Mafuyu wanted to be a bunny as a child, fan wiki); the chain necklace from her old gang; carrier pigeons and pen pals; cross-dressing as both disguise and freedom; lost childhood memories recovered in the final year.

**Traditions it echoes.** Japanese yankii manga and school comedies, turned upside down by a girl bancho. For Christian readers it can echo the prodigal's second chance and the call to keep one's word; for Confucian readers, filial duty and the teacher-student bond, though Takaomi is no model teacher.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarianism and classical virtues win; might makes right is undone by friendship; expressive individualism is played as comedy (girly hopes, cute-loving tough guys, cross-dressing) rather than argued.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-5 (ch. 1-28): founding the club and facing the bancho. Vols. 6-22 (ch. 29-128): the long Student Council conflict and sister schools (details thin in sources). Vols. 23-29 (ch. 129-170): the final year, Takaomi's resignation, Mr. Maki and Mafuyu's memories, the festival and the ending.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Mafuyu promised her mother not to fight. Did she keep her promise? What does keeping your word really mean?
2. Why do so many of the Student Council members have painful family stories? How does the club help them?
3. Takaomi is funny and protective but also bends rules and pushes students around. Is he a good teacher?
4. The story waits until after graduation for the romance. Why do you think the author chose that?
5. Who in your life is a "rival" who could become a friend?
