**Premise.** It is their last year at Touichikou, an all-boys high school. Rihito Sajō, studious, reserved and anxious, and Hikaru Kusakabe, an easygoing guitarist in an amateur band, have been dating since the previous year. In Winter, Sajō's mother is hospitalized with a tumor; the stress overwhelms him, and he must learn to lean on Kusakabe (Seven Seas; AIPT). Sajō introduces Kusakabe to his mother as his boyfriend, and is accepted to study pharmacology at Kyoto University, while Kusakabe means to stay in Tokyo for music and plays a festival in Nîmes, France, that keeps him away until just before graduation (Wikipedia). Their music teacher, Hara, is attracted to Sajō (AIPT; fan wiki). In Spring, the couple quarrel over the future; at graduation they visit the classroom where they met, face their diverging plans, and Sajō finally says he loves Kusakabe. They have sex there, and Kusakabe proposes that they marry when they turn twenty, which Sajō accepts (Wikipedia; StoryGraph reader review).

## Creator and publisher context

- Asumiko Nakamura debuted in 2000 in Monthly Manga F (later Manga Erotics F), an erotic comics magazine where she had drawn stories about male homosexuality; Classmates was her first boys' love series and first in a BL magazine, created after Opera's editors approached her (Wikipedia).
- On-record intent (Wikipedia, citing the 2011 official fanbook and interviews): she wanted a slow, serious love that was almost clichéd, chose the all-boys school setting knowingly, and wanted to balance BL's exaggerated romance with realism about LGBT issues and identity. Critics note Classmates is softer than her darker, more erotic works such as Double Mints.
- Her work outside BL includes the mystery Utsubora (Vertical) and collaborations with director Kunihiko Ikuhara (Wikipedia).
- No statements on religion or politics were found.
- Publisher: Akane Shinsha (Opera). English: Seven Seas, which rates the two volumes Teen and Older Teen.
- No controversies involving the creator were found.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What happens to first love when school ends? Sajō and Kusakabe fell in love in a classroom. Now one is bound for Kyoto and pharmacology, the other for Tokyo and a guitar, and Sajō's mother is ill. The volumes ask whether a love can survive two people growing up in different directions.

**The answer the story gives.** It can, if both are honest. Sajō must learn to rely on someone else instead of carrying everything alone (Seven Seas, Winter). Kusakabe must stop dodging the question of his future (fan wiki). At graduation, in the room where they met, Sajō finally says the words he has held back, and they promise to marry at twenty (Wikipedia).

**Characters as arguments.** Sajō argues for duty and caution; the story shows how fear makes him lonely. Kusakabe argues for openness and following one's heart; the story shows that he, too, avoids hard questions. Hara argues, without meaning to, for the danger of an adult who confuses his own loneliness with a student's need (AIPT; Anime Feminist; a StoryGraph reader reads him with sympathy). Sajō's mother, meeting her son's boyfriend in the hospital, stands for acceptance.

**Symbols and motifs.** The empty classroom where it all began. Gloves bought for winter (Winter ch. 3). Food in the Spring chapter titles (okonomiyaki, fried egg, grilled mochi, barbecue), small domestic pleasures. Kyoto as the distance to be crossed.

**Traditions it echoes.** The school romance and the graduation story; the BL genre's conventions, which Nakamura uses on purpose and tempers with realism (Wikipedia). For religious readers, the themes of faithfulness and honoring a parent are familiar, set inside a romance many traditions do not affirm.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism and progressive acceptance are affirmed; human love is the source of meaning. Filial duty is present and respected.

**Shifts across the series.** Winter (8 chapters): the mother's illness, coming out to her, college plans, Hara's interest. Spring (8 chapters): arguments over the future, Kyoto, graduation, the proposal. The series continues in Sora and Hara, O.B. and Blanc (Wikipedia).
