**Premise.** Arihito Atobe, an overworked office worker (an ex-"corporate slave," Yen Press), dies in a bus crash and is reborn in the Labyrinth Country, a city of monster-filled dungeons. Among the others reborn with him are his feared workaholic boss, Kyouka Igarashi, and two younger passengers, Suzuna and Misaki (Wikipedia). He writes "Rearguard" on his job form and gets a unique class that powers up allies from behind but cannot protect himself (vol. 4). He hires Theresia, a silent lizard-woman: demi-humans are humans cursed after dying in a labyrinth and are rented out as mercenaries or slaves who can only obey (anime ep. 1). Arihito treats her kindly, pays to keep her (ep. 2-4), and makes breaking her curse his goal. His party grows: Kyouka, abandoned by her first party; Elitia, the "Death Sword" berserker; Suzuna, a shrine maiden; Misaki, a gambler he rescues from seekers who used her as bait (ep. 3; vol. 2); then Madoka the merchant and Melissa the dissector. They awaken Ariadne, a sealed "Hidden God," and become her devotees (ep. 6; vol. 4), win promotion to District 7 (vols. 5-7), and in vols. 9-11 take on Beyond Liberty, a top alliance hoarding the best hunting grounds, with Four Seasons, an all-female party (Yen Press; BookWalker). All the women grow attracted to Arihito (Wikipedia; Manga Bookshelf).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What is strength, if the strongest man is the one who never stands in front? Arihito's class cannot protect himself; it only makes others stronger.

**The answer the story gives.** Strength is support: listening, planning and making each person better. The old office taught Arihito that supporting others meant being used; the new world rewards it. He also refuses to treat Theresia as property, though the world does.

**Characters as arguments.** Arihito argues for servant leadership. Kyouka, the former tyrant boss, argues that authority can be humbled and become partnership. Elitia argues that a curse (her sword's bloodlust) can be mastered with help. Theresia, silent, argues by her loyalty that a person is still inside the "property." Bergen and Beyond Liberty argue for taking, and lose.

**Symbols and motifs.** The back line; the silent lizard mask over a human face; cursed weapons that cannot be put down; contribution points as worth that can be measured; a sealed goddess missing her parts.

**Traditions it echoes.** The isekai game-world, the party-building RPG, the harem comedy, and the old story of the master who frees a slave. Some Christian readers may hear the call to lead by serving; Jewish and Muslim readers may hear teaching on freeing captives. The harem framing cuts against those same readings.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarianism and virtue ethics are affirmed. Hedonism appears as harem comedy and is never judged. Pagan elements are game mechanics. Might makes right belongs to the villains.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-4: forming the party, freeing Theresia, Ariadne. Vols. 5-7: the stampede, promotion and the Shrieking Wood rescue. Vols. 8-11: a living weapon and the fight with Beyond Liberty beside Four Seasons; the light novels' later volumes (reviewed by Manga Bookshelf) bring Theresia's curse to a crisis and Elitia's brother as a villain.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Arihito's power only works for others. Is that a weakness or a strength?
2. The world treats Theresia as property. How does Arihito treat her differently, and does buying her contract make her free?
