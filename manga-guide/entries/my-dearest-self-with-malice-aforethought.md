**Premise.** Eiji Urashima is a 20-year-old university student, shy and cheerful, who has never had a girlfriend. He hides one fact: his birth father, Makoto Hachinoi, was named as "LL," a serial killer who tortured and killed young women 15 years earlier and then died in a fire. Eiji wakes after a night of drinking to find Kyoka Yukimura, a beauty-contest candidate, in his bed, saying they are dating; he has lost three days. Friends describe things he does not remember. A quiet classmate, Rei Shinmyoji, tells him he has a second personality, which she names "B1." Cash, a bloodied metal bat and a copycat LL murder of a woman, Yoko Hatanaka, follow, and Eiji investigates himself. B1 turns out to be investigating the LL case to clear his father and take revenge on the real killer, working among a gang (SKALL) that runs a prostitution ring (Japanese Wikipedia; ciatr; Kodansha synopsis on AniList and Apple Books).

## Life message

**What a person is comes from what he chooses, not from his father's blood or his own past: hate and revenge pass harm from one person to the next ("malice begets malice," as one AniList reviewer puts it), and the way out is to seek the truth and refuse to become a killer.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Are we made by our blood, by our past, or by our choices? Eiji fears that being "LL's son" makes him a killer. The story then raises the stakes: it suggests he may already be one, without knowing it.

**The answer the story gives.** Blood does not decide. The man everyone called a monster was innocent; the kind adoptive father who raised Eiji was the killer (ciatr). Yet the past does shape people. Eiji's gentle self was a persona created by a traumatized boy, B1, to carry what he could not bear. In the end B1, who has lived for revenge, chooses not to kill the real LL, even though that killer wants to be killed. He gives him to the law and serves his own sentence. The answer is that a person can refuse to pass malice on, but not without loss: the Eiji persona does not survive.

**Characters as arguments.** B1 is the argument for revenge, and later for restraint. Eiji is the argument for gentleness and acceptance; one AniList reviewer reads his last act, accepting Kyoka after learning she manipulated him, as the most human moment in the book. Kyoka is the argument that suffering can turn into worship of violence: abused as a child, she saw her sister's killer as the one who freed her, and sought out Eiji hoping he would become a killer too. Kiichi, who feels no pain, is the argument that a man without feeling is the true monster. The corrupt police argue that institutions can protect the guilty. Rei argues for facing the past: she warns Eiji that running from it will bring disaster (Japanese Wikipedia).

**Symbols and motifs.** The title, a letter "to my dear self" written "with malice aforethought," frames the split self as both addressee and threat. Lost days and missing memory. The bloodied bat and the stash of cash. Eiji's habit of touching his ear, which returns in B1 at the end as a sign that Eiji lives on (ciatr). The client list that ties respectable men to the prostitution ring.

**Traditions it echoes.** Dr. Jekyll and Mr. Hyde, which one AniList reviewer names; Japanese crime fiction about wrongful conviction and the families of criminals; the "unreliable self" thriller. Fans compare its mood to Shuzo Oshimi's psychological manga.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Existentialism is the frame: identity is made, not inherited. Nihilism appears in Kiichi and is judged. Critical views of police and institutions are given evidence in the plot. No religious lens appears. Feminist readers would note both the exposure of sexual exploitation and the use of graphically murdered women as plot material.

**Shifts across the series.** The early volumes are a mystery told from Eiji's side, about lost days and a copycat murder in the SKALL underworld. After the Kyoka reveal, the story switches to B1 as narrator and retells events from his childhood on (AniList reviews), turning into a revenge thriller about the LL case and the police. The final volumes reveal the real LL, then jump ahead to the trial and B1's release.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Eiji was treated as guilty because of his father. Is it ever fair to judge someone by their family?
2. Why do you think B1 chose not to kill the real LL, even when he asked to be killed?
3. Kyoka saw a killer as someone who "saved" her. How can suffering twist what a person admires?
4. Some readers say the story uses a mental illness mostly as a shock twist. Do you agree? What would a fairer portrayal look like?
5. The title talks about "malice aforethought" toward oneself. What does it mean to be your own enemy?
