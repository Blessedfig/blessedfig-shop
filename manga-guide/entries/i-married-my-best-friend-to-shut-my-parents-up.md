**Premise.** Machi Morimoto, an office worker (early thirties per Wikipedia; late twenties per ANN), has spent her life trying to meet her parents' demands, and now they want her married to a man. Her friend and former junior Hana Agaya, an out lesbian who has loved Machi since their teens and needs a place to live, proposes a sham marriage: the two move in together and register under Shibuya's same-sex partnership certificate. When they show the certificate to Machi's parents, the parents react with anger, accuse Hana of corrupting their daughter and order them out (ANN). Through shared daily life, the return of Hana's ex-girlfriend Ayaka and Machi's growing jealousy, Machi realizes her feelings, stands up to her mother and chooses a real relationship with Hana (Wikipedia; ANN; Lesbrary; Smart Bitches). The volume closes with a separate short story about two girls on a track team (Okazu).

## Life message

**A marriage worth having rests on real love and freely given commitment, and a grown daughter may choose her own life and partner even when her parents reject that choice.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What makes a marriage real: the paperwork, the parents' blessing, or the love between two people? Machi agrees to a marriage that is meant to be fake, and finds it becoming the truest thing in her life.

**The answer the story gives.** Love freely given makes the marriage; approval from outside does not. Machi grows from a woman who always did what was expected into someone who knows what she wants and says so (Otaku USA; Smart Bitches). The cost is her relationship with her parents, which the story does not repair (ANN).

**Characters as arguments.** Machi argues that you can discover yourself late. Hana argues for patient, open love, though reviewers say she sometimes pushes too hard (ANN; Lesbrary). The parents argue for duty and convention, but only as pressure and anger, so their side is not given a real case (Otaku USA). Ayaka's return tests whether the bond is real.

**Symbols and motifs.** The partnership certificate: a real document used for a fake marriage, then made true. The shared bed and meals as the slow building of a home. Jealousy as the moment Machi admits her feelings.

**Traditions it echoes.** The fake-relationship romance and the "marriage first, love later" story, here applied to two women. Okazu reads it as yuri comedy more than an LGBT issues story.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism and progressivism win; traditional marriage expectations and filial duty are the obstacles. Reviewers bring in feminist concerns about consent that the story itself passes over lightly.

**Shifts across the series.** A single volume: the setup and move-in (early chapters), the visit to the parents and the fallout, Ayaka's return, and the turn to a real relationship, followed by the bonus "Fake Married Life" and an unrelated track-team short (AniList; Okazu). Chapter-level order not confirmed.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Machi agreed to a fake marriage to stop her parents' pressure. What else could she have done?
2. Hana keeps trying even after Machi says no. When is that romantic, and when is it not okay?
3. What do grown children owe their parents, and what do parents owe their grown children?
4. The story starts with a lie and ends with honesty. What changed?
5. Is a marriage made by paperwork, by family approval, or by the people in it?
