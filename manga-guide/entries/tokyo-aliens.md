**Premise.** Japan is a top-ten destination for aliens, who live among humans as students, tourists and workers; a secret police agency, the A.M.O., manages them (Square Enix). Akira Gunji, 15, an awkward first-year with the worst grades in his year, wants to be a police officer like his late father Akio. On a train he sees a fight between an alien and his classmate Sho Tenkubashi, the A.M.O.'s ace, and is carried off by the alien, Tomiko, who has overstayed her visa to stay beside her dying human husband; Akira sympathizes and confronts Sho (Anime UK News). Given the choice of having his memory erased or joining, he joins Special Riot Police Unit 3 under the charming, manipulative Reiji Amamiya, and learns that his father was an A.M.O. agent killed nine years ago by the alien Hakugin while saving the young Sho and his sister (fan wiki; OASG). Sho, turned into an alien himself by that attack, believes Akira somehow saved him, perhaps through a time loop; Akira's own body may host an alien power. Missions follow: guarding aliens, a black-market auction of alien creatures, a wanted alien girl, Raika, and her weaponized double, and a ring that abducts and sells humans, in which Akira is captured and experimented on (Square Enix vols. 2-10).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** When the law says someone does not belong, who are they still? Akira's first case is an alien who has overstayed her visa to stay with her dying husband; his whole career begins with deciding she deserves sympathy (Anime UK News).

**The answer the story gives.** Protect people, not categories. Akira joins a police agency to protect others as his father did, his weapon is a shield, and he takes the side of a fugitive he believes innocent even against his partner (Square Enix vol. 6). The story's villains are the ones who treat beings as goods: smugglers and traffickers (vols. 5, 9-10).

**Characters as arguments.** Akira argues for mercy and courage. Sho argues for duty, wounded by self-hatred; the partnership softens him. Amamiya argues that peace justifies using people, and the story gives him a tragic reason rather than calling him simply evil (fan wiki). Tomiko argues that love can outweigh the rules.

**Symbols and motifs.** The shield gloves Akira inherits from his father; the train where his old life ends; memory erasure as the price of not joining; the photo of father and son in Akira's train pass (fan wiki).

**Traditions it echoes.** Men in Black and secret-agency fiction; shonen partner stories; the police procedural (ICv2). Faith readers will hear the biblical command to love the stranger in Tomiko's case.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Virtue (courage, loyalty) is affirmed; the agency's authoritarian and utilitarian methods are questioned but not overthrown; progressive sympathy for migrants and same-sex attraction is present without argument.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-4 (ch. 0-18): Akira joins, the bodyguard and assassin missions, the mystery of his father and of Sho's past. Vols. 5-8 (ch. 19-38): black markets, Amamiya's past, Raika and her double, Natsuki and Chizuru. Vols. 9-10 (ch. 39-48 and on): human trafficking, Akira's capture and experiments, and the question of the alien inside him. Vols. 11-13 not covered by sources opened.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Tomiko broke the rules to stay with her dying husband. Was she wrong?
2. Amamiya lies to Akira for a good cause. Can a good cause justify lying?
3. Akira's weapon is a shield. Why might the author have chosen that?
4. What does Akira learn about his father, and how does it change him?
5. The villains sell living beings. Why is treating people as things so serious?
