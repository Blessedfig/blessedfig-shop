**Premise.** Ten horror short stories. In "Billions Alone" (1), groups of people are found murdered, naked and sewn together with fishing line across Japan, while a recluse who has not left his room in seven years links the killings to radio broadcasts urging the lonely to be friendlier (Comics Beat; Anime UK News). "The Human Chair" (2) follows a woman novelist who comes to believe a man is living inside her chair, and her husband dismisses her (ANN). In "An Unearthly Love" (3), a wife in an arranged marriage finds her husband secretly visiting a doll in a storeroom (ANN). The title story (4) has Mariko, the daughter of a UFO club's founder, fade from the sight of the men who adore her, and the men turn violent over who may claim her (ANN; Anime UK News). "The Licking Woman" (5) kills people with a poisonous tongue; a woman named Miko coats herself in cyanide to stop her (Anime UK News). "Master Umezz and Me" (6) is a light autobiographical chapter about Ito's love of horror artist Kazuo Umezz. In story 7, a misanthropic professor who scorns a would-be writer is haunted after she tries to drown herself (Anime UK News). In "The Enigma of Amigara Fault" (8), an earthquake exposes human-shaped holes in a mountain and people feel called to enter "their" hole (But Why Tho; ANN). "The Sad Tale of the Principal Post" (9) is a short, nearly comic tale (Otaku USA), and in "Keepsake" (10) a man learns his dead wife gave birth in her grave (Anime UK News; ANN).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What happens when a longing (to be loved, to belong, to be near someone) grows past all limits?

**The answer the story gives.** It devours. The men of the UFO club want Mariko so badly that they stop seeing her (story 4). A man wants to be close to women so badly that he becomes furniture (story 2). The lonely of story 1 are forced into a monstrous togetherness. The Amigara holes call each person to a place made "just for them," and the place is a trap (story 8). The collection does not offer a remedy; it shows the cost.

**Characters as arguments.** The novelist of story 2, who is not believed, argues for trusting a person's account of her own experience (ANN). Professor Kirida, who scorns love, is haunted by it (story 7). The husband who loves a doll (story 3) shows a heart that cannot love a real person. Miko (story 5) is the rare fighter. Ito himself (story 6) is the one warm presence: a boy who loved scary stories and a mentor he admired.

**Symbols and motifs.** Eyes and blind spots (story 4); chairs, dolls and pillars: people turned into objects and objects that hold people; holes shaped exactly like you (story 8); thread that sews strangers together (story 1).

**Traditions it echoes.** Edogawa Ranpo and Robert Hichens (adapted directly), Lovecraft's cosmic dread (from general knowledge), Kazuo Umezz's horror manga, and Japanese folk legends of sacrifice.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Existentialism and nihilism shape the mood: people face an unexplained universe and lose. Critics bring a feminist lens (ANN), finding both a critique of objectification and a fear of women. Spirits appear as folk horror, not as a religion.

**Shifts across the series.** Not a series: ten separate stories from different years, from 1990s classics (Amigara, Principal Post, first in English with Gyo) to newer pieces (Billions Alone, Venus, Licking Woman, Keepsake, new to English in 2020 per Anime UK News).

**Discussion questions.**
1. In the title story, why can't the men see Mariko? What does that say about how they see her?
2. Why is the woman in "The Human Chair" not believed? Has anyone ever not believed you?
3. Why do the people at Amigara Fault walk into the holes even though they are afraid?
4. Which story frightened you most, and what real fear is underneath it?
5. Is there anything hopeful in the book? Why do you think Ito included the Umezz chapter?
