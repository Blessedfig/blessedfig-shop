**Premise.** Kasane Fuchi is mocked all her life for her face, while her late mother Sukeyo was a celebrated beauty and actress. Sukeyo left her a lipstick: when Kasane wears it and kisses someone, they swap faces for about twelve hours. In grade school she takes her bully Ichika's face to play Cinderella; afterward the two struggle on a roof and Ichika falls to her death, leaving Kasane with a scarred cheek (v1; Wikipedia). In high school she drugs a kind senior, Iku, to take her role (v1). Years later Sukeyo's old director, Kingo Habuta, pairs Kasane with Nina Tanzawa, a beautiful actress with no talent and a sleeping disorder, who lends her face so "Nina" can become a star. Nina grows bitter, tries to kill herself and is left in a coma kept alive in Kasane's home, and Kasane takes her name (v2-6). Kasane's half-sister Nogiku, who escaped years of abuse by their father, wants revenge, then becomes Kasane's new face ("Saki") and rival (v4-11). The truth emerges that the woman known as Sukeyo was Izana, who stole the real Sukeyo's face, and that Kasane is Izana's daughter (v9-13). In the end Kasane gives up the lipstick and performs under her own face, but Nina's mother takes Kasane's body by the lipstick and dies, leaving Kasane in an older body, charged with a murder and living alone; Habuta finds her, and Nogiku finds some happiness (v14; TV Tropes; renote).

## Life message

**Beauty, talent and a self cannot be stolen: living through borrowed faces destroys others and yourself, and only by standing as yourself, scars and all, can you be whole, though the cost of what you did still comes due.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** If you could take someone else's face, would you still be you? Kasane has a great actor's gift and a face others will not look at. The lipstick seems to solve the injustice of a world that judges by looks, and the story asks what it costs her and the people whose faces she wears.

**The answer the story gives.** A stolen face cannot make a self. Every borrowing takes something from someone (Ichika's life, Iku's role, Nina's career and life, Nogiku's revenge turned into a new captivity), and Kasane grows more willing to cross lines as her beauty gives her confidence (TV Tropes). Only when she gives up the lipstick and stands on stage as herself does she find peace, and even then the past comes due (v14).

**Characters as arguments.** Kasane argues that talent deserves the stage whatever her face. Nina argues the reverse case: beauty without talent is its own prison. Habuta argues for art above morality and corrupts her (TV Tropes). Nogiku, the most wronged, argues for revenge and is talked toward a new life by Amagasaki. Izana is the end of the road Kasane is on: a woman who stole a whole life.

**Symbols and motifs.** The red lipstick and the kiss. Masks and mirrors. The scar on Kasane's cheek. The plays: Cinderella (the transformed girl), The Seagull (the failed actress), Salome (desire and death), Macbeth (ambition and blood). The Pleiades (Subaru) and the planetarium in the late chapters.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Edo ghost story of Kasane, The Phantom of the Opera and Black Swan (MAL reviewers), and classical tragedy (Aristotle, Macbeth, cited by readers). For Christian readers it echoes 1 Samuel 16:7 and the warning that envy rots the bones (Proverbs 14:30); for Buddhist readers, craving and the illusion of a fixed self.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Existentialism is the frame: the self is what you choose to stand behind. Feminism and critical theory are present as a critique of beauty standards, without slogans. Consumerism and expressive individualism are critiqued as roads to ruin. Karma appears in a chapter title and in the way every act returns.

**Shifts across the series.** Volume 1 (ch. 1-8) is childhood and high school: Ichika, Iku and the first thefts. Volumes 2-6 (ch. 9-53) are the Nina years and her collapse. Volumes 4-11 (ch. 27-98) bring in Nogiku and the "Saki" identity. Volumes 9-13 (ch. 72-116) reveal Izana's past and the truth about Kasane's mother. Volume 14 (ch. 117-126) is the final performance and its cost.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Kasane was bullied for her face. Does that excuse what she did with the lipstick?
2. Nina agreed to lend her face. Who was using whom?
3. What does the story say beauty is worth, and what talent is worth?
4. Nogiku wanted revenge. What helped her choose something else?
5. Kasane finally performs with her own face. Why does that matter, even with the ending she gets?
