**Premise.** In June, Yuri Kariya, a dreamy, single-minded second-year at Kagachi High, confesses to Ryuhei Aono from the next class, and they start dating. Two weeks later he dies in a car accident. As she prepares to follow him in death, his ghost appears and asks her to live, and she agrees (Wikipedia; Japanese Wikipedia; ANN). They carry on a relationship in which they cannot touch; she pretends to be on the phone when she talks to him. When Yuri suggests he try possessing her, he does, briefly; from then on a darker side, "Black Aono," appears, tries to possess people around her and pulls Yuri toward the other world. Possession ends when the host's body has an "impurity" such as a nosebleed or menstruation (ANN). When Aono possesses his best friend Masayoshi Fujimoto, another student is hurt (Japanese Wikipedia), and Fujimoto, Yuri and Mio Horie, a shut-in classmate who loves horror films, begin investigating (Wikipedia). Later arcs (Kodansha USA copy; ciatr): Yuri finds she is bound to Aono by a "sacrificial contract" that changes her body (her hair turns white, her eyes red); a summer job at an after-school program draws her into the Yotsukubi-sama ritual, in which fourth graders wished back a dead little brother (vols. 4-7); Aono's younger brother Teppei and the ghost of their mother Hitomi reveal a painful childhood (vols. 5, 9, 11); classmates stage a ritual at the culture festival to summon Aono and offer Yuri (vols. 10-12); Aono gains a living body while Yuri weakens, the two sharing one heart (vol. 13). On Yuri's birthday a storm traps the town, and in the spirit world Yuri is told to kill Aono to save herself; she refuses and is stabbed. Her friends and the townspeople lend her their lives; she reaches Aono and takes her life back, he confesses his feelings, they dance, and he dies without taking her with him. In the epilogue she wakes in a world without him and chooses to live (Apple Books vol. 14 copy; ciatr).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What does love owe the dead, and what does it owe the living? The title states Yuri's first answer: she would die to touch him.

**The answer the story gives.** Love that clings becomes a curse, for both. Aono's first act as a ghost is to tell Yuri to live (ch. 1); his last is to let her go (vol. 14). Between them, every attempt to hold on (possession, rituals, contracts) costs blood or life. Yuri's own growth is to refuse to kill even to save herself, and then to accept life without him.

**Characters as arguments.** Yuri argues for total devotion. Black Aono shows what devotion becomes when it is driven by an old wound. Fujimoto argues for loyal love that does not take. Mio argues for understanding through investigation. Aono's mother Hitomi shows how neglect is passed down (vol. 11).

**Symbols and motifs.** Touch, which they cannot have; blood as the impurity that breaks possession (ANN); Yuri's hair turning white and eyes red as the price of the contract; one shared heart (vol. 13); a dance at the end.

**Traditions it echoes.** Japanese ghost tales of the dead who cannot let go (and the living who cannot), the Orpheus and Izanagi myths of following a beloved into death, and the horror genre's warning against careless rituals. Many religious readers will hear their own teaching that despair must be resisted and the dead entrusted to another care.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Existential questions of love and death are explored openly. The occult is real and dangerous. Communitarian rescue wins at the end. Expressive, all-consuming love is critiqued.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-2: a strange, funny ghost romance turns to horror. Vols. 3-4: family backstory and the sacrifice contract. Vols. 4-7: the Yotsukubi-sama ritual with the children. Vols. 8-9: Yuri changed by the contract; Aono's mother's ghost. Vols. 10-13: the culture festival ritual, Aono's past and his living body. Vol. 14: the storm, the choice and the ending (Kodansha copy; ciatr).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why does Aono ask Yuri to live, right at the start?
2. What do the rituals in this story have in common, and why do they all go wrong?
3. How do Yuri's and Aono's families shape the way they love?
4. ANN's reviewer raises consent questions about possession. What does consent mean when one person can enter another's body?
5. Your family's view: what does your family believe about the dead, and about grief, and how does that shape how you read this story?
