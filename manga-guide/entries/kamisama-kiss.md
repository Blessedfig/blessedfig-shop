**Premise.** Sixteen-year-old Nanami Momozono, mocked at school for being poor, comes home to find her gambling father has run off and debt collectors have taken the apartment (ch. 1). In a park she rescues a man from a dog; he is Mikage, the land god of a run-down shrine, who offers her his home and, with a mark on her forehead, his title. At the shrine she meets the spirit attendants and Tomoe, a fox yokai who served Mikage and scorns a human god; she binds him as her familiar with a kiss when he will not otherwise save her from a demon (ch. 3) (Wikipedia; fan wiki). As a fledgling god she learns talismans and the kagura dance (ch. 27-30), attends the gods' assembly at Izumo, and gathers friends: a snake familiar, Mizuki, who first kidnaps her to marry her (ch. 15-16); Kurama, a crow tengu working as a pop idol who first wants to eat her heart; and the catfish princess Himemiko, whose romance with a human boy she helps. Nanami falls for Tomoe, who refuses because humans and yokai should not love. When a curse from his past threatens his life, she travels 500 years back three times, learns that the woman he once loved was herself, and saves him (ch. 79-101). In the last arcs the demon king Akura-ou returns in the body of a dead boy, Kirihito; Tomoe chooses to become human; the two marry and have a son (Wikipedia; VIZ vol. 25).

## Life message

**Kindness and stubborn effort can make a home and win real love across deep differences; love means choosing a shared, ordinary human life over power.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can someone who has been thrown away find a home, and can love cross the line between kinds of beings? Nanami starts with nothing; Tomoe starts certain that humans and yokai must never love.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, through kindness and persistence. Nanami wins over the shrine, her rivals and finally Tomoe by refusing to give up on anyone. Love is shown as costly: Nanami travels into a dangerous past to save Tomoe, and Tomoe gives up immortal power to become human (Wikipedia; VIZ vol. 25).

**Characters as arguments.** Tomoe argues for separation and self-protection; his past as a killer and his lost love explain why. Mizuki shows loneliness that first grabs (a kidnapping) and then learns to serve. Kurama argues for fame and ambition, then chooses friendship. Akura-ou is pure appetite for power, and even he is shown mercy. Mikage, the absent god, quietly arranges everything to prove humans and yokai can love (Wikipedia).

**Symbols and motifs.** The forehead mark of a god; the kiss that binds a familiar; a hairpin from Tomoe carried through time (ch. 80-82); fox fire; the shrine restored as Nanami grows; the underworld's gate; the human life Tomoe chooses.

**Traditions it echoes.** Japanese fox-wife and animal-bride tales, Urashima Taro (Kotaro Urashima's name), the robe-maiden (hagoromo) legend reworked in Okinawa (ANN vol. 18), and Western 'Beauty and the Beast' stories of a fierce being tamed by love.

**How each lens is portrayed.** The Shinto spirit world is the setting and is celebrated. Community and found family win. Tradition is honored but bends to love. Nonviolence appears as mercy and Tomoe's no-killing promise, not as a rule. Individual choice (to love, to become human) is affirmed within a traditional ending of marriage and family.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-6 (ch. 1-36) set up the shrine, rivals and school life, with episodic comedy. Volumes 7-13 (ch. 37-79) bring Izumo, the tengu arc and Himemiko's wedding. Volumes 14-17 (ch. 80-101) are the time-travel past arc, the darkest stretch. Volumes 18-25 (ch. 102-149) cover Akura-ou's return, the underworld, Tomoe's choice to become human and the ending.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Nanami had nowhere to go and trusted a stranger. What should she have done, and what would you do?
2. Mizuki kidnaps Nanami to marry her, and later kisses her while she sleeps. How does the story treat that? How should it?
3. Why did Tomoe believe humans and yokai should never love? What changed his mind?
4. Tomoe gives up his powers to live a human life. What does love ask people to give up?
5. Nanami becomes a 'god' by accident. What did she actually do that made her good at it?
