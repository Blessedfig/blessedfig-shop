**Premise.** Makoto Misumi, a plain-looking high school archer, is summoned to another world under a pact his parents made with its Goddess, but she finds him ugly, revokes his role as Hero, leaves him unable to speak the common tongue of humans and throws him into a wasteland at the edge of the world (ch. 1-2). The Japanese moon god Tsukuyomi gives him his blessing and tells him to live freely; Makoto turns out to have nearly limitless magic. He makes pacts with a dragon, Tomoe (ch. 4-5), and a calamity spider, Mio (ch. 6-7), who take human form and become his devoted followers and, in time, lovers, and later with a lich, Shiki (ch. 41-42). In a pocket world (the Demiplane) he shelters orcs, dwarves and other peoples (ch. 8-9), then enters human society as a masked merchant, founding the Kuzunoha Company (ch. 15-21). Humans ("hyumans") in this world prize beauty and despise demi-humans. Early on he rescues an adventurer, Toa, from debt bondage in the frontier town of Zetsuya (ch. 11-14). Later arcs bring war with the demons, a clash with the Goddess (ch. 50-56), and a post as a teacher at Rotsgard Academy (from ch. 60) (Wikipedia; fan wiki; MangaUpdates).

## Life message

**Worth is not looks or a goddess's favor: someone cast out as ugly can build a home where every kind of being is welcome, and should use great power to live freely and protect his own, though power without restraint can numb a conscience.**

| Socio-cultural | A comic critique of lookism and racial hierarchy: the beautiful hyumans despise demi-humans and the plain-faced hero, who finds friends among outcasts. ANN calls it an affectionate spoof of the isekai trend. A merchant fantasy honoring trade and craft. |

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What is a person worth if the highest power in the world says he is ugly? Makoto is summoned to be a Hero and discarded on sight (ch. 1). The series asks whether worth comes from appearance, from divine favor or from what one builds and how one treats others.

**The answer the story gives.** Worth comes from how you live with others. Rejected by the beautiful, Makoto finds his friends among those the Goddess's world despises: a dragon, a spider, a lich, orcs, dwarves. He builds them a home (the Demiplane) and builds himself a business. The answer has a shadow: he also has near-limitless power, and the story lets him notice that killing has become too easy (fan wiki).

**Characters as arguments.** Makoto argues for patience and fairness, and against judging by looks, though he slides into judging all hyumans. Tomoe argues for loyalty and also checks him when he is unfair. Mio argues for devotion and appetite. The Goddess argues that beauty is goodness and that a deity may break her word; she is the story's clearest villain. Tsukuyomi argues for grace without conditions. The adventurers of Zetsuya argue for exploitation, and lose.

**Symbols and motifs.** The moon (Tsukuyomi's blessing and the title's "guided by the moon"). Makoto's mask, hiding the face the world rejects. The Demiplane as a world made right. The common language he cannot speak, a curse of exclusion. The merchant's ledger in place of the hero's sword.

**Traditions it echoes.** The rejected hero who becomes great (Joseph, the ugly duckling); Japanese myth through Tsukuyomi; the isekai power fantasy, which ANN says it spoofs with affection. Faith readers may hear 1 Samuel 16:7 in its lesson about looks while objecting to its picture of a sole deity.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarian and anti-discrimination lenses are affirmed; the Goddess's authoritarian order is critiqued. Might makes right is explored but not resolved: power solves problems, and the story only begins to ask what it costs. Hedonism and harem comedy are celebrated rather than questioned.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-2 (ch. 1-14) are the wasteland, the pacts, the Demiplane and the darker Zetsuya arc. Volumes 3-7 (ch. 15-49) are merchant life in Tsige and the first clashes with hyuman heroes. Volumes 8-9 (ch. 50-63) bring war and the Goddess. From volume 9 (ch. 60) the academy arcs begin, with students, nobles and later wars.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why does the Goddess reject Makoto, and what does that say about her world?
2. Makoto starts to distrust all hyumans. Is that fair? How does Tomoe answer him?
3. Makoto worries he feels too little after killing. Why does that matter?
4. What would a world without ranking by looks look like?
5. Does the story escape the beauty standards it criticizes?
