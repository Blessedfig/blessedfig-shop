**Premise.** In the Clover Kingdom everyone is born with magic except Asta, an orphan raised with his rival Yuno at a church in Hage, a village in the poor Forsaken Realm. At 15 Yuno receives a legendary four-leaf grimoire and Asta a black five-leaf grimoire holding anti-magic swords and a devil (Liebe) (ch. 1-10). Asta joins the Black Bulls, a squad of misfits under Captain Yami, with the royal Noelle Silva; Yuno joins the elite Golden Dawn. Both aim to become Wizard King, the kingdom's protector. The arcs run through a dungeon, an undead attack on the capital, a terrorist group (the Eye of the Midnight Sun) whose leader avenges the massacre of the elves at the kingdom's founding (ch. 150-228), a war with the Spade Kingdom's Dark Triad and their devils (ch. 229-331), and a final arc in which Lucius Zogratis, hidden for years inside the Wizard King Julius, tries to remake humanity with soul magic and angel-like Paladins, among them Asta's beloved Sister Lily (ch. 332-392). Asta and Yuno kill Lucius, Julius undoes the day's deaths, and six months later Asta defeats Yuno in a duel to become Wizard King (ch. 390-392; fan wiki).

## Life message

**A good life is earned by effort, not birth: you refuse to give up, you lift up the people others look down on, and you build a family out of misfits.**

**Where traditions disagree.** Almost every tradition welcomes the anti-discrimination message, the found family and the reconciliation of old enemies. The divide is spiritual: the hero's power comes from a devil who becomes his friend, magic runs the world, and angel imagery belongs to the villain. Pentecostal and Jehovah's Witness readers (D) and Eastern Orthodox, Adventist, Orthodox Jewish and Muslim readers (C) weigh that heavily, while Atheist, Agnostic, secular, Sikh and Bahá'í frames read devils and magic as fantasy and grade the message A. Progressive readers split: the class critique is welcome, the nun-proposal gag and fan service are not.

## Creator and publisher context

- Yuki Tabata, from Fukuoka Prefecture; assistant to Toshiaki Iwashiro; won Jump's Golden Future Cup with the one-shot Hungry Joker (2011), serialized 2012-2013 and canceled; Black Clover began as a one-shot in Shonen Jump Next!! (Wikipedia, Tabata).
- On-record intent (Wikipedia, Production): he blamed Hungry Joker's failure on its quiet, dark lead and, on friends' advice, made an energetic, happy-go-lucky hero closer to himself; he modeled Asta on himself and Charmy on his wife. He wanted a shonen equivalent of Berserk, drawing also on Dragon Quest: The Adventure of Dai, The Lord of the Rings, Harry Potter, Guillermo del Toro's films, Dragon Ball, YuYu Hakusho and Bleach.
- Early in the run he said he slept about three hours a night from stress, later about six; he inked and toned by hand at least through vol. 7 (Wikipedia).
- No statements on religion, politics or the devil and angel imagery were found. **Status: Needs more info on the creator's thematic intent.**
- Publisher: Shueisha (Weekly Shonen Jump, then Jump Giga). VIZ Media publishes it in English, rated T. No controversies involving the creator were found.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Does birth decide what you are worth? In a kingdom where magic is everything and the poor live in a "Forsaken Realm," Asta has no magic at all. The series asks whether effort and loyalty can overturn a world built on inherited gifts.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, though never alone. Asta's anti-magic only works because he trained his body to the breaking point, and he wins because friends, rivals and former enemies stand with him. His last opponent, Lucius, wants to fix the world by remaking everyone and calls the magicless "flaws"; Asta answers that his imperfect life belongs to him (fan wiki).

**Characters as arguments.** Asta argues for effort and never giving up. Yuno argues for talent joined to humility and rivalry. Noelle argues that someone scorned by her family can find her worth. Yami argues that misfits deserve a chance. Licht argues for revenge after a historic massacre, and is reconciled. Lucius argues for a perfect world by force, wrapped in angelic imagery; Julius, dying, answers that no one can be sure what brings peace, and people try anyway (fan wiki).

**Symbols and motifs.** The five-leaf clover, a leaf for a devil. The black grimoire. Anti-magic: the power of the person who has nothing. The church in Hage. The Wizard King's title. Angels as the false savior's soldiers. The final duel between the two orphans.

**Traditions it echoes.** The shonen underdog story (Naruto and Dragon Ball, which Tabata names), Tolkien and Harry Potter fantasy (Wikipedia), Berserk's dark fantasy in shonen form. Christian readers may hear the lowly lifted up (1 Samuel 2:8; Luke 1:52) and the warning against false saviors; Sikh and Bahá'í readers, the rejection of caste and prejudice.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Progressivism and communitarianism are the heart: the excluded join a family that includes them. Pagan/occult elements are the furniture of the world, not its message. Might makes right and authoritarian "salvation" are the villains' creeds and are defeated. Patriotism appears as service to the kingdom, while hatred between peoples is condemned.

**Shifts across the series.** Ch. 1-74 (v1-8): joining the knights, first missions, the capital attack, the Seabed Temple. Ch. 75-149 (v8-16): the Witches' Forest, the Royal Knights tournament. Ch. 150-228 (v16-23): the elves' return and the kingdom's founding sin. Ch. 229-331 (v23-33): the war with the Spade Kingdom's devils. Ch. 332-392 (v33-38): Lucius, Judgment Day and the Wizard King duel.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Asta was born without magic. What did he do instead of giving up?
2. Why do some nobles look down on Asta, and what changes their minds?
3. The elves were wronged long ago. How does the story make peace possible?
4. Asta keeps proposing to Sister Lily after she says no. What do you think of that joke?
5. Lucius wanted to make a perfect world. Why do the heroes refuse his kind of perfect?
