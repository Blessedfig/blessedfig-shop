**Premise.** Ran Takane is tall, beautiful, stylish and top of her class, so admired that no boy dares approach the "mountaintop flower." In fact she is shy and painfully unsure of herself. On the school gardening committee she soaks her classmate Akira, a cheerful, popular boy whose family runs a flower shop, and he sees the gentle girl behind the image (vol. 1). A summer day in a sunflower field brings them closer (vol. 2); Ran holds back when she learns Akira's mother is in poor health (vol. 3), then they confess and begin dating (vol. 4). At Christmas Akira kisses her cheek, and her father, learning she has a boyfriend, firmly objects and lets slip a hint about her past (vol. 5). A junior-high classmate of Akira's, Nanami, has feelings for him on the school ski trip (vol. 6); their first kiss comes on Valentine's Day (vols. 6-7). In the second year, Megumu, a flower farmer's son who resents his family's trade, joins Ran's gardening club and lashes out at Akira (vols. 7-8). Alone at her family's beach house, the couple is found on the couch by her furious father, who orders her to cut ties (vol. 9). Akira learns the past Ran had hidden, she runs, then decides to face it and her junior-high classmate Mochizuki (vol. 10) (Kodansha and Apple Books blurbs).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What does it cost to be admired? Ran has everything a girl at school is supposed to want, beauty, grades and style, and it has left her alone. Everyone sees the "mountaintop flower"; no one sees the shy girl underneath.

**The answer the story gives.** Being known matters more than being admired. Akira wins Ran not by reaching the peak but by noticing who she is, and the story asks her, in turn, to let him see everything, including the past she has hidden (vol. 10). Love here is patient and gentle, a matter of small steps: a shared lunch, a cheek kiss, a first kiss on Valentine's Day.

**Characters as arguments.** Ran argues that an image can be a prison. Akira argues that kindness and attention are a kind of strength. Ran's father argues for protection, and the story lets his worry be real even as it sides with the couple. Megumu argues, by his bitterness, that family duty can feel like a cage; Akira's joy in his family's shop is the answer the story offers. Mochizuki, from Ran's junior high, carries the past she has to face.

**Symbols and motifs.** The flower on the high peak, beautiful because unreachable. The orchid in Ran's name (ran means orchid), a flower that needs patient care. The water Ran spills on Akira at their first meeting. The sunflower field (vol. 2). The flower shop and the flower farm as two ways to feel about inherited work.

**Traditions it echoes.** The shojo romance of the misunderstood beauty; the folk wisdom, shared by many faiths, that the heart matters more than appearance (Christian readers will hear 1 Samuel 16:7). Its view of love as gradual and chaste fits many religious families' hopes, though the dating relationship itself is what more traditional families will weigh.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism is affirmed in its gentle form: being yourself means being honest, not defiant. Classical virtues (kindness, patience, courage) are rewarded. Family duty is explored through Megumu and through Ran's father, without contempt for either.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-4 are the slow approach and confession. Volumes 5-7 bring dating, a rival, a disapproving father and the first kiss. Volumes 8-10 turn to other people's burdens (Megumu) and then to Ran's own hidden past, which sources opened do not describe.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why do you think nobody talked to Ran before Akira did?
2. Is it a compliment or a burden to be called "the mountaintop flower"?
3. Ran's father objects to Akira. What might he be afraid of? Was he right about the beach house?
4. Megumu is angry at his family's business. Why does seeing Akira happy at the flower shop upset him?
5. Ran decides to face her past instead of running. When is it brave to tell someone about something you are ashamed of?
