**Premise.** Yuuri, an elementary-school boy (Mangapedia) from a wealthy family, is left alone in a big country manor after his parents die in a car accident; he dismisses the servants he cannot pay and fails to run the house himself. A beautiful, dark-skinned maid named Lilith appears and offers to work for nothing. She is perfect at everything, so Yuuri decides she must be using magic on him, especially since his chest feels tight when he looks at her. He "investigates" her, and his innocent compliments keep turning her teasing back on her (anime eps. 1-2). A rich classmate, Tsukasa, cheers on what she imagines is a forbidden romance, while Tsukasa's butler Fujisaki, Lilith's old schoolmate, keeps her in check (ep. 3). Yuuri slowly remembers that his parents once took in a lonely girl who sang him his mother's lullaby: Lilith (ep. 8). He realizes he loves her and decides to tell her (vol. 5), a former fellow maid, Natsume, tries to take her back (vol. 5; eps. 9-11), and Lilith explains that his parents raised her after her own parents vanished (ep. 11). Later volumes have Yuuri try to grow up and be dependable (vol. 7) and Lilith's real parents found; Yuuri lets her go to them, then resolves to tell her honestly how he feels (vol. 8 blurb) (Wikipedia; Yen Press).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What is the strange feeling in Yuuri's chest when he looks at his new maid? He decides it must be magic. The series asks what love is, as seen by a child who has lost his parents and cannot name what he feels.

**The answer the story gives.** It is love, and it began long before: Lilith was the girl his parents took in, who sang him his mother's lullaby (anime ep. 8). She came back to repay them by caring for him (ep. 11). The story's answer is warm about family and treats the boy's first love as sweet, though it never states Lilith's age.

**Characters as arguments.** Yuuri argues for saying what you feel without guile, which is why Lilith's teasing never works on him. Lilith argues for gratitude as a reason to give your life to someone. Natsume argues that a master must be rich and accomplished; she is gently proven wrong. Tsukasa reads everything as a romance novel, and is half right.

**Symbols and motifs.** Lilith's purple eyes, printed in a second color on every page; the lullaby; chess with his dead father; morning glories compared to her eyes (ep. 2); the empty mansion filling up with friends.

**Traditions it echoes.** The maid romantic comedy, the orphan taken in by a stranger, and the older theme of a debt of kindness repaid. Its romance across an age gap follows a Japanese teasing-comedy form that Western critics often question.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarian and Confucian ideas (family, gratitude, loyal service) are affirmed. Expressive honesty is the comic engine. No lens is argued against.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-2: teasing comedy and "investigations." Volumes 3-4: school life, Tsukasa and Fujisaki, Yuuri's returning memories. Volume 5: Yuuri decides to confess and Natsume arrives. Volumes 6-7: school troubles, a ski camp and Yuuri's effort to grow up. Volume 8: Lilith's real parents and Yuuri's resolve to be honest (publisher blurbs; the ending itself not confirmed).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why does Yuuri think Lilith uses magic? What is he really feeling?
2. Why did Lilith come to work for Yuuri for no pay?
3. Is Lilith more like a big sister, a parent or something else to Yuuri? What do you think the story wants?
4. Natsume thinks Yuuri is not a worthy master because he is not rich. What changes her mind?
5. Some readers find the romance between a child and his older maid uncomfortable. Why might that be?
