**Premise.** Miyu Suzuki is a cheerful, pink-haired gyaru who goes along with the crowd even when it wears her out. She sits next to Yusuke Tani, a quiet boy with glasses who says exactly what he thinks, and she has fallen for him for that very honesty. When she confesses, he says he feels the same, and they start dating (ch. 1-3). The story widens into an ensemble: the airheaded Yamada and the anxious, laugh-prone Nishi slowly become a couple (ch. 46); the self-critical Taira, who believes in a school social hierarchy and was hurt by a past rejection, and Azuma, who keeps tolerating boys who treat her badly, become close friends while Azuma falls for him; and friends Sato, Watanabe and Honda round out the group. The last arc follows college exams: Tani is accepted at a faraway university, the couple choose a long-distance relationship, and an epilogue shows the friends still together after high school (Wikipedia; AniList; ANN vol. 8).

## Life message

**Being honest about who you are, and learning to respect people who are not like you, makes better love and better friendship than trying to fit in.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can two people who are nothing alike love each other without one of them changing to fit? Suzuki shapes herself to whoever she is with; Tani does not shape himself at all. The series asks what each can learn from the other.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, if both are honest. Tani learns to say what he feels, and Suzuki learns she does not have to perform. Anime Feminist notes that the story lets both leads grow without forcing either to become the other.

**Characters as arguments.** Tani argues for honesty; his bluntness becomes kinder as he learns to explain himself. Suzuki argues that being liked matters, and learns that being known matters more. Taira argues that school is a ranking and that it is safer not to hope; his friends' warmth proves him wrong, though he still cannot answer Azuma's love. Azuma argues that you should never hate someone you once liked, and Taira helps her see she deserves better (likely ch. 19-20). Nishi argues for staying quiet so as not to fail, and learns to choose for herself (likely ch. 58-60).

**Symbols and motifs.** Seats side by side in class; matching keychains and gloves (ch. 2, 35-36); Taira's convenience-store counter as the group's meeting place; the final group photo after high school (epilogue).

**Traditions it echoes.** The opposites-attract romantic comedy (CBR compares it to My Dress-Up Darling, via Wikipedia) and the ensemble school story. Its stress on honest speech echoes many traditions' teaching on truthful speech, from the ninth commandment to Buddhist right speech.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism is affirmed in a gentle form: being yourself means honesty and self-respect, not rebellion. Communitarianism is affirmed through the friend group. The school-hierarchy critique is mild and personal.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-2 (ch. 1-14) start the couple and the friend group. Volumes 3-4 (ch. 15-31) bring the class trip and Azuma's and Taira's stories. Volumes 5-6 (ch. 32-49) cover Christmas, New Year and Valentine's Day as Yamada and Nishi become a couple. Volumes 7-8 (ch. 50-65) turn to exams, futures and the long-distance decision.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Suzuki likes Tani because he says what he thinks. When is honesty kind, and when can it hurt?
2. Taira thinks school is a ranking. Is he right? What changes his mind?
3. Azuma keeps forgiving boys who treat her badly. What does Taira help her see?
4. When Nishi and Yamada are alone, he stops to ask what she wants. Why does that matter?
5. Suzuki offers to break up so Tani can follow his dream. Was that the right choice? What would you do?
