**Premise.** Asuna Yuuki, a top student studying for her high-school entrance exams, borrows her brother's virtual-reality headset and is trapped with ten thousand players in Sword Art Online, a game in which dying in the game means dying in real life. Afraid of what her absence means to her parents and peers, she applies her study habits to swordplay, nearly works herself to death, and is drawn by the solo player Kirito toward the front line (Yen Press blurb). Volume 1 retells the first floor from her side, up to the raid on the Kobold King, with the information broker Argo, "the Rat" (ANN). Volumes 2-4 cover the second floor, where the blacksmith Nezha, who cheated players, tries to make amends at the floor boss (Yen Press, vol. 4). Volumes 5-7 cover the third floor's Elf War quest, where Kirito and Asuna side with Kizmel, a dark-elf knight who seems more human than a program, and stop the mysterious player Morte from tricking the guilds into fighting each other (Yen Press, vols. 5-7).

## Life message

**Even trapped in a deadly world, you can choose to live and not just survive, and you grow strongest by learning to trust and work with others.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** If your life could end any day, would you spend it surviving or living? Asuna, who has only ever studied, first fights as if racing toward death (vol. 1).

**The answer the story gives.** Live, and do it with others. Through Kirito and Argo she learns that the game world has food, friendship and meaning (ANN on how her cooking motive is explained). Her study habits become an asset instead of a cage.

**Characters as arguments.** Asuna argues for discipline learning to make room for joy. Kirito argues for quiet self-sacrifice. Nezha shows that a cheat can repent and make amends (vol. 4). Morte argues for chaos, setting players against each other for his own ends (vol. 7). Kizmel raises the question of who counts as a person.

**Symbols and motifs.** The tower of Aincrad climbed floor by floor; the bath as a moment of rest in a war; cream on bread, a small joy; the elven war as a mirror of human division.

**Traditions it echoes.** The coming-of-age story, the death game, and old questions about appearance and reality (for Buddhist readers, maya; for Christian readers, perseverance under trial).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Transhumanist questions are raised, not answered; communitarian cooperation is affirmed; existential choice resolves toward hope.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1: the first floor from Asuna's side. Vols. 2-4: the second floor and Nezha's scam and atonement. Vols. 5-7: the third floor, the Elf War, Kizmel and Morte.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Asuna treats the game like an exam. Is that a strength or a problem at first?
2. Why does Kirito let people think badly of him?
3. Nezha cheated other players. What does he have to do to make it right?
4. Is Kizmel a person? How would you decide?
5. Why do you think the creators included a bath scene? How did it fit the story?
