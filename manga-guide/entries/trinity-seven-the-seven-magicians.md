**Premise.** Arata Kasuga, a 17-year-old high school student (fan wiki), lost his parents young and lives with his cousin Hijiri. One day a Black Sun appears and a "Breakdown Phenomenon" wipes out his town; Hijiri vanishes after hanging a grimoire around his neck, and Arata unknowingly wishes his ordinary life back, so the grimoire builds a false copy of it. Lilith Asami, a mage who is Arata's age but teaches at the Royal Biblia Academy, breaks the illusion and gives him a choice: give up the grimoire or be put to death. He keeps it, enrolls at the academy to rescue Hijiri, and is identified as a "Demon Lord Candidate," whose power could destroy the world. Magic is sorted into seven archives named after the seven deadly sins (Superbia, Ira, Invidia, Luxuria, Gula, Avaritia, Acedia); each mage's "thema" is the opposite of their own temperament, so the prudish Lilith studies under Luxuria (lust) (Wikipedia; TV Tropes). The Trinity Seven are the academy's seven top mages, all girls: Lilith, Arin (who looks like Hijiri and calls herself Arata's wife), the ninja Levi, Mira of Grimoire Security, Akio, Yui and Lieselotte. Arata's own magic cancels other spells, which tends to strip the clothes off the girls around him, and he copies their magic by "disassembling" it this way (fan wiki). Later arcs include an inter-academy school festival where Hijiri reappears on a rival team (vols. 10-14), Arata losing and regaining his Demon Lord element, journeys through other worlds with Norse names (Laevateinn, a Norn giant; vol. 25), and a war against demons called Error Grades (vol. 31) (Yen Press synopses; The Fandom Post).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What would you give up to get back the person you lost? Arata's first act is a wish: he wants his ordinary days back, and his grimoire builds him a false world to live in. Lilith offers him the truth at the cost of his life or his power. He chooses the truth and the long road to Hijiri.

**The answer the story gives.** Loyalty and friendship turn a feared power into a protecting one. Arata is told he may become the Demon Lord who destroys the world; instead he becomes the one who stops Breakdown Phenomena and is rescued, in turn, by the friends he has made (vol. 13). Alongside this runs a second answer the story never questions: that the pleasures of a harem comedy are harmless fun.

**Characters as arguments.** Arata argues that you can be easygoing, even shameless, and still be dependable when it counts (fan wiki). Lilith argues for duty and modesty and is teased for it. Mira, head of Grimoire Security, argues for rules and purity and slowly warms to him. Arin argues for doing what you want without shame. Akio, who lost her faith when her town was destroyed, carries the series' rare serious note about belief. Hijiri, now on the enemy side, argues that the only way to free Arata is to end the cycle of worlds.

**Symbols and motifs.** The Black Sun and the Breakdown Phenomenon: a world unmade by an out-of-control wish. The false hometown of vol. 1: a comfortable illusion. The seven deadly sins as archives, where each mage studies the opposite of her temperament. The grimoire girls Sora and Ilia, books that become people.

**Traditions it echoes.** The magic-school story, the harem comedy, and the "chosen one who might be the destroyer" plot. Its use of the deadly sins and saints borrows Christian vocabulary as fantasy color, much as other Japanese fantasies borrow Norse and Celtic myth (Gungnir, Laevateinn, the Norns).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Hedonism is affirmed through comedy. The occult is explored as a school subject. Communitarian loyalty is the emotional core. Christian names appear without Christian ideas. A feminist reader would find capable heroines undercut by the fan service around them.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-7 (the anime's span, to ch. 29) introduce the academy, each Trinity Seven member and Liber Academy. Vols. 7-9 cover the Sky Library and the film-adapted arc. Vols. 10-14 are the inter-academy school festival with Hijiri and the Saint of Resurrection, ending with Arata losing his Demon Lord candidacy. Later volumes move into other worlds, Norse giants (vol. 25) and war with the Error Grades (vol. 31). Sources opened give little detail past vol. 14.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Arata chose to remember a painful truth instead of living in a happy illusion. Was that the right choice? Why?
2. The magic here is named after the seven deadly sins. Why might a story use sins as the names of powers?
3. Many jokes involve girls' clothes coming off. How do the girls react, and does the story care how they feel?
4. The girls risk themselves to save Arata. What makes a friendship worth that kind of risk?
5. Akio lost her faith when her town was destroyed. What do you think would help someone through that?
