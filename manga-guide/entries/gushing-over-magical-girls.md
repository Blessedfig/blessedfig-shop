**Premise.** Utena Hiiragi is a shy 14-year-old middle schooler (fan wiki) obsessed with Tres Magia, the trio of magical girls who guard her town. A black mascot, Venalita, offers her powers, but makes her a villain, Magia Baiser, of the evil organization Enormita, and keeps her in line by threatening to post a video of her online (Wikipedia). In her first fights Utena discovers that she enjoys tormenting and humiliating her heroes, using a riding crop that turns objects into monsters. She gathers a villain circle (Kiwi, a 14-year-old influencer who falls for her; Korisu, a silent 9-year-old who plays with toys; the singer Matama and her friend Nemo), overthrows Enormita's former leader Lord Enorme (Michiko, 20), and becomes its leader. Later arcs add rival groups, Utena's split into "Reason" and "Lust," a monstrous loss of control and her effort to master herself, a robot girl (Roboko, ch. 58-59), legendary magical girls of the past, and Venalita's betrayal (ch. 66-67) (Wikipedia; fan wiki).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What if the girl who loves heroes most discovers she would rather be the villain? Utena's devotion to Tres Magia is real, and so is the thrill she feels when she has power over them. The series asks whether admiration and the urge to dominate are two sides of one desire.

**The answer the story gives.** Accept the desire and learn to steer it. Utena's power grows as she stops fighting her nature (fan wiki), and the later arcs show her losing control and training to bring her two sides together. The series never asks whether the heroines, all 14, consent; their humiliation is the joke.

**Characters as arguments.** Utena argues that the hidden self should be embraced. Kiwi argues for devotion and also for fame. Michiko (Lord Enorme) argues for domination and then changes her mind out of love for Randa. Venalita, the cute mascot, is the real manipulator, a parody of genre mascots who recruit children.

**Symbols and motifs.** The riding crop that turns ordinary things into monsters; stars that multiply on the skin as power surges; La Verità, the "true form" unlocked when feelings overflow; the dollhouse and video-game worlds of a 9-year-old's toys.

**Traditions it echoes.** Magical-girl parody (Magical Girl Ore, cited by ANN), villain-protagonist comedy, and the old tension between the hero and the fan. Readers of faith may hear a dark inversion of the struggle between reason and desire described by Plato, Paul (Romans 7) and the Buddhist path, here resolved in favor of desire.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Hedonism and expressive individualism are celebrated without reservation in the early volumes; later volumes add self-mastery but keep the indulgence. Postmodern parody is the frame that lets the sexual content pass as comedy.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-5 (ch. 1-25, the anime's span) set up Utena, Kiwi, Korisu and the Lord Enorme and Loco Musica arcs. Middle volumes bring the Lord's Legion, Utena's split into Reason and Lust, and her corrupted form. Volumes 12-13 (ch. 56-65) bring Roboko, the Shio-chans and legendary magical girls of the past; ch. 66-68 open Venalita's betrayal.
