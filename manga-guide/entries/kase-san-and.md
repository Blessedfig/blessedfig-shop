**Premise.** Yui Yamada, a shy, clumsy girl on her school's greenery committee, loves tending the flower beds. Tomoka Kase, a tall, boyish track star surrounded by rumors, notices her. They bond over morning glories, bike rides and shoe shopping, and volume 1 ends with a confession and kiss in the nurse's office (Manga Bookshelf). Through their final year of high school they deal with Yamada's low self-esteem, a school trip to Okinawa, rumors that a track senior was Kase's ex (proved false, Wikipedia), jealousy, and the coming separation when Kase heads to a sports university in Tokyo. Yamada, pushed by her mother toward a local college, decides to study horticulture in Tokyo to stay near Kase (ANN vol. 4; OVA summary).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can someone who thinks she is ordinary believe she is loved? Yamada's main obstacle is not the world but her own low opinion of herself, which keeps leading her to assume the worst.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, by talking. Each volume sets up a misunderstanding (a rumored ex, a bath on a school trip, a separation after graduation) and resolves it when the two say what they feel. Okazu sums up vol. 4's lesson as speaking your mind.

**Characters as arguments.** Yamada argues that worth must be earned; Kase answers that she is attractive and valued as she is (Okazu). Mikawa represents protective suspicion and gossip. Yamada's mother stands for the safe, expected path, which Yamada respectfully sets aside.

**Symbols and motifs.** Gardens and flowers: love tended slowly. Running: Kase's talent and Yamada's attempt to improve herself (vol. 1). Food (bento, shortcake): everyday care. Each title pairs Kase with an ordinary object.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Japanese 'pure yuri' tradition of school romance; the slice-of-life genre's focus on small moments; the coming-of-age story of finding a voice.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism wins quietly: Yamada chooses her own future. Progressivism is assumed rather than argued. Virtue ethics appears in patience and honesty.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1 is courtship and a first kiss. Vols. 2-3 are the senior year, with the most physical content and fan service. Vols. 4-5 turn to jealousy, self-confidence and the future, with fan service much reduced.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Yamada often believes the worst about herself. Where does that come from, and what helps her change?
2. How do rumors cause trouble in this story?
3. Yamada chose a different college than her mother expected. How should a teenager talk with parents about a big choice?
4. What does Kase do that makes Yamada feel safe?
