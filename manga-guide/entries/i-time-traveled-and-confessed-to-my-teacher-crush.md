**Premise.** Seiji Sanada, an overworked office worker near thirty (AniList's character page says 27), wakes up in his own body as a high-school second-year in spring. He has always regretted never confessing to his world-history teacher, Haruka Hiiragi, so he does, and she says yes. They begin dating in secret, and she turns out to be a devoted partner who spoils him (vol. 1, Square Enix; Manga UP! Global). The publisher's blurbs promise more romance and sexual situations in each volume, including a school trip, a hot-spring trip for two and an accidental visit to a love hotel (vols. 2-3); Haruka's sister appears (vol. 2). Seiji meets Haruka's strict father and career-minded mother, whose reaction will decide the relationship's future (vol. 4). After an incident opens a rift, Seiji tries to time-leap again to change the outcome and concludes that he must change himself to be happy; the series ends happily (vol. 5), and database tags point to a proposal and a wedding in a final time skip (MangaUpdates).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** If you could go back and act on your biggest regret, would it fix your life? Seiji, a burned-out office worker, wakes up at sixteen or seventeen with an adult's memories and confesses to the teacher he once loved (Manga UP! Global).

**The answer the story gives.** At first, yes: the confession works, and the relationship is shown as bliss. By the last volume the story adds a second answer: when a rift opens, Seiji tries time travel again and learns he must change himself (vol. 5, Square Enix). The ending is happy, with tags pointing to a proposal and wedding (MangaUpdates).

**Characters as arguments.** Seiji argues for seizing the chance you missed. Haruka argues for devoted, indulgent love, and her role as a teacher is never shown to weigh on her in sources. Her strict father stands for the protective parent whose approval must be won (vol. 4). No character in sources argues that a teacher should not date a student.

**Symbols and motifs.** The time leap as a do-over; the secret kept from the school; trips away from home (school trip, hot spring, love hotel) as tests of the secret; a second leap that fails, pushing Seiji to change himself.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Japanese time-leap romance and the "second youth" fantasy. Readers from faith traditions will recognize the theme of regret and repentance (changing oneself rather than circumstances), but set inside a relationship their traditions treat as wrong.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Hedonism and expressive individualism are celebrated: the pleasure of the relationship is the point. Traditionalism and virtue appear only at the end, in meeting parents, changing oneself and marriage.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1: the leap and the confession. Vols. 2-3: escalating romance and fan service (school trip, hot spring, pool, festival, love hotel). Vol. 4: Haruka's parents. Vol. 5: the rift, the second leap and the finale.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Seiji has an adult's memories but a teenager's body. Does that make the relationship okay? Why do schools have rules about this?
2. What does a teacher owe a student, whatever the student feels?
3. Why does the relationship have to stay secret? What does that tell you?
4. In vol. 5 Seiji learns that changing himself matters more than changing the past. What would you change about yourself?
5. If you could relive one regret, would you? What might go wrong?
