**Premise.** Haruto Amakawa, a Japanese college student, dies in a bus crash before he can meet his childhood friend Miharu Ayase again. His memories wake in Rio, a seven-year-old orphan in the slums of the Beltrum Kingdom who watched a man murder his mother and lives for revenge (Wikipedia; J-Novel Club). Two very different people now share one mind. Rio rescues the kidnapped Princess Flora, is suspected of the kidnapping and beaten by royal knights trying to force a false confession, then cleared and rewarded with a place at the Royal Academy, where noble students shun him as a commoner; his only friend there is Celia Claire, a young teacher (Wikipedia; anime ep. 1-2). Years later he is falsely accused of trying to kill a princess and flees east toward his mother's homeland (Wikipedia). On the way he defeats and frees Latifa, a child beastkin assassin enslaved by a magic collar, who becomes his adopted little sister; she also carries the memories of a girl who died on Haruto's bus (fan wiki; Wikipedia). In the Forest of Spirits the spirit folk first attack him for being human, then welcome him and teach him spirit arts (anime ep. 5). In Yagumo he finds his grandparents and learns of his royal descent (ep. 7-8). Back in the west, his contracted spirit Aishia awakens; he rescues Miharu and two other children from slave traders (vol. 8), stops Celia's forced marriage to his old enemy Charles Arbor (vols. 10-11), and heads to the city of Amande, where he meets Liselotte, a noble businesswoman who is also reincarnated from Japan (vols. 12-13) (J-Novel Club). In the light novel, which runs far ahead of the manga, Rio later kills Lucius, the man who murdered his mother (Wikipedia character list; fan wiki).

## Life message

**A person split by grief and two lives can become whole by finding his roots and protecting the people he loves; rank and birth do not decide a person's worth, and the story keeps asking whether a life aimed at revenge can also hold happiness.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Who are you when you carry two lives? Rio has a slum orphan's hatred and a gentle Japanese student's memories; he must decide which will steer him, and whether the vow he made as a child, to kill his mother's murderer, still belongs to him.

**The answer the story gives.** So far, the answer is to protect. Each arc ends with Rio saving someone (Flora, Latifa, Miharu, Celia), and he finds a family in his grandparents and an adopted sister. The revenge thread stays open: Rio believes it makes him unworthy of an ordinary happy life (fan wiki), and in the light novel he carries it out. Readers of the manga meet the question without the answer.

**Characters as arguments.** Charles Arbor and the knights argue that rank excuses cruelty and are humiliated. Duke Huguenot shows how far using people can go: he enslaved his own daughter. Latifa is the counterargument: an enemy sent to kill Rio becomes his sister because he chose mercy. Celia argues that learning and kindness cross class lines. The spirit folk, who first attack Rio for being human and then apologize, show that prejudice can be undone.

**Symbols and motifs.** The slave collar and its breaking; Rio's black hair, the mark of his mother's people; the great tree Dryas; Rock House, a hidden home inside a stone; the forced wedding interrupted (vols. 10-11).

**Traditions it echoes.** The isekai power fantasy, the revenge tale of a son seeking his parent's killer, and the fairy-tale rescue of a bride from an unwanted marriage. Its reincarnation premise borrows the language of Buddhist and Hindu rebirth without their teaching.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Virtue ethics and communitarian loyalty are celebrated. The spirit world is honored, not feared. Strength without conscience is condemned in the nobles. Bushido-style honor appears in Yagumo but is softened by Rio's doubts about vengeance.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-3: childhood, academy, false accusation and flight. Vols. 4-7: Latifa, the spirit village and Yagumo. Vols. 8-11: return west, Aishia, Miharu's rescue and Celia's wedding. Vols. 12-13: Amande, Liselotte and court politics (J-Novel Club). The light novel's later arcs (Lucius, wars, gods) lie ahead.
