**Premise.** Yogiri Takatou, 17, a second-year high school student, sleeps through the moment his class's tour bus is pulled into another world by Sion, a Sage who kills the teacher and driver for questioning her and hands the students powers, called Gifts, through a game-like System. Yogiri and his classmate Tomochika Dannoura receive nothing (her Heian-era family ghost, Mokomoko, blocked the System), and the class leaves them on the bus as bait for a dragon. Yogiri kills the dragon with a thought: he can make anything die simply by willing it. As the two travel to rejoin the class and look for a way home, Yogiri kills whoever tries to kill or harm them, including classmates who planned to enslave Tomochika (vol. 1), a classmate whose power lets him enslave people (vol. 2), Sages and a Dark God (vols. 3-6). His past is gradually revealed: a child worshiped and contained by a village cult, he killed everyone around him when loved ones died, was calmed by a Buddhist priest's recorded sutra, and was raised in a secret facility by Asaka, who taught him to kill only when he or someone he loves is threatened (vols. 6-7). In the capital, an abandoned classmate, Ayaka, returns as an artificial human to kill the class one by one, and Sion forces the survivors into a battle royale (vols. 9-11) (Wikipedia; fan wiki; Yen Press synopses).

## Life message

**Power is safest with someone who was taught restraint and cares about one person; those who use power to abandon, dominate or prey on others end up destroyed by it.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What happens when the most powerful being in a world has no interest in power? Isekai stories usually hand their heroes gifts and let them rise; here the gifted classmates immediately turn cruel, and the one boy who received nothing can end any life with a thought.

**The answer the story gives.** Power is safest with someone who does not want it, and those who want it most are the most dangerous. Yogiri kills only when threatened, by a rule his foster mother gave him, and the people who used their Gifts to abandon, enslave or prey on others are destroyed by him (ANN; fan wiki).

**Characters as arguments.** Yogiri argues for indifference held in check by one loyalty. Tomochika argues for conscience: she questions his reckless killings (fan wiki). The classmates argue, by their acts, that power reveals character. Sion argues that summoned people are tools, and is humbled. Ayaka, abandoned and remade as a weapon, shows what the class's cruelty produced. Asaka, who chose to treat a feared child as a person, is the story's quiet moral voice.

**Symbols and motifs.** The bus left as dragon bait: abandonment as the original sin of the class. AΩ, alpha and omega, Yogiri's code name: the beginning and the end. The headset sutra that put a raging child to sleep. The System's levels and classes, mocked by a power that ignores them.

**Traditions it echoes.** The overpowered-hero isekai it parodies; One-Punch Man's deadpan invincibility (from general knowledge; to verify). Its villains' fate echoes reap-what-you-sow morality. Yogiri's title, Alpha and Omega, borrows a biblical name for God, here given to a death-dealing being, which Christian readers may notice (from general knowledge; to verify).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Strength rules is mocked in the classmates and confirmed in Yogiri, which is the series' main tension. Duty ethics appears in one rule kept faithfully. Authoritarianism, in the Sages, is the target. Spirits and gods are props, not beliefs.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-2: the bus, the would-be enslavers and Tachibana, the most sexually threatening stretch. Vols. 3-6: Sages, the tower trial and an accidental god-killing. Vols. 6-7: Yogiri's past and Asaka. Vols. 8-11+: the capital, Ayaka's revenge and Sion's battle royale.

**Discussion questions.**
1. The class left Yogiri and Tomochika on the bus as dragon bait. What does that choice say about them?
2. Yogiri kills anyone who tries to kill or rob him. Is that justice, self-defense or something else?
3. Tomochika sometimes tells Yogiri he went too far. When is she right?
4. Asaka gave Yogiri one rule. Why did it matter so much?
5. The villains threaten Tomochika in ugly ways. Why do you think the author wrote them that way?
