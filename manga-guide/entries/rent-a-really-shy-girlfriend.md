**Premise.** Sumi Sakurasawa, 19, a first-year college student, is so shy she can barely speak to strangers; even buying a donut is an ordeal (ch. 1). Inspired by an idol group, she works as a rental girlfriend at the agency Diamond, earning money while practicing people skills, but she must keep high customer ratings (Kodansha USA; fan wiki). On dates she communicates with gestures and expressions (ch. 2). The agency's top girlfriend, Chizuru Mizuhara, befriends and advises her (ch. 3); she crosses paths with Ruka (ch. 4) and Mami (ch. 5) from the main series. After her manager warns her about poor reviews and a hard-won date still ends in a bad rating, she cries alone, then Chizuru arranges a practice date with a client she can trust, Kazuya (ch. 6-9, retelling volume 6 of the main series from Sumi's side). Later chapters follow everyday challenges: conveyor-belt sushi, a profile photo shoot, walking her dog, ramen, a nail salon, a magical-girl anime event, and a new client who turns out to be a child ("Sumi and the Boyfriend," ch. 13-14, 17; Kodansha vol. 3) (Wikipedia chapter titles). Kazuya's pet lungfish narrates in places (fan wiki).

**Small brave steps count: shyness can be faced a little at a time, with patience from others and from yourself.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** How do you change when the thing you most need to do is the thing you most fear? Sumi takes a job that makes her face strangers every day.

**The answer the story gives.** Little by little, with help. A handshake counts (ch. 2); a laugh and a held hand count (ch. 6). Bad reviews hurt, but Chizuru and Kazuya give her safe places to practice.

**Characters as arguments.** Sumi argues that effort matters more than results. Chizuru argues for professionalism in service of a dream (she wants to be an actress, ch. 3). Mami shows social skill used to manipulate (ch. 5), a version Sumi admires but does not copy. The lungfish narrator offers dry outside commentary.

**Symbols and motifs.** The star rating as a public measure of a private struggle. The wall Sumi likes to sit against (fan wiki). The donut won after waiting (ch. 1).

**Traditions it echoes.** Slice-of-life stories of social anxiety (MyAnimeList reviewers compare A Silent Voice's Shoko); workplace comedies; the parent romantic comedy seen from a side character's view.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism and virtue-by-practice are celebrated together. The consumer side of rental dating is accepted without critique.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1 (ch. 1-6): Sumi's job and first mentors. Vol. 2 (ch. 7-13): the practice date with Kazuya and daily challenges. Vol. 3 (ch. 14-19): a child client, outings and a magical-girl event; then the series pauses (MyAnimeList: on hiatus since 2022).

**Discussion questions.**
