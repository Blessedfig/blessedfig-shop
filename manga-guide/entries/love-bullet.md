**Premise.** Fifteen-year-old Koharu Sakurada is the matchmaker of her class but has never been in love. Just as her best friend Aki Tamaki is confessing to her, a metal pole falls from a construction site; Koharu pushes Aki clear and is killed (ch. 1). Five years later the Goddess of Love brings her back as a Cupid: one of the winged, immortal girls who died before knowing romance and who now make humans fall in love, using guns, sniper rifles and grenades instead of arrows. Cupids earn karma for each match and can return to life at the age they died once they have enough, but they also spend karma on ammunition and gear, and they cannot feel romantic love themselves (Wikipedia; The A.V. Club; Siliconera). Under her lazy mentor Kanna, Koharu's first job is to help Aki, still grieving at college, fall for a classmate, Sakura Kasuga (ch. 2-6). The volume opens with a later mission in which rival Cupids settle a love-triangle dispute with a shoot-out (ch. 0). In volume 2 the trigger-happy Cupid Chiyo drags them to a mountain village, where Koharu helps a shy girl, Ito Kitano, reach a classmate who has turned delinquent, and they clash with Ena, a Cupid who guards her own territory and has a hidden past tied to a classmate injured in a landslide (ch. 7-14).

Outlook: Bittersweet — Koharu is already dead when her story begins, and her first act as a Cupid is to help the girl who loved her love someone else; Okazu calls it bittersweet with the emphasis on the bitter. The promise of a return to life keeps hope open, and the series is unfinished.
Hope source: L — the story's way through is love between two people, offered selflessly: Koharu's love for Aki takes the form of wanting her happy with someone else, and the reluctant friendship among Cupids (F) is the close second.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What does it mean to love someone if you cannot have them? Koharu dies at the moment her best friend confesses to her, and comes back unable to feel romantic love at all. Her job is to make other people fall in love, and her first target is the girl who loved her.

**The answer the story gives.** Love means wanting the other person happy, even with someone else. Koharu shoots Aki with a love bullet to match her with Sakura, an act Okazu calls both horrible and moving. Grief is honored, but it is not meant to last forever: Aki is allowed to live again.

**Characters as arguments.** Koharu argues for care: she looks for a solution that respects a girl's real feelings instead of picking a winner (ch. 0). Chiyo argues that the fight is the fun and might settles things; she cheats, loses and softens. Kanna argues for patient mentorship and perhaps quiet sacrifice. Ena argues for guarded independence, and her past (ch. 14) suggests why.

**Symbols and motifs.** Guns in place of Cupid's arrows, making love both violent and comic. Heart-shaped explosions. Wings that can be folded away. Karma as a currency that buys ammunition. The construction pole that ends Koharu's life and starts her afterlife.

**Traditions it echoes.** Roman Cupid myth, the afterlife-job story, and the Japanese yuri tradition of confessions left unsaid. For religious readers, Koharu's death for her friend echoes John 15:13, while an afterlife earned by karma points contrasts with teaching on grace.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Virtue ethics (self-giving love, courage) is affirmed. Might makes right is shown in Chiyo's shoot-outs and gently corrected. Progressive acceptance of love across genders is assumed without argument. The spiritual setting is fantasy furniture rather than a claim about the real world.

**Shifts across the series.** Volume 1 (ch. 0-6) is the bittersweet origin: death, grief and the first match. Volume 2 (ch. 7-14) turns to the Cupids' own relationships, the karma economy and Ena's mystery. The series is ongoing; later shifts are not yet known.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Koharu helps the girl who loved her fall in love with someone else. Is that loving her or giving up on her?
2. If a love bullet only works on people who already fit together, is the love still their choice?
3. Aki grieved for five years. When is it right to move on, and does moving on mean forgetting?
4. Chiyo likes the fight more than the matchmaking. Why does Koharu look for a different answer?
5. The Cupids pay for their ammunition with the karma they earn. What does that say about doing good for a reward?
