**Premise.** Yasuke Arahabaki, 15, lives at his family's Shinto shrine, where his older sister Miyano is the head priestess. Just as he is about to confess to his childhood friend Akari Inamori, he breaks the shrine's sacred mirror and frees Seoritsuhime ("Orihime"), a goddess of misfortune who takes his soul into herself. If he lets go of her he dies, and only her kiss within a few minutes revives him; if she is sad, a dark miasma brings calamity on everyone near her, so the curse lifts only when she is made happy (Wikipedia; Kodansha Japan, vol. 1). Her younger sister, the sun goddess Amaterasu ("Hinata"), comes to seal her away again but falls for Yasuke and confesses at the end of volume 3; Izuna, the fox god of a forgotten mountain shrine he honors on a school trip, declares herself his wife with a kiss and argues for polygamy (vol. 4). Akari confesses (vol. 9); Momoko, a distant relative raised to think of herself as his fiancee, arrives (vol. 11). The curse finally lifts and Yasuke regains his soul (vol. 12); the goddesses vanish, Yasuke decides to become a Shinto priest and inherit the shrine, and the series ends with a joint wedding of Yasuke and all the heroines, Orihime purified, everyone living together (Kodansha Japan, vol. 13; Wikipedia).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What do we owe the gods, or anyone we only ever ask for things? Taguchi's starting point was a shrine visit and the thought that the gods must be lonely, since people only make requests of them (Wikipedia). Maga-tsuki turns that thought into a comedy: a goddess of misfortune who can only be freed by being made happy.

**The answer the story gives.** Kindness, attention and treating the other as an equal. Orihime's misery causes calamity, and Yasuke's patience calms it (vol. 1); Amaterasu, the most powerful of them, loves him because he treats her as a friend (vol. 3); Izuna loves him because he alone noticed her forgotten shrine and paid his respects (vol. 4). The reward is a home in which no one is lonely.

**Characters as arguments.** Yasuke argues for gentle decency, even when he is a typical harem lead swept along by events. Akari argues for honest, single-hearted love, and for being met as oneself. Izuna argues that love need not choose. Momoko argues for an idealized partner and learns that the real one is ordinary (vol. 11). Miyano, the shrine priestess who shirks her chores, is the comic voice of practicality.

**Symbols and motifs.** The broken mirror (a shrine's sacred object, in Shinto often the body of the kami; from general knowledge; to verify). Held hands as the price of staying alive. The dark miasma of sadness. Purification. The shrine passed to the next generation.

**Traditions it echoes.** Japanese myth (Amaterasu, the heavenly plain Takamagahara, the purification deities) and the goddess-girlfriend comedy of Oh My Goddess!, which reviewers say it turns on its head (Neo, via Wikipedia). For Christian, Jewish and Muslim readers, the image of gods who need human kindness is the reverse of a God who needs nothing; for Shinto readers it is close to the idea that kami are honored and comforted by worship and care.

**How each lens is portrayed.** The Shinto world is affirmed warmly but lightly. Communitarian and traditional values win in the end (a shared home; the shrine carried on). Hedonism is played for laughs throughout, and the harem's logic (everyone gets the boy) is the payoff rather than a question.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-3 set up the curse and the sisters; volume 4 adds Izuna; volumes 5-8 are episodic fan-service comedy (dates, lingerie shop, beach, gender swap); volumes 9-11 bring confessions, guilt and Momoko; volumes 12-13 lift the curse and end in marriage and priesthood (Kodansha Japan).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Taguchi says the gods might be lonely because people only ask them for things. What do you ask for, and what do you give?
2. Why does Amaterasu fall for Yasuke? Is being treated as an equal a good reason to love someone?
3. Akari asks Izuna to meet the person she loves as herself. Why does that matter?
4. The ending gives everyone what they want. Is that realistic, and what might it cost?
5. Why do you think Yasuke decides to become the shrine's priest?
