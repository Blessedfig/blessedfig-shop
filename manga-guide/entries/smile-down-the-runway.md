**Premise.** Chiyuki Fujito, 158 cm tall and the daughter of the head of the modeling agency Mille Neige, dreams of walking in Paris Fashion Week though everyone says she is too short. Her classmate Ikuto Tsumura, the eldest child of a poor single mother with three younger sisters, sews brilliantly but plans to give up designing to support his family. Chiyuki asks Ikuto to make her a dress for an audition; it wins her a contract and leads Ikuto to a job under the abrasive designer Hajime Yanagida, a backstage rescue at Tokyo Collection (v1-2), the Geika Institute fashion show against the prodigy Toh Ayano and the reluctant model-turned-designer Kokoro Hasegawa (about v3-10), and work at the top brand Aphro I Dite, where he runs a youth line (about v11-15). Ikuto finally founds his own brand, EGAO ("smile"), with colleagues Mii, Hanaoka and Kokoro (about v16-20). In the final arc Mille Neige struggles, Chiyuki's father overworks and Chiyuki loses her smile; Ikuto builds his first Tokyo Fashion Week show around her, she walks with a real smile, and an epilogue jumps years ahead to EGAO in Paris (v21-22) (Wikipedia; Japanese Wikipedia; ciatr; Kodansha vol. 22 blurb).

## Life message

**A dream other people call impossible can be reached through relentless work, real skill and people who believe in you, and the point of making things is to make people smile.**

## Creator and publisher context

- Kotoba Inoya (story and art). Few public biographical facts were found in sources opened; this is the creator's best-known serialization. **Status: Needs more info on the creator.**
- On-record intent (Japanese Wikipedia, citing interviews): Inoya loves the friendship and heat of sports manga and chose fashion because, unlike most sports, boys and girls can compete on the same stage; to give non-fashion readers a goal like a "national tournament," the leads' future goals are shown early; the story centers human drama with problems readers can understand.
- The creator visited fashion schools, fabric shops and contests; from vol. 7 the designer HASEGAWA KAZUYA (FUKAMI) and Tokyo Fashion School supervised (Japanese Wikipedia). A MyAnimeList reviewer says the creator's note in the last volume cites COVID and serialization limits for the compressed ending (to verify).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What do you do when everyone, including people who love you, says your dream is impossible? Chiyuki is too short for the runway; Ikuto is too poor for fashion school. The title itself names an impossibility: runway models are taught not to smile (Japanese Wikipedia, citing writer Lido Hanamori).

**The answer the story gives.** Keep going, get better, and let others help. Each lead's dream is reached through work and through people who take a chance on them: Chiyuki's father, Yanagida, Mai Ayano, and each other.

**Characters as arguments.** Chiyuki argues for refusing limits; Ikuto for humble craft and duty; Kenji Fujito for protective realism that turns out to be love; Igarashi for practical calculation rooted in her own insecurity; Toh for genius that must step out of a mentor's shadow; Kokoro for choosing your own path over others' plans.

**Symbols and motifs.** The smile on the runway, forbidden and then chosen; the thread left untied to save a dress (anime ep. 3); the brand name EGAO ("smile"); measuring tapes and heights.

**Traditions it echoes.** Sports manga built on rivals, tournaments and effort; the Cinderella story turned into a career story; for religious readers, vocation and stewardship of gifts.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism is affirmed but tied to duty and teamwork; consumerism is the field, not the goal; critics' feminist reading is mixed, praising class awareness and doubting its challenge to beauty norms.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-2 (Tokyo Collection) set up both dreams; about vols. 3-10 the Geika show; about vols. 11-15 Ikuto's rise at Aphro I Dite; about vols. 16-20 his own brand; vols. 21-22 a rushed finale and time skip (ciatr; MyAnimeList reviews).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Chiyuki's father kept telling her she could not be a model. Was he wrong, cruel or protective?
2. Ikuto almost gave up his dream for his family. When is that the right choice?
3. Chiyuki first hid that Ikuto made her outfit. Why does honesty about credit matter?
4. Some reviewers say the story makes overwork look good. What does healthy hard work look like?
5. What would it mean to make things "that make people smile" in your own life?
