**Premise.** Raito Sasaki, a high-school shut-in who wants to become a top first-person-shooter (FPS) player, pays for a professional gaming coach. The coach who arrives is Rion Suzuki, a fashionable gyaru who turns out to be his classmate and a gifted player (Yen Press vols. 1-2). Her constant praise helps him improve, but her physical closeness during coaching flusters him (Yen Press vol. 1; Wikipedia). They start a gaming club at school, and Rion begins to fall for him (vol. 2). His tomboyish childhood friend, Yuu Kunioka, competes for his attention, which turns into a rivalry between the two girls (vols. 3-4). In vol. 5 Rion competes in a worldwide FPS tournament, and Raito comes to her aid (Yen Press vol. 5). All three leads are high-school students (Yen Press; MangaUpdates).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can someone's encouragement change you? Raito has shut himself in with his games; what he wants is to be good at something. The series sets up a lonely boy and an expert who praises him, and asks what that praise does.

**The answer the story gives.** Praise and practice together make him better, and the coaching relationship becomes friendship and romance (vols. 1-2). Later volumes shift to a love triangle and a tournament (vols. 3-5).

**Characters as arguments.** Rion argues for encouragement and for the gyaru as more than a stereotype: she is the expert. Raito argues that a shut-in can grow. Yuu argues for the claims of an old friendship. The fan service runs alongside these and, by the publisher's description, is a large part of the appeal.

**Symbols and motifs.** The headset and the match; praise as a reward; the club room; the tournament stage (vol. 5).

**Traditions it echoes.** The gyaru-meets-otaku romantic comedy and the sports-coaching story (from general knowledge; to verify).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism is affirmed: follow your dream. Hedonism (play and flirtation) is enjoyed without cost. No religious or political lens appears.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-2: coaching and the gaming club. Vols. 3-4: the love triangle with Yuu. Vol. 5: the world tournament. The series is ongoing.

**Discussion questions.**
1. How does Rion's praise change Raito? Have kind words ever helped you?
2. What is the difference between encouragement and flattery?
3. Rion is the expert, not Raito. Why does that matter in the story?
4. Yen Press rates this book for adults. Why do you think that is, and how do you feel about how the girls are drawn?
5. How much gaming is healthy, and what else helped Raito besides the game?
