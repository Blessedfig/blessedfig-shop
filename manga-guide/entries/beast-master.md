**Premise.** Yuiko Kubozuka, 17, loves animals so eagerly that they all run from her. Leo Aoi, a scarred, wild-eyed transfer student, rescues her cat from a tree, and animals flock to him. Classmates are terrified of him, but Yuiko finds he is gentle and childlike, raised in remote places and new to ordinary life. There is a catch: when Leo is in danger and sees blood, he goes berserk and does not remember it afterward; his guardian Kyle Tokizaburo Rosenburg ("Toki"), a former soldier and his late grandfather's secretary, stops him with tranquilizer darts, and Yuiko turns out to be the only one who can calm him by herself. In volume 2, as Leo's 18th birthday nears, it emerges that he is heir to a huge fortune and that relatives have tried to kidnap and kill him for it. After another attempt puts him in the hospital, his father moves him away; Yuiko says goodbye, but Leo comes back to her (Wikipedia; Soliloquy in Blue; Bookreporter; MyAnimeList user review).

## Life message

**Look past a frightening surface to the person underneath, and learn that real love calms and frees someone instead of smothering them.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Who is the real beast? Leo looks dangerous and sometimes is, but the people who mean him harm are the ones who look ordinary: relatives willing to kill for money. The series asks whether we judge by appearance or by what someone does.

**The answer the story gives.** Look closer, and love wisely. Yuiko is the first person not to be afraid of Leo, and she finds a sweet, childlike boy. But the story is also about Yuiko growing up: her love of animals is so eager it drives them away, and she must learn that love gives the other room (Bookreporter). By the end, Leo can choose a life among people, and he chooses her (v2).

**Characters as arguments.** Yuiko argues for courage and understanding over fear. Leo is the argument that the frightening outsider may be the gentlest person in the room, and that anger can be calmed rather than unleashed. The gang leader shows that an enemy can become a friend once he understands (v1). The relatives, unseen and unnamed, stand for greed that would destroy family. Toki shows protective love in a comic key.

**Symbols and motifs.** Animals that flee from Yuiko and flock to Leo; blood as the trigger of the beast; birds landing on Yuiko when Leo hugs her (v1); tranquilizer darts versus a calming voice; the farewell at the car (v2).

**Traditions it echoes.** Beauty and the Beast with the roles reshaped; the wild child raised apart from society; the shojo romance of the misjudged delinquent. Its warning against greed within a family echoes many religious traditions, and its tenderness to animals echoes teachings of compassion for all creatures.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Classical virtue ethics and communitarianism carry the story: virtues grow, and belonging heals. Might makes right is rejected in both gang and family form. The spirit one-shot touches folk spirituality lightly.

**Shifts across the series.** Volume 1 (ch. 1-4) is school comedy and first friendship with violent flashes. Volume 2 (ch. 5-7) turns to danger, the inheritance plot, the confession and the farewell. The two one-shots are unrelated stories about following a dream ("Fly") and reconciling with the help of a spirit ("Summer Cactus Surprise").

**Discussion questions.**
1. Everyone is afraid of Leo at first. Have you ever been wrong about someone because of how they looked?
2. Yuiko loves animals so much that they run from her. What is the difference between loving someone and smothering them?
3. Leo's relatives want his money. Why does greed turn family into enemies?
4. Leo's father wants him safe abroad, and Leo comes back. Was that the right choice?
5. What calms Leo when he is angry? What calms you?
