**Premise.** At Yagami High School, cheerful, dim Tenma Tsukamoto has an obsessive crush on her eccentric, curry-loving classmate Oji Karasuma but cannot manage to confess. Unknown to her, Kenji Harima, a reformed delinquent who once saved her from a thug, attends school only to be near her, and is just as tongue-tied; he begins drawing manga as an outlet (Wikipedia). His bungled confessions keep landing him in embarrassing situations with Tenma's rich, lonely friend Eri Sawachika, who goes from despising him to loving him; Tenma's gifted younger sister Yakumo, who can read the minds of people attracted to her, becomes his manga editor and falls for him too (Wikipedia). Around them, class 2-C (the kenpō-trained Mikoto, the mysterious Akira, the rule-bound class president Hanai and a crowd of oddballs) fill 345 chapters of gag comedy and romantic mix-ups. Near the end Tenma confesses and Karasuma returns her feelings, but he loses his memory to an illness; she devotes herself to studying medicine to help him and turns Harima down at graduation (Wikipedia; ch. 273). The epilogue School Rumble Z shows the adults: Tenma with Karasuma, Mikoto married to Hanai, and Harima married to Eri with a child (Wikipedia; TV Tropes calls the image's meaning unclear).

## Creator and publisher context

- Jin Kobayashi, born May 25, 1977, in Chiba Prefecture; began drawing seriously at university; School Rumble was his debut serial; later Natsu no Arashi! (2006-2010) and Ichiro Heian! (2011-2012) (Wikipedia).
- On-record intent (Kawaii Kon, Honolulu, 2006, via Wikipedia): he found the idea of a delinquent falling in love interesting; about 30% of Harima reflects himself; he builds stories on misunderstandings because without them there is no funny story. In 2009 he said he ended the series to do other things and would like to draw the characters' adult days in a seinen magazine (Wikipedia).
- Publisher: Kodansha (Weekly Shōnen Magazine). Del Rey's English edition was rated 13+ and stopped at vol. 16; Kodansha USA completed it digitally (Wikipedia; ANN).
- Wikipedia notes that rights-holder enforcement against fansubs was believed by fansub supporters to have hurt the anime's North American release; this concerns the anime's distribution, not the creator.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What do you do when you love someone who loves someone else? Harima loves Tenma; Tenma loves Karasuma; Eri and Yakumo love Harima. Almost everyone in School Rumble is in that position.

**The answer the story gives.** Keep loving well. The series grows its characters through their failures (Wikipedia): Harima channels heartbreak into manga, Eri drops her pride, Tenma gives her future to a man who no longer remembers her (Wikipedia; ch. 273 onward). Winning is not the point; the epilogue rewards constancy anyway.

**Characters as arguments.** Harima argues that a rough man can become gentle through love. Tenma argues for devotion beyond reason. Eri argues that pride hides loneliness. Hanai argues for rules and is constantly undercut by his own lapses. Imadori and Nishimoto are the comic case against lechery: they are always punished, though never reformed.

**Symbols and motifs.** Harima's sunglasses and disguise (hiding from Tenma, Wikipedia); his manga, in which his stand-in always wins; Tenma's pigtails that move with her mood; Karasuma's curry; the "sharp" and "flat" chapter numbering, main story and side story as in music.

**Traditions it echoes.** Japanese romantic comedy and the delinquent-redeemed story; film parodies in every chapter title (Easy Rider, Unforgiven). Its insistence on faithful, selfless love will resonate with religious readers; its pervert gags with no one.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Absurdism is the mode; virtue is the heart. Expressive individualism shows in confessions and art. Christian imagery is incidental (Sarah's church, the St. Francis joke). Hedonism appears only as the perverts' joke.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-6: gags and the triangle. Vols. 7-13: the polygon grows (Eri, Yakumo), Harima on a tuna boat (vol. 7), the festival war (vols. 8-10). Vols. 14-16: Harima and Eri, the Kyoto trip, Mikoto's breakup. Vols. 17-22 (English only digitally): Karasuma's illness, Tenma's confession and graduation. School Rumble Z: the adult epilogue.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Harima never manages to confess to Tenma. What does he do instead to show his love?
2. Is Tenma's choice to study for Karasuma brave, foolish or both?
3. The class perverts get beaten up but keep doing it. Is that funny? What would respect look like?
4. Eri acts proud but feels lonely. How do her friends help her?
5. How would you want someone to tell you how they feel?
