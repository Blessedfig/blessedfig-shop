**Premise.** Nagamasa Ryumatsu is an elderly yakuza in Kabukicho, Tokyo, of the old school that survived the postwar chaos: known for loyalty and humanity, with a tattoo of the goddess Kichijoten on his back (Mangapedia), and proud that he never touched drugs or extortion and kept his turf peaceful (Calvin Pitt). Betrayed by his sworn younger brother and a gang of young thugs, he drowns (Calvin Pitt; Mangapedia). He wakes in another world in the body of Sanaria Lyu (Ryu) Lundburg, a princess of the Lundburg kingdom, about 14 or 15 years old (TV Tropes; Mangapedia says "in her teens"), whose brother has just tried to have her killed (TV Tropes). A goddess is implied to have chosen her to fight the Demon King, whose body was cut into fragments and sealed by elves, dwarves and humans 3,000 years ago (TV Tropes). Ryu keeps the yakuza's strength, tattoo and code and ignores every expectation of ladylike behavior. With her loyal knight Giovanni and the dark-elf mage Nyui, who cannot bear to be near men (Calvin Pitt; TV Tropes), she flees a royal power struggle; in the town of Tashtria, overrun by a drug called "witchin" and ruled by corrupt knights, she makes the knights answer for it and goes after the drug trade (vol. 1; Mangapedia; Calvin Pitt). Her brother Volff has their father murdered, frames her for it and declares her executed (TV Tropes; vol. 10). Later arcs (PRH synopses): outlaw towns and a former corrupt cop (vol. 3), mermaids and pirates (vol. 4), a secret society's faction war and a marriage proposal (vol. 5), the release of one of the Demon King's six fragments (vol. 6), a black-market trade in "skill trees" and cruel experiments on the poor (vols. 7-8), a people hated for their ancestors' service to the Dark Lord (vol. 9), the old king's death (vol. 10), demon raids (vol. 11), the Demon King's child possessing a girl named Yomi (vol. 12), a knight seeking atonement (vol. 13), the elven kingdom, where Nyui becomes Spirit King and turns on Ryu (vols. 14-15), and a witch from another world whose past is tied to Ryu's yakuza life (vol. 16).

## Life message

**Real strength is a code kept in any body and any world: protect ordinary people, keep faith with those who trust you, and never profit from their ruin.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What makes a person who they are: the body they live in, the world they live in, or the code they keep? Ryumatsu loses his body, his world and his age, and keeps only his code.

**The answer the story gives.** The code. Ryu protects the weak, refuses to profit from ruin and stays loyal, in a teenage princess's body as in an old gangster's. The series presents the chivalrous yakuza as an ideal, and the young gangsters who killed him as what happens when the code is lost.

**Characters as arguments.** Ryu argues for honor as personal law. Volff is the case against power without trust: a brother who learned from trauma to kill first (TV Tropes). Giovanni stands for knightly propriety, scandalized by his princess. Nyui is loyalty with a hidden wound, and later a loyalty that breaks (vol. 15). The drug dealers and corrupt knights are the case against profit from others' ruin.

**Symbols and motifs.** The Kichijoten tattoo carried into a new body. Drugs born from demons. The Demon King cut into six pieces. Skill trees inherited from past lives. A barrel of alcohol drained by a teenage princess, played as the old man still inside.

**Traditions it echoes.** The ninkyō yakuza film, the isekai rebirth story and the gender-swap comedy. For Christian readers it raises the question of whether a code is the same as conversion; for Hindu and Buddhist readers, of what karma carries into a new life.

**How each lens is portrayed.** The honor code is affirmed without irony. Drugs and corruption are condemned. Authority is judged by whether it protects people. The supernatural is world-building. The fan service and drinking are played as comedy, not judged.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-5 are a road of town-by-town clean-ups (drugs, outlaws, mermaids, a secret society). Vols. 6-12 bring the Demon King's fragments, black markets and the brother's plot. Vols. 13-17 turn to the elves, atonement, a companion's betrayal and a witch tied to Ryu's yakuza past.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Ryumatsu was a gangster, but he refused to sell drugs. Can someone in a bad trade still have good rules?
2. Ryu keeps her old code in a new body and a new world. What about you would stay the same if everything else changed?
3. The story makes jokes about an old man in a young girl's body. Are any of them uncomfortable? Why?
4. Ryu takes the law into her own hands when the knights are corrupt. When is that right, and when is it dangerous?
5. The "imp-ure" are hated for what their ancestors did. Is it fair to blame people for their families' past?
