**Premise.** Kindergarten Noir, "the world's safest kindergarten," cares for the children of presidents, celebrities and executives. Its teachers are convicted criminals who guard the children against assassins and kidnappers in exchange for shorter sentences. Rita, Convict No. 999, 20, once the legendary assassin "the Witch," teaches the Dandelion class with Doug, a womanizing American swindler, and later Hana Bradley, from a family of assassins. Rita wants above all to find a handsome boyfriend; a running gag is that she falls for good-looking assassins, quizzes them about their tastes, and shoots them when they answer wrong (Wikipedia; fan wiki, ch. 1-2). Over time the school's real purpose comes out: it protects Lyla, a small girl who is a clone of Lyla Clark, a scientist who a century ago drugged the world's food to control minds and caused mass suicides and a world war to "reset" an overpopulated planet; the New World Order, led by Henry de Lusé, wants the clone to repeat it (fan wiki, Lyla Clark and Henry de Lusé pages). Rita's twin brother Leo works for the New World Order. Rita and Doug share an accidental first kiss (ch. 26) and later become a couple (vol. 12); Luke, a former London police officer who turned killer after his girlfriend was murdered, dies fighting the assassin Aoba (Luke arc, ch. 56-70). The New World Order raids the school (ch. 92-102); Doug is shot and taken, the school moves to New York, and the final arc follows the teachers to Las Vegas against the New World Order (ch. 103 on; fan wiki; volume blurbs 14-18).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can people who have done terrible things become good, and what would they have to do? Every teacher at Kindergarten Noir has killed or stolen; the school bets that protecting children can change them.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, slowly and at a cost. At first Rita kills as a joke (ch. 1-2); by the Natasha arc she holds a dying enemy's hand and says they could have been friends (ch. 18), and later she spares enemies out of compassion (fan wiki). Luke, sent to the school wanting to die, decides to live when a child hugs him (ch. 67), and then dies protecting his comrades. Love helps: Doug, who trusted no one, learns to trust Rita.

**Characters as arguments.** Rita argues that even the Witch deserves a normal life. Doug argues that everyone betrays you, then learns otherwise. Natasha argues that anyone can be broken, and is the clearest picture of what Rita might have become. Henry de Lusé argues that the world can be saved only by killing most of it and controlling the rest; the whole cast stands against him. Erina, the principal, argues for ruthless order in service of the weak.

**Symbols and motifs.** The Dandelion apron over a gun. The ramen question that decides whether a man lives. The ten super-strong poodles named after ramen flavors. The burned orphanage church. The clone child as both innocent and weapon.

**Traditions it echoes.** SPY x FAMILY and Sakamoto Days (ANN; Otaku USA), Murciélago (Otaku USA), the redemption-through-service plot of war films about convict squads. Christian readers may hear the parable of the lost sheep and Matthew 18; Buddhist readers may see craving and compassion in Natasha's death.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarianism and virtue are affirmed. Might makes right, utilitarian mass murder and mind-control authoritarianism are the villains' creeds and are condemned. Pacifism is explored but not adopted: the story wants fewer killings, not none.

**Shifts across the series.** Ch. 1-12 are gag-of-the-week action; the Natasha arc (ch. 13-20) introduces real death and the Lyla mystery; Asakusa and Parents' Day (ch. 21-55) build the romance; the Luke arc (ch. 56-70) is the turn to tragedy; Yokohama and quiet days (ch. 71-91) bring Rita and Doug together; the raid (ch. 92-102) and the America arc (ch. 103 on) are the war with the New World Order.

**Discussion questions.**
1. At the start Rita shoots men who give the wrong answer to her questions. Is it still funny later in the story? Why or why not?
2. Luke wanted to die until a child hugged him. What gives people a reason to keep going?
3. Henry de Lusé says killing most people would save the world. What is wrong with that argument?
4. Can someone who has hurt others become a good person? What would they need to do?
5. Why do the teachers become a family when their own families failed them?
