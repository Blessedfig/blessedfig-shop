**Premise.** Four early short stories. **"A Couple Clucking Chickens Were Still Kickin' in the Schoolyard"** (2011): in 2019 shapeshifting aliens have conquered Earth and eat humans like livestock; two survivors, Yūto and Ami, hide at a school disguised as chickens, which most aliens find disgusting to eat. A kind alien student, Yōhei, finds them and opposes the slaughter; a transfer student from a planet where aliens do eat chicken endangers them. Yūto turns out to be an alien who chose to live as a human and dies protecting Ami; the closing narration says Ami and the rest of humanity were killed soon after (TV Tropes; Anitay; Wikipedia). **"Sasaki Stopped a Bullet"** (2013): Sasaki reveres his teacher, Ms. Kawaguchi, as a god. During spring-break classes a former classmate of hers, Kuwano, who failed his university entrance exams and blames her for his ruined life, takes the class hostage with a gun and demands that she have sex with him. Sasaki steps in front of the gun and stops the bullet, talks the man into believing he still has a future, and twenty years later reaches the moon as an astronaut; a last twist suggests Kawaguchi really was a god (TCJ; Anitay; Real Sound; TVmaze). **"Love is Blind"** (2013, his first published story): on the eve of graduation, student council president Ibuki walks his vice president Yuri home to confess; a teacher, a knife-wielding mugger and an alien sent to demolish Earth for a space highway interrupt, and his sincerity wins over the mugger and the alien (TV Tropes; Real Sound; Anitay). **"Shikaku"** (2014): Shikaku, an assassin abused as a child who feels no empathy, is hired by Yugeru, a 3,500-year-old vampire, to end his bored life for $100 million. She cannot kill him; she falls in love; when police corner and shoot her, Yugeru tears through them and turns her into a vampire, and they marry (TCJ; TV Tropes; TVmaze; Wikipedia).

## Life message

**Wholehearted devotion, to a dream, a teacher, a confession or one lonely person, is the best answer to sudden violence and despair, even when it cannot save the world.**

| Meta lens | How it reads the series |
|---|---|
| Evolutionary biology | Predator and prey at their starkest: aliens treat humans as livestock, and survival depends on disguise. Courtship drives two stories (Ibuki's confession; Shikaku and Yugeru), and a rejected man's sexual demand at gunpoint drives a third. A protective outsider (Yūto, an alien) dies for a human. |
| Pragmatic | Plans rarely work: disguises fail, the bullet is stopped by a miracle, the confession fires early. What carries characters through is nerve and stubbornness rather than cleverness; Real Sound's critic stresses violence that arrives without warning. |
| Religious | No religion is named. Sasaki calls his teacher a god, and the last twist plays with whether she was one (Anitay). Death and immortality frame "Shikaku": a vampire who wants to die is given a reason to live. Faith readers weigh the stories' mercy and devotion against an assassin heroine, a vampire marriage, a classroom hostage-taking with a sexual demand and a bleak extinction ending. |
| Socio-cultural | AIPT and Endless Mode read the alien story as a fable about race, fascism and bigotry; Real Sound reads the mugger and the hostage-taker as pictures of the young man Fujimoto feared becoming, and the alien as a stand-in for natural disaster. In his afterword (paraphrased by Real Sound) Fujimoto links the stories to feeling helpless since age 17, including after volunteering in the 2011 Tohoku earthquake relief. |

Outlook: Bittersweet — three of the four stories end in hope (a dream reached, a confession heard, a despairing immortal given a reason to live), but the first ends with humanity wiped out.
Hope source: L — love between two people is the main way through: Ibuki's confession stops an invasion, and Shikaku and Yugeru rescue each other from emptiness; effort toward a dream (E, Sasaki) is the close second.

## Creator and publisher context

- Tatsuki Fujimoto (story and art), born 1992 or 1993 in Nikaho, Akita; took oil-painting classes alongside his grandparents as a child; graduated in Western painting from Tohoku University of Art and Design (2014). These four stories are his earliest: "Chickens" was his first submission at 17; "Love is Blind" his first publication (2014). He went on to Fire Punch, Chainsaw Man (Harvey Awards 2021-2023; Shogakukan Manga Award) and Look Back (Wikipedia).
- On-record intent: Real Sound paraphrases his afterword: he has felt helpless since 17, volunteered in disaster relief after the 2011 Tohoku earthquake and felt each tragedy made his work seem useless; he names the teacher Takechi in Suzuki-sensei (Kenji Takefu) as a strong influence on "Sasaki." TV Tropes says he was reluctant to republish the one-shots because he drew them in hard times. AIPT reports "Sasaki" is his personal favorite.
- Publisher: Shueisha (Jump SQ., Jump SQ.19, Shōnen Jump+). VIZ Media (owned by Shueisha and Shogakukan; from general knowledge) publishes it under SHONEN JUMP, rated T+.
- No controversies involving the creator were found in sources opened. **Status: Needs more info on the creators** (no interview about these stories was found).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What do you hold on to when violence arrives without warning? Real Sound's critic sees this as Fujimoto's signature from the start: aliens, a gunman, a mugger and an assassin break into ordinary days.

**The answer the story gives.** Hold on to a person or a dream. Sasaki holds to his teacher's lesson, Ibuki to his confession, Yūto to Ami, Shikaku to Yugeru. It does not always work: the first story, drawn at 17, ends in extinction. The later ones answer violence with talk, sincerity and love.

**Characters as arguments.** Sasaki argues for belief in the impossible; Kuwano is the same young man turned bitter, and the story offers him a future instead of only punishment. Ibuki argues that sincerity can move even a mugger. Yūto, an alien who chose to be human, argues that kindness can cross any line, and dies for it. Shikaku argues, darkly, that a person without empathy can still learn to love.

**Symbols and motifs.** Chickens: the conquered as livestock. The stopped bullet: faith made literal. The space highway: indifference on a cosmic scale, halted by one boy's honesty. The vampire's immortality: life without meaning until it is shared.

**Traditions it echoes.** Invasion fables, comic school romances, and the gag-to-sci-fi escalation of Suzuki-sensei (Real Sound). AIPT and KGUN 9 see the seeds of Chainsaw Man, and AIPT compares Shikaku to Power.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Absurdism is the method; existential questions are the content. Expressive individualism (follow your dream) is affirmed, most openly in "Sasaki." Nonviolence wins twice and violence twice, with no verdict between them.

**Shifts across the series.** The book runs chronologically from a crude, bleak first submission (2011) to a polished, hopeful romance (2014); every critic opened sees the art and storytelling improve story by story (TCJ; AIPT; Anitay).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why does Sasaki talk to the gunman instead of just stopping him?
2. Yūto was an alien who chose to live as a human. What made him choose that?
3. Ibuki puts his confession ahead of everything else. Is that brave, selfish or both?
4. Shikaku does terrible things but ends happily. Does the story forgive her too easily?
5. The first story ends with everyone dying. Why might a 17-year-old artist have ended it that way?
