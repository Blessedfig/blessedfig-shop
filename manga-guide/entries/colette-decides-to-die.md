**Premise.** Colette lost her family at six when an epidemic wiped out her village; the travelling apothecary Anno took her in, and at 17 (Mangapedia) she is the only apothecary in her mountain town, worn down by endless calls. One day, exhausted, she drops into a well said to lead to the underworld (ch. 1). There she meets Hades, who after centuries of work underground has developed an allergy to sunlight with rash and fever, and he asks her to be his physician. Given a divine protection, she travels back and forth, treating Hades, his skeleton servants, the ferryman Charon, Cerberus (who splits into three puppies when hungry), and visiting gods: Zeus (who visits as a cat), Dionysus, Apollo, Heracles and others. Her late teacher Anno arrives in the underworld blaming himself for misdiagnosing his wife's pneumonia thirty years before; Hades sends him to heaven, and Colette helps him forgive himself (vol. 1-2; Japanese Wikipedia; ANN). Over many volumes Colette and Hades fall slowly in love; Hades holds back because she is mortal (Honcierge). They become a couple in the middle volumes (English vol. 6), the underworld faces its greatest crisis and Hades makes "a fateful decision" (English vol. 8, VIZ), and in the sequel they are married and Colette has become the goddess Persephone (Comic Natalie; Mangapedia).

## Life message

**Caring for others is a calling worth keeping, but only if you also rest and let yourself be cared for; good work done with love gives life meaning, and even guilt and death can be met with mercy.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What do you owe others, and what do you owe yourself? Colette gives everything to her patients until she has nothing left. The title sounds dark, but Rebecca Silverman (ANN) stresses that the series is not about suicide: it is about a tired young woman who learns how to keep living.

**The answer the story gives.** Care for others with your whole heart, but rest, accept help and let yourself be loved. Hades is the mirror: a ruler who has worked so long underground that sunlight makes him ill. Each heals the other. Caitlin Moore (ANN) sums up the lessons as self-care, compassion and finding your way.

**Characters as arguments.** Colette argues for service; Hades for duty carried quietly. Anno argues that guilt deserves punishment, and Hades' verdict answers that a life of service outweighs a fatal mistake (vol. 1-2). Zeus argues for pleasure and is gently laughed at. Heracles, obeying a greedy king, learns to apologize. The apprentices Paula and Sera show that care can be shared.

**Symbols and motifs.** The well between worlds. Sunlight as both health and harm. Cerberus as three puppies. The blue dress and blond braid that mark an apothecary. The marriage form given away with the sequel.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Hades and Persephone myth, turned from abduction into a slow courtship the woman chooses; the healer-saint of folk tales; Japanese stories of kindly gods and a just judge of the dead. Christian readers may hear echoes of the works of mercy and of Christ's invitation to the weary to rest.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarian care and duty are celebrated. The Greek gods are honored as characters but never worshipped; no real religion is criticized. Secular readers find practical medicine at the center; religious readers find mercy, forgiveness and vocation in a pagan frame.

**Shifts across the series.** Early volumes are episodic patient stories and Anno's judgment. Middle volumes widen to heaven, Elysium and the Olympians, and the romance becomes mutual (English vol. 6). Late volumes bring the underworld's greatest crisis and Hades' decision (English vol. 8); the sequel shows Colette as Persephone in married life.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Colette never said no to anyone. Why was that a problem, and what helped her?
2. Anno wanted to be punished for a mistake. Was Hades right to send him to heaven?
3. Hades works through pain. Is that admirable or a warning?
4. How is this Hades and Persephone story different from the Greek myth?
5. Who looks after you when you are tired?
