**Premise.** Hiroto Amamiya, an orphan raised by an abusive uncle, dies with his class when terrorists sink their ferry on a school trip (ch. 1). The god of reincarnation Rodcorte sends the dead students to another world with special gifts, but mixes Hiroto up with a popular classmate of nearly the same name and sends him with nothing but a huge mana pool. In the world of Origin he is sold as a baby to a secret lab, becomes Test Subject D-01, discovers death-attribute magic, is tortured by researchers, dies, rises as an undead in revenge and is killed by his former classmates (ch. 1-2). Rodcorte curses him before his third life, hoping he will die quickly or kill himself (MangaUpdates). Reborn in Lambda as Vandalieu, a dhampir (son of a vampire father and a dark-elf mother), he loses his mother Darcia at six months old: she is tortured and burned at the stake on the orders of High Priest Gordan of the god Alda for bearing a dhampir (ch. 4-5; Japanese Wikipedia). Keeping her spirit in a fragment of her bone, he raises an army of undead, takes revenge on the town (ch. 9), rescues the ghoul elder Zadiris (ch. 12-14), becomes Ghoul King after destroying an orc horde (vols. 5-6), leads the ghouls to the ruined giants' city of Talosheim, becomes its king (ch. 40) and wipes out the imperial expedition sent against it (ch. 42-47). Later chapters follow him into human lands (ch. 51-54 and after) (fan wiki; Japanese Wikipedia).

## Life message

**An outcast wronged in three lives can build a home and a people of his own by his own power, protecting those the powerful call monsters and repaying cruelty in full.**

| Meta lens | How it reads the series |
|---|---|
| Evolutionary biology | Survival of a hunted child through alliances and kin: Vandalieu gathers a band (undead, ghouls, giants) and grows it into a nation. Breeding and status are explicit in the world's rules: ghoul women gain rank by bearing children (ch. 15), orcs have a Breeding skill and raid other races for mates (fan wiki). |
| Pragmatic | A game-like world of levels, skills and experience points: Vandalieu has his minions kill bandits and monsters to level up (ch. 7-8), plans defenses with golems and catapults, and justifies killing every invader because his "monster" nation cannot hold prisoners (ch. 44). |
| Religious | Religion drives the conflict: the church of Alda, god of law, burns Darcia (ch. 4-5) and its "heroes" persecute the races descended from Vida, goddess of life; Vandalieu is hailed as Vida's Holy Son (Japanese Wikipedia), raises the dead and destroys souls. Faith readers weigh love for a mother and protection of persecuted peoples against celebrated necromancy, revenge and mass killing. |
| Socio-cultural | A narō-style isekai revenge fantasy: the bullied, forgotten boy becomes a king. Discrimination against "monster" races, slavery and imperial conquest are the setting's injustices (MangaUpdates tags; Japanese Wikipedia). |

Outlook: Bittersweet - Vandalieu gains a family, friends and a kingdom, and his mother's spirit stays with him, but every gain comes through loss, revenge and killing, and the gods who wronged him are still at work.
Hope source: F - the way through is found family and community: the ghouls, the undead and the people of Talosheim who accept him; protecting them (P) and his own growing power (E) are close behind.

## Creator and publisher context

- Densuke (story) serialized the web novel on Shōsetsuka ni Narō from June 2015 to November 2021 and won the 4th Net Novel Award in 2016; the LN has been published by Hifumi Shobō's Saga Forest imprint since December 2016 (Japanese Wikipedia; Wikipedia). In the afterword to LN 1, Densuke says he wrote the book because he could not find the kind of story he wanted to read (AnimeNation).
- Takehiro Kojima draws the manga from Ban!'s character designs. The fan wiki says the manga adds detail to the Origin chapters (ch. 1-2) that the web novel only summarizes.
- No statements by the creators on religion, politics or the series' sexual content were found. **Status: Needs more info on the creators.**
- Publisher: Kadokawa (ComicWalker / KadoComi; MF Comics). One Peace Books publishes the English manga and LN; no age rating is shown on its series page.
- No controversies involving the creators were found.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What does a person owe a world that has wronged him three times? Hiroto is abandoned, experimented on, killed by his own classmates and cursed by the god who sent him; in his third life his mother is burned alive for having him. The series asks whether he can still build a good life, and on what terms.

**The answer the story gives.** Build it yourself, for your own people, and destroy whoever comes for them. Vandalieu's stated dream is simple happiness and a warm family (fan wiki), and he pursues it with remarkable tenderness toward allies. But the story treats revenge as justice: the town that applauded his mother's death is wrecked (ch. 9), the priest who burned her dies and is raised against his own disciples (ch. 45-46), and an invading army is wiped out to the last man (ch. 44).

**Characters as arguments.** Vandalieu argues that love and ruthlessness belong together. Darcia argues for gentleness: in the novels she tells Heinz, who helped capture her, that she forgives what he did to her but not what he did to her son (fan wiki), a line the story admires. Gordan is the argument against religious zeal without mercy. Rodcorte is the argument that a god who treats souls as tools is no god worth obeying. The ghouls and the giants of Talosheim argue that "monsters" are people.

**Symbols and motifs.** The bone fragment that holds Darcia's spirit. The undead as loyal family. Talosheim, a dead city made alive. Luxury as healing: food, baths and mayonnaise after lives of deprivation. The title itself: no fourth life.

**Traditions it echoes.** The revenge epic (The Count of Monte Cristo), the persecuted-witch story, and Japanese web-novel isekai. Its church of a law god burning a woman for her child echoes the history of inquisitions; Christian, Jewish and Muslim readers will note that the story's alternative is not mercy but a rival goddess and the hero's own power, and that necromancy, which their scriptures forbid, is the hero's gift.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Pagan/occult is affirmed: spirits and death magic are the hero's strength, and the good goddess is the true one. Communitarianism is affirmed in the found family. Authoritarian religion and empire are critiqued. Might makes right is explored and largely confirmed: strength is what keeps the weak alive. Patriotism appears in its liberation form as Talosheim is refounded and defended.

**Shifts across the series.** Volume 1 (ch. 1-5) covers the first two lives and the death of Darcia. Volumes 1-3 (ch. 5-14) are revenge and wandering with the undead. Volumes 3-6 (ch. 12-26) are life with the ghouls and the orc war. Volumes 7-9 (ch. 27-41) are Talosheim, the vampires and the coronation. Volumes 9-10 (ch. 42-48) are the massacre of the expedition. Volume 10 onward (ch. 49-79) open the gods' history and take Vandalieu into human lands, where, per the novels, he frees enslaved refugees (likely later chapters).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Vandalieu was wronged in every life. Does that make his revenge right?
2. Darcia forgives Heinz for what he did to her, but not for what he did to her son. Is that fair?
3. Why does the story make a church the villain? Have real religious groups done things like this?
4. Was it right to kill every soldier in the invading army? What else could he have done?
5. What makes the ghouls and undead a family?
