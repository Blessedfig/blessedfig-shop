**Premise.** Yui, Mio, Ritsu and Tsumugi (Mugi), the four seniors of Afterschool Tea Time, start college together after high school graduation, leaving Azusa behind. They move into a dorm, adjust to life away from their families and join the college's pop music club, where they meet another freshman band, the Girlz: Akira Wada (guitar), Sachi Hayashi (bass) and Ayame Yoshida (drums). Yui's dozy friendliness (she falls asleep and drools on Akira's shoulder at their first meeting) and Ritsu's teasing of Mio carry over from the high school strips; the two bands become friends and musical rivals. Yui's sister Ui appears as the responsible one. The volume is four-panel gag strips about dorm life, classes, rehearsals and gigs (Yen Press blurb; Manga Bookshelf; J!-ENT).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What happens to a high school friendship when school ends? K-On! College asks whether the band, and the friendship it carried, can survive the move to a dorm and a new club.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, and gently: the four stay together, join the pop music club and meet a new band, the Girlz. Growing up means doing your own laundry and living away from family, but the friendship travels with you.

**Characters as arguments.** Yui argues for openness and play: she befriends the serious Akira by falling asleep on her. Akira argues that music should be taken seriously, and her friendship with Yui suggests both attitudes can share a stage. Ui stands for responsibility; Mio and Ritsu for the comfort of old habits.

**Symbols and motifs.** Tea time as home carried to a new place; the dorm room; the club room; the stage shared by two bands.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Japanese school-club comedy and the "cute girls doing cute things" four-panel tradition; graduation and starting over are seasonal rites in Japanese storytelling.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarianism is warmly affirmed; expressive individualism appears as each band's own sound. Consumerism is background (gear, shopping). No religious or political lens is present.

**Shifts across the series.** A single volume: arrival at college and the dorm, the pop music club, meeting the Girlz, performances; Manga Bookshelf found the ending less satisfying than the high school finale.

**Discussion questions.**
1. What did the friends bring with them from high school, and what did they have to leave behind?
2. Yui and Akira are very different. What makes their friendship work?
3. How would you make new friends in a new place?
4. Is it better to take a hobby seriously or to have fun with it?
