**Premise.** Uka Ishimori was bullied all through middle school and nicknamed "Stone" ("Rocky" in the English edition) because she could not show feelings or stand up for herself; overprotective parents are named as part of the reason she withdrew (Japanese Wikipedia). In the winter of her last middle-school year a chance meeting with Kai Miura, a boy with lemon-dyed hair, leads her to enroll at his school, Hachimitsu High, a private school with a free and flashy student culture (Japanese Wikipedia). Kai is aloof with most people but quietly helps Uka make friends: Ayumi Endo (her first friend), Satoru Seto, Tomoya Takamine, and Kai's middle-school ex-girlfriend Serina Kanno, who was herself bullied after defending a bullied classmate. Her chief middle-school bully, Remi Kojima, turns up at the same school and keeps harassing her until Kai and his friends confront her; once her past bullying becomes known she and her friends leave the school (Japanese Wikipedia). Arcs follow the school year: a welcome hike (vol. 2), Serina (vol. 3), the culture festival (vol. 4), Uka's father demanding she transfer schools until she tells her parents about the bullying (vols. 5-6), Uka confessing at the sports festival (vol. 7) and dating Kai (vol. 8), a classmate, Nano, copying Uka to win Kai (vols. 9-10), a school camping trip and Kai's family secret (vol. 11), and rivals for each of them in later volumes. Kai, whose mother died when he was small and whose father left him, lives alone (fan wiki). By the end the couple plan to live together after graduation; Uka wants to become an architect and build a house for Kai (fan wiki; the last chapter's content was not found in sources opened).

## Life message

**Being bullied does not decide what you are worth: with friends who see you and your own courage to speak up, a person can heal, find her voice and learn to enjoy life.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can a person who has been told for years that she is nothing learn to believe she is worth something? Uka arrives at high school unable to laugh, cry or even say hello (Yen Press). The series asks what it takes to undo that.

**The answer the story gives.** Someone has to see you, and then you have to choose to speak. Kai notices Uka and pushes her to say what she thinks; Ayumi becomes her first friend; the friend group defends her when her father wants to move her (vol. 6). But the turning points are Uka's own: telling her parents the truth, confessing to Kai (vol. 7). ANN's reviewer sums it up as learning that you deserve to save yourself.

**Characters as arguments.** Uka argues that a quiet person can become brave. Kai, aloof but kind, argues that real care can look gruff, and his own loneliness (vol. 11) shows that the helper needs help too. Serina, bullied for defending someone, shows that bullying can strike the strong. Remi, who never changes, is the case for consequences. Nano, who copies Uka to be loved, is the case that envy is a kind of self-hatred, and she is answered with forgiveness (vols. 9-10). Uka's father argues for protection and learns to let go.

**Symbols and motifs.** Lemon soda: sharp and sweet, Kai's drink and his hair. The nickname "Stone" set against Uka's name, written with the characters for feather and flower (fan wiki, ch. 46). The house Uka wants to build for Kai, a boy without a home.

**Traditions it echoes.** The classic shojo makeover-and-confidence story, and the Ribon tradition of first love for younger readers. For some Christian readers it echoes the lifting up of the lowly; for Buddhist readers, right speech and the harm of cruel words.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarianism is the engine: the group heals. Expressive individualism is celebrated in its gentle form, becoming yourself rather than throwing off all ties. Family authority is respected and softened. Romance is treated as the natural reward of growth, which is where religious frames part company in later volumes.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-8 (ch. 1-31) are the healing arc: bullying, friends, the transfer crisis and the confession; this is what the anime covers (to ch. 28). Vols. 9-17 (ch. 32-67) are second year: rivals, the camping trip, Kai's past. Vols. 18-31 (ch. 68-123) add new juniors (Sho), more rivals, third year, and the road to graduation and a shared future.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why do you think Uka could not laugh or cry when she started high school?
2. Kai is often gruff. Is that kindness, or should he have been gentler?
3. Uka hid the bullying from her parents. Why, and what changed when she told them?
4. Nano copied Uka to be liked. What did she really want, and why did Uka forgive her?
5. If you saw someone being treated like Uka was, what could you do?
