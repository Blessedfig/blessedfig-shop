**Premise.** Yomina Sakuba ("Yomi") is the girl everyone at school notices: beautiful, top of the class, good at sports, and seemingly aloof. Ordinary high-school boy Fuyuki Hino accidentally opens the door of the girls' changing room and sees her undressing, with wings: she is a half-succubus (ch. 1, "Contract"; MangaUpdates; database synopsis). In truth she is painfully shy, longs for friends, and, as a succubus, is terrible at anything sexual (Japanese vol. 1 blurb). Fuyuki keeps her secret and helps her make friends, while dimly remembering a promise he made to her as a child; memory and fate are tied together in the plot (GANMA!). Bullies target Yomi (MangaUpdates; Mangamo). An angel, Eru Enji, a "pure" angel with a domineering streak, watches the pair, and the angels come to see Yomi as a threat to the world, turning the comedy toward a supernatural conflict over her power (print vols. 2 and 4 blurbs). Later chapter titles point to a pledge or marriage-like bond between Fuyuki and Yomi (ch. 68 "Marry," ch. 80-81, title only) and to other succubi (ch. 113, title only). The ending was not found.

**Where traditions disagree.** Every tradition welcomes the anti-bullying heart and the promise-keeping. The split is over two things. Second, the ecchi framing of high-school characters, which conservative faith and secular progressive readers both flag, for different reasons.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can you be good if your nature says you are not? Yomi is a half-succubus, a creature defined by seduction, and she is shy, kind and bad at the one thing her kind is for.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes: who you are is what you do and who loves you. Fuyuki keeps her secret and helps her find friends; the bullies and the angels who judge her by her kind are the obstacles. Whether the ending fully resolves this was not found.

**Characters as arguments.** Yomi argues that nature is not destiny. Fuyuki argues for acceptance and faithfulness to a promise. The angel Eru argues for purity and control, and, if the "Enemies Become Friends" tag holds, she may be won over. The bullies argue nothing; they are the cruelty of a crowd toward the odd one out.

**Symbols and motifs.** Wings hidden under a school uniform; a contract (ch. 1); a childhood promise half-remembered; erased memories.

**Traditions it echoes.** The monster-girl and secret-identity romcom (MangaUpdates compares it, through one reader, to My Monster Secret / Jitsu wa Watashi wa), Beauty and the Beast with the roles turned, and the old fairy-tale lesson not to judge by appearance. For religious readers the reversal of angels and demons cuts against their own stories.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism is affirmed: Yomi should be loved as she is. Communitarianism is affirmed in friendship. Pagan and occult elements are comic furniture. Christian imagery is borrowed and turned around.

**Shifts across the series.** Early chapters (ch. 1-35) are school comedy and Yomi's first friendships; ch. 36-50 bring a rescue and angels (title only); ch. 60-90 a sister plot and a pledge or marriage-like bond (title only); later chapters add more succubi and angels (ch. 113-116, title only). Nothing after ch. 119 was checked.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Yomi is judged for what she is. Have you ever been judged for something you did not choose?
2. Fuyuki keeps a promise he barely remembers. Why do promises matter?
3. How should someone respond when a classmate is bullied?
4. In this story the angels distrust the demon. How is that different from the stories your family tells?
5. Who is the fan service in the story for? How might Yomi feel about it?
