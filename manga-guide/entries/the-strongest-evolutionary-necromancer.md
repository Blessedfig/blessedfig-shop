**Premise.** Kim Jinhwan, a former special-forces soldier, has cleared the game Peccata (Korean title 죄악, "Sin") twelve times. After a mysterious phone call he wakes inside it as Adrias Cromwell, a weak, cowardly necromancer and the underling of a mid-level boss, fated to be killed, along with his master, for practicing forbidden magic (Tapas; Kakao Webtoon). If his necromancy is exposed he will die at once, and even hidden, a death event waits in six months (Tapas; Kakao's Korean blurb says one year). His one advantage is a bonus trait, "Evolution," which lets him and his summoned undead grow explosively. He trains, gathers corpses to raise skeletons, and uses his knowledge of the game to change events (Tapas; Kakao Webtoon). Database tags point to an academy setting, nobility, a cult, vampires and a revenge plot (AniList; MangaUpdates); a reader notes that chapter 31 explains why he was brought into the game world, and names a recurring character called Lucifer (MangaUpdates user comments; to verify).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** If you knew the script of your life and it ended in your execution, could you rewrite it? Kim Jinhwan has played Peccata twelve times. Now he is inside it as a minor character whose only purpose is to die.

**The answer the story gives.** Knowledge plus effort. He knows what is coming and trains, gathers resources and grows his undead with the Evolution trait. Sources opened do not reach the later arcs, so the full answer is not known.

**Characters as arguments.** Adrias argues that a side character can become the protagonist. His master, a practitioner of forbidden magic, stands for the risk of power outside the law. Database tags point to a cult, vampires and a revenge plot whose arguments could not be found (to verify).

**Symbols and motifs.** A game called "Sin"; a phone call that pulls a man into another life; skeletons that grow stronger with their master; a countdown to execution.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Korean web-fiction loop of regression and transmigration, the Faust-like bargain of forbidden knowledge for power (my own framing), and the fantasy necromancer, usually a villain, recast as the hero.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Strength rules and Pagan/occult are affirmed by the premise: power from the dead is the hero's path. Pragmatism carries the plotting. Faith readers will want to talk about why so many traditions forbid conjuring the dead.

**Shifts across the series.** Season 1 (ch. 1-46) covers the countdown and, per a reader, the chapter 31 explanation of why he was brought to the game; seasons 2-3 (ch. 47-120) were not described in sources opened.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Adrias knows the game's script. Is it fair to use that knowledge against people who don't?
2. Why do so many stories make necromancers villains? What changes when one is the hero?
3. What would you change if you knew you had six months?
4. Is courage something you are born with, or something you train?
5. Many faiths forbid calling up the dead. Why might that be?
