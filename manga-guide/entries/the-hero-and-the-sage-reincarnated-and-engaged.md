**Premise.** For fifty years the Hero Raid Freeden and the Sage Eluria Caldwin, champions of two nations at war, fought each other as equals. Both, it turns out, were kind people who kept their duels to themselves to spare the soldiers around them. When word comes that Eluria has suddenly died, Raid charges alone through enemy lines, mortally wounded, to see her coffin, and dies there (ch. 1; Manga Bookshelf, novel 1). A thousand years later he is reborn as a village commoner in a peaceful world where everyone uses magic, except him: he breaks magic devices by touching them, which makes him a second-class citizen. At about 16 he is found by Eluria, reborn as a human noble with her memories, who has searched for him for over a decade. To keep him close (and to be able to fight him again) she gets engaged to him, and the two enter the Royal Institute of Magic, where they gather friends (the comic Millis, bespectacled Wisel), humble an arrogant noble (Fareg), protect a girl with dragons whose mother is cold to her (Lufus), and investigate why Eluria died and who is trying to kill her now (novels 1-2). Later novels add a mystery about time and a parallel world, a war, and Raid's and Eluria's families; the couple confess their love (end of novel 4) and Raid proposes (novel 7) (Manga Bookshelf).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What happens when the one person who truly understood you was your enemy? Raid and Eluria knew each other only across a battlefield, yet each was the other's equal and the only one who saw the kindness behind the fighting.

**The answer the story gives.** Respect can become love, and a second life is a chance to be on the same side. Their engagement starts as a way to stay together and keep sparring, and grows into a mutual confession (novel 4) and a proposal (novel 7).

**Characters as arguments.** Raid argues that strength is for protecting others, even without magic. Eluria argues for devotion and brilliance. Fareg shows pride corrected. The Magicians' Association stands for judging people by talent. The emperor stands for using people as tools.

**Symbols and motifs.** The coffin Raid died beside; broken magic devices; the engagement as a pretext that becomes real; the duel as their way of talking.

**Traditions it echoes.** Lovers reunited across lives in folk tales and rebirth stories; the knightly rival who becomes a friend; the "power couple" light novel (from general knowledge; to verify).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Virtue and loyalty are honored; might is enjoyed but tied to protection; nationalism is a past evil; magic and rebirth are simply the world.

**Shifts across the series.** The early manga follows the school arcs (the Institute, exams, the plot against Eluria); the source novels then move to a parallel world, war and the cosmic mystery of the reincarnation (Manga Bookshelf). The manga's current position in that arc is unconfirmed.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Raid and Eluria were enemies but respected each other. Can you respect someone you disagree with?
2. Raid is treated as second-class because he cannot use magic. Where do people get judged like that in real life?
3. Why did Raid run through enemy lines to see Eluria one last time?
4. Is it fair that the heroes win every fight easily? What makes a story exciting?
5. What does your family believe happens after death, and how is this story different?
