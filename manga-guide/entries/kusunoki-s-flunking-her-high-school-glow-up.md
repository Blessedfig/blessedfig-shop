**Premise.** Keisuke Shizuki was a bespectacled loner in middle school; after his first crush turned him down, he developed a fear of pretty girls. He remade his look and manner for his high-school "debut" and it worked. Then he learns that the prettiest girl at his new school, Shizuka Kusunoki, is a former classmate who was an outcast too. Her new look succeeded, but her social skills did not, and she asks him to coach her (v1, ch. 1-7). Complications follow: Ririsa Hebikawa, the girl whose mean-spirited jabs since elementary school are the root of Shizuki's fear, attends the same school and offers Kusunoki a friendship of uncertain motive that becomes real (v2-3); a study session at Kusunoki's home goes awry and a hostile newcomer claims to be Kusunoki's childhood friend (v3). Shizuki realizes he has fallen for Kusunoki (ch. 19, from chapter title). At the school festival Kusunoki is cast as Belle in an all-girls Beauty and the Beast, with Shizuki as her dance coach, while Hebikawa and a classmate, Moe Otobe, show interest in him (v5-6) (Kodansha USA volume copy; Wikipedia).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can you become someone new? Shizuki and Kusunoki both escaped middle school by changing how they look. The series asks what a new look can and cannot do.

**The answer the story gives.** A new look opens doors, but walking through them takes practice and a friend. Kusunoki needs coaching to match her looks; Shizuki needs to face the fear his past left him (Kodansha vols. 1-2). By chapter 39 the theme turns to not rejecting the old self (from chapter title).

**Characters as arguments.** Shizuki argues for reinvention and avoidance: leave the past behind and stay away from pretty girls. Kusunoki argues that reinvention is unfinished until you can live it. Hebikawa, the source of Shizuki's fear, argues that people are more than their worst words: she becomes Kusunoki's friend (v3), though she still resents her (v6).

**Symbols and motifs.** Glasses (both leads wore them before their debuts, Wikipedia); the makeover; Beauty and the Beast, a tale about seeing past appearances, staged at the festival (v5-6).

**Traditions it echoes.** The makeover romance and the "ugly duckling." Readers from many faiths will recognize the contrast between outward appearance and the heart (1 Samuel 16:7; 1 Peter 3:3-4).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism is explored, not simply praised: the self you create must still make peace with the self you were. Consumerism's idea that looks equal worth is shown to fail.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-2 (ch. 1-14) set up the coaching and Hebikawa. Volumes 3-4 (ch. 15-30) deepen friendships and Shizuki's feelings. Volumes 5-6 (ch. 31-42) are the festival and the play, with more rivals for Shizuki's attention. Volumes 7-8 (Japanese only) are not covered by sources opened.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Kusunoki looks different now, but still feels the same inside. Why?
2. Shizuki avoided pretty girls because of what happened before. Is avoiding something the same as getting over it?
3. Hebikawa hurt Shizuki years ago and now wants to be friends. Can people change?
4. What did Beauty and the Beast have to say about looks, and does this story agree?
5. If you could have a "debut" somewhere new, what would you change, and what would you keep?
