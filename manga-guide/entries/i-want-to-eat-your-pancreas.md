**Premise.** A withdrawn high school boy who prefers books to people finds a journal titled Living with Dying in a hospital waiting room and learns that its author, his cheerful, popular classmate Sakura Yamauchi, has a terminal pancreatic illness she is hiding from everyone but her family (Wikipedia; fan wiki). Sakura draws him into her remaining months: grilled meat and offal, a trip to Fukuoka where the hotel puts them in one room by mistake, games of truth or dare, her house, and the hospital (likely ch. 2-7). Her ex-boyfriend Takahiro punches him out of jealousy (likely ch. 6), and her best friend Kyoko distrusts him. After she is discharged, Sakura is killed by a random attacker on her way to meet him, not by her illness (likely ch. 8). He reads her journal at her parents' house, learns how much he meant to her, and keeps her wish by befriending Kyoko (ch. 9-10). His name, Haruki Shiga, is revealed only at the end (Wikipedia; fan wiki).

## Life message

**No one is promised tomorrow, so the value of a day lies in how we spend it with others; we meet people not by chance but by the choices we make, and opening yourself to others is what makes a life.**

| Socio-cultural | A Japanese school story about the pressure to be liked: the most popular girl hides her illness to keep normal life, and the loner learns that refusing others is its own illness. ANN's Kim Morrissy reads the anime film as more about learning to make friends than romance. |

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** How should you live if you knew you had a year? And how should you live if you did not know, which is everyone's real case? Sakura knows her pancreas is failing; Haruki thinks he has all the time in the world and spends it alone with books. The story asks what each of them owes the other.

**The answer the story gives.** Spend your time on people. Sakura fills her months with food, travel and one deliberate friendship. Haruki, who believes no one is interested in him (ch. 1), learns he needed her as much as she needed him. Then the story removes the certainty it gave: Sakura dies not of her illness but at random, on the way to meet him (likely ch. 8). The lesson is turned on the reader: you do not know how long you have either.

**Characters as arguments.** Sakura argues that choices, not fate, bring people together (fan wiki), and that being honest about death frees you to live. Haruki argues for self-protection and is answered by grief. Kyoko argues for loyal, protective friendship, and becomes his friend in the end. Takahiro argues that love is possession, and his violence ends his place in her life (likely ch. 6). Haruki's own violent moment, pinning her to the bed (likely ch. 5-6), shows the same danger in him, and his shame and apology show the way out.

**Symbols and motifs.** The journal Living with Dying. The title phrase, first a joke about folk medicine, then a confession of wanting to become like someone (fan wiki). The Little Prince, Sakura's favorite book, which she lends him (fan wiki). His name, hidden until the end. The text message she read but never answered.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Japanese genre of the dying girl and the changed boy (ANN's Morrissy calls the setup familiar); memento mori and Psalm 90 for Christian readers; maranasati (mindfulness of death) for Buddhists; existentialist talk of authentic choice. It offers no afterlife: the dead live on in what they changed.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Existentialism is the frame and is affirmed. Communitarian and virtue lenses are affirmed through friendship and repentance. Absurdism enters through the random murder but does not win: meaning survives the senseless death.

**Shifts across the series.** Volume 1 (likely ch. 1-5) is the secret, the outings and the Fukuoka trip. Volume 2 (likely ch. 6-10) is the conflict with Takahiro, the hospital, the murder and the journal.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why does Sakura choose Haruki to share her secret with?
2. Sakura says choices, not chance, brought them together. Do you agree?
3. Haruki pins Sakura down and then runs away ashamed. What did he learn?
4. They lied to their parents to take the trip. Was it worth it? What else could they have done?
5. Sakura died suddenly, not from her illness. Why do you think the author made that choice?
