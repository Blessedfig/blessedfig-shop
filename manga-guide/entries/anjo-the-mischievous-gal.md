**Premise.** In a high school in Kasugai, Aichi, Seto is a serious, bespectacled second-year student and class president who cares mainly about his grades. His classmate Anna Anjo is a flashy, outgoing gyaru (a girl in a bold fashion subculture often stereotyped as delinquent) who starts teasing and hanging around him every day, often in suggestive ways. She liked him for his helpfulness from the start, and when Seto stands up for her against a classmate who speaks badly of her behind her back, her crush deepens (ch. 14). The teasing slowly becomes a romance as Seto grows more confident. Their friends pair off too: Anjo's friends Toyoda (a cool, Kansai-dialect girl who secretly loves cute gacha toys) and Chita (a quiet big eater) with Seto's friends Inuyama (an otaku artist) and Tokio (Toyoda's popular brother). Later chapters turn to the future: Anjo wants to be a beautician like her mother but struggles with schoolwork (ch. 6, 179), and Seto is unsure of his ambitions (ch. 98) (Wikipedia; character names come from Aichi cities).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can you know someone by how they look? Seto assumes the flashy gyaru beside him is a troublemaker; the class assumes the same. The series asks what lies under a reputation, and whether two very different people can meet in the middle.

**The answer the story gives.** People are kinder and more serious than their surfaces. Anjo liked Seto for his helpfulness, and her feelings deepen when he refuses to let a classmate talk her down (ch. 14). Over many volumes, daily closeness becomes love, Seto becomes braver, and both begin to plan real futures (ch. 98, 179).

**Characters as arguments.** Anjo argues for openness: say and show what you feel. Seto argues for restraint and diligence, and learns that holding back can also be a fear. Toyoda shows a cool exterior hiding a love of cute things (ch. 57); Inuyama shows a first attraction to looks growing into real care (ch. 7, 57-63, 90). Ms. Komaki is the adult who listens.

**Symbols and motifs.** Anjo's hair and makeup practice on Seto (ch. 6); Seto's glasses; gacha toys as hidden softness; the friend group pairing off like a set.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Japanese teasing-romance genre (Takagi-san, Nagatoro, Uzaki-chan; Wikipedia); the old story of opposites who attract; and the moral that one should not judge by outward appearance, which several religious traditions teach directly. Many of those traditions, and many secular readers, will also object to how the story sexualizes its teenage characters for adult readers.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism and everyday virtue are affirmed. Hedonism appears as playful desire, never questioned by the story. Communitarian friendship is warm. Feminist readers can note Anjo's agency, though she is also the main object of the fan service.

**Shifts across the series.** Early chapters (v1-4, about ch. 1-54) are short teasing episodes with the heaviest fan service according to reviewers. Middle volumes (v5-9, about ch. 55-115) widen to the friend group and bring Seto's serious feelings (ch. 113-114). Later volumes (v10-17, about ch. 116-220) turn to the other couples and to the future; MangaUpdates commenters say real development starts around ch. 90 and improves further around ch. 147.

**Discussion questions.**
1. What did Seto assume about Anjo at first, and what changed his mind?
2. When is teasing friendly, and when does it cross a line?
3. Why does Seto's defense of Anjo in front of the class matter so much to her?
4. This series is published for adult readers but is about high school students. Why might that matter?
5. Each friend has a dream for the future. What is yours?
