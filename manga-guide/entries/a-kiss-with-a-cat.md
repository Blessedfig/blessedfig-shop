**Premise.** Erina Wada, a high-school girl, looks like a cool, haughty "queen" but secretly adores cats; she can't stand her aloof classmate Kou Nekoyama (Kodansha; AniList). One day she rescues an injured, chubby stray in her yard and kisses it, and a naked handsome boy appears in its place: Nekoyama, who turns into a cat when any living creature kisses him and back again with another kiss (ch. 1, "The Cat and the Queen"; BOOK☆WALKER; Mechacomic). To make sure she keeps his secret, he declares he will watch her and moves into her home as her pet cat, Noriben, where they sleep in the same room and he even ends up in the bath with her in cat form (Kodansha Japan vol. 3; Mechacomic reviewer). Erina's irritation turns to love as he shows jealousy, protects her and grows fond of her (ch. 2-7 titles: "A Cat and His Prey," "Living with a Cat," "Jealousy of a Cat"); the two become a couple by vol. 4-5. In vol. 5 a friend who doesn't know his secret sees him change during summer vacation; in vol. 6 a mysterious cat briefly comes between them and they want more than kisses (Kodansha USA). Other animal-changing people exist (Mechacomic reviewer), and a friendly classmate, Inui, is part of the circle. Readers say the ending comes quickly once he stops transforming (Mechacomic reviewer); database tags indicate the couple's first sexual experience in the last volume (see Sexuality).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can you know someone from the outside? Erina looks like a cold "queen" but melts around cats; Nekoyama looks aloof but turns out to be a cat who grows deeply fond of her. Each misjudges the other.

**The answer the story gives.** No: love begins when you see the hidden self. Living together under the cat secret forces the two to see each other at home, unguarded, and the irritation of the first chapters turns into a faithful romance (ch. 1-7 titles; Kodansha blurbs).

**Characters as arguments.** Erina argues that a person's image can hide a soft heart, and that kindness (rescuing a hurt stray) reveals who you are. Nekoyama argues that coldness can be shyness; his jealousy (ch. 6) and protectiveness show how much he cares, though some readers will find him pushy. Friends who might see his transformation (vol. 5) stand for the outside world the couple must manage.

**Symbols and motifs.** The kiss: the switch between two selves. The chubby cat Noriben: the vulnerable self Nekoyama shows only to Erina. The bath and the shared room: intimacy that comes before either is ready to admit it. The end of the transformations: the couple no longer needs the secret.

**Traditions it echoes.** Fairy tales where a kiss transforms (The Frog Prince, reversed), Japanese bakeneko folklore of shapeshifting cats, Fruits Basket's animal-transformation curse, and the shōjo cohabitation romance. A MyAnimeList reviewer compares the kiss rule to Tokyo Mew Mew.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism is affirmed in a gentle form: be your real self. Hedonism is explored through physical affection more than stated as a value. Folklore magic is used for wonder and romance, not belief.

**Shifts across the series.** Volume 1 (ch. 1-4): the discovery and the start of living together. Volume 2 (ch. 5-8): catnip, jealousy, growing closeness. Volumes 3-4 (to about ch. 16): feelings admitted; the two become a couple. Volume 5: summer vacation; a friend sees him change; readers say ch. 18 turns spicy. Volume 6 (to ch. 24): a mysterious cat, the wish for more than kisses, and the ending (Kodansha; Goodreads; MangaDex chapter titles).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why does Erina hide how much she loves cats?
2. What did Erina and Nekoyama get wrong about each other at first?
3. Is it okay for Nekoyama to live in Erina's room without her parents knowing?
4. When does jealousy show care, and when does it become control?
5. The story ends with the couple wanting more than kisses. What makes someone ready for a deeper relationship?
