**Premise.** Rui Kobayakawa is the gloomiest boy in class: he pretends to sleep through breaks, no one remembers his name, and he vents his anger by painting portraits of people who annoy him. When he jumps into a river to save a cat and nearly drowns, a plain-faced girl pulls him out: it is Misaki Hoshino, the most popular gyaru in school, without makeup. Since middle school her makeup has been done every day by her childhood friend Ayano Yuge, now the art teacher, and Misaki fears that if anyone saw her bare face her friends would leave. Having seen Kobayakawa's sharp portrait of the made-up Misaki, Yuge asks him to take over as her secret makeup artist. Without makeup, Misaki also helps people anonymously as the athletic "Jersey Girl" (Japanese Wikipedia; K Manga; MangaUpdates). Arcs follow the art-club girl Iori Matsukata, bullied by a rival girls' group until Kobayakawa shouts down the crowd of onlookers; a volleyball player fighting her club's unfair rules; a baseball player's crush; an idol's stalker at the culture festival; a delinquent who confesses to Misaki at the sports festival; and Yuge's decision to leave the school (Japanese Wikipedia). By MyAnimeList forum accounts, Kobayakawa and Misaki break up near the end and he withdraws from his friends into a new school year; an epilogue followed (to verify).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What happens if people see who you really are? Misaki's popularity rests on a face she cannot make herself; Kobayakawa's invisibility rests on never letting anyone in. The series asks both to close their eyes, as the title says, and to trust someone with what is underneath.

**The answer the story gives.** Being seen is frightening but worth it. Matsukata, Kano and others each reach a point where they show their real face or real work and are accepted (Japanese Wikipedia). The ending complicates this: readers report that the two leads part and Kobayakawa withdraws, so the series closes on a less tidy note than its side stories (MyAnimeList; to verify).

**Characters as arguments.** Kobayakawa argues that the crowd is not right just because it is large. Misaki argues, at first, for safety through image. Yuge shows love that is also dependence: she became a teacher to keep watching over Misaki, and must leave for both to grow. Kano shows how the bullied can become bullies. Matsukata shows talent coming out of hiding.

**Symbols and motifs.** Makeup as a mask and as an art. The portrait: seeing someone truly. The anonymous "Jersey Girl," good deeds done without the famous face. Closed eyes: trust.

**Traditions it echoes.** The secret-identity romantic comedy, the ugly-duckling makeover story turned inside out, and the honne/tatemae theme of Japanese school fiction.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism is celebrated, but within friendship. Critiques of school hierarchy are woven into episodes rather than argued.

**Shifts across the series.** Early volumes: the secret, the makeup and the bullying of Matsukata. Middle volumes: club and festival episodes with side characters. Late volumes: Yuge's departure, the romance and the disputed ending (chapter ranges not confirmed).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why is Misaki so afraid to be seen without makeup? Is she right to be?
2. Kobayakawa yells at the people watching the bullying, not the bullies. Why?
3. Kano bullied others after she was bullied. How did that happen?
4. Is keeping Misaki's secret a kind of lying?
5. Did the ending feel right to you? What would you change?
