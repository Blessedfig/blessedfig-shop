**Premise.** Ten years after the hero Rovel Vankreift saved the kingdom of Tenbahl from monsters and vanished, he returns with a wife, Origin, the Queen of Spirits and goddess of creation, and their daughter Ellen. Ellen is half spirit and remembers a past life as a scientist in modern Japan; because she knows the periodic table perfectly she can create or unmake matter (gold, diamonds, medicine). Her doting father is more childish than she is, so she often plays the adult. The family is drawn into the affairs of Rovel's noble house (his brother Sauvel's ruinous marriage to Princess Agielle, then his marriage to the barmaid Aria and their daughter Rafilia) and into a 200-year-old crime of the royal family: a past king tortured spirits to force the Spirit Queen to serve him, and the tortured spirits now cling to the royal bloodline as a curse. The cunning Crown Prince Ravisel wants Ellen married into his line to break the curse. Ellen refuses to help until the royals truly repent, while she quietly cures miners' lung disease and a national epidemic (anime ep. 1-12, ch. 1-38) (Wikipedia; AniList; Manga UP! Global).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can an old wrong be forgiven by people who did not commit it, and should power be used to force the issue? Two centuries ago a king tortured spirits to make the Spirit Queen serve him. His descendants carry those suffering spirits in their blood and cannot form contracts with spirits. The crown prince wants Ellen, the half-spirit daughter of the Spirit Queen, to fix it for him.

**The answer the story gives.** Forgiveness has to be earned by the guilty, not demanded or inherited. Ellen tells Gadielle that his ancestor never regretted the crime, that time means nothing to immortal spirits, and that his father's schemes show no remorse (ep. 11). Meanwhile she uses her power the other way: to heal, quietly and at her own cost (ep. 7-12). Love inside a family and honesty inside a marriage are what keep people safe.

**Characters as arguments.** Ellen argues for knowledge joined to conscience; her chemistry cures a "curse" that was really lung disease. Rovel argues for loyal, doting love, played for laughs. Ravisel argues for raison d'état: anything that secures the crown is fair, even a child's kidnapping; he loses. Agielle is greed and lies; she is stripped of marriage and touch. Aria shows a weak will that can still turn: her mark fades only as her thoughts change. Gadielle is the sincere heir who must learn that his sorrow alone is not enough.

**Symbols and motifs.** The periodic table as a spell book. The thorn mark on Aria's wrist. Miasma that is really trapped, suffering spirits. The yearly Spirit Festival, a prayer without repentance. Gold that has to be hidden in a mine so it will not ruin anyone.

**Traditions it echoes.** The isekai "reincarnated with modern knowledge" template; fairy-tale court intrigue; the biblical and rabbinic debate over whether children bear their fathers' sins (Exodus 20:5; Ezekiel 18:20); Japanese ideas of nature spirits who curse when wronged. The goddesses Vor and Var take their names from Norse goddesses of awareness and of vows (from general knowledge; to verify).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Pagan/occult is the world's reality, and it is moral: spirits punish coercion and goddesses punish broken vows. Communitarianism and traditionalism win: the good house prospers. Pragmatism is Ellen's method, and the story approves even when it costs others. Secular humanism appears as Ellen's method but not her worldview.

**Shifts across the series.** Ch. 1-10 (likely; anime ep. 1-3): return of the hero, Agielle's fall. Ch. 10-19 (ep. 4-6): Aria's mark and the truth of the curse. Ch. 20-38 (ep. 7-12): the mines, the epidemic, the kidnapping and the medicine war. After ch. 38 sources opened give only that Ellen and Gadielle grow close and that Ellen later becomes a god (Wikipedia); details to verify.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Ellen will not help the royal family until they are truly sorry. Is that fair to Gadielle, who is sorry?
2. Ellen lets people suffer a little longer so that Ravisel will pay a fair price for medicine. Was she right?
3. Why do you think the story makes the selfish princess fat? Is that fair?
4. Ellen hides the gold she can make. Why might too much easy money hurt a family or a town?
5. The miners thought their sickness was a curse. How did Ellen find out what it really was?
