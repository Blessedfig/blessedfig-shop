**Premise.** Yu Inuzuka's naturally villainous face and awkwardness keep people away, so he gives up live-streaming to start over in high school and moves into the school dormitory. It turns out to be a share house of six 16-year-olds, three girls and three boys, with a no-romance rule, and every one of them is secretly a streamer (GANMA!; Wikipedia; TV Tropes). Hakua Arisugawa streams as "Usagidon" (chapter 5 asks whether she is Usagidon); Yuzuko Jimi streams as "Puchineko"; Sora Takane is a net idol; Aruvina ("Vina") Satou is also a streamer; and Souta Saotome cross-dresses to set a trap for Yu. To protect Hakua's secret, Yu lets the house believe he collects women's cosplay costumes, and Yuzuko uses that to blackmail him into running errands. Hakua, Yuzuko and Sora all come to like Yu, Souta likes Yuzuko, and Yu is tempted back into streaming (TV Tropes; Mangamo; AniList).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Who are you: the face people see, the persona you stream as, or someone else? Yu's frightening face hides a gentle boy; his housemates' cheerful online characters hide lonely or guarded teenagers.

**The answer the story gives.** As far as sources show, that people become known through trust: secrets shared and protected turn strangers into friends. Yu's first act in the house is to take a humiliating cover story on himself to protect Hakua (TV Tropes). The title suggests that falling in love is what brings him back to streaming.

**Characters as arguments.** Yu argues for kindness behind appearances. Yuzuko argues, at first, that secrets are leverage. Hakua argues for family after loss. Souta's cross-dressing trap is rivalry played as farce.

**Symbols and motifs.** The animal motifs (dog, rabbit, cat, alpaca, bat, white crow) behind each character's name and persona (TV Tropes); the stream, a window that shows and hides at once; the house rule against romance.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Japanese share-house and dorm romantic comedy, the harem comedy, and the secret-identity story; the boy misjudged for his looks.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism and found-family communitarianism are affirmed lightly; no lens is argued at length.

**Shifts across the series.** Early chapters: arrival and discovering Hakua's secret (ch. 1-5 titles, GANMA!). Later development, the love triangle's resolution and the ending are not documented in sources opened.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Yu's face makes people afraid of him. Has anyone ever misjudged you by how you look?
2. Why did Yu take the blame to protect Hakua's secret?
3. Is it fair for Yuzuko to use someone's secret to get what she wants?
4. What is the difference between an online persona and a lie?
5. What should teens think about before streaming to strangers?
