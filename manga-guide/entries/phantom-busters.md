**Premise.** On the first day at Ryuoh High School in Kamakura, Eugene Korekishi, a top student of British descent who refuses to believe in ghosts because his grandmother never came back as she promised, meets Mogari Shishikuno, the runaway heir of a clan of spirit mediums from the mountains of Akita. Mogari cannot see ghosts, but he exorcises them by eating them. They recruit Kaoru Kanzaki, who sees ghosts and has suffered for it since childhood, and Kotaro Tamon, who can hear and talk to the dead but is so anxious around living people that he vomits from nerves. Together they form the Paranormal Research Club, the Phantom Busters, with Kotaro's condition that they always try to talk to a spirit before exorcising it (ch. 1-4). Their cases range from a karaoke-obsessed ghost to a lost boy killed by a truck and a resentful goddess at an abandoned shrine. Meanwhile, Mogari's clan requires him to perform 1,000 exorcisms in a year to stay in school, sends an assassin, then drags him home; his friends fight to bring him back for the school festival (ch. 23-30) (fan wiki; Wikipedia; VIZ).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What do the living owe the dead, and what do friends owe each other? Each boy is haunted, literally or not: Eugene by a grandmother who never came back, Kaoru by ghosts he never asked to see, Kotaro by a world of the living he cannot face, Mogari by a clan that owns his future.

**The answer the story gives.** Listen first, then act; and do not face it alone. The club's founding rule, Kotaro's condition that spirits be heard before they are exorcised (ch. 4), becomes its ethic, and most cases end with a ghost at peace rather than destroyed. The friends answer each other's hauntings the same way: by showing up (the sleepover and the shrine, ch. 11-12; the rescue from the clan, ch. 23-30).

**Characters as arguments.** Mogari argues for freedom and loyalty: he fled a life chosen for him and will not let anyone hurt his friends. Eugene argues for reason and is respected; he ends neither a believer nor a denier. Kotaro argues for compassion even toward the dead. Kaoru argues that a gift can be a wound. The clan argues that rank and duty matter more than a person, and loses. Ren shows that obedience can be unlearned.

**Symbols and motifs.** Eating ghosts: Mogari takes others' darkness into himself, which costs him (purification rituals, ch. 9; the sorcerer's attack, v5). The sea at Kamakura, where the story begins with a near-drowning. Kaoru's scars. The 1,000-exorcism quota as a trap. The clubroom, a haunted room turned home.

**Traditions it echoes.** Ghostbusters (the name, ANN), Natsume's Book of Friends (ANN compares it), and older Japanese tales of angry spirits enshrined as gods (from general knowledge; to verify). For Buddhist readers, the stress on regret and letting go echoes Obon; for Christian readers, the friendship echoes "bear one another's burdens," while the spirit contact raises cautions.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarian friendship and individual freedom are both affirmed: Mogari leaves one community (the clan) for a better one. Authoritarian control is critiqued through the clan. The occult is the world's furniture rather than a creed. Pacifist and Buddhist ideas surface in Kotaro's rule. Secular reason is respected but not made the final word.

**Shifts across the series.** Ch. 1-8 (v1-2): forming the club, Angel, the knife incident, the assassin. Ch. 9-12 (v3): Kaoru's family and the shrine goddess. Ch. 13-15 (v4): finding an advisor and Kotaro's rock festival. Ch. 16-22 (v4-6): summer, the sorcerer Botan and Mogari's past. Ch. 23-30 (v6-8): the clan kidnaps Mogari before the school festival. Ch. 31-37: interludes (fan wiki).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Kotaro asks the club to talk to spirits before exorcising them. Why does that matter, and does it apply to people too?
2. Eugene does not believe in ghosts but trusts Mogari. Can you trust someone without sharing their beliefs?
3. Kaoru hid his family's problem from his friends. Why was it hard to ask for help?
4. Mogari left his family's clan. When is it right to break with family, and when is it not?
5. What does your family believe about the dead, and how is it different from the story?
