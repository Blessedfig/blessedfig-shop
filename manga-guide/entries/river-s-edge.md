**Premise.** In a run-down suburb of Tokyo in the early 1990s, beside a polluted river and a field of tall weeds, high schooler Haruna Wakakusa rescues her classmate Ichiro Yamada, who has been beaten and shut in a locker by her boyfriend Kannonzaki's gang (Women Write About Comics). To thank her, Yamada shows her his "treasure": a decomposing corpse hidden in the riverside field, which he visits for courage (From Cover To Cover; TCJ). The only other person who knows is Kozue Yoshikawa, a teen model with bulimia who is attracted to Haruna (Japanese Wikipedia; TCJ). Yamada is gay and dates Kanna Tajima to hide it; Kanna becomes obsessed and stalks Haruna. Haruna's friend Rumi sleeps with many men, dates older men for money and gifts, sleeps with Kannonzaki, and becomes pregnant; Rumi's older sister reads her diary and resents her (Japanese Wikipedia; WWAC). Kannonzaki deals drugs and is neglected at home. The stories collide in one violent night of fire and attempted murder in which one character dies (WWAC; ANN; TCJ), and afterward the survivors take small steps in new directions (WWAC).

## Creator and publisher context

- Kyoko Okazaki (story and art), born December 13, 1963, debuted in 1983 in an adult men's magazine while studying at Atomi University, then wrote for fashion, josei and seinen magazines; her major works are Pink (1989), River's Edge (1993-1994) and Helter Skelter (1995-1996) (Wikipedia, Kyoko Okazaki).
- Scholarship cited by Wikipedia describes her characters as "material girls" who consume goods and bodies and end in doubt; she reappropriated girlhood imagery for adult women; Alwyn Spies links her to third-wave feminism. She wrote of love as fierce and bound up with capitalism (Wikipedia, quoting her on Pink).
- In May 1996 she and her husband were struck by a drunk driver; she has not published since. Her works continue to be reprinted and filmed (Wikipedia). No statement by her about River's Edge specifically was found in sources opened. **Status: Needs more info on the creators.**
- Publisher: Takarajimasha (CUTiE, a fashion magazine). Kodansha USA rates the English edition 18+.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What does it take to feel alive in a world that feels dead? The teenagers of River's Edge have money, freedom and almost no one watching them, and they feel nothing. Yamada finds courage only by looking at a corpse.

**The answer the story gives.** There is no tidy answer. Sex, drugs, shopping and status all fail. What survives is one honest friendship, between a girl who rescued a bullied boy and the boy who trusted her with his secrets. WWAC's reviewer says the reader senses these kids will be okay eventually, probably.

**Characters as arguments.** Haruna begins detached, sleeping with her boyfriend out of curiosity, and slowly learns to see others (From Cover To Cover). Yamada argues that a hidden self can survive cruelty. Kozue shows perfection as self-destruction. Rumi shows worth measured in gifts. Kannonzaki shows insecurity turned into violence, and is given moments of vulnerability. Kanna shows love turned into possession.

**Symbols and motifs.** The corpse in the weeds: a secret, a place outside the gossip of school, and finally part of the soil (TCJ, Chazan). The polluted river and smokestacks. Kittens cared for and killed. Fire on the climactic night.

**Traditions it echoes.** The 1986 American film River's Edge (WWAC), the alienated-youth novel, and Okazaki's own Pink and Helter Skelter; critics compare later artists such as Inio Asano (ANN).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Existentialism and nihilism are explored, not preached; consumerism and hedonism are shown as empty. No religious or traditional answer is offered, and none is mocked.

**Shifts across the series.** A single volume: the rescue and the corpse (opening chapters), the widening web of secrets, jealousy and pregnancy (middle chapters), and the violent night and its quiet aftermath (final chapters). Chapter numbers not confirmed.

**Discussion questions** (for older readers).
1. Why does Yamada call the corpse his treasure? What is he missing?
2. Where are the adults in this story? What difference would they make?
3. Haruna starts out numb. What changes her?
4. Rumi buys brand goods with gifts from men. What is she really after?
5. Is there any hope at the end? Where?
