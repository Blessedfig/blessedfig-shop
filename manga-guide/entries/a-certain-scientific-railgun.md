**Premise.** Academy City is a walled, near-future city of students, about 80% of its population, where a "Power Curriculum Program" develops psychic powers and ranks every esper from Level 0 to Level 5 (Wikipedia; Seven Seas blurb). Mikoto Misaka, a middle-school student at the elite girls' school Tokiwadai, is an electromaster and the third of only seven Level 5s, nicknamed "Railgun." With her devoted junior and roommate Kuroko Shirai (a teleporter in Judgment, the student disciplinary force), Judgment hacker Kazari Uiharu and powerless Level 0 Ruiko Saten, she stumbles into the city's hidden research: the Level Upper, a program that boosts powers and puts users in comas, built by researcher Harumi Kiyama to save comatose orphans from an earlier experiment (v1-3, ch. 1-17); the Sisters, 20,000 clones made from Mikoto's donated DNA to be killed by the top esper, Accelerator, so he can reach "Level 6" (v4-7, ch. 18-about 39); the Daihasei sports festival plot (v7-10); a juvenile-hall "jailbreak" arc (v14-17); and a final prequel arc about Mikoto's first year amid Tokiwadai's clique wars and illegal research by a company called Antikythera (v17-21, ch. 131-170) (Wikipedia chapter list; Toaru wiki).

## Life message

**No person may be used as a thing, not even a clone made in a lab, and friends who stand together can face a powerful system that ranks and uses people; you do not have to carry that fight alone.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What is a person worth if she can be made, numbered and replaced? Academy City ranks children by power and spends them on research; the Sisters, clones of Mikoto, are produced to be killed. The series asks whether anything can outweigh one life.

**The answer the story gives.** Nothing can. Mikoto's rage at the death of Misaka 9982 (v4) turns into a campaign against the labs (v5), then into despair and a resolve to die (v6), and finally into the lesson that she needs others: Tōma stops Accelerator and the clones are saved (v7). Later arcs repeat the pattern with Saten, Uiharu and Kuroko taking their turns as rescuers.

**Characters as arguments.** Mikoto argues for conscience and power used for others. Accelerator argues for strength as worth. Kiyama argues that good ends justify terrible means, and is shown both wrong and pitiable. Saten, a Level 0, argues that worth is not rank. Kuroko argues loyalty, in a form readers find both devoted and troubling.

**Symbols and motifs.** The coin Mikoto fires as a railgun: small, ordinary, decisive. The Gekota badge she gives Misaka 9982, which the clone will not give back. The "Level" numbers. The windowless building where the city's ruler lives (v10).

**Traditions it echoes.** Frankenstein and later clone stories; the Kantian rule never to use people merely as means; for faith readers, every life as made in God's image; for Buddhist readers, compassion for all beings; Japanese school-life comedy.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Transhumanism and utilitarian calculation are the villains' creeds. Duty to persons and friendship win. Feminist readers find capable heroines and object to the gags; religious readers find a strong pro-life ethic about clones alongside sexual humor among minors.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-3 (ch. 1-17): the Level Upper. Volumes 4-7 (to about ch. 39): the Sisters arc, darkest and most praised. Volumes 7-10: the Daihasei festival. Volumes 11-13: lighter cases and the heaviest fan service (v11). Volumes 14-17: the jailbreak arc. Volumes 17-21 (ch. 131-170): Mikoto's first year and Tokiwadai's clique wars, ending with "Tokiwadai's Railgun" (ch. 170).

**Discussion questions.**
1. The Sisters were made in a lab to be killed. What makes them people?
2. Tree Diagram said 20,000 deaths were a fair price. Why is that wrong, even if the math works?
3. Mikoto tried to stop the experiment alone. Why did she need others?
4. Is Kuroko's behavior toward Mikoto funny, or is it harassment? Why do some reviewers object?
5. Saten is a Level 0. How does the story show that rank does not equal worth?
