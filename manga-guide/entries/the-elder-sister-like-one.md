**Premise.** Yuu Hasunuma lost both parents in a car crash at five and was passed among relatives who did not want him; he learned to hide his feelings so as not to be sent away (Wikipedia). At 14 (ANN; The Fandom Post) he lives in the country house of his mother's cousin Ryou, under one rule: stay out of the storehouse (MyAnimeList). When Ryou is hospitalized, Yuu goes in and summons a horned, goat-legged being with tentacle-like hair who offers to grant his deepest wish in exchange for what he values most. Overwhelmed and frightened, he wishes for family, and asks her to be his elder sister; she agrees and seals the contract with a kiss (ch. 1; Wikipedia). The next morning she looks like a young woman, calls herself Chiyo and tells the village she is his sister. The series follows their summer days, cooking, shopping and playing in the countryside, with Chiyo slipping into her monster form for comedy (Wikipedia; The Fandom Post, vol. 2). From vol. 3-4 a second being, Haru, a well-liked figure in the shopping district, becomes another "sister" (Yen Press, vols. 4-5). Vols. 5-6 turn to the past: Ryou loved his cousin Youko, Yuu's mother, and after her death tried an occult ritual to bring her back; Haru wears Youko's face, and Ryou is still trying to restore her (Yen Press, vols. 5-6; Moegirl, unconfirmed).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What would you give to be loved? Yuu has been passed around since he was five, learning to be no trouble. Offered anything in exchange for what he values most, he asks only for an older sister. The series asks whether love that comes by contract, from something not human, can still be real.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, at least for now. Chiyo's devotion heals Yuu, and the daily life of the pair is drawn as warm and funny (Silverman: "a surprisingly heartwarming tale of a boy and a buxom demon cohabitating"). What she took in payment is not stated in any source opened, and the narration hints at trouble ahead (The Fandom Post). The older story of Ryou and Youko shows the dark side of refusing to let a loved one go.

**Characters as arguments.** Yuu argues that a child needs to be wanted. Chiyo argues that love can come from outside the human order. The relatives are the case against duty without care. Ryou is the mirror of Yuu: he too made a forbidden bargain out of love, and it left him broken (v5-6). Haru, a demon wearing a dead woman's face, is the warning that what answers such a call may not be what it seems.

**Symbols and motifs.** The forbidden storehouse. The kiss that seals the contract. The word "Sis." Goat horns and tentacle hair, cosmic horror turned domestic. A face borrowed from the dead (v6).

**Traditions it echoes.** H. P. Lovecraft's Cthulhu Mythos (Shub-Niggurath); the Faust story of a bargain with a demon; the Japanese monster-girl and older-sister romantic comedies; folk tales of the lonely child taken in by a spirit. Faith readers will also hear the warning that evil can come as an angel of light.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Pagan/occult content is presented as wonder, not threat, though shadowed. Communitarianism is affirmed in a small way: a made family in a village. Hedonism is present mainly as fan service aimed at the reader. No secular or religious argument is made.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-2: the summoning and summer slice of life, with most of the fan service reported. Vols. 3-4: Haru appears; Chiyo holds her captive, Yuu wins her release and she stays (Yen Press, vol. 4). Vols. 5-6: the past of Ryou, Youko and Haru; the series has not continued since vol. 6 (2022).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why did Yuu ask for a sister rather than wealth or power? What does that say about his life before?
2. What did his relatives owe him, and what did they fail to give?
3. Can love that comes by a contract be real?
4. Ryou also made a bargain for love. How is his story a warning?
5. Critics asked whether the boy needed to be 14. Why does his age matter to how readers see the story?
