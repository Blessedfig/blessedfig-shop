**Premise.** In 2006, Satoru Fujinuma, 29, a struggling manga artist who delivers pizza, has an involuntary power he calls "Revival": he is thrown back a few minutes before a deadly incident and can prevent it. When his mother Sachiko is murdered in his apartment and he is framed (ch. 1-5), Revival sends him back to February 1988, into his 11-year-old body in Hokkaido, weeks before a serial killer began taking his classmates (ch. 6). He sets out to save Kayo Hinazuki, a lonely girl beaten by her mother, by befriending her, and later hides her and brings in his friends and his mother (vols. 2-5). The killer is his trusted homeroom teacher, Gaku Yashiro, who drives Satoru into an icy river (ch. 31). Satoru wakes in 2003 after a 15-year coma (ch. 33), with his friends and mother having kept faith in him, and finally traps Yashiro (ch. 42-43). In the epilogue (ch. 44, 2012) Kayo is married to Hiromi with a son, and Satoru meets Airi again (Wikipedia; TV Tropes; Yen Press).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What would you do if you could go back and save someone? Satoru is given that chance, and the series asks what it takes: power, or courage and other people.

**The answer the story gives.** Courage and other people. Satoru's first attempt to save Kayo fails (vol. 2-3); he succeeds when he brings in his friends and his mother (vols. 4-5). When he is lost to a coma, the same friends and mother carry him (vols. 6-8).

**Characters as arguments.** Satoru argues that reaching out to a lonely child is worth any risk. Sachiko argues for watchful love. Kayo argues that a hurt child can learn to trust. Kenya argues for justice through law. Yashiro is the argument from emptiness: a man who feels nothing except when he controls others; the story shows where his story began (ch. 32) without excusing it. Jun Shiratori shows how easily the different are blamed.

**Symbols and motifs.** The blue butterfly that signals Revival. Snow, cold and the town at night. Kayo's essay about an island and a town without her, answered by the title, The Town Where Only I Am Missing, when Satoru is the one missing. The spider's thread the killer sees above people (ch. 32 title).

**Traditions it echoes.** Time-loop rescue stories, the detective thriller, and the Japanese story of the hidden monster in a small town. Readers of many faiths will hear "do not stand idly by" (Leviticus 19:16) and the Good Samaritan.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Courage and community are affirmed; might-makes-right and the killer's nihilism are condemned. Institutions are criticized for failing children, but the answer is people who act, not a political program.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-3 (ch. 1-18) set up 2006, the first return to 1988, a failure and a return to 2006 as a fugitive. Volumes 4-5 (ch. 19-30) are the rescue of Kayo and the hunt for the killer. Volume 6 (ch. 31-35) is the near-drowning, the killer's past and the coma. Volumes 7-8 (ch. 36-44) are recovery and the final trap. Erased: Re (vol. 9) adds side stories (from general knowledge; to verify).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Satoru noticed Kayo standing alone. What does it take to notice someone who is struggling?
2. His first plan failed. What did he do differently the second time?
3. Why did no adult help Kayo at first? What should an adult do if they suspect a child is being hurt?
4. The killer was a trusted teacher. How can you tell when an adult's behavior is not right, and whom would you tell?
5. Satoru's friends and mother waited fifteen years. What does it mean to keep faith in someone?
