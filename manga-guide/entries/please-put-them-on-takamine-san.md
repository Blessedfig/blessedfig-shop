**Premise.** Takane Takamine is her high school's flawless student council president. Koushi Shirota, a friendless, below-average classmate, sees her changing and then sees her take off her underwear after a test score of 98, after which she somehow has 100. Her secret: removing a piece of underwear lets her rewind time, and the garment vanishes. Because he saw her naked, Shirota now remembers each rewind too. When he refuses to become her "closet," the one who carries spare underwear and puts it back on her in secret, she screams that he assaulted her, then rewinds once he agrees (ch. 1). The comedy follows his panicked attempts to dress her unseen, her sexual teasing at school and at her home, a childhood memory that explains her feelings for him, his friend Eri (a model with a girlfriend), and a school play that ends in a kiss (about ch. 29) (Wikipedia; anime episode summaries).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What would you do if you could undo every mistake, and what would it cost the person who has to help you? Takamine's power keeps her perfect; Shirota pays for it.

**The answer the story gives.** Perfection is lonely; the person who sees you at your most exposed and stays is the one worth keeping. That answer is wrapped in sexual comedy and begins with coercion the story never questions.

**Characters as arguments.** Takamine argues for control: of her image, her mistakes and Shirota. Shirota argues for loyalty without reward, which the story eventually rewards. Eri shows an easy, honest relationship (with Rurika) as a contrast. Ouji is vanity unmasked.

**Symbols and motifs.** Underwear as both power and vulnerability; the bag Shirota carries; the cat Kuro, tied to the childhood memory that explains Takamine's feelings (ch. 6, 12); Cinderella, the play in which the servant becomes the prince.

**Traditions it echoes.** The ecchi romantic comedy and the teasing-girl romance; Cinderella; time-loop stories. ANN's reviewers set it against fan-service comedies that do not depend on a false assault charge.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Hedonism is celebrated. Power over another person is played as flirtation. Virtue appears only as Shirota's loyalty.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1-2 (ch. 1-13): the setup and school gags. Vol. 3-4 (ch. 14-26): sleepover, Eri and the culture festival. Vol. 5 (to ch. 29): the play and the kiss (end of the anime). Vol. 6-11: not covered by sources opened; the series ends in January 2027.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Takamine threatens Shirota with a false accusation. Why is that serious, even in a comedy?
2. If you could undo every mistake, would you? What would you lose?
3. Is Shirota's loyalty a strength or a problem?
4. How is Eri's relationship different from Takamine and Shirota's?
5. What makes a joke about someone's body or consent funny to some readers and hurtful to others?
