**Premise.** In a peaceful age, adventurers and demons no longer kill each other: dungeon raids are a ranked entertainment industry, fought with magical substitute bodies (avatars), watched by fans, and the only real loss is money (Comikey; TV Tropes). Remme, a black mage who specializes in debuffs (status effects), is pushed out of the fourth-ranked party because his teammates think he adds nothing, though its leader, his childhood friend Phoenix (Fenix), argues for him. No other party wants a black mage. Milla, a woman who once fell for him after he rescued her from an assault, recruits him; she is Carmilla, a vampire and one of the Four Generals of the Demon King's Castle, the one dungeon nobody has ever cleared. The Demon King, Lucy, sees that he was trained by her grandfather, the former Demon King Lucifer, and makes him chief of staff under the name Lemegeton, a horned demon persona that hides his identity (TV Tropes). From inside the castle he rebuilds dungeons, faces his former party (ch. 14-22, title only), fights in a tag tournament (ch. 32-41), and runs the castle's floors against invading heroes, from the hellhound floor (ch. 53-56) to the vampires' floor (ch. 58-61) and the succubi's floor (ch. 64-67) (MangaDex and Manga UP! chapter titles).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Who decides what you are worth? Remme's party judged him by what fans and rankings could see, and his debuffs did not look like much.

**The answer the story gives.** Worth shows in the right setting, among people who understand your gifts. The castle values him, and his old party does worse without him (TV Tropes). He does not need revenge; being recognized as a hero by the monsters is enough.

**Characters as arguments.** Remme argues for quiet competence and humility. Phoenix argues for friendship over rank: he defended Remme and keeps his secret. The other teammates argue, mistakenly, that only visible results count. Lucy argues that leaders should know talent when they see it. Milla argues for devotion, played as comedy.

**Symbols and motifs.** The horned mask of Lemegeton (a second self that lets Remme be strong openly); the undefeated castle; avatars that make defeat cost only money; floors themed on the old enemies of heroes.

**Traditions it echoes.** The 'expelled from the hero's party' web-novel genre, the reverse-villain story (working for the Demon King), and Western demonology used as fantasy dressing. For faith readers, the rejected stone that becomes the cornerstone is the nearest echo, though the setting's 'demons' will trouble some families.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarianism and pragmatism win. Consumerism is the setting rather than the target. Pagan and occult names are fantasy furniture, not a belief system.

**Shifts across the series.** Ch. 1-13 set up the firing, the recruitment and the castle; ch. 14-22 are the first battle with Remme's old party; ch. 23-31 rebuild dungeons and settle debts; ch. 32-42 are a tag tournament; ch. 43-67 are a new siege, floor by floor (all from chapter titles).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Remme's party thought he was useless. What did they miss, and why?
2. Phoenix stood up for Remme. What does a good friend do when the group turns on someone?
3. Remme doesn't seek revenge. Would you?
4. The story calls its friendly characters 'demons' and names one Lucifer. Does that matter to you or your family? Why?
5. Milla's crush is played for laughs. When does persistence stop being cute?
