**Premise.** Moegi Urushibara is a university student who performs as Urumin in the small underground idol group Merry☆Universe. She cannot really sing or dance and is not good at being sweet to fans, so she has exactly one: Kimiya Okuda, a deadpan student at the same university, who spends his time and money on oshikatsu (supporting a favorite idol) and vows to cheer for her with the strength of eight billion people. The group's other members are the leader Airu (a former delinquent and the oldest), Rei (boyish in looks, a fan of boys' love manga), Mū-chan (a gyaru in style but the smartest member, a graduate student) and Popochi (19, the youngest). Volume 3: a clip of Urumin goes viral, new fans flood her events and she falls apart, and Kimiya steps in. Volume 4: the group's first outing with fans, at the beach. Volume 5: Kimiya works so many part-time shifts to pay for her birthday event that he collapses in the street, and Urumin ends up nursing him alone in his room (Wikipedia; Ichijinsha synopses).

## Life message

**Steady, wholehearted support from one person can matter more than talent or popularity, and the one being supported can learn to accept it and give care back.**

Outlook: Hopeful: a warm comedy in which an awkward idol and her one fan grow closer, her group holds together, and setbacks (going viral, Kimiya's collapse) bring them nearer (Ichijinsha synopses; MyAnimeList reviews).

## Creator and publisher context

- Kishirimo (キシリモ) began the series as a Twitter manga in December 2022. In a January 2023 Real Sound article, Kishirimo says the idea came from wanting to draw a flustered girl and a straight-faced boy who says embarrassing things, that Urumin draws on their own experience as an idol fan and on a hard-working performer who struggled with dance, and that SKET DANCE taught them the balance of comedy and seriousness.
- Kishirimo describes an oshi as someone who brightens your life and says they would rather be close to an idol's everyday life than date her (Real Sound). No other interviews were opened. **Status: Needs more info on the creator.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What is one person's support worth? Urumin has no talent for the job she loves and one fan to show for it. Kimiya has no reason to choose her except that he does.

**The answer the story gives.** Everything, if it is sincere. The story turns the usual idol plot upside down: the idol is not perfect and the fan is not one face in a crowd. Kimiya's unshakable support gives Urumin a reason to keep going, and in time she learns to care for him back (vol. 5).

**Characters as arguments.** Kimiya argues for devotion without conditions, said with a straight face. Urumin argues, by her prickliness, that being loved is hard to accept when you do not think you deserve it. The group members show that a small team can hold together without fame. The flood of new fans in vol. 3 argues that popularity is not the same as support.

**Symbols and motifs.** Eight billion people's worth of cheering from one person. The idol's birthday event, paid for by part-time shifts. Paid photos and handshakes as the only allowed contact, slowly giving way to ordinary friendship.

**Traditions it echoes.** The 'one true fan' romance; Kishirimo cites SKET DANCE for its blend of comedy and heart (Real Sound). The story's view of encouragement fits many traditions' teaching on faithful love, while its setting raises the old question of when admiration becomes idolizing.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Consumerism is the setting and is treated warmly, with one clear warning (Kimiya's collapse). Virtue (loyalty, humility) is what the story rewards. No political or religious lens appears.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-2: the premise and the group. Vol. 3: Urumin goes viral and struggles with sudden attention. Vol. 4: a beach outing with fans. Vol. 5: Kimiya's overwork and Urumin's care for him, the first big step in their relationship.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why does Kimiya keep supporting Urumin when she is not good at singing or dancing?
2. Urumin struggles when she suddenly has many fans. Why might more attention make things harder?
3. Kimiya works so hard to pay for her birthday event that he gets sick. What would a healthy amount of support look like?
4. What is the difference between admiring someone and making them the center of your life?
5. Who has supported you when you were not good at something yet?
