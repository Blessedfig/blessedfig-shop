**Premise.** Kaede Sekime is beautiful, smart and kind to everyone in class, except Riku Takadono, a short, cheerful second-year whom she avoids and will not look at. Takadono, who is used to being liked, assumes she hates him and corners her for an explanation (ch. 2, per Stitch's Media Mix); it turns out she likes him so much she cannot face him without turning bright red. They start dating almost at once (vol. 1, BOOK☆WALKER blurb). The comedy is in Sekime's panic: she cannot walk home with him or talk to him in public and runs off at top speed (Stitch's Media Mix). Volume 2 moves through the sports festival, a training camp, summer vacation and a fireworks festival; in volume 3 the couple tells the class and dates openly, the culture festival begins, and Sekime's childhood friend Kyūhōji makes Takadono jealous before the story ends (BOOK☆WALKER blurbs). Takadono's friends Omiya and Miyakojima and his little sister Sora round out the cast (AniList).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What does it mean to know someone? Everyone in class admires Sekime's calm, kind face; only Takadono sees her turn red. The series asks how love lets one person see another's unguarded self.

**The answer the story gives.** Patience and kindness. Takadono does not push; Sekime slowly grows able to be with him in public, and by the final volume they date openly (BOOK☆WALKER blurbs). The biggest obstacle is themselves, as Stitch's Media Mix puts it, and it is not much of one.

**Characters as arguments.** Takadono argues that gentle confidence and good humor win people over. Sekime argues that shyness is not coldness. Their friends argue that friendship survives a romance. The childhood friend in volume 3 tests trust.

**Symbols and motifs.** The blush, a sign only one person reads. The height difference. School festivals as steps in a relationship.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Japanese school romantic comedy (Komi Can't Communicate, The Dangers in My Heart, per Stitch's Media Mix), minus the bullying and rivalry common to the genre.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism appears gently: the true self behind the public face is shown as precious, not as rebellion. Communitarianism is affirmed in a class that wishes the couple well.

**Shifts across the series.** Volume 1 is the misunderstanding and the start of dating. Volume 2 moves through the school year's events with growing closeness. Volume 3 is the public relationship, the culture festival and the childhood friend, and the ending.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why does Sekime act cold to the person she likes most?
2. How does Takadono show patience? Is patience part of love?
3. Why were Takadono's friends worried, and how did they change?
4. Is there someone who sees a side of you no one else does?
