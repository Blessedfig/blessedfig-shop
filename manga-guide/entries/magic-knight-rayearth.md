**Premise.** On a school trip to Tokyo Tower, three eighth-grade girls from different schools, the tomboyish kendo student Hikaru, the proud fencer Umi and the gentle, bookish Fuu, are pulled into Cephiro, a world where will and belief shape reality and which is held together by the prayers of its Pillar, Princess Emeraude. The child-like but 745-year-old mage Clef tells them they are the legendary Magic Knights, summoned to rescue Emeraude from the high priest Zagato. With the creature Mokona as guide, they gain magic, have weapons forged by the smith Presea from the ore Escudo (v1-2), are helped by the swordsman Ferio, and win over or defeat Zagato's servants Alcyone, Ascot, Caldina and the mind-controlled Lafarga while awakening the three Rune Gods, giant guardian robots (v2-3). They destroy Zagato, and only then learn the truth: Emeraude loved Zagato, could no longer pray only for Cephiro, imprisoned herself, and summoned them to kill her, since no one of Cephiro could. Her grief-maddened side attacks, and the girls, with no other choice, kill her and find themselves back in Tokyo, weeping that it is not fair (fan wiki, Volume 3; Wikipedia).

## Life message

**Friendship and a heart that refuses to give up can carry ordinary girls through great trials, but a heroic quest can hide hard truths: when love and duty collide, doing what must be done can still break your heart.**

## Creator and publisher context

- CLAMP formed in the mid-1980s as a dōjinshi circle and debuted professionally with RG Veda (1989); by 1993 it had its present four members. Ohkawa writes and storyboards; the artists' roles shift by series (Wikipedia, Clamp).
- On-record intent: Nakayoshi editor Hideki Yamaguchi asked for a series; Ohkawa says Sailor Moon's success let them pitch one with robots; she says the story deliberately traces an RPG world but is not a simple princess-rescue tale (Wikipedia). Ohkawa has also said that putting one's life on the line, more than love, makes women grow (Wikipedia, Clamp).
- Illustrator Takeshi Okazaki, a friend, coined "Rayearth" (Wikipedia).

- Publishers: Kodansha (Nakayoshi); in English Tokyopop, Dark Horse and now Kodansha USA (13+).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What does it mean to be a hero when you have not been told the whole story? Three girls are told they are the legendary knights who will rescue a princess, and the story asks what their courage is worth when the quest turns out to be something else.

**The answer the story gives.** Friendship and a strong heart are real and necessary: they carry the girls through every trial. But the adults' deception and Emeraude's impossible position mean that even right action can end in grief. Part one ends without consolation; part two answers it (Wikipedia).

**Characters as arguments.** Hikaru argues for the heart and loyalty. Umi argues for pride turned to protectiveness; she persuades Ascot to stop fighting (v3). Fuu argues for reason and gentleness. Clef argues for wise authority, and is shown to have hidden the truth. Emeraude and Zagato argue for love against duty, and pay with their lives.

**Symbols and motifs.** Tokyo Tower as the door between worlds; the three elements and their shrines; armor that grows as the heart grows (v2-3); the ore Escudo; the Pillar who holds up a world by prayer; the illusions of loved ones at the Spring of Eterna.

**Traditions it echoes.** Japanese RPG quests, the magical-girl transformation, super-robot anime and the knightly romance. Its duty-versus-love dilemma echoes classic tragedy and, for some readers, the Gita's dilemma of duty. Christian readers may see self-giving friendship (John 15:13) set beside a troubling mercy killing.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Virtue ethics and community are celebrated. Duty ethics is honored and questioned at once. The New Age idea that will shapes reality is the world's law but is not argued for our world.

**Shifts across the series.** Volume 1 is setup and first magic; volume 2 forges the weapons and awakens Umi's Rune God; volume 3 awakens the other two, defeats Zagato and reveals the truth (fan wiki).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why did the three girls call themselves sisters? What made them trust each other?
2. Clef did not tell the girls the truth. Was that wrong? What would you have wanted to know?
3. Emeraude could not stop loving Zagato. Was her choice to be killed brave, selfish or something else?
4. Umi stopped Ascot by showing him he was hurting his friends. When does mercy work better than force?
5. Hikaru says it is not fair. Is she right? What does the story want us to feel at the end?
