**Premise.** Three stories about two young women soldiers of Attack on Titan, set during the first part of the main series. **Wall Sina, Goodbye** (vol. 1, ch. 1-5): on the day before the 57th Expedition, when she is secretly to capture Eren Yeager, Military Police recruit Annie Leonhart takes a day off and agrees to find a missing heiress, Carly Stratmann. The trail leads through a bar, a slum, a new illegal drug called Coderoin, a dead body under a bed, two hired thugs who try to kill her, and the truth that Carly and her father make and sell the drug; Annie, who must hide her own secret as a Titan shifter, lets Carly escape and covers for her father. **Lost in the Cruel World** (vol. 2, ch. 6-9): Mikasa Ackerman, lying exhausted in the rain after hearing that Eren has died, dreams a life in which her parents were never killed; in it she meets Eren as a child, and even there she cannot stop him from chasing the world beyond the walls, and he dies. She wakes and chooses to fight. **Lost Girls** (vol. 2, ch. 10): as Annie's Titan falls in Stohess, she remembers a talk with Mikasa during training about a ring with a hidden blade and why each of them became a soldier (fan wiki; Wikipedia).

## Life message

**People bound to cruel duties still long for ordinary lives and loves; what they choose to do with one free day, or one dream, shows who they really are.**

Outlook: Bittersweet — both girls are tied to grim missions and the dream of a normal life is shown only to be lost again, but each story ends with a choice made from love or loyalty rather than despair.
Hope source: F — the hope is in the bonds the girls hold to: Mikasa wakes and fights for Eren, and Annie keeps going so she can return to her father (fan wiki); effort and will (E) are the close second.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What does a person do with a little freedom when her life belongs to a mission? Annie has one day off before she must try to capture a classmate; Mikasa has a dream in which her parents are alive. Both stories ask whether a different life was ever possible.

**The answer the story gives.** Not really, but the choices still matter. Annie could do nothing; she chooses to find a missing girl, and in the end she lets her go free (ch. 5). Mikasa could stay in the dream; even there she cannot change Eren, and she wakes up to fight (ch. 9). The world is cruel, and love and loyalty are what keep the girls moving.

**Characters as arguments.** Annie argues for cool self-reliance: she reasons through every option, trusts no one and keeps a private code. Elliot Stratmann is her mirror, a parent who knows his daughter only at the dinner table and protects his family by crime; Annie's thoughts about her own demanding father run beside his story (ch. 1-2). Mikasa argues for devotion. The Mirror Man is the voice of fate, telling her she can be made a killer and cannot save Eren (ch. 9).

**Symbols and motifs.** Annie's ring with a hidden blade, a gift from her parents and her "baggage" (ch. 4, 10). The red scarf and the caged butterfly Eren gives Mikasa (ch. 9). The walls, which keep the Titans out and the dreamers in. Dinner as the only bond between Carly and her father.

**Traditions it echoes.** Hard-boiled detective fiction (the bar, the corpse, the double-cross) and the "road not taken" dream story. For some Christian readers the dream of a lost family echoes the longing for Eden; for Buddhist readers, the suffering of attachment.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Existentialism is explored rather than preached. Duty ethics is complicated: Annie keeps her mission and also breaks the law to show mercy. Communitarianism wins quietly: the girls survive for the people they love.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1 (ch. 1-5) is Annie's crime story; vol. 2 (ch. 6-9) is Mikasa's dream; ch. 10 is a short coda that ties the two together at the moment one defeats the other.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Annie had one free day before a hard mission. Why do you think she chose to look for a missing girl?
2. Was it right for Annie to let Carly escape and cover for her father? Who was helped and who was hurt?
3. In Mikasa's dream her parents are alive, but she still loses Eren. What do you think the dream is telling her?
4. Both girls say they became soldiers because they had to. Is that true for them? What choices did they still have?
5. What "baggage" do you carry, the way Annie carries her ring?
