**Premise.** College student Tokiwa is invited to a three-on-three mixer (gōkon) by his classmate Suou and brings his friends Asagi and Hagi, expecting three women. Instead they are met by three handsome "men": Suou and her coworkers Kohaku and Fuji, women who work at a cross-dressing bar where they play princely, bossy or lazy male personas. On campus Suou keeps a low profile and is mistaken for a man, the "SSR Prince" (Wikipedia). The mixer goes well, and three slow romances follow: teasing Suou and flustered Tokiwa; Fuji, who secretly draws erotic boys' love manga, and the eager, naive Asagi, who helps her without knowing what it is; and serious Kohaku and nervous Hagi, who at first wonders whether being drawn to Kohaku's male persona means he is gay, then realizes he likes her as herself (Wikipedia episode summaries; ANN; ciatr). Through volume 8 none of the couples has fully confessed; Kohaku learns Hagi likes her at the university festival and Tokiwa asks Suou on a date (ciatr). All main characters are university students and adult coworkers.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** When you meet someone in a role, a costume or a persona, how do you get to know the person underneath?

**The answer the story gives.** Slowly, kindly, and by spending ordinary time together. The men meet "princes" and end up liking the women. Hagi's arc states it most plainly: confused by his attraction to Kohaku's bossy male persona, he realizes he likes her for who she is in both forms (anime eps. 9-12; ANN).

**Characters as arguments.** Suou argues for confidence: she enjoys her persona and uses it to tease Tokiwa. Tokiwa is the ordinary viewer, flustered but decent. Asagi argues for acceptance without fuss; reviewers find him the most likable (ANN, Eisenbeis). Fuji argues for unashamed hobbies, though she hides what her manga contains. Kohaku and Hagi argue for seeing past appearances.

**Symbols and motifs.** Costumes and uniforms (the bar suits, Asagi in a bartender's uniform, ep. 8); the "SSR Prince" nickname from gacha games (a rare card); the wish bell rung together (ep. 12).

**Traditions it echoes.** Japanese romantic comedy of mistaken identity; reviewers compare it with Monthly Girls' Nozaki-kun (ANN, Moore) and contrast it with I Cross-Dressed for the IRL Meetup (ANN, Silverman).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism and a light progressivism shape the premise; critics on both feminist and general sides find the gender theme left on the surface (ANN, Farris; Anime Feminist). Communal friendship carries every romance.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-6 (ch. 1-34, the anime's range) are the mixer, outings, camping and the beach. Volumes 6-8 bring jealousy, the university festival and Tokiwa's first real date invitation (ciatr). Volumes 9-12 (ch. 53 onward) were not covered by sources opened.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why do Tokiwa and his friends feel nervous around the "princes"? What changes?
2. Hagi wonders what his attraction to Kohaku means. What does he decide, and why?
3. Suou kisses Tokiwa while he is asleep. Was that okay? Why or why not?
4. Is it fair that the show treats women in suits as cool but a man in a girls' uniform as a joke?
5. What does your family believe about dressing as the other sex, and why?
