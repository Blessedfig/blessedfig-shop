**Premise.** Having brought the last millennium safely to a close, Jesus and Buddha take a vacation on Earth and share a cheap apartment in Tachikawa, a Tokyo suburb, trying to keep their identities secret (Kodansha blurb; Wikipedia). Buddha is calm and pinches pennies; Jesus is eager, loving toward everyone and prone to impulse buys, and keeps a blog. The comedy comes from their miracles leaking out at the wrong moment (Jesus turns the public-bath water into wine; Buddha glows when moved; animals offer themselves to Buddha as food), from Jesus's crown of thorns blooming when he is happy and his stigmata bleeding under stress, and from the two meeting ordinary Japanese life: shrine festivals, beer and fireworks, video games, bargain shopping, a landlady who does not know who they are, and a yakuza, Ryuji, who takes Jesus's scars and three-days-dead story for a gang boss's past (Wikipedia; Tricycle; TV Tropes). Their worlds visit: disciples (Peter, Andrew, John, James, Thomas, a guilt-ridden Judas; Ananda, Rahula, Sariputra), the four archangels, Mary and Joseph, Buddha's parents, the devas Brahma, Taishakuten and Benzaiten, the tempter Mara, Lucifer as a sulky older brother to Michael, and God, shown as a dove (Wikipedia; TV Tropes).

## Creator and publisher context

- Hikaru Nakamura, born April 21, 1984, in Shizuoka Prefecture; debuted in 2001; best known for Arakawa Under the Bridge and Saint Young Men; also drew Juni Taisen and created Black Night Parade (Wikipedia, Hikaru Nakamura).
- On-record intent: she wanted a comedy with a very powerful hero, first planned Jesus, then added Buddha to make the gags work; their opposite personalities came from watching her sister and brother-in-law; she used only her own knowledge of religion and added modern touches (yakuza, blogging) because they fit, not to criticize (Wikipedia, Production). She is interested in Christian iconography but not a believer, and kept the jokes simple (Global Voices, citing a Jump SQ interview). She and her publishers have stressed it is not meant to proselytize (Tricycle).
- Publisher: Kodansha (Monthly Morning Two); Kodansha USA in English, rated 16+. The Japanese licensor long refused an English release for fear of offending Americans (Wikipedia; ANN, Jason Thompson); the English edition adds notes, including one saying Buddha's beer is non-alcoholic (Tricycle).
- No controversies involving the creator were found.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What would the founders of two great faiths be like as ordinary neighbors? The series asks how holiness looks on a budget, at a bathhouse, at a festival.

**The answer the story gives.** Kind. Jesus loves everyone and everything (including shopping); Buddha is patient and careful; both stay gentle even when people behave badly (critic Kaoru Nagayama, via Wikipedia). The story does not decide between their faiths; it shows them getting along.

**Characters as arguments.** Jesus argues for exuberant love; Buddha for calm and restraint; together they make a good household. Mara and Lucifer argue for spite and pride and are ignored or pitied. Judas stands for guilt that will not let go.

**Symbols and motifs.** The crown of thorns that blooms with joy; the stigmata that bleed with stress; Buddha's glow and his forehead mark; the shared apartment; the T-shirts Buddha prints; the festival mikoshi (Tricycle).

**Traditions it echoes.** Japanese gag manga and the manzai double act; the "odd couple" comedy; Osamu Tezuka's Buddha, which this Buddha loves to read (Wikipedia). Religious-studies teachers use it to discuss religion in modern Japan (Tricycle).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Christian and Buddhist ideas are explored through jokes, not argued; for some readers this reduces both to "be nice" (Moralistic therapeutic deism). Community and good manners are affirmed. Consumerism is gently mocked.

**Shifts across the series.** Episodic throughout. Early volumes center on the two roommates; later volumes bring in more visitors from heaven (disciples, archangels, family, devas, Mara, Lucifer), which ANN's reviewer says adds depth through callbacks. Contents after the English omnibus volume 10 were not checked.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Jesus loves shopping and Buddha loves saving. Who do you take after?
2. Why do you think the two try to hide who they are?
3. Some religious readers find the jokes about Jesus or Buddha disrespectful. Where would you draw the line?
4. What do Jesus and Buddha have in common in this manga? What is different?
5. How do the neighbors treat the two strangers? How do you treat newcomers?
