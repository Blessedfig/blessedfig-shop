**Premise.** Sixteen years after the Seven Deadly Sins ended the Holy War, Percival, a cheerful boy, lives with his grandfather Varghese on God's Finger, a pillar of land above the clouds. On his sixteenth birthday a red-armored knight, Ironside, Varghese's own son, kills Varghese and wounds Percival (ch. 1; Plugged In). Percival learns he is one of four knights prophesied to destroy the kingdom of Camelot and kill its king, Arthur Pendragon, and Camelot's knights are hunting all four. He sets out with Sin, a talking fox who is really Lancelot, the half-fairy son of Ban and Elaine; Donny, a cowardly failed knight trainee; Nasiens, a poison-mixing herbalist; and Anne (Anghalhad), a governor's daughter who can sense lies (Wikipedia). In Liones they meet King Meliodas, his son Tristan and the fourth knight, Gawain, daughter of Escanor and Merlin (ch. 51-62). Arthur, now ruling a rebuilt Camelot, plans a human-only utopia by purging Britannia of non-human races; Wikipedia says this Arthur is really Cath Palug in Arthur's body. His Knights of Chaos attack Liones (ch. 57-81). Percival learns he is a life spirit bound to a dead infant in Ironside's experiment to save his son, and in guilt leaves his body at the end of Part 1 (ch. 128); after two years he returns (Wikipedia; fan wiki). In Part 2, by the fan wiki's account, Percival proposes a three-way relationship with Anne and Nasiens, and their future children appear in a Holy Grail arc (from ch. 232); the latest chapters center on "Chaos Lancelot" fighting the demon Zeldris (ch. 256).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** If you were told you would destroy the world, would you believe it? Percival is a kind boy whom a prophecy names as one of four knights of the apocalypse, and a whole kingdom hunts him for it. The series asks whether destiny is written or chosen, and who gets to decide what kind of world is worth saving.

**The answer the story gives.** Chosen, and chosen together. Percival's power is called Hope, and it grows when his friends believe in him (Wikipedia). The "apocalypse" turns out to threaten Arthur's Camelot, a kingdom built on purging fairies, giants and demons; the heroes defend the very beings Arthur would erase.

**Characters as arguments.** Percival argues for trust and kindness without guile. Anne argues for honesty: her power punishes lies. Donny argues that a coward can become brave; Nasiens that identity can grow out of love. Gawain argues for pride and learns humility. Ironside argues that the greater good excuses anything and kills his own father for it. Arthur argues for a perfect world for some; Ardbeg, who lost a daughter to demons, shows how grief becomes hatred and how it can be released.

**Symbols and motifs.** God's Finger, the isolated pillar where Percival grows up. The four horsemen recast as knights. The fox disguise (Lancelot). The Coffin of Eternal Darkness. Chaos as a power that erases memory and love. The life spirit that outlives the body.

**Traditions it echoes.** Arthurian romance (Percival, Lancelot, Tristan and Isolde, Gawain, the Grail) and Revelation's horsemen, mixed with shōnen adventure. Christian readers will note that the title borrows Revelation but the theology does not; Jewish, Muslim and Bahá'í readers may hear echoes of teaching that every people has worth (from general knowledge; to verify).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarian friendship is celebrated; authoritarian control and might-makes-right are condemned; "greater good" reasoning is shown as the villains' logic. Pagan myth is the world's fabric. Progressive themes (gender, coexistence) are woven in gently. Christianity appears as names and images, not doctrine.

**Shifts across the series.** Part 1, v1-6 (ch. 1-50): Percival's journey and the gathering of his friends. V7-10 (ch. 51-86): Liones, Tristan, Gawain and Camelot's attack. V10-15 (ch. 87-128): Guinevere, the Demon Realm and Percival's origin. Part 2 (ch. 129-262): the return after two years, the Holy Grail arc with future children (ch. 232+), and war in the demon world (ch. 255-262).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Percival is told he will destroy the world. Why does he not believe it?
2. Arthur wants a world without suffering for humans. What is wrong with his plan?
3. Ardbeg hated demons because one killed his daughter. What changed him?
4. Anne can tell when people lie. Would you want that power?
5. Some scenes show teenage characters undressed. Why do you think the artist includes them, and what do you think of it?
