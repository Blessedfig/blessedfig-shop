**Premise.** Hodaka Saotome, a small, gentle first-year whose family runs a tailor's shop, enrolls in the fashion course at Moteshiro Prefectural High School, a former girls' school that has just gone co-ed. He is the only boy among 360 students, and many of the girls, mostly gyaru and delinquents, tease, bully and try to extort him; he considers dropping out (Japanese Wikipedia). Hanako Kuzuryū, a tall, strong, blonde yankee from the commerce course who was trained in karate by her mother Irina, rescues him and becomes his first real friend. Through his sewing, kindness and help to others, Hodaka wins over the class representative Kasumi Takae (who first schemed to extort him), Yuna Ushigome (whom he and others rescue from a paid-dating encounter that turns into an attempted assault), his childhood friend Madoka Tsuruhashi and many more, while Hanako opens up to the whole school. The series is a long harem comedy built on school events: sports days, festivals, Christmas parties, a seaside school trip, a karate camp where Hanako confesses her feelings (v18), a culture-festival fashion show, a student council election, an Okinawa school trip where Yuna and Kasumi each kiss him (v29-30), and a national fashion contest (v28). As of volume 34 he has not chosen anyone (BookWalker blurbs; Wikipedia).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can a small, gentle boy belong in a world where he is outnumbered 359 to one and treated as prey? And can a girl everyone fears be known for who she is?

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, through kindness, patience and useful skill. Hodaka does not fight back; he mends clothes, helps, apologizes when wrong (v21-22) and keeps showing up. Hanako, in turn, finds that being seen gently lets her become friendly with the whole school (Wikipedia).

**Characters as arguments.** Hodaka argues for meekness that is not weakness. Hanako argues that strength is for protecting. Kasumi, who planned to extort him, shows that a schemer can become sincere. Yuna's rescue from paid dating argues that adults who buy teenagers' time are a real danger. The many rivals argue, comically, that attention is a scarce good.

**Symbols and motifs.** Needle and thread: Hodaka mends what others tear. The nickname "Kuzu" (trash) that Hanako rejects. Festivals and contests as tests of belonging.

**Traditions it echoes.** The harem romantic comedy, the yankee manga, and the "gentle giant" heroine; for some readers, the Beatitudes' blessing on the meek.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarian belonging and expressive individualism are both rewarded. Hedonism appears only as fan service. No religious or political lens is in play.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-10: first year, from isolation to friendship (school events, Christmas, Valentine's Day). Volumes 11-20: second year, new first-years, seaside school and summer, Hanako's confession (v18). Volumes 21-34: culture festival, student council election, sports day, fashion contest, Okinawa trip with Yuna's and Kasumi's kisses, and a second Christmas.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Hodaka never fights the girls who bully him. Why does kindness work here, and when might it not?
2. Hanako hates the nickname "Kuzu." What do nicknames do to people?
