**Premise.** In a world at constant war, an orphanage-school takes in children and trains them as magical weapons. Sheena Totsuki, 14, unremarkable at magic and opposed to killing, loses her roommate in battle and hides her grief. That night she meets Mimi Kagari, a cheerful girl covered in blood, who becomes her new classmate and roommate. Mimi is the school's secret weapon: she kills without hesitation and comes back from wounds that destroy her body, because she is already dead, resurrected long ago by her mother through a forbidden spell in which one person gives their life to make another immortal (ch. 1-7). Kissing is a form of healing magic that only works between people with a special bond, and the girls' relationship grows from shock to friendship to love (ch. 2-4, 19, 23). The war draws in more students: their friend Seiran volunteers for the front to keep her girlfriend Ali from being conscripted, and dies (ch. 14); Ali grieves (ch. 15, 24). Sheena discovers a rare healing magic and resolves to share the burden Mimi carries alone; after a quarrel, she rescues Mimi and they agree to support each other (ch. 17-23). Later volumes continue the war story (chapter titles only in sources) (Wikipedia; fan wiki; reviews).

## Life message

**Love and the wish to protect one person can give a reason to live, and to refuse the idea that children are weapons; a war that uses children is a tragedy, not a glory.**

| Socio-cultural | A critique of using children for war: ANN reads it as a story about conditioning to violence and about identity; Anime Atelier notes students known by numbers and an us-versus-them war that is never shown from the other side. |

Hope source: L — love between two people is the way through: Sheena and Mimi give each other a reason to live, and Ali carries on as the person Seiran loved (ch. 24); friends and community (F) are the close second.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What is love worth in a world built to use children up? Sheena wants the war to end and cannot end it. Mimi cannot die and does not fear death. The title asks whether love can last until the end, and whose end.

**The answer the story gives.** Love does not stop the war, but it gives the girls a reason to live and a way to refuse what the war makes of them. Sheena answers violence with healing; Ali answers grief by living as the person Seiran loved (ch. 24). In the volumes covered, the system is not defeated.

**Characters as arguments.** Sheena argues that killing is wrong even when ordered. Mimi argues, at first, that death is nothing to fear and that she should carry the burden alone; love teaches her otherwise (ch. 23). Seiran argues for fighting to protect the one you love, and pays with her life (ch. 14). Fran argues the hard arithmetic: without Mimi, everyone goes to the front (ch. 10). Haru, unsure of her gender, is told to take her time (ch. 19).

**Symbols and motifs.** The healing kiss that works only with a special bond. Flowers: Sheena revives them, Mimi carries a flower crown into battle (ch. 11). Lockets and letters left behind (ch. 14-15). Gratitude Day, when the dead's belongings are given away (ch. 23).

**Traditions it echoes.** Child-soldier stories and the anti-war strand of postwar Japanese manga (see the Pacifism lens), the magical-girl genre turned dark, and the Class S and yuri school story. Mimi's mother, who gives her life to make her child live, echoes sacrificial love; John 15:13 for Christian readers.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Pacifism is the heart; authoritarian use of children is condemned; utilitarian logic is presented fairly but resisted. Progressive readers will find same-sex love and gender questioning treated as normal; traditional readers will weigh that, and the young age of the couple.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-2 (ch. 1-10): meeting, the healing kiss, Mimi's secret. Vols. 3-5 (ch. 11-24): Seiran's death, grief, Sheena's healing magic, the quarrel and reconciliation (the anime ends about here). Vols. 6-9 (ch. 25-42): known only by chapter titles ("Wound," "A Sweet Fever," "Hope," "The Shape of Us," "Mama," "Liar"); content to verify.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Sheena refuses to kill even though the school expects it. Is that brave or naive?
2. Seiran fought so that Ali would not have to. What do you think of her choice?
3. Why did Ali say no to bringing Seiran back?
4. Fran says Mimi's sacrifice keeps everyone else safe. Is it fair to ask one person to suffer for many?
5. The kisses in this story are also healing magic. How does the story connect love and healing?
