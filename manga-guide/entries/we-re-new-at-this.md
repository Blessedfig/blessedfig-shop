**Premise.** Ikuma Saeki has finally married Sumika, his childhood friend and the girl he has loved for years. Both are inexperienced, and the newlyweds find physical intimacy awkward and frightening; the first chapter is a "wedding night do-over" (ch. 1). The comedy follows their attempts, misunderstandings and small fights as they learn to talk to each other, move into a house of their own (ch. 58-60), meet friends and family, and, in the last volumes, prepare for a baby, go through a pregnancy and welcome twins (ch. 132-140). The finale returns to a second "wedding night do-over" (ch. 142) (Wikipedia chapter titles; Kodansha USA blurb; MangaUpdates).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What happens after "happily ever after"? Most romances end at the wedding; this one starts there, with two people who love each other and do not know how to be close.

**The answer the story gives.** Closeness is learned, slowly, by talking honestly, laughing at failure and trying again. The chapter titles move from "Wedding Night Do-Over" (ch. 1) through "Failure" and "I Want to Cry" (ch. 29-30) to "Beyond the First Night" (ch. 32), and on to parenthood.

**Characters as arguments.** Ikuma argues for devotion that has to learn courage; Sumika, outwardly cool, argues for honesty about her own wishes. Friends and parents appear as advisers rather than obstacles (titles; to verify).

**Symbols and motifs.** The wedding night, redone at the start (ch. 1) and again at the end (ch. 142). The new house (ch. 58-60). The twins (ch. 140).

**Traditions it echoes.** The Japanese married-couple comedy and the long tradition of love songs within marriage; for some Christian and Jewish readers, the Song of Songs, which celebrates married love while its teachers urged it be read with care.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Traditionalism and hedonism meet: the story honors marriage and family and treats pleasure inside it as good. Feminist readers may notice that the wife's desire is treated as normal, and also the fan service.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-4 (ch. 1-34) are about getting past the wedding night. Volumes 5-11 (ch. 35-91) cover everyday married life, a new house and friends. Volumes 12-16 (ch. 92-131) turn toward becoming parents. Volumes 17-18 (ch. 132-147) cover the pregnancy, the twins and the finale.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Ikuma and Sumika loved each other for years but still had to learn how to be close. What does that say about love?
2. Why is talking honestly hard, even with someone you trust?
3. The story makes a married couple's private life into a comedy. What do you think should stay private?
4. How do the two make up after a fight?
5. What changes when they learn they will be parents?
