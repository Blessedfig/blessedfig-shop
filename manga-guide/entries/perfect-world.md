**Premise.** Tsugumi Kawana, 26, an interior designer in Tokyo, meets her high school crush Itsuki Ayukawa again at a work gathering. Once a star basketball player, he is now an architect who uses a wheelchair after a spinal cord injury in college (ANN; Wikipedia). She first thinks she could never date a man in a wheelchair, then confronts her own prejudice (Penguin Random House). Volume 1 is frank about his daily life: incontinence, pressure sores, an infection that puts him in hospital, clients who resist accessible design, and former classmates who gossip (ANN; A Case Suitable for Treatment). They begin dating (v2), but Tsugumi's ailing father objects to her taking on a partner who may depend on her, Itsuki's caregiver and former nurse Nagasawa loves him, and Tsugumi's old friend Koreeda loves her (v2; Anime UK News). Stress puts Tsugumi in hospital (v3); Itsuki ends the relationship (v4); in her hometown of Matsumoto she cares for her father and meets Kaede, a wheelchair user, and Keigo, who wants to build her a barrier-free home, which Itsuki designs (v5-6) (Penguin Random House). After an earthquake strands Tsugumi and Itsuki overnight at an evacuation site (v8; MangaUpdates Earthquake), they commit to each other, Koreeda and Nagasawa accept it, and Itsuki goes to win her father's approval (v9). As newlyweds they struggle to conceive (v10), decide to adopt (v11) and adopt a baby boy, Koki (v12) (Penguin Random House; Kodansha).

## Creator and publisher context

- Rie Aruga, from Omachi, Nagano; dreamed of drawing manga from childhood but debuted only in her thirties, while working at a company (Wikipedia). Debut one-shot Tenmon Kansoku (2011) won Kiss's new-artist award; Perfect World is her longest work; Sheltering Eaves (2022-) runs in Kiss (Wikipedia).
- On-record intent (Slate.fr, 2019): Kiss asked her to combine love and disability; she began knowing almost nothing and researched by interviewing a wheelchair-using architect repeatedly, other disabled people he introduced, rehabilitation staff and an association for disabled earthquake survivors; she hopes manga can change attitudes a little and says readers wrote that they felt less awkward around disabled people. In an afterword she says her own long experience as a family caregiver shaped Tsugumi (Anime Feminist).
- Publisher: Kodansha (Kiss, a josei magazine); Kodansha USA in English, rated 16+.
- No controversies involving the creator were found.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can love be wise when it knowingly takes on hardship? Tsugumi's parents, Itsuki himself and two rivals all argue that she would be happier with someone else.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, if it is honest about the hardship. The couple separates twice (v4, v8) because Itsuki fears being a burden, and comes back each time with clearer eyes; marriage and adoption (v10-12) are presented as the fruit of that honesty.

**Characters as arguments.** Tsugumi argues that love chooses the whole person. Itsuki argues, at first, that loving someone means sparing them; he learns that letting her in is also love. The father argues from protective fear and is given real reasons. Nagasawa argues that the best caregiver is the best partner; Koreeda that an easy life is the kind one. Kaede and Keigo show a couple who have already made the choice.

**Symbols and motifs.** Architecture: Itsuki designs spaces where disabled people can live fully (v6), and the "perfect world" of the title is a world built to fit everyone. Basketball, then wheelchair basketball (v1). Steps and ramps.

**Traditions it echoes.** Josei drama about adult relationships; disability narratives (Anime Feminist notes both the "super cripple" and "tragedy" tropes it sometimes leans on); in Japan, earlier disability love stories on TV.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Virtue ethics and communitarianism are affirmed; progressivism appears as attention to access and attitudes; expressive individualism is tempered by the choice to win the parents over rather than break with them.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-3: reunion, dating and opposition; vols. 4-6: breakup, Matsumoto, Kaede and Keigo; vols. 7-9: rivals, the earthquake and reunion; vols. 10-12: marriage, infertility and adoption (Penguin Random House synopses).

**Discussion questions.**
1. At first Tsugumi thinks she could not date a man in a wheelchair. What changes her mind?
2. Why does Itsuki keep pushing Tsugumi away? Is he being kind or unfair?
3. Tsugumi's father opposes the relationship. What is he afraid of, and is he right to be?
4. What makes a building, or a town, a "perfect world" for everyone?
5. How do Tsugumi and Itsuki prepare to tell Koki he was adopted, and why does it matter?
