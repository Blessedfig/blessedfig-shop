**Premise.** In an alternate Edo Japan, the "redface pox" kills about three in four men. Women take over farms, trades and, from the third shogun on, the shogunate itself; men become scarce breeding stock, and the shogun's Inner Chambers fill with hundreds of men. The story opens in 1716 with Mizuno, a poor samurai who joins the Inner Chambers, and Shogun Yoshimune, who cuts waste and starts reading the secret Chronicle of the Dying Day (vol. 1). The chronicle tells how Lady Kasuga hid the male Iemitsu's death, forced his illegitimate daughter Chie to take his place, and pressed a monk, Arikoto, into the Inner Chambers to father an heir (vol. 2-4). Later arcs follow each female shogun: Tsunayoshi's troubled reign and murder (vol. 5-7), the Ejima affair (vol. 8), Yoshimune's and Tanuma Okitsugu's fight against the pox with Dutch learning and variolation (vol. 9-11), the poisoner Harusada and her son Ienari, under whom a safe vaccine restores the male population (vol. 11-13), Iesada, who was sexually abused by her father (vol. 13-15), and the end of the shogunate and the bloodless surrender of Edo, after which the new government erases the women's history (vol. 16-19) (Wikipedia; Japanese Wikipedia; reviews).

## Life message

**Power, custom and history are made by people and can trap anyone born into the "wrong" role; love, loyalty, knowledge and courage let people bear what they cannot change, and what women achieved deserves to be remembered.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What if the roles we call natural are only the result of circumstance? Remove most of the men, and women farm, trade and rule; men become precious, idle and owned. Yoshinaga asks what stays the same: the hunger for power, the cruelty of systems, and love.

**The answer the story gives.** Systems crush people of either sex. Chie is forced into a dead father's role; Arikoto is forced out of his vows; men are rented by the night. Good rulers are those who see people rather than roles (Yoshimune sparing Mizuno, Iemitsu admitting women heirs). In the end the new government erases the women's history, but a man tells the young Tsuda Umeko that women once ran the country.

**Characters as arguments.** Lady Kasuga is the state that sacrifices persons to the line. Arikoto is compassion inside an unjust system. Chie shows trauma becoming leadership. Tsunayoshi shows a woman reduced to a womb by her father's ambition. Harusada is power as boredom and cruelty. Aonuma, Gennai and Kuroki are science against superstition and politics.

**Symbols and motifs.** The Chronicle of the Dying Day, the secret record that makes the story possible; names (women ruling under men's names); the cat Arikoto loves (vol. 2); the bear and the bear-pox vaccine; the dandelion of the NHK drama's theme song.

**Traditions it echoes.** Feminist science fiction (Joanna Russ, per Strange Horizons), the Japanese historical drama (jidaigeki), and the court tragedy. Religious readers may hear Ecclesiastes on the vanity of power, and Buddhist teaching on suffering and impermanence.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Feminism is affirmed but not simple: women in power can be as cruel as men. Authoritarianism is critiqued through the costs it imposes. Secular humanism wins the plague war. Confucian duty and bushido honor are shown both as dignity and as a trap.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1 is a lighter court drama (Mizuno and Yoshimune); vol. 2-4 the brutal founding (Iemitsu and Arikoto); vol. 5-8 the decadence of Tsunayoshi and the Ejima affair; vol. 9-13 the science arc (Strange Horizons: from melodrama to immunology and politics); vol. 13-19 the return of male rule, Perry and the fall of the shogunate.

**Discussion questions.**
1. If most men disappeared, which roles in your town would change and which would not?
2. Lady Kasuga did terrible things to keep the peace. Was she right about anything?
3. Arikoto broke his vows to save lives. What would you have done?
4. Why did the new government want people to believe all shoguns were men?
5. Which ruler in the series governed best, and why?
