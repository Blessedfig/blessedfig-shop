**Premise.** A commoner sorcerer with no noble blood or talent, scorned at a nobles' academy, dies in a duel he walked into because he wanted to see a noble's magic up close (Japanese Wikipedia). He wakes as Lloyd, the ten-year-old seventh prince of Saloum, with his memories, vast mana and no interest in the throne, women or romance: only magic. He hides his strength so nobles will leave him alone to study (Wikipedia). He frees the demon Grimoire from a sealed book and makes him a goat-shaped familiar, studies qi with the adventurer Tao, and is guarded by his devoted maid and sword teacher Sylpha. Arcs follow: a demon experimenting on beasts (anime ep. 4-5), the Assassins' Guild of "Blighted" outcasts whose uncontrollable powers Lloyd fixes (ep. 7-8), the demon Guisarme, who steals the guild leader Jade's body and possesses a whole territory (ep. 9-12), and, in season 2 (ch. 43-88), a corrupt Holy Church where a secret lab makes chimeras and a pope who lost his faith stages chaos to see whether the gods will punish him (Japanese Wikipedia). Later manga arcs add an alchemy festival, golems and a demon army invasion.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What would you do with a second life and every advantage your first one lacked? Lloyd's past self died because he cared more about seeing magic than about living. Reborn a prince, he chooses the same thing again, but now he has the power to keep it.

**The answer the story gives.** Follow your passion freely, and good things follow: friends, safety, the rescue of outcasts. The joy of discovery drives every arc, and Lloyd's detachment from rank, wealth and romance is treated as a kind of purity of purpose.

**Characters as arguments.** Lloyd argues for curiosity above all, even above his own body (Japanese Wikipedia). Grimoire, a once-terrible demon reduced to a goat, argues that power can be tamed by a greater power. The Blighted argue that a gift feared as a curse can be healed and used. Pope Guitane, whose wife and son were murdered, argues that a just God would punish evil, and tests it; he is stopped and taken in. Anastasia, the murderer's daughter who believes in no god but reveres Guitane, argues that guilt can become devotion.

**Symbols and motifs.** The sealed book and the freed demon; mana as a visible black mass; the Blighted's curses turned into tools; the angel as a fat yellow chick; golems and colored pages in later arcs (AniList review).

**Traditions it echoes.** The isekai power fantasy; the Western grimoire and Faustian bargain, played for comedy; the fantasy "corrupt church" common in Japanese light novels. Its question about a God who does not punish evil is the old problem of theodicy, answered not by doctrine but by mercy.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism is celebrated. Might makes right is the engine but softened by kindness. The Christian-like church is criticized as an institution while simple faith (Escher) is honored. The sexual comedy around a child is the series' most criticized element (ANN; database tags).

**Shifts across the series.** Ch. 1-42 (vols. 1-5; anime season 1): Grimoire, Tao, Pazuzu, the Assassins' Guild and the Lordost/Guisarme arc. Ch. 43-88 (vols. 5-10; season 2): the Holy Church arc. About ch. 85-100: a side story and festival arcs; from about ch. 100: golems and a tournament; from about ch. 126: full color (AniList review). Later: a demon army invasion and a multiverse-traveling mimic (Japanese Wikipedia).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Lloyd died for his love of magic once. What is the difference between passion and recklessness?
2. Why do you think Lloyd wants nothing to do with being king?
3. The Blighted were feared for powers they could not control. Who is treated like that in real life?
4. Guitane lost his faith after a terrible loss. How does the story treat him afterward?
5. Some jokes in this series are about adults being attracted to a child. Why might parents object to that kind of joke?
