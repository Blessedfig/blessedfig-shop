**Premise.** Decades after an unexplained collapse, corporate city-states rule from behind walls while most people starve in the slums outside. Hunters scavenge "Old World" ruins for advanced relics, guarded by mutant bioweapons and robots. Akira, a homeless slum orphan of about 15 (fan wiki; age unknown in story), goes into the ruins as a last hope and is nearly killed, until he meets Alpha, an artificial intelligence only he can see through augmented reality. She offers to train and guide him if he will one day explore a dangerous ruin for her. With her help Akira buys gear, survives monster stampedes, wipes out a slum gang that comes for his money (manga v2), and takes the gang leader's favorite girl, Sheryl, under his protection; she builds a new gang of slum children around his name (v3). He works with the veteran hunters Elena and Sara, clashes with the large hunter group Drankam and its golden boy Katsuya, and is drawn into corporate politics (Wikipedia; J-Novel Club volume blurbs; fan wiki).

## Life message

**In a world that throws the poor away, you survive by your own strength, wits and the help you can trust, and the debts you repay and promises you keep are what stop you from becoming just another killer.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What does a person owe anyone in a world that owes him nothing? Akira starts with nothing but his life, and the ruins offer either riches or death.

**The answer the story gives.** Survive by your own strength, trust only what is proven, and keep strict accounts: repay help, repay harm. The light-novel reviewers note that Akira's apathy is softened by guilt and by people who are decent to him (Operation Rainfall), so the answer is not pure self-interest.

**Characters as arguments.** Alpha argues for calculation: she cares for Akira as an asset and hides her aims (fan wiki). Katsuya argues for heroic help to everyone and causes deaths by ignoring risk (fan wiki). Elena and Sara show that competence and decency can coexist. Sheryl shows survival by attachment turning into real leadership.

**Symbols and motifs.** The wall between city and slums; the Old World ruins and their relics; Alpha's changing clothes, which Akira reads as the strange taste of a lost civilization (fan wiki); the number 14 that follows Akira (fan wiki).

**Traditions it echoes.** Post-apocalyptic scavenger fiction, cyberpunk corporate dystopia, and the Japanese web-novel ascent story with a hidden-purpose guide.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Strength rules and pragmatism describe the world; the story neither praises nor fully condemns them. Transhumanism is presented with wonder and suspicion. The critique of corporations is in the setting rather than argued.

**Shifts across the series.** Manga vols. 1-2 (ch. 1-9): first hunts, Alpha, the slum gang. Vols. 3-4 (ch. 10-19): monster stampede, Sheryl's gang, powered suit, city patrol. Vols. 5-9 (ch. 20-44): Katsuya and Drankam, larger corporate jobs, Sheryl's rise (fan wiki volume pages). Later chapters (to ch. 88) not documented in sources opened.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Akira kills people who came to rob him. When is that self-defense and when is it something else?
2. Alpha helps Akira but hides why. Should he trust her? How would you decide?
3. Why does the AI look the way she does, and who is that for?
4. What would it take to change the slums, rather than escape them?
