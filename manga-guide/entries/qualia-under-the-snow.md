**Premise.** Akio Kobayashi, a university student in a snowy town who loves plants and avoids people, lives in the same dorm as Umi Ohashi, a younger, outgoing student whose relationships with men never go beyond one-night stands. Umi attaches himself to Akio; Akio slowly opens up. Early on Umi remarks that Akio is the first person who stuck around without being slept with (Kuma blurb). Both carry old wounds: Akio's parents left him (his father, whose cigarette brand he still smokes, is a central absence), and Umi's first love, a straight man, slept with him and then rejected him and married a woman. The two find two stray cats, share the dorm through the seasons, and children who look like them appear in the background as images of their younger selves. In the last chapter Akio invites Umi into his bed, kisses him and tells him the name of a flower his father once said to share with someone dear; the story stops short of a stated declaration (MyAnimeList reviews by KursedArcana, lolcatinti and Slvmber).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can one person ever know what another feels? The title borrows "qualia," the philosopher's word for the private feel of experience. Akio cannot begin to understand Umi's pain (Kuma blurb); Umi assumes love has to be paid for. The book asks how two people with opposite defenses learn to reach each other.

**The answer the story gives.** Slowly, through presence. Akio stays without asking anything, and that is what Umi had never had (Kuma blurb). Akio, in turn, thaws with the seasons (KursedArcana). The answer is shown, not spoken: a shared bed, a kiss, a flower's name.

**Characters as arguments.** Umi argues that closeness without commitment is safer, and the story shows it is lonelier. Akio argues that solitude is safer, and the story shows it is colder. The first love argues for convention over honesty and leaves damage. The mentor argues for patience, like a gardener's.

**Symbols and motifs.** Snow and the change of seasons; plants that need patient tending; two stray cats, one dark and one light, like the two men; children who look like their younger selves; the father's cigarettes; a flower whose name means holding on (KursedArcana; lolcatinti).

**Traditions it echoes.** The iyashikei (healing) slice of life, the BL romance between opposites, and the old idea that love is shown by staying. For religious readers it echoes the restless heart that finds rest only in love; its view of sexuality follows contemporary affirming readings.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Hedonism is critiqued gently: Umi's hookups are a wound. Expressive individualism is explored as learning to want openly. Progressivism appears as unremarked acceptance of a same-sex couple. Nature is a quiet healer.

**Shifts across the series.** Likely ch. 1-2: the dorm, Umi's one-night stands and his attachment to Akio. Likely ch. 3-5: the cats, the pasts of both men, Umi's first love. Likely ch. 6-7: the child doubles, the bed scene, the kiss and the flower (KursedArcana). Chapter placements are estimates.
