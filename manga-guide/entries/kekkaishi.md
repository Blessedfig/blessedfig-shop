**Premise.** Four hundred years ago, the legend says, a lord whose power drew ayakashi (supernatural creatures) lived at Karasumori, and the demon exterminator Tokimori Hazama founded a clan of barrier-users to guard the land. Today the land lies under a school, and two heirs of rival branches of that clan guard it every night: Yoshimori Sumimura, a second-year junior-high student who loves baking sweets, and his neighbor Tokine Yukimura, 16 (Wikipedia; VIZ). They trap and destroy ayakashi with kekkai, magical barriers, while their grandparents feud over which family is the true successor. Yoshimori's goal is to seal Karasumori's power forever so that no one, especially Tokine, gets hurt again (VIZ vol. 5). Help and trouble come from Yoshimori's older brother Masamori and the Shadow Organization (Urakai), a secret society of ability-users. The half-ayakashi boy Gen Shishio joins them and is killed by Kaguro of the ayakashi group Kokuboro (vol. 10); Yoshimori goes after Kaguro (vols. 11-13). Later arcs bring corrupt council members, attacks on sacred sites and their guardian deities, and Tokine's arrest for killing a deity to save Karasumori (vols. 14-29). The truth comes out: the power of Karasumori is Chushinmaru, Tokimori's son, whom Tokimori tried to make powerful with forbidden arts and then sealed beneath the land, inventing the legend (vols. 30-31; Wikipedia). With the help of Yoshimori's long-absent mother Sumiko and of Tokimori himself, they move Chushinmaru to a new site taken back from the Urakai's founder, and Sumiko seals the site with herself inside (ch. 344; fan wiki). The Urakai is reformed, the families' duty ends and their rivalry is laid to rest (Wikipedia).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What does it mean to guard something? Yoshimori and Tokine inherit a nightly duty to protect a cursed piece of land. Yoshimori, who blames himself for an old injury to Tokine (from general knowledge; to verify), wants not just to guard Karasumori but to end its danger for good (VIZ vol. 5).

**The answer the story gives.** Guarding is not enough: the curse rests on a lie, and only the truth and a sacrifice can end it. The founder hid his son under the land and invented a legend; the cure is to face what was done and give the boy a better place (vols. 30-35). Strength, the series says, comes from a clear mind and from the wish to protect, not from rage (vols. 24-29).

**Characters as arguments.** Yoshimori argues for protecting people over winning titles. Tokine argues for skill and caution, and pays for breaking the rule against harming a deity (vols. 21-22). Gen is the outsider who finds a place and dies for it. Masamori shows power used inside a corrupt system to change it. Kaguro is strength without care. Tokimori is the founder whose pride caused 400 years of harm and who helps put it right. Sumiko, the mother everyone calls absent, turns out to have spent ten years preparing the cure (fan wiki).

**Symbols and motifs.** The kekkai (barrier) as both protection and imprisonment; the castle beneath the school; the mark (houin) on Yoshimori's hand that fades when the land is sealed (fan wiki); sweets and cakes, Yoshimori's gentle hobby; the 'No Thought' box.

**Traditions it echoes.** Japanese yokai and exorcist tales; the onmyodo-style shikigami (from general knowledge; to verify); the shonen training story. Its 'empty mind' discipline echoes Zen teaching; its sins-of-the-founder plot echoes the biblical idea that a father's wrong passes to later generations until it is repaired.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Tradition and duty are honored and then examined: the duty ends when the truth is known. Folk religion is the setting and is treated with respect. Power-seekers fail. A Buddhist-like calm is the key to strength.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-6 (ch. 1-55): school-night monster fights and the families. Vols. 7-13 (ch. 56-125): Gen, Kokuboro and Kaguro. Vols. 14-29 (ch. 126-285): the Shadow Organization's politics, attacks on sacred sites, Tokine's arrest, training to empty the mind. Vols. 30-35 (ch. 286-345): the truth of Karasumori, Tokimori, Sumiko and the final sealing.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Yoshimori wants to end the danger forever instead of guarding it every night. Is that a better goal? Why?
2. Gen dies protecting his friends. How do the others carry his memory?
3. Tokine breaks a serious rule to save the people she loves. Was she right?
4. The founder of the clan told a lie that lasted 400 years. Why do people keep family secrets, and what do they cost?
5. Yoshimori must learn to empty his mind to get stronger. What helps you stay calm under pressure?
