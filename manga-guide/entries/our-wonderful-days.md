**Premise.** On the first day of high school in a sleepy country town, Koharu Hanamura is delighted to find her friends Nanaya Asuka and Minori Kazami in her class, and stunned by a beautiful transfer student from Tokyo who turns out to be Mafuyu Shirotsuki, her childhood best friend, who had moved away years before (Seven Seas vol. 1; YuriMother). The four do ordinary things: checking out clubs, eating lunch, getting caught in the rain, ice cream, convenience stores, homework and chores (Okazu vol. 1; MangaUpdates). Nanaya and Minori are classmates, best friends and roommates who live together outside town (Seven Seas vol. 2); volume 2 tells how they came together, and Okazu likens their bond to a Boston marriage, two women who form a household without being relatives or (yet) lovers. Mafuyu's feelings for Koharu go beyond friendship, and Koharu may feel the same (Okazu vol. 1); Koharu sleeps over at Mafuyu's house (vol. 2). Volume 3 is summer vacation, as Mafuyu's crush grows and she wonders whether to confess (Seven Seas vol. 3). The series closes after three volumes with what one reviewer calls a barely shown kiss between two of the girls and an abrupt ending (MyAnimeList user review; which couple, and whether a confession is made, is not confirmed).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can a life with no big events be a good one? Our Wonderful Days answers by refusing to add any: Okazu calls it a tale of lives being lived, and YuriMother says nothing happens and there are no stakes.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes. The good life is made of convenience-store runs, rain, ice cream, homework and chores done with people you love. Feelings deepen inside that routine rather than breaking it: Mafuyu's crush on Koharu grows over a summer (vol. 3), and Nanaya and Minori's shared home is a love story of its own kind (vol. 2).

**Characters as arguments.** Koharu argues for open, cheerful friendship. Mafuyu, the city girl who comes home, argues that roots matter and that change is survivable (MyAnimeList review). Nanaya and Minori argue for the family you build: two friends who keep house together. YuriMother notes Mafuyu's sharp tongue, a reminder that teasing can wound even among friends.

**Symbols and motifs.** The countryside and its seasons; the convenience store as the town's small hub; trains and waiting (Koharu and Mafuyu waiting for a train, vol. 1); the shared house outside town.

**Traditions it echoes.** The iyashikei genre (Non Non Biyori is the MyAnimeList reviewer's comparison), and the long literary praise of quiet country life; the title's "tsurezure" recalls Essays in Idleness (Tsurezuregusa), a classic of reflective Japanese writing (from general knowledge; to verify).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarianism is affirmed: friends and home are the source of happiness. Expressive individualism is present but soft: feelings are noticed, not proclaimed. Religious lenses are absent; most families will find little to object to beyond the same-sex crushes.

**Shifts across the series.** Volume 1: reunion and the group forming. Volume 2: Nanaya and Minori's past and present. Volume 3: summer vacation and Mafuyu's growing feelings, ending abruptly (MyAnimeList review; MangaUpdates).

**Discussion questions.**
1. What small, ordinary things made your last week good?
2. Mafuyu moves back from Tokyo to the country. What would be hard about that, and what might be better?
3. Nanaya and Minori live together and share the chores. What makes a home feel like a home?
4. Mafuyu sometimes teases her friends sharply. When does teasing stop being friendly?
5. The story never has a big crisis. Do stories need conflict to be worth reading?
