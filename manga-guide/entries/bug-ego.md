**Premise.** Makoto Hitsujiya, a friendly transfer student who was bullied in middle school, offers social tips to his lonely, tiny classmate Takehiro Kokudo. Kokudo answers with real "hacks" from a mysterious notebook: precise, absurd sequences of actions that glitch reality, like pressing a vending machine's buttons in a set order so a bird drops on a chosen spot (Otaku USA; scrmbl; Wikipedia). The hacks have rules, and mistakes are punished. In ch. 1 a time-travel hack requires a naked dance on a soccer field during a solar eclipse; Kokudo uses it to ace a test, but Hitsujiya botches it, is tackled by a sports team and lives out 44 isolated years, ending homeless, before he meets an older Kokudo, succeeds and loops back to the start (The Outerhaven; scrmbl). A lucid-dream hack draws both boys into a shared nightmare where they are attacked (ch. 2), and a museum of curiosities traps one of them inside a cursed wooden carving while a double takes his place (ch. 3-4; sources differ on which boy) (scrmbl; The Outerhaven; Otaku USA). The two form a walking club, "Hacon," to roam the city looking for hacks, and stand up for the outcast Ko Moesawa against delinquents. Hashira Yuki, an overworked student driving herself toward a top university, joins after investigating the hacks herself, and the trio discovers other hack users, including dangerous ones (VIZ vol. 2; The Outerhaven vol. 2; scrmbl). A MyAnimeList reviewer at ch. 15 lists a cult of bug users, beings from other dimensions, parallel worlds and loss of self among later threats.

## Life message

**Shortcuts that bend the rules of reality come with a price, and the real prize is the slow work of becoming someone's friend.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What happens when you find a shortcut through the rules of reality, and can a friendship that starts with something useful become real?

**The answer the story gives.** Shortcuts always cost more than they seem: Hitsujiya loses decades to one mistake (ch. 1). The friendship survives because both boys keep coming back for each other (The Outerhaven; Otaku USA).

**Characters as arguments.** Kokudo argues for secret knowledge and caution, but his loneliness makes him share. Hitsujiya argues for kindness and trying things, sometimes recklessly. Yuki argues for reason and effort, pushed to the point of strain. The bullies argue for the school's pecking order and lose.

**Symbols and motifs.** The notebook (forbidden knowledge with rules). The eclipse and the naked dance (exposure as the price of power). The wooden carving and the double (losing your place in your own life). The sticker that changes in the notebook (hints of other users).

**Traditions it echoes.** Urban-legend horror, The Twilight Zone and Black Mirror-style trap stories (Otaku USA), Alice in Wonderland (The Beat), and ONE's own pairing of a bland-seeming hero with absurd power.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Absurdism and existential questions are explored, not resolved. Pragmatic testing is affirmed. Ritual-like magic is explored as eerie science, never as worship.

**Shifts across the series.** Early chapters are self-contained hack episodes (ch. 1-4); from vol. 2 the club forms and Yuki joins; later arcs add other hackers, a cult and parallel worlds (MyAnimeList review), which some reviewers feel moves toward standard supernatural action (scrmbl).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Would you try a hack if there was a one percent chance it was real? Why or why not?
2. Hitsujiya used a hack to try to pass a test. What does the story say about cheating?
3. Kokudo breaks the notebook's rule against sharing hacks because he wants a friend. Was that right?
4. Yuki pushes herself very hard. What does the story show about pressure at school?
5. What makes a friendship real rather than useful?
