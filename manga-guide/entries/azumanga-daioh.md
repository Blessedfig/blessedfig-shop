**Premise.** Three years at an ordinary Tokyo-area high school, told in four-panel gags. Ten-year-old prodigy Chiyo Mihama skips five grades into the first year of high school and is adopted by a group of classmates: Tomo, loud and reckless; Yomi, her long-suffering friend; Ayumu "Osaka" Kasuga, a dreamy transfer student; and Sakaki, a tall, shy girl who longs to pet cats that bite her. Kagura, a competitive swimmer, joins the class in the second year. Their homeroom teacher Yukari is lazy, childish and a terrible driver; her old friend Nyamo, the gym teacher, is the responsible one; Kimura, the classical-Japanese teacher, is a running joke for his open interest in teenage girls. The year turns through exams, sports and culture festivals, New Year's dreams, summer trips to Chiyo's beach house (v1 ch. 6; v2 ch. 21), a school trip to Okinawa where Sakaki meets a wild kitten (v4 ch. 33), the kitten's return to her (v4 ch. 37), entrance exams and graduation (v4 ch. 41) (Wikipedia; fan wiki).

## Life message

**Ordinary school days with good friends, silly and small as they are, are worth treasuring; growing up means carrying those friendships with you when the days end.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What is worth remembering about being young? Azumanga Daioh has almost no plot: lessons, lunches, a beach house, a festival, exams. It asks whether that is enough, and answers by making the reader miss it when it ends.

**The answer the story gives.** Ordinary days with friends are the good life in miniature. No one wins anything important; the most dramatic events are Sakaki finally getting a cat (v4 ch. 37) and the class graduating (v4 ch. 41). Reviewers' word for the effect is nostalgia (THEM; ANN).

**Characters as arguments.** Chiyo argues that goodness and diligence can be small and young; her friends argue that the strong should shelter the small. Tomo is impulse without thought, tolerated because she is loyal. Yomi is reason that keeps losing to appetite. Osaka is a different way of seeing that turns out to be its own kind of intelligence. Sakaki shows that people are not what they look like. Yukari is an adult who never grew up; Nyamo is what a grown-up should be, and even she slips (v2 ch. 21). Kimura is the uncomfortable exception: an adult whose interest in the girls the story treats as a harmless joke. Parents may not share that judgment.

**Symbols and motifs.** The month titles of the chapters, turning the year. Sakaki's bitten hand and the kitten who chooses her. The Yukari-mobile. Chiyo's pigtails. The shrine charm Yomi wants filled with Chiyo's brains. The final walk out of the school gate.

**Traditions it echoes.** The four-panel newspaper strip, the Japanese "school days" story and the moe comedy that it helped define; Thompson calls Azuma a quiet master of the form (via Wikipedia). Readers have compared it with Maria-sama ga Miteru for its world of girls' friendships (Marc Hairston, via Wikipedia).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarianism wins quietly: the class and the friend group are where life happens. Absurdism is play, not despair. Expressive individualism is gentle: quirks are accepted, not celebrated as rebellion. No religious or political view is argued.

**Shifts across the series.** Volume 1 (first year): introductions, Chiyo finding her place, the first beach trip. Volume 2 (second year): Kagura joins; the second summer house trip with the teachers' drinking (ch. 21). Volume 3 (third year begins): Kaorin moves to Kimura's class (ch. 30). Volume 4: the Okinawa trip (ch. 33), Maya the kitten (ch. 37), exams and graduation (ch. 41). The 2009 bonus chapters add a short epilogue.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Chiyo is five years younger than her classmates. How do they make room for her, and when do they take advantage of her?
2. Which friend are you most like, and which one would you want beside you at exam time?
3. Kimura is meant to be a joke. Is it funny? What should a school do about a teacher who acts like him?
4. Yukari and Nyamo are both teachers. What makes one of them a better example?
5. The story ends at graduation. What do you think the friends will keep from those three years?
