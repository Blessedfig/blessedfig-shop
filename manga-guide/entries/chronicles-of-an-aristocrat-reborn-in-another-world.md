**Premise.** Shiinya Kazuya, a Japanese high-schooler, dies stopping a knife attacker who was going after two girls, and wakes as Cain von Silford, the three-year-old third son of a frontier lord in the Kingdom of Esfort. The world's seven gods (of creation, death and rebirth, magic, war, technology, commerce and earth) bless him so heavily at his fifth-birthday blessing that his abilities soar, and they quietly name him their Apostle against a sealed evil god, Aaron (Wikipedia; Seven Seas vol. 1). At ten, Cain saves Princess Telestia and Lady Silk from orcs and is engaged to both (vol. 2); he is made a baron with a mansion (vol. 3), trains for years in a pocket world under Esfort's founding king, Yuuya, who became a god (vol. 5), accidentally summons a demon lord as his servant (vol. 6), destroys a fragment of Aaron and becomes viscount of the troubled town of Drientl (vol. 7). Later volumes follow his reforms, freeing innocents sold by a slave merchant (vol. 8), guarding the Holy Woman Hinata against assassins sent by a corrupt Pope (vols. 9-10), hunting a marquis behind child kidnappings (vols. 11-13) and killing him after he becomes the evil god's disciple (vol. 14), then brokering peace with the Demon Empire (vols. 14-15). He gathers five fiancées along the way (Wikipedia).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What should someone do with power no one else has? Cain is given abilities that make him a demigod by his teens (vol. 6), and must decide whether to hide, rule or serve.

**The answer the story gives.** Use it to protect, quietly and under good authority. Cain hides his strength, accepts the king's oversight and the gods' tasks, rescues captives and children, and in the latest volumes seeks peace rather than conquest (vols. 14-15). The story never asks whether he should have this power; it asks only that he use it well.

**Characters as arguments.** Cain argues for benevolent strength. The king argues for order that can restrain even a demigod (his lectures). Corgino argues for greed and is destroyed. Aaron, a god of entertainment who made people suffer for fun, is the dark mirror of the playful gods. Seth, the demon lord who submits to a stronger master, shows strength recognizing strength.

**Symbols and motifs.** The status screen with ever-rising numbers. The contract witnessed by the god of commerce. Reversi, a game from Earth, as the gods' delight. Drientl, a run-down town remade.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Narō isekai genre, the fairy-tale hero who wins princesses, and the European knightly romance. Readers from many faiths will hear the saying that much is required of those given much (Luke 12:48).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Pagan/occult and traditional hierarchy form the world and are affirmed. Might makes right is softened by benevolence. Peacemaking appears late as a real value. Critiques of monarchy, slavery and polygamy are absent.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-4: childhood, blessings, engagements and the capital. Volumes 5-7: training with the founding king, the demon lord and the first fight with Aaron (the anime ends about here). Volumes 8-13: governing Drientl, slaves freed, the Holy Woman and the Corgino investigation. Volumes 14-15: Corgino's defeat and diplomacy with the Demon Empire.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Cain hides his power. Is that modesty, or is he avoiding responsibility?
2. The king arranged Cain's engagements when he was ten. How is that different from how families choose partners today?
3. Cain freed captives but slavery still exists in his world. What would you change if you were lord of Drientl?
4. Why do you think the gods chose Cain?
5. Is making peace with an enemy braver than defeating them?
