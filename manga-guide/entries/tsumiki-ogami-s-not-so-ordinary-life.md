**Premise.** In a Japan where humans live alongside "mythfolk" (genjin: werewolves, vampires, ogres, a spider, a succubus and more), Yutaka Shinso, a first-year who spent junior high friendless and always reading the room, changes to Keiki High School far from home to start over. He meets Tsumiki Ogami, a cheerful, strong and popular werewolf who jumps out of windows, buries sandwiches for later and decides to help him come out of his shell (v1). Short episodes follow a school year and more: sports festivals, a summer festival and fireworks (v3), a cosplay cafe and Mokuri, a cold spider girl famous online for silk art (v4), the student council and Ako Yumenaka, a succubus who likes boys falling for her (v5), Halloween and a Christmas date (v6), Valentine's Day (v7), White Day and a trip to Chinatown that looks like a date (v8), then a second year with a new first-year, Kojo, and a summer at the beach (ch. 71-103) (Wikipedia; VIZ synopses; Beneath the Tangles).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** How do you stop living for what others think? Yutaka has always tuned himself to his surroundings and has forgotten his own feelings (MangaUpdates blurb). The series asks what it takes to open up.

**The answer the story gives.** Someone has to reach out first, and then you have to reach out too. Tsumiki takes Yutaka into her world (v1); he in turn helps Mokuri (v4) and Yumenaka (v5). Differences between people, even between species, are a source of delight rather than threat.

**Characters as arguments.** Tsumiki argues for being yourself without apology, and for kindness as strength. Yutaka argues for caution and fitting in, then learns its cost. Mokuri argues for keeping people away; Yumenaka argues for being liked by everyone, and both find something better in real friendship.

**Symbols and motifs.** The wolf's tail and nose touch (ch. 74); buried sandwiches; the moon (VIZ: learn to howl at the moon); Mokuri's silk; school festivals marking the year.

**Traditions it echoes.** The monster-school comedy and the "timid boy, popular girl" romance (Beneath the Tangles compares it to Komi Can't Communicate). Its welcome to the stranger echoes teachings in many faiths; its gentle coexistence theme echoes modern pluralism.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism and community are joined: being yourself is how you find belonging. Progressivism appears as coexistence, never as politics. Folklore beings are friendly neighbors, which some religious families will want to discuss.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-3 (ch. 1-25) set up Tsumiki, Yutaka and their circle, with the most reported fan service. Volumes 4-6 (ch. 26-52) widen to Mokuri, the student council and holidays; Beneath the Tangles calls them the best so far. Volumes 7-9 (ch. 53-79) bring Valentine's, White Day and a new school year. Chapters 80-122 (v10 onward) add Kojo, a sports festival and a beach summer; contents not checked.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Yutaka cares a lot about what others think. How does that hold him back?
2. Tsumiki is different from everyone, and popular anyway. Why?
3. Mokuri pushes people away. What helps her change?
4. The mythfolk have different instincts and values. How should friends handle that?
5. Who could you reach out to the way Tsumiki reached out to Yutaka?
