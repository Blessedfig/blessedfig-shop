**Premise.** Kaito Ukei was summoned from Japan to another world as its hero. He defeated the Demon Lord alongside companions he trusted, and they stabbed him to death (MangaUpdates blurb). A voice tells him the "tutorial" is over, and he wakes four years earlier, in the summoning chamber, on the day he first met the princess who later betrayed him, with all his memories and strength (Yen Press; AniList; TV Tropes). He vows to make every traitor suffer and lose everything they value before they die (Yen Press vol. 1 blurb). In the light novel's first volume he begins by beating and torturing the princess and her guards in the summoning room, cuts apart thugs who attack him, and buys Minnalis, a rabbit-eared beastfolk girl sold into slavery, as a companion bound by a slave contract who cannot betray him (Anime UK News, light novel vol. 1). Minnalis has her own grudge against those who sold her, and in the manga's second volume the two train together and feed their shared hatred (Yen Press vol. 2 blurb; Kadokawa vol. 2 blurb). The manga ends after these first steps; the light novel goes on to many more acts of revenge.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** If the people you trusted most killed you, and you were given your life back, what would you owe them? The series asks this and answers at once: pain.

**The answer the story gives.** Revenge, carried out with laughter. Kaito returns to the moment of his summoning knowing who will betray him and strikes first (Yen Press; ANN). The only safe relationship is one that cannot break: a slave contract (Anime UK News). The manga stops before the revenge is complete, so the reader is left with the project itself as the story's meaning.

**Characters as arguments.** Kaito argues that betrayal cancels every obligation. Minnalis argues the same from the other side of the slave market: she was sold out by people she trusted, and she binds herself to the one person who promises her revenge. The princess and her court stand for institutions that use people up; in the light novel the summoning itself cost the lives of slaves and summoned classmates (TV Tropes). No one in the two manga volumes argues for mercy; Anime UK News notes that in the light novel Kaito's own limit (not hurting the uninvolved) comes late.

**Symbols and motifs.** The laugh in the title; the reset to "tutorial mode"; the slave contract as the only trustworthy bond; the summoning circle where the hero was first used.

**Traditions it echoes.** The revenge tragedy (The Count of Monte Cristo is the classic model of a betrayed man's return, though Dumas ends on mercy; my own comparison), and the online genre of betrayed heroes who return stronger. Religious ethics almost all set themselves against it: "vengeance is mine" in Christianity, Leviticus 19:18 in Judaism, the Dhammapada's teaching that hatred does not end hatred, and the Qur'an's preference for pardon.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Might makes right is affirmed: strength decides everything. Nihilism is explored: nothing but the grudge has value. Authority is critiqued, but only to clear the way for the hero's own unaccountable power.

**Shifts across the series.** Volume 1 (ch. 1-5): the return and the first revenge in the summoning chamber (likely). Volume 2 (ch. 6-11): Minnalis joins and the two train for revenge (Yen Press). The manga then stops; later light-novel volumes go much further, including much darker material (TV Tropes).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Kaito punishes some people for things they have not done yet in this timeline. Is that justice?
2. Why does Kaito choose a slave as his companion? What does that say about trust?
3. Is there any difference between Kaito and the people who betrayed him?
4. What would a second chance used for something other than revenge look like?
5. Why might a story make the reader enjoy cruelty? How did it feel to read?
