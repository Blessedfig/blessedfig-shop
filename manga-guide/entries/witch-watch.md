**Premise.** Nico Wakatsuki, a teenage witch just back from her training, moves in with her childhood friend Morihito Otogi, whose family of ogres has served her family of witches as familiars for generations. Nico's mother, a witch of prophecy, has foreseen a disaster striking Nico within a year, so Morihito must guard her; Nico, meanwhile, is in love with him and her well-meant spells keep going comically wrong. Witches in this world are bound to help people; those who use magic selfishly are called warlocks and want Nico, the 'Witch of Thousand' born once in a millennium, to revive Jura, a warlock leader who once waged war on humanity. The household grows: Kanshi, a tengu; Keigo, a werewolf whose other self 'Wolf' takes over when he sees a crescent; Miharu, a middle-school vampire from a family of exorcists; later Ban, a lazy, hard-drinking dragon familiar. On the prophesied day Nico gives up her own time to save Morihito and becomes a five-year-old (ch. 131); Part 2 follows her familiars raising her while they recover her lost years. Once she is restored, Morihito admits his feelings (ch. 222-224), and Part 3 pits the household against the warlock Shikimi, who wants to resurrect his fiancee Hrotsvitha, a witch burned by inquisitors centuries ago. Hrotsvitha's power is fear; the friends defeat her by taking that fear away, and Shikimi and Hrotsvitha become a tree in the park. Seven years later Nico and Morihito are married with a daughter, Miko (ch. 262-270; fan wiki).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What is power for? Nico is the most powerful witch in a thousand years, and the warlocks want to use her to remake the world for witches. The series asks whether great power should serve oneself, one's own kind, or everyone.

**The answer the story gives.** Power is for helping, and family is how you keep it pointed the right way. Witches are oath-bound to do good; Nico's gem grows with each person she helps. When the disaster comes, Nico spends her own years to save Morihito (ch. 131), and the household spends the next ninety chapters giving them back. The final enemy is defeated not by greater force but by taking away the fear she feeds on (ch. 262-270).

**Characters as arguments.** Morihito argues for duty and restraint: he puts his oath ahead of his own heart until the mission is done. Nico argues for impulsive generosity. Keigo and Wolf are a divided self learning to share one life. Miharu is a vampire from an exorcist family, monster and hunter at once. Shikimi is the case against revenge: his grief over a real injustice, the burning of his fiancee, turned into centuries of hatred. Kokuryo shows a villain who changes sides when faced with death.

**Symbols and motifs.** The prophecy of 'dogs and rain'; the witch gem that brightens with thanks; butterflies carrying Nico's lost years; the childhood note that became a word-curse on Morihito's heart (ch. 61-62); the tree in the park where the villains end.

**Traditions it echoes.** Japanese yokai comedy and the shonen found-family; European witch lore and the history of the witch trials; the Jump battle arc. Its rejection of revenge for real historical wrongs echoes Christian 'overcome evil with good' and Buddhist teaching on hatred for many readers, though the series cites neither.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Found family is the clear winner. Witchcraft is affirmed as a calling to service, and its misuse condemned. Duty is honored but finally reconciled with love. Revenge, even for a real wrong, is shown as a trap.

**Shifts across the series.** Part 1 (ch. 1-131, vols. 1-15) is mostly gag comedy with growing battle arcs, ending in Nico's sacrifice. Part 2 (ch. 132-224) is a domestic comedy about raising a five-year-old witch, with the Phantom Blaze arc (ch. 179-185) and the confession (ch. 222-224). Part 3 (ch. 225-270) is a single long battle story ending in a seven-years-later epilogue.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Witches in this story must help people. Should having a gift mean you owe something to others?
2. Shikimi's anger began with a real injustice. Where did he go wrong?
3. Nico gave up her years to save Morihito. What would you give up for a friend?
4. Morihito waited to date Nico until his duty was finished. Was that right?
5. Ban drinks and gambles but is loyal. Can a 'bad influence' still be a good friend?
