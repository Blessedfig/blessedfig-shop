**Premise.** A man who died in an embarrassing accident is reborn, after a mysterious gacha-style draw, as Ein Roundheart, eldest son of a count in the Kingdom of Heim (Wikipedia; Nextory copy of LN vol. 1). His only skill, Toxin Decomposition EX, looks useless beside his half-brother Grint's rare Holy Knight skill, and his father Rogas, Heim's commander, who values people by their skills, names Grint heir and leaves Ein behind at a party (Mangapedia). His mother Olivia divorces Rogas and takes Ein home, revealing that she is a princess of Ishtarica, the continent's strongest kingdom; her father, King Silverd, makes Ein crown prince and cuts ties with Heim (Mangapedia). Ein turns out to be a Dryad with the rare Absorption trait: he can eat monsters' magic stones and take their power (Mangapedia). He absorbs the national treasure, the Dullahan's magic stone (vol. 2), trains for a year and a half, enters the royal academy and is engaged to Krone, a duke's granddaughter from Heim who admires him; his guard and sword teacher is Chris, an elf knight (Mangapedia; MyAnimeList synopsis). Later arcs: he defeats a sea dragon and gains an Ice Dragon skill; meets Marco, a loyal black knight of the old Demon King, cursed by the secretive Red Fox, and learns the truth about Ishtarica's first king; and at the end of the Demon King's castle arc evolves into a Demon King himself (Kadokawa, vols. 8-10). Angered by a letter from Heim, he resolves to settle accounts with his old homeland, which leads to a tense summit (vols. 10-11). With the feud over, the Red Fox attack a port city, and an invitation takes Ein and Chris to the elves' village, where a sealed sanctuary and a strong enemy wait (vols. 12-13). In the latest volume, Heim's second prince is murdered, a rival state declares war, and the allied nation of Eulo falls (vol. 14) (Kadokawa).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What is a person worth? Rogas measures his sons by their skills and finds Ein lacking. The rest of the story argues against him.

**The answer the story gives.** Worth comes first from being loved, and then from what you do with your gifts. Olivia and Silverd value Ein before they know what he can do; Lloyd, whose own skill is mere tailoring, shows that effort matters (Mangapedia). Ein's "useless" skill turns out to be the key to his power, but the story's heart is the family that took him in.

**Characters as arguments.** Rogas is the case against judging by talent. Olivia argues that a mother's duty is to her child. Silverd shows a ruler's protective love. Krone and Chris show loyalty freely given. Marco, the cursed knight, is the case for faithfulness to a lost lord (vol. 9). The Red Fox stands for hidden manipulation.

**Symbols and motifs.** Magic stones as food and power; the Dullahan, a headless knight, as Ein's first great inheritance; the first king's empty grave (vol. 9); a colorless village where time has stopped (vol. 13).

**Traditions it echoes.** The rejected son raised up (Joseph in Genesis; many folk tales of the unpromising child) and the education of a prince (the mirror-for-princes tradition), set in the Japanese isekai genre.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Traditionalism and loyalty to family and crown are affirmed. Might-makes-right is mixed: strength is the reward, but judging people only by power is the first villain's sin. Patriotism grows as Ishtarica faces Heim and then war.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-2: rejection, rescue, the Dullahan stone. Vols. 3-7: academy life, a betrothal and a sea dragon (from MangaUpdates and Kadokawa vol. 8; to verify). Vols. 8-10: the Demon King's territory, Marco and Ein's evolution. Vols. 10-12: the reckoning with Heim. Vols. 12-14: the Red Fox, the elves' village, and war (Kadokawa).
