**Premise.** Four hundred years after an apocalypse, the kingdom of Metallicana is attacked by the Dark Rebel Army, which seeks four seals to revive the destruction goddess Anthrasax. The high priest's daughter, Tia Noto Yoko (15 at the start; fan wiki), must kiss her adoptive brother Lucien Renlen (15; Viz's blurb says 14), because a virgin's kiss releases the wizard sealed inside him: Dark Schneider, the army's arrogant, lecherous former leader, who appears in an adult body. He fights his old generals (the half-dark-elf Arshes Nei, his adopted daughter turned lover; the ninja Gara; the ice-dragon Kall-Su; the scheming Abigail) for his own reasons, chiefly Yoko, and wins most of them back to his side; he tears out his own heart to break a curse on Nei (OVA ep. 5; manga around vols. 6-7). After a two-year time skip the war against Kall-Su's Sorcerer Shoguns and Anthrasax (sealed in Princess Sheila) ends in Anthrasax's defeat, and the story turns into an apocalypse in which angels sent by God descend to kill a third of humanity for worshipping false gods, demons and Satan break out of Hell, and Lucien is revealed as the reincarnation of the fallen angel Lucifer, of whom Dark Schneider is a fragment. Dark Schneider, sent to Hell, refuses Satan, returns, and fights both the demons and the archangels in the "Immoral Laws" arc (vols. 17-27), which breaks off unfinished (Wikipedia; fan wiki; TV Tropes).

## Life message

**Raw power, desire and loyalty to one's own matter more than rules, gods or morality; a selfish man can still be a savior when he fights for the people he loves.**

ANN's Jason Thompson calls him an id-driven, unstoppable antihero and the series sexist. Loyalty to his "pack" (Yoko, his old generals) is his one constant. |

| Socio-cultural | A 1980s Shonen Jump power fantasy steeped in heavy metal, Dungeons & Dragons and Fighting Fantasy gamebooks (Wikipedia); readers of the original boys' magazine complained about its content (ANN). It moved to an adult magazine in 2000 and grew more explicit. |

Outlook: Bittersweet — the heroes defeat Anthrasax and many enemies become allies, but the world is in an unfinished apocalypse, populations are sent to Hell, and the story has been on hiatus since 2010 (Wikipedia; ANN).
Hope source: P — the story's way through is protecting others: Dark Schneider, for all his cruelty, keeps sacrificing himself for Yoko and his friends (OVA ep. 5; fan wiki); effort and growing stronger (E) is the engine of the plot.

## Creator and publisher context

- Kazushi Hagiwara (born 1963, Tokyo) dropped out of high school, later graduated from Tokyo Designer Gakuin College, and assisted a hentai artist and then Izumi Matsumoto on Kimagure Orange Road before debuting in Weekly Shonen Jump (Wikipedia, Kazushi Hagiwara).
- On-record intent (Wikipedia, Production): he was inspired by Go Nagai's Devilman, Dragon Quest and Fighting Fantasy gamebooks, and is a heavy metal and Dungeons & Dragons fan who named characters and spells after bands; in 2023 he said he was still storyboarding the Hell arc and wanted the finished work to make readers ask, "Is this really Bastard!!?"
- Production: the slow schedule came from redrawing and heavy screentone work; vol. 23 took three years and he fell ill during the move to digital drawing (fan wiki).
- His studio (Studio Loud In School) publishes dōjinshi, and MangaUpdates notes many companion dōjinshi for Bastard!! are adult (Wikipedia; MangaUpdates). **Status: Needs more info on the creator.**
- Publisher: Shueisha (Weekly Shonen Jump, then the seinen Ultra Jump). English: Viz, 17+, cancelled after vol. 19; ANN's Thompson suggests the untranslated Michael scene may be why.
- No controversies involving the creator beyond the content complaints noted (Jump readers in the 1980s, ANN) were found.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What if the only one who can save the world is its worst villain? Metallicana must awaken the wizard who once tried to conquer it, and the story asks what such a savior is worth.

**The answer the story gives.** Power and loyalty are what count. Dark Schneider never becomes good; he becomes loyal, mostly to Yoko and his old generals, and that is enough to win. His cruelty and lust are not judged; they are part of the fun.

**Characters as arguments.** Dark Schneider argues that strength and desire need no justification. Yoko argues for faithful love and restraint, and is the one person he fears. Arshes Nei's dream of a world without orphans is mocked as childish. The angels argue for obedience to divine law and are shown as executioners; Lucifer, the original rebel, is the hidden good. Abigail argues for destruction.

**Symbols and motifs.** The virgin's kiss that unleashes the adult hero from a boy's body; heavy metal names as spells; the sealed goddess Anthrasax inside a princess; the hero tearing out his own heart; Hell's gates opening.

**Traditions it echoes.** Devilman's apocalypse and sympathetic Lucifer (Wikipedia, Production), Milton's heroic Satan, the Book of Revelation (a third of humanity killed; Michael against the dragon), sword-and-sorcery antiheroes, and D&D.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Might makes right and hedonism are celebrated; Christian cosmology is inverted (angels as villains, Lucifer as good); the occult is the setting's physics; communitarian loyalty is real but narrow. Critics read the whole as self-aware parody that still delivers what it parodies (ANN).

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-7 (the Dark Rebel Army arc): gag-heavy sword and sorcery in a boys' magazine. Vols. 8-16 (Sorcerer Shoguns and Anthrasax): darker, after a two-year time skip. Vols. 17-27 (the "Immoral Laws" apocalypse, Ultra Jump): Heaven, Hell and Satan, the most explicit content, much of it untranslated. The story stops in 2010.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Dark Schneider saves the kingdom, but he also hurts and harasses people. Is he a hero?
2. Why is Yoko the one person he listens to?
3. Arshes Nei wants a world without orphans, and he calls it childish. Who is right?
4. The story turns angels into villains and Lucifer into a good figure. Why might an author do that, and how does it sit with your family's beliefs?
5. Many critics enjoy the series but call it sexist. Can a story be fun and still treat people badly?
