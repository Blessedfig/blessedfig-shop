**Premise.** From a bus window, high school student Sakura Sakurakouji, a gentle-looking girl who is secretly a martial-arts expert, sees a boy her age burning people alive with blue flames; the next day there are no bodies, and the boy is the new transfer student in her class, Rei Ogami (Wikipedia; AniList). Rei is Code:Breaker 06, an assassin for Eden, a secret organization serving the government, made of people who officially do not exist; they kill wrongdoers the law cannot reach, and his motto adds "evil for evil" to "an eye for an eye" (Wikipedia, characters). His flame cannot burn Sakura, who turns out to be a "Rare Kind" who cancels others' powers; convinced that killing is wrong, she sets out to stop him and to reach the person behind his coldness (AniList). The early arcs pit the Code:Breakers against a gang, corrupt police, a politician running an organ-trafficking lab that experiments on children with powers, assassins sent after Sakura, whose adoptive father heads a respected yakuza clan, and the renegade ex-Code:Breaker Hitomi (ch. 1-33, fan wiki; anime). Later arcs uncover Rei's brother "The One Being Sought," whom Rei kills; the "Seven Flames," powers named for the demons of the seven deadly sins that Rei inherits through the arm of "Code:Emperor"; the "December 32" massacre in Rei and Sakura's forgotten childhood; Sakura's real parents, one of them a founder of Eden; and a war with the "Angels" (ch. 34-230, fan wiki). Five years on, Sakura is a medical student and Rei still secretly punishes corruption the law cannot judge (ch. 230.1).

## Life message

**Evil must be stopped, but no one has the right to decide who deserves to die; a killer can be changed by someone who refuses to give up on the value of every life.**

Outlook: Hopeful — the friends survive the wars, Rei comes to value life, Sakura studies medicine to save patients and the epilogue ends with the group together watching fireworks (ch. 230.1), though many die along the way and Rei still works outside the law.
Hope source: R — the way through is redemption: Sakura's refusal to give up on Rei slowly turns a cold killer toward mercy (sparing Haruto, ch. 92); friendship (F), the found family of the Shibuya mansion, is the close second.

**Where traditions disagree.** Every frame welcomes the condemnation of corruption, trafficking and the abuse of children, and most welcome Sakura's defense of life. The divide is twofold. First, vigilante killing: traditions that reserve punishment to lawful authority or reject violence (Catholic, Reformed, Anabaptist, Jewish, Muslim, Historically Black Protestant) weigh Rei's early "evil for evil" heavily, while Satanist readers find it congenial. Second, the demonic power system: Baptist, Pentecostal, Orthodox, Adventist, Jehovah's Witness and Latter-day Saint readings treat a hero empowered by Satan, Lucifer and Beelzebub as a serious concern, while secular and Pagan readers see fantasy furniture.

**Profile notes for parents:** The series turns on fairness (the guilty must pay) set against care (every life matters), and care slowly wins. Loyalty is strong (found family, adoptive parents). Sanctity is low: demons are a power system, and groping is played for laughs. Del Rey rated it 16+; the main concerns are frequent burning deaths, a child-trafficking lab and the comic groping of a teen heroine.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** If the law cannot touch the guilty, may someone else kill them? Rei Ogami says yes: he is "evil for evil," the garbage man who cleans up what courts cannot (The Fandom Post). Sakura Sakurakouji says no: no one has that right. The series begins as an argument between them that neither can win by force, since his fire cannot burn her.

**The answer the story gives.** Mostly hers. Rei begins sparing enemies (Haruto, ch. 92), comes to say that death brings only regret (fan wiki), and learns that as a child he had vowed never to kill. Yet the story never fully gives up its hero's vigilantism: five years later he is still punishing corruption the law cannot judge (ch. 230.1).

**Characters as arguments.** Rei argues for retribution; Sakura for the sanctity of life; Toki for loyalty to a sister above all; Eden and the Prime Minister for the state's right to use people as tools, which the story condemns. "The One Being Sought," Rei's brother, argues that the strong may use and discard the weak, and dies for it. Code:Emperor's attempt to prevent catastrophe by killing Rare Kinds (December 32) shows where "killing for the greater good" leads.

**Symbols and motifs.** Rei's black glove, a gift from his brother kept as a reminder to kill him. Blue flame that leaves no ash: judgment with no trace. The Seven Flames, named for the seven deadly sins, each bought with a piece of himself. Sakura's hug, which cancels power. Fireworks in the last chapter, which once frightened her and now give her courage.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Code of Hammurabi, which Rei cites; Christian demonology and the seven deadly sins as a fantasy power system (Binsfeld's classification, Wikipedia); the shōnen anti-hero redeemed by a kind heroine (from general knowledge; to verify). Faith readers will notice the reversal of Romans 12:17 in Rei's motto.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Retributive duty ethics is presented sympathetically, then questioned; pacifism is carried by Sakura and gains ground; authoritarian state power (Eden) is critiqued; might makes right is defeated in each villain. The occult imagery is a power system, neither worshipped nor mocked.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-5 (ch. 1-39): the killing debate, organ lab, Haruto and Hitomi; the most graphic executions and the anime's range. Volumes 6-10 (ch. 40-83): Re-Codes and Rei's brother. Volumes 11-17 (ch. 84-148): Code:Emperor, the Seven Flames, Code:Names and the past. Volumes 18-26 (ch. 149-230): December 32, Pandora's Box, the Angels and the ending.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Rei says some people are beyond the law. Is anyone? Who should decide?
2. Sakura cannot be burned by Rei's fire. What does that say about her role in his life?
3. Rei spares Haruto but puts a fire in his chest that will kill him if he does wrong again. Is that mercy?
4. Why do you think the author named the hero's powers after demons and sins?
5. The jokes about Nenene grabbing Sakura: funny, or not okay? Why?
