**Premise.** Miku Okazaki is the flashiest gyaru (gal) at her high school and is failing every class. When the principal tells her to bake cookies for the teachers she has disappointed, she asks young home economics teacher Shinji Yabe for help, and after several failures she succeeds (vol. 1). Moved by her joy, Yabe starts a cooking club; Miku is its only member, and she enjoys teasing and flirting with "Yabecchi," hoping he will fall for her (Wikipedia; Seven Seas). Over the next volumes she learns home cooking, works off summer weight gain (vol. 2), helps save the club from strict student council member Koharu Fujiwara (vol. 3), faces rivals for Yabe's affection (vols. 4-8), and in her third year must choose between her feelings and her dream of becoming a chef (vol. 9). After graduation she works toward becoming a home economics teacher herself, and only then, as equals, does the romance resolve (vol. 10; Seven Seas). User reviews say the last chapters jump ahead in time and end in marriage (MyAnimeList; to verify).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What can a teacher's attention do for a student everyone has given up on, and where must it stop? Miku is failing everything; Yabe is the first teacher who pushes her to try instead of sending her to makeup classes (MangaUpdates synopsis).

**The answer the story gives.** Attention and encouragement change her life: she learns to cook, gains confidence and, by her third year, has a career dream. On the romance, the publisher's final-volume synopsis frames the answer as waiting: only after she graduates and they stand on equal footing can her feelings be answered. Reviewers note that the comedy along the way leans on the very pursuit the ending defers.

**Characters as arguments.** Miku argues for going after what you want, openly and cheerfully. Yabe argues, often weakly, for professional restraint; UK Anime Network thinks he knows he is crossing lines. Fujiwara argues for rules and order, and becomes a rival in love herself. The gal friends argue for loyalty.

**Symbols and motifs.** Cookies baked as a bribe that become a real success (vol. 1). Home-cooked dishes as steps of growth. The cooking club as a room for two. Graduation as the line the story says must be crossed first.

**Traditions it echoes.** The gyaru comedy, the food manga and the teacher-student romantic comedy common in Japanese seinen magazines. For religious and secular readers alike, the questions it raises are about trust between adults and the young, the subject of safeguarding rules in schools and churches.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism is celebrated in Miku's style and pursuit. Virtue ethics shows in her diligence. Duty ethics appears in the rule Yabe struggles to keep. Hedonism is present in the food and in fan service aimed at the reader.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-3 are cooking lessons, flirting and saving the club. Volumes 4-8 add rivals and escalating romantic comedy (the cheek kiss, the camp, the sleepover). Volumes 9-10 turn to Miku's future and resolve the romance after graduation, with time skips that users call rushed.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Yabe pushes Miku to try harder instead of giving up on her. Has a teacher ever done that for you?
2. Miku flirts with her teacher as a joke, then means it. What should a teacher do when a student has a crush?
3. Why do schools have rules about teachers and students spending time alone or dating?
4. The story says the romance had to wait until Miku graduated. Does that make the earlier chapters okay? Why or why not?
5. Miku decides to become a teacher herself. What did she learn from Yabe that she would want to pass on?
