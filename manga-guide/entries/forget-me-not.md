**Premise.** Yusuke Serizawa, in his twenties and working at a divorce lawyer's office, crashes his scooter after glimpsing a familiar face. The woman who distracted him also called the ambulance, then phones him at the hospital; she knows him well, but will not say who she is (Kodansha; ANN). Searching his memory, he relives the girls and women he loved and, he realizes, hurt. In seventh grade he treated a quiet girl he called "Nobuta" badly under pressure from classmates; in high school a prickly classmate slapped and hit him; at cram school he fell for a shy young college student who taught there, nicknamed "Hermès," and helped her overcome stage fright (v1-2, ANN; Penguin Random House). A male classmate tells Serizawa he has feelings for him, and Serizawa responds kindly (v2, Manga Bookshelf). In college his quirky friend Tsukushi Makino helps him date Yamaguchi while his heart belongs to Tsukushi (v3-4). As an adult, after a dark time and a chance meeting with Nobuta (v4-5), he dates Sam-soon, a woman whose wealth makes him feel he must prove himself (v5-6), and finally becomes involved with his boss's daughter and her friend Nana, a musician (v7). His father left his mother for another woman (v4, Manga Bookshelf). The final volume reveals who saved him; MyAnimeList reviewers call the mystery a minor thread and split over the ending.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Who are the people we hurt without meaning to, and do they remember us? A stranger saves Serizawa and knows him well, and he cannot tell which of the women from his past she is.

**The answer the story gives.** That the past is not past: each memory shows a moment when he chose comfort, peer approval or his own feelings over another person (ANN; MyAnimeList). Growth comes slowly and imperfectly (Manga Bookshelf on v4). Reviewers say the mystery itself matters less than the reckoning (MyAnimeList).

**Characters as arguments.** Nobuta is the case for noticing quiet people. The tsundere classmate and Hermès show how little a teenage boy understands the girls he likes. Tsukushi shows a cheerful face hiding pain (AIPT). Sam-soon brings adult questions of money and equality (v6). Serizawa himself is the argument: a gentle man is not therefore a kind one.

**Symbols and motifs.** The accident and the unseen rescuer; nicknames that hide real names (ANN); the phone, which brings both the mystery caller and Sam-soon (v5); rain and an umbrella (v7).

**Traditions it echoes.** The confessional novel and the Taiwanese youth romance (Mag Hsu's TV work); English marketing compared it to Five Centimeters per Second and How I Met Your Mother (Penguin Australia). Religious readers will hear examination of conscience and repentance; Buddhist readers, karma.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Existential responsibility and virtue formed through failure are explored, not preached. Expressive individualism, following one's heart, is shown as a source of harm when it ignores others.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-2: school-age loves (Nobuta, the tsundere, Hermès). Vols. 3-4: college and the Tsukushi triangle; the dark adult period. Vols. 5-6: law internship, Sam-soon, the job hunt. Vol. 7: the brewing company, the "princess" and Nana, and the mystery's answer.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why did Serizawa treat Nobuta badly when he liked her? Have you seen peer pressure work like that?
2. Most of the women are known by nicknames. What does that say about how he saw them?
3. How should Serizawa make amends to someone he hurt years ago?
4. Is it wrong to date one person while loving another?
5. Who from your past would you most want to thank, or apologize to?
