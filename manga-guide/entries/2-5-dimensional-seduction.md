**Premise.** Masamune Okumura, a second-year high school student, is the only member of his school's manga club and insists he loves only Liliel, a heroine from an anime. His mother left the family when he was nine and a girl he liked bullied him, so he has sworn off "3D" girls (fan wiki). Then Lilysa Amano, a first-year who has dreamed of cosplaying Liliel since a cosplay event turned her away as a middle schooler, joins the club and makes him her photographer. The club grows: his childhood friend Mikari Tachibana, a popular model who has loved him for years; Noa, a lonely, tongue-tied cosplay fan; Aria Kisaki, a gyaru whose manga-artist father left the family; and the club's new adviser, Mayuri Hanyu, a teacher who secretly was the famous cosplayer Mayura. Cosplay events, photo books (ROMs) and rivalries with top cosplayers such as 753♡ (Nagomi) fill the story. In the end Lilysa steps aside, and Okumura and Mikari confess and become a couple (ch. 200, fan wiki).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can loving a fictional character be a safe place that becomes a prison? Okumura loves Liliel because she cannot leave him the way his mother did. When a real girl steps into Liliel's costume, the line between the two worlds, the "2.5th dimension," starts to blur.

**The answer the story gives.** Passion shared with others is a bridge, not a wall. Through photographing friends, Okumura learns to see cosplayers as people rather than figurines (fan wiki), faces his past with the help of friends, and finally chooses a real person (ch. 200). Lilysa, Noa, Aria and Mayuri each find courage through the hobby.

**Characters as arguments.** Lilysa argues for pure, joyful passion. Mikari argues for faithful love that waits; she wins. Noa argues that the shy can change (ANN, episode 15). Mayuri argues against shame about one's body and against quitting a dream because of age (Anime Corner). Nagomi argues that passion must also be professional. Higarashi, the failed artist kept going by one fan letter, shows the power of encouragement (Wikipedia).

**Symbols and motifs.** The camera: a way of seeing that can objectify or honor; the story claims the second while drawing the first. The costume: transformation and disguise. Liliel, the angel: an idol who must be outgrown. Lilysa as "bride Liliel" saying goodbye (fan wiki).

**Traditions it echoes.** My Dress-Up Darling (critics compare them directly), Genshiken-style otaku club stories, and the harem romcom. Its "choose a real person over an image" theme echoes warnings against idolatry for some religious readers, and Buddhist teaching on attachment, though the series' presentation leans on the very display it questions.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism and community are celebrated. Hedonism is the medium. Consumerism is the setting, mostly unquestioned. Feminism appears inside the story (Mayuri's arc) and against it (Anime Feminist). A same-sex couple is welcomed without fuss.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-9 (ch. 1-70, the anime) build the club, the first events, Noa and Aria's arrivals and Mayuri's return. Later volumes deepen Okumura's past and trauma (ch. 154 is cited for his talk with Mayuri; fan wiki) and narrow the romance to Lilysa and Mikari, ending in Mikari's confession (ch. 200) and the final chapter (MANGA Plus ch. 201, December 2025; Wikipedia).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why does Okumura say he only loves a fictional girl? What is he protecting himself from?
2. Lilysa wants to become Liliel. What is the difference between admiring a character and becoming one?
3. The story praises cosplay as art but often draws the girls for the reader's eye. Do those two things fit together?
4. Okumura refuses Mikari's offer because she is too precious to him. What does that show about him?
5. Noa says she can change after a mistake. Who helps her, and how?
