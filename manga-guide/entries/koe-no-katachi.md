**Premise.** The one-shot opens in the present, when Shoya Ishida gets a phone call inviting him to a study group and hears the name Shoko Nishimiya. It then goes back to sixth grade, told from Shoya's point of view. Shoko, a deaf transfer student, sometimes stops lessons because of her disability, and the whole class comes to dislike her. Shoya bullies her relentlessly. When his cruelty goes too far, the class turns on him in a public reckoning and he becomes the new target. Shoko still worries about him, but the two never manage to understand each other, and she transfers away. Afterward, Shoya says something heartless, and the teacher and classmates again switch sides to agree with him; seeing this, he realizes for the first time how frighteningly wrong his class is, and he begins to act on Shoko's behalf (Japanese Wikipedia, plot of the original version). MyAnimeList adds that the teachers do nothing about the bullying and that the story covers remorse and redemption. Unlike the serial, this version shows Shoko's inner thoughts directly in several scenes (fan wiki, describing the first one-shot), and nearly every character except Shoko and Shoya is unrepentantly unpleasant (TV Tropes).

## Life message

**Cruelty to someone who is different is wrong, and a whole group can share the guilt; seeing that clearly, and turning to stand with the one you hurt, is where making amends begins.**

| Pragmatic | Adults who fail to act let bullying spread: the teachers do nothing (MyAnimeList) and side with whoever the class blames (Japanese Wikipedia). Seeing the pattern is the first practical step to stopping it. |
| Religious | No religion appears. Faith readers can see conscience, conviction of wrong and the first step of repentance in Shoya's realization; the one-shot stops before forgiveness or reconciliation. |
| Socio-cultural | A Japanese classroom where harmony is kept by scapegoating, first the disabled girl, then the boy who got caught. Its publication history shows how sensitive the subject was: held back for over two years, then printed unchanged at the Japanese Federation of the Deaf's request (Japanese Wikipedia). |

Outlook: Bittersweet — Shoko leaves and the two never reconcile within the one-shot, but Shoya sees what he did and turns toward making it right.
Hope source: R — the hope is redemption: a bully's conscience wakes and he begins to act for the girl he hurt; the serial later carries this through to forgiveness.

## Creator and publisher context

- Yoshitoki Oima (story and art), born March 15, 1989, in Ogaki; her mother worked as a sign-language interpreter, which inspired the story, and her mother and sister helped with it (English Wikipedia, citing an Egmont interview).
- She drew this version in 2008 as a newcomer-award entry; her first serial was the Mardock Scramble adaptation (2009-2012) in Bessatsu Shonen Magazine, and its success helped get this one-shot printed (Japanese Wikipedia).
- On-record intent (Kodansha interview, 2016): the series is about how hard communication is, and indifference (drawn later as X marks over faces); she had the full story in mind early. In the fanbook interview she said that turning it into a serial meant it would have to include the suicide scene, so everything before it became steps toward that point (fan wiki).
- Later works: A Silent Voice (2013-2014), To Your Eternity (2016-2025).
- Publisher: Kodansha (Bessatsu Shonen Magazine, shonen). Kodansha USA published the one-shots in English only as extras in the 2022 Complete Collector's Edition, rated 13+.
- Publication controversy: held back from 2008 to 2011 over its subject; Kodansha consulted lawyers and the Japanese Federation of the Deaf before printing (Japanese Wikipedia; English Wikipedia calls it months of legal dispute).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** When a whole class hurts someone, who is guilty? The one-shot answers: everyone, though only one boy is punished, and the first honest person is the one who finally sees it.

**The answer the story gives.** Shoya leads the bullying and deserves blame. But the class that cheered him on turns on him to save itself, and the teacher goes along. When Shoya watches them switch sides yet again after Shoko leaves, he understands what the group is and what he was. The one-shot ends with that understanding and a resolve to act for Shoko; it does not show forgiveness (Japanese Wikipedia).

**Characters as arguments.** Shoya argues for the herd, then against it. Shoko argues for patience and kindness under cruelty; this version lets readers see her thoughts (fan wiki). The teacher argues for self-protection over justice. The class, drawn as nearly all unpleasant (TV Tropes), is the argument that crowds have no conscience.

**Symbols and motifs.** Voice and hearing: the title's old character for voice combines voice, hand and ear (Japanese Wikipedia, cited in this site's A Silent Voice entry). The phone call that opens the story frames the past as something that will come back.

**Traditions it echoes.** The scapegoat story, in which a group restores peace by blaming one member; and the moral awakening of a wrongdoer. For faith readers it echoes the first step of repentance: seeing the wrong for what it is.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Virtue ethics wins: conscience and courage. Conformity and might-makes-right are condemned. Secular humanism frames the change: empathy, not punishment, turns Shoya.

**Shifts across the series.** A one-shot has no later volumes; the shift that matters is to the serial. The serial adds the reunion years later, sign language, the friend group, the suicide plan and attempt, and a long arc of forgiveness. The one-shot is a blunter, darker sketch that stops where the serial's first volume does. Parents who know the film or the serial should not expect the same hopeful ending here.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why did the class turn on Shoya so quickly?
2. What should the teacher have done?
3. Shoko was kind to Shoya after he hurt her. Why might she have done that?
4. What made Shoya finally see what he had done?
5. Have you seen a group pick on one person? What could one person do?
