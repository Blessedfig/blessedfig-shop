**Premise.** Hitomi Takano, a first-year high school transfer student, is very tall, strong and athletic, with sharp teeth and a glare that frightens everyone; inside, she is painfully shy and longs for friends. On the way to school, second-year Yuu Usami, a smaller, kind and studious boy, realizes she is gentle and befriends her; he tutors her in math. His outgoing, athletic younger sister Kaoru, Hitomi's classmate, becomes her friend too. Running gags turn on Hitomi's scary face when she is nervous, misunderstandings with strangers and her figure. Across 12 volumes the two move through a Christmas, Valentine's Day, a ruined date, a night stranded by a flood, Yuu's confession (vol. 8), a summer of dating (vols. 9-10), the school festival and Yuu's graduation year (vols. 11-12). A MyAnimeList reviewer notes a later mention of the couple having a child (Wikipedia; Seven Seas and Penguin Random House blurbs; MyAnimeList).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What happens to a gentle person whom everyone fears on sight? Hitomi's height, teeth and glare make strangers flinch, and her shyness makes her face look even fiercer. She wants friends and cannot get past the first second.

**The answer the story gives.** One person who looks twice changes everything. Yuu notices her gentleness, helps her with math, and keeps showing up; Kaoru adds energy and acceptance. With friends, Hitomi grows bolder, and the friendship becomes a slow, faithful romance (vols. 8-12).

**Characters as arguments.** Yuu argues for patience and attention. Hitomi argues that courage can grow in a shy heart. Kaoru argues for cheerful, sometimes pushy, friendship. The strangers who scream and run argue nothing; they are the mistake the story corrects.

**Symbols and motifs.** Hitomi's name ("eye" or "pupil") inside the word for shyness: the series is about how we look at people. The shark teeth and glare as a mask. The cat that runs from her, borrowed from Azumanga Daioh (ANN).

**Traditions it echoes.** Azumanga Daioh's Sakaki, the gentle giantess (ANN), and the recent wave of tough-girl, soft-boy romcoms (ANN). For religious readers, 1 Samuel 16:7 and teachings on seeing the person rather than the face; for Buddhists, seeing past appearances to what is real.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Virtue (kindness, patience, fidelity) is simply right. Community is warm. The fan service sits awkwardly beside a message about not judging by bodies, a tension ANN's reviewer and some MyAnimeList users point out.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-3 (ch. 1-36): meeting and friendship. Volumes 4-7 (ch. 37-84): holidays, a hot spring, a failed date and the flood night, as feelings grow. Volumes 8-10 (ch. 85-120): confession, dating and summer. Volumes 11-12 (ch. 121-144): festivals, Yuu's exams and graduation, and an epilogue.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why do people run from Hitomi before they know her? Have you ever judged someone that fast?
2. What does Yuu do that others do not?
3. Kaoru jokes about Hitomi's body a lot. Is that friendly, or does it make Hitomi uncomfortable?
4. How does Hitomi change between the first and last volumes?
5. Why do you think the author made Hitomi tall and strong and Yuu small and gentle?
