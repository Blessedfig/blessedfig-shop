**Premise.** Naoto Watari, a high-school student whose parents died two years earlier, lives with his reclusive aunt Tamayo and has made himself parent to his little sister Suzushiro, a fourth-grader; classmates call him a siscon (Kodansha USA; Anime Feminist). He has a crush on Yukari Ishihara, a quiet member of the school's beautification committee. Then Satsuki Tachibana, the childhood friend who six years ago wrecked his family's vegetable garden and vanished, transfers to his school, kisses him without warning and follows him everywhere (ch. 1). The story moves from uneasy comedy to drama: Naoto dates Ishihara (v4-7) under her protective mother's rules, follows Satsuki to the country inn of the family that adopted her and learns of her neglectful birth mother (v8; anime ep. 13-14, 22), and loses Ishihara through her insecurity. Makina, a former track star from his middle school, also falls for him. In the end Naoto confesses to Satsuki, plans to become a chef, and the two spend a summer living together while choosing to wait for sex until after graduation; at the last, he asks her to live with him after graduation with marriage as the goal (anime ep. 23-26; Apple Books vol. 16).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can someone who has lost everything risk loving again? Naoto has made his little sister his whole life, partly from love and partly as a shield (ep. 2). Satsuki's return forces him to face the friend who hurt him and the feelings he buried.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, if he tells the truth and waits for the other person to be ready. Naoto learns that Satsuki wrecked the garden because she was losing him at the end of a summer (ep. 22), forgives her, says plainly that he loves her (ep. 23) and accepts her fears about motherhood (ep. 25). Love is a choice made slowly, not a rescue.

**Characters as arguments.** Naoto argues for duty, then learns duty can hide fear. Satsuki argues for desire, and is revealed as a neglected child afraid of being left. Ishihara shows how insecurity can destroy what it wants to keep. Makina shows that talent without effort collapses. Mio and Tamayo argue for adult caution and quiet sacrifice.

**Symbols and motifs.** The garden: wrecked, rebuilt, tended together. The censored word in the title. The festival kiss 'curse.' The watch Naoto gives Satsuki at the end (ep. 26), a promise of time.

**Traditions it echoes.** The childhood-friend romance and love triangle of Japanese romantic comedy, turned toward drama; the yandere type, later softened. Christian readers may hear the Song of Songs' counsel not to awaken love before its time; Buddhist readers, the letting go of a grudge.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarian family duty is honored but shown to need balance with honesty. Expressive individualism is affirmed in careers. Hedonism is refused, and traditional marriage becomes the goal, though the couple live together first.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-3 (ch. 1-17): Satsuki's return, stalking comedy and dark undertones. Volumes 4-7 (ch. 18-41): Naoto and Ishihara's relationship, Makina, Mio's rules. Volume 8 (ch. 42-46): Satsuki's hometown and adoption. Volumes 9-14: breakups, the festival, the old house, the school trip. Volumes 15-16: confession, a summer together, the semi-proposal.
