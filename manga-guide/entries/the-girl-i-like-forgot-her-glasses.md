**Premise.** Shy middle schooler Kaede Komura has a crush on his deskmate, Ai Mie, from the first day of school (Wikipedia; Mangapedia: a first-year middle school class). Ai is badly nearsighted and keeps forgetting or breaking her glasses; without them she squints fiercely and leans in close to see, which leaves Komura flustered. He helps her through each day, sharing textbooks and guiding her, and comes along when she buys new glasses (Wikipedia; Mangapedia). His classmate Ren Azuma, who knows of his crush, quietly plays wingman; Komura is jealous of him anyway. They first met at an ice cream shop when both were 8, and an elementary-school classmate, Ise, used to steal her glasses (Wikipedia). Valentine's Day misunderstandings (vols. 1-2), a summer of near-dates (a bathhouse run-in, a video-call "sleepover," a summer festival with Ai's father, vol. 8) and talk of different high schools (vol. 8) lead to the final volume, where Ai no longer forgets her glasses, the two have become an official couple, and they face middle school graduation and separate high schools (vol. 12 blurb; The Outerhaven).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What does it mean to really see someone? Ai cannot see without her glasses, and Komura can hardly look at her without blushing. The series asks how two shy young people come to know each other.

**The answer the story gives.** By paying attention and helping, day after day. Komura's love shows first as service: sharing a textbook, guiding her down a hall, choosing glasses with her. Ai notices that he is kind like her father (Wikipedia), and by the end she no longer forgets her glasses: she wants to see him (vol. 12 blurb).

**Characters as arguments.** Komura argues that kindness comes before confession. Ai argues for honesty and trust. Ren shows friendship that asks nothing back. The parents show what a welcoming home looks like (vol. 8).

**Symbols and motifs.** Glasses as attention: forgetting them and remembering them; Ai leaning close to see; the aquarium she loves; the ice-cream shop where they first met at 8 (Wikipedia).

**Traditions it echoes.** The Japanese school-deskmate romance (like Teasing Master Takagi-san) and the iyashikei mood; for many faith traditions, the virtue of small, daily kindness.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Virtue ethics in its simplest form: good habits of kindness make a good person and a good relationship.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-6 (the anime) are episodic school days; vols. 7-9 bring summer and growing closeness; vols. 10-12 bring the confession or official relationship (exact volume not confirmed) and graduation.
