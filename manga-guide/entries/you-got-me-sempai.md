**Premise.** High school first-year Serina Koshino finds love-song lyrics scratched into her desk by Asuma Mizukawa, a second-year who seems cold but is only awkward. She asks him out, and the series follows the couple through their relationship rather than the chase: a school festival and a popular upperclassman, Higuchi, who takes an interest in Serina (vol. 2); a winter trip and a girl from Mizukawa's past (vol. 3); Valentine's and White Day (vol. 4); Serina's second year and sports day (vol. 5); their first anniversary, when Mizukawa insists on meeting her family before their relationship becomes sexual (vol. 6); his college entrance exams, Higuchi's confession and a planned pause in dating until a promised Christmas (vols. 7-9); and Mizukawa's graduation (vol. 10) (Kodansha Japan and Kodansha USA volume descriptions).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What makes a relationship healthy? The series is not about getting together but about staying together: misunderstandings, jealousy, rivals, families and the pressure of exams (ANN; Kodansha descriptions).

**The answer the story gives.** Talk honestly, apologize, respect each other's pace and keep your promises. Reviewers praised Serina as communicative and Mizukawa as quick to make amends and to let her decide how close they get (ANN). The pair pause dating during his exams on a promise to reunite at Christmas and keep it (vols. 9-10).

**Characters as arguments.** Serina argues for saying what you feel. Mizukawa argues that an awkward, quiet person can be deeply caring. Higuchi, a popular boy who pursues Serina, tests whether charm can break a steady bond; it cannot (vols. 2, 5, 8).

**Symbols and motifs.** The love-song lyrics on the desk (vol. 1), the anniversaries the couple counts, the school calendar of festivals and holidays, and the promised Christmas as a goal to wait for.

**Traditions it echoes.** Shojo school romance and its milestones (first date, first kiss, first time). The emphasis on communication and consent matches modern relationship advice. For many religious readers, the patience and faithfulness echo their own teaching, while the sexual relationship outside marriage does not.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Relationship virtues are affirmed; individual choice about intimacy is affirmed without debate; families are included rather than opposed. No worldview is argued against.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-3: first months as a couple, the festival and the winter trip. Vols. 4-5: holidays and Serina's second year. Vols. 6-7: the anniversary, families and the couple's first time. Vols. 8-10: exams, Higuchi's confession, the pause, Christmas and graduation (Kodansha descriptions).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Serina asks Mizukawa out first. What do you think about that?
2. How do Serina and Mizukawa handle disagreements? What could other couples learn from them?
3. Mizukawa wants to meet Serina's family before their relationship goes further. Why might that matter?
4. The story treats the couple's decision to have sex as a happy step. What does our family believe about that, and why?
5. They take a break so he can study. Was that a good idea?
