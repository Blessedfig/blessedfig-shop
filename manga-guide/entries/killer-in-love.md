**Premise.** Ryuto Suzuki was a lonely otaku in high school and tries to remake himself at university, smiling through his new friends' teasing about his virginity. At a drinking party he meets Kokoa Yoshizaki, a beautiful classmate who also hates such parties, and agrees to pose as her boyfriend to keep off a stalker. At a barbecue in Okutama the stalker stabs a jealous classmate, Arata, and tries to take Kokoa; struggling with him, Ryuto stabs him by accident. Later the stalker breaks into Ryuto's room to force a murder-suicide with Kokoa, and Ryuto kills him with a frying pan. Ryuto tries to kill himself; Kokoa stops him and asks him to live with her, and he commits to a life of hiding what he did (volume 1; Mangapedia). The first chapter opens with him dismembering a body in a bathtub. More of Kokoa's obsessed exes and stalkers appear, a separate string of attacks on students is linked to her, and Ryuto kills again and again to protect her while the two flee (honcierge; MyAnimeList reviews). When he finally leaves her he is quickly found by the police (MyAnimeList review). The last two chapters turn to Kokoa's past and loneliness; she walks into the sea, then turns back and walks along the shore, implied to start a new life alone (AniList review 20714; MyAnimeList review).

## Life message

**A love that makes one person your whole world, so that you will kill for her, destroys you and everyone near you; the only way out the story offers is learning to stand on your own.**

| Pragmatic | Each killing "solves" a problem and creates a bigger one; covering up (burying a body, spending the dead man's money) makes escape impossible. Practical reasoning loses to obsession. |
| Religious | No religion in the story. Faith readers will weigh murder made sympathetic, suicide attempts and despair against a story whose outcome condemns the path and ends with a choice to live. |
| Socio-cultural | College drinking culture, mockery of a "virgin" loner, the pressure to look outgoing, stalking and the loneliness of a woman valued only for her looks; one reader reads it as a warning about toxic family, friendship and drinking cultures (MyAnimeList review). |

Outlook: Bleak — nearly everyone around Ryuto is hurt or killed, he is caught, and Kokoa is left alone; the last pages offer only a thin hope as she chooses to live (AniList review 20714).
Hope source: S — the story's only way out is being true to yourself: Kokoa stops relying on men for safety and walks away from the sea to start again alone (AniList review 20714); love between the two (L) is shown as the cause of the ruin, not the hope.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What would you do for someone you love? Ryuto's answer is anything. The series asks what happens when a lonely person makes another person the whole meaning of his life.

**The answer the story gives.** It destroys you, and her. Each killing begins as protection and ends as possession; friends who could help are pushed away; the police find him the moment he is alone (MyAnimeList review). The final chapters suggest the truer love is learning to live without leaning on someone (AniList review 20714).

**Characters as arguments.** Ryuto argues that love justifies anything. Kokoa, at first, argues that being protected is being loved; by the end she sees the cost. The stalkers are the mirror: men who also claim to love her. Ichihara and Riko argue for honesty and help, and lose.

**Symbols and motifs.** The bathtub where a body is cut apart (ch. 1); the fake-boyfriend role that becomes real; the frying pan, an everyday object turned weapon; the theme park paid for with a dead man's money; the sea at the end.

**Traditions it echoes.** The yandere romance and the doomed outlaw couple (Bonnie and Clyde). Readers of Proverbs, of 1 Corinthians 13 or of the Buddhist teaching on attachment will find the opposite of the love those texts describe.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Nihilism dominates the middle; existential choice frames the start and the end; a feminist reading appears only in the last chapters. No religious frame is present.

**Shifts across the series.** Volume 1: the party, the first killing, the suicide attempt, hiding the body. Volumes 2-4: flight, more stalkers and killings, friends lost. Volume 5: capture and Kokoa's story.

**Discussion questions.**
1. After the first stalker died, what else could Ryuto have done?
2. Friends warned Ryuto. Why didn't he listen?
3. Is protecting someone the same as loving them?
4. What does Kokoa learn at the end?
5. Why do stories make killers sympathetic, and what does that do to readers?
