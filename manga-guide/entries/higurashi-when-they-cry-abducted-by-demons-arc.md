**Premise.** June 1983. Keiichi Maebara, a city boy of middle-school age (fan wiki), has moved with his parents to the mountain village of Hinamizawa, where the whole school is one classroom. He joins an after-school games club with four girls: kind Rena Ryugu, club leader Mion Sonozaki (heir of the village's most powerful family), and the younger Satoko Hojo and Rika Furude, whose family keeps the shrine of the village god, Oyashiro-sama. Losers face "punishment games" such as marker on the face (ch. 1). A photographer, Tomitake, hints at a past dismemberment murder at an abandoned dam site, and Rena's flat denial unsettles Keiichi (ch. 1). After the Watanagashi festival (ch. 2), Tomitake is found dead, having torn open his own throat, and the nurse with him is missing. Detective Ooishi tells Keiichi about four years of festival-time deaths and disappearances that villagers call Oyashiro-sama's curse, and hints that his friends' families may be behind them (vol. 1; fan wiki). His friends turn evasive; Rena coldly calls him a liar and is found to have stood outside his door listening to his call with Ooishi (vol. 1; Yen Press blurb). In vol. 2 he finds a needle in a rice cake (ANN's review says razor blades), learns that Rena once smashed every window at her old school, and starts carrying a bat. When Rena and Mion come to his house and Mion produces what she calls a syringe, Keiichi blacks out and wakes to find he has beaten them both to death (ch. 6-7; fan wiki). He leaves a written message for whoever finds it, phones Ooishi to say the curse is real while clawing at his own throat, and dies in hospital (final chapter; fan wiki; ANN). The arc gives no answer; later arcs reveal that a disease causing paranoia drove these events (Wikipedia).

## Life message

**Fear and suspicion, fed by secrets and silence, can turn a boy against the friends he loves; when people stop trusting and talking to each other, tragedy follows.**

## Creator and publisher context

- Ryukishi07 (original story) created Higurashi as a dōjin sound novel for his circle 07th Expansion (first arc released at Comiket, August 2002). Over 500,000 copies of the games had sold by 2007 (Wikipedia).
- On-record intent (Yen Plus interview, December 2008, via Wikipedia): the story grew from an unpublished theater script, Hinamizawa Bus Stop; he wanted the contrast between a fun, ordinary life and something terrifying; Seishi Yokomizo's mysteries were a major influence; the truth was designed to come out through several overlapping stories.
- Vol. 2 afterword (fan-wiki transcription): Ryukishi07 calls this arc the simplest picture of the themes of the whole series and says one important key is hidden in it among many fake keys. Vol. 1 afterword: he notes the arc's contrast between light and dark sides of the village.
- Karin Suzuragi (art), from Aichi Prefecture, mainly works on video-game anthology and adaptation manga for Square Enix; she later drew the Atonement, Festival Accompanying and Dice Killing arcs (Wikipedia). Her afterword thanks Ryukishi07 for checking every rough draft.
- Publisher: Square Enix (Gangan Powered). Yen Press (Hachette Book Group; from general knowledge) publishes it in English, rated OT.
- Context: in September 2007, after a 16-year-old girl killed her father with an axe, Japanese media linked the crime to violent anime including Higurashi, and several stations canceled broadcasts of episodes of the second anime season (Wikipedia). This concerned the anime, not the manga's creators.
- No statements by the creators on religion or politics were found. **Status: Needs more info on the creators.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What happens when you stop trusting the people closest to you? Keiichi has never been happier than in his first weeks in Hinamizawa, playing games after school with new friends. One hint of a hidden murder, and one secret talk with a detective, are enough to turn every kindness into a threat.

**The answer the story gives.** In this arc, the answer is a warning by tragedy. Keiichi keeps his fears to himself, trusts a stranger over his friends, arms himself, and kills the people he loved. Ryukishi07 says this arc shows the themes of the whole series most simply (vol. 2 afterword); later arcs make the lesson explicit by showing what changes when the friends confide in each other (from general knowledge; to verify).

**Characters as arguments.** Keiichi argues for self-protection and loses everything. Rena, warm and odd, becomes terrifying through his eyes; the reader cannot tell whether she is threat or victim. Ooishi argues for rational suspicion of the village, and his reasonable-sounding warnings feed the fear. Mion stands for the village's old families and their secrets. Tomitake, the friendly outsider, is the first to die.

**Symbols and motifs.** The cicadas' cry of the title; the baseball bat (an object of play turned weapon); the marker pen of the punishment games and the "syringe"; the rice cake with a needle; the telephone, which carries the detective's suspicions and Keiichi's last call; the written message left for whoever finds it. Throats torn open by the victims' own hands recur.

**Traditions it echoes.** Japanese village horror and Yokomizo-style mysteries (Wikipedia); the unreliable narrator; folklore of people spirited away by demons. Religious readers may hear echoes of warnings against rash judgment and suspicion (Catholic, Jewish and Muslim teaching all name it), and Buddhist readers the teaching that the mind creates its own suffering.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Pagan/occult lore supplies the dread but is never confirmed; tradition and community are shown as closed and menacing; the postmodern trick is that the narrator himself cannot be trusted. Secular reason (the detective, the reader's own deduction) is invited but not rewarded within this arc.

**Shifts across the series.** Volume 1 (ch. 1-3) is mostly cheerful school comedy and the festival, turning at Tomitake's death; volume 2 (ch. 4-7) is pure dread and ends in killing. The later Higurashi arcs (separate entries) retell the same summer with different outcomes and finally explain it.

**Discussion questions.**
1. When did Keiichi first start keeping secrets from his friends? What might have happened if he had told them?
2. Ooishi was a police detective. Why did Keiichi trust him more than his friends? Was that wise?
3. How does the artist make cute characters frightening? What does that say about how fear changes what we see?
4. Rena says everyone has secrets. Is it wrong to have secrets? When do they become dangerous?
5. Keiichi's last message asks the reader to find the truth. What do you think really happened?
