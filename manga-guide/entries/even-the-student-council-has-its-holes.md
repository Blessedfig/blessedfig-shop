**Premise.** Ume Mizunoe, a first-year at the private Fujinari (Fujinasu) Academy who is strong in literature and hopeless at math and science, joins the student council as secretary so that his grades will not make him repeat the year. The council is a set of eccentrics: president Hisako Kotobuki (third year), a capable athlete who is secretly obsessed with sex and gets flustered by it; Komaro Michinoku (first year), tiny, accident-prone and orphaned, who lives next door to the teacher who took her in; treasurer Arisu Terui (second year), polite on the surface and a former junior-high delinquent who beats Tan when he goes too far; and public-relations officer Tan Otori, a feminine-looking third-year middle-school boy who teases boys, especially Ume, with suggestive remarks. Their adviser, homeroom teacher Satomi Hiratsuka, is lazy, careless and a smoker, but cares for her students. Most chapters are four-panel gags about council work (club budgets, patrols, pool cleaning, a hot-spring trip), with a slow mutual crush between Ume and Hisako that never quite gets confessed, and occasional serious backstories (Komaro's loneliness after her parents' deaths, Tan's absent parents) (English and Japanese Wikipedia; Seven Seas blurb).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Where does a person who does not fit in find a place? Ume joins the student council only to save his grades, and finds that everyone there is stranger than he is.

**The answer the story gives.** In a group that accepts you as you are. Komaro, who ate lunch alone after her parents died, becomes cheerful because of the council and Ume (anime ep. 6); Tan, whose mother is rarely home, spends his photo money on gloves for everyone (ep. 8). The answer is warm, but it is delivered almost entirely through sexual gags.

**Characters as arguments.** Ume is the voice of common sense who learns to enjoy chaos. Hisako argues that a perfect public face can hide ordinary, embarrassing desires. Arisu shows politeness and force side by side. Tan is provocation itself, and the story both indulges and punishes him (Arisu's fists). Satomi argues that a careless adult can still be a good guardian.

**Symbols and motifs.** The "holes" of the title: a sexual pun and a confession that everyone has flaws. The suggestion box, the council room on a cold day with everyone huddled together (ep. 8), and the rice balls Komaro sells (ep. 7).

**Traditions it echoes.** Japanese student-council gag manga (Seitokai Yakuindomo) and ecchi school comedy, with the slow, never-confessed romance of the love-comedy genre.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarianism and expressive individualism are celebrated without argument. Hedonism appears as comedy without consequence. No religious or political view is engaged.

**Shifts across the series.** Sources do not describe arcs; the series stays a chapter-by-chapter gag comedy, with occasional serious backstories (Japanese Wikipedia, citing Da Vinci) and a growing cast of club members.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why does Ume stay on the council after his grades are safe?
2. Komaro changes a lot after meeting the council. What did they do for her?
