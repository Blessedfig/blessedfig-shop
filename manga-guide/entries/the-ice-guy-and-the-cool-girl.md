**Premise.** In a modern Japan where some people descend from spirits of folklore, Himuro, a new office worker, is descended from the yuki-onna (snow woman): when his feelings run high it snows, things freeze, and in heat he melts into a child-sized version of himself. On his first day his fear freezes his feet to the pavement, and a calm coworker, Fuyutsuki, stays with him over tea until the ice melts; he falls for her at once (ch. 1). Their office includes Komori (descended from a fox spirit), the ordinary Saejima, Himuro's childhood friend Katori (a phoenix descendant) and Katori's manager Otonashi, a kindly chief who resembles the Great Buddha, and a company president descended from a nurarihyon (Wikipedia, episode summaries; fan wiki). Short episodes follow company trips, dates that one of them does not realize are dates, holidays, a shrine visit and a cat. When Fuyutsuki is posted overseas, Himuro confesses before she leaves (vol. 9, Square Enix); the two are together by volume 10, where Himuro must handle jealousy of Sakuba, a returning coworker descended from a succubus; in volume 11 they meet each other's families, including Himuro's sick grandmother (Square Enix product pages).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** How do two reserved people let each other know they care? Himuro's body gives him away (it snows when he is happy), while Fuyutsuki's face gives nothing away. The series asks whether kindness can speak when words cannot.

**The answer the story gives.** Small acts add up: tea on a cold morning, a surf drum to practice with, cat photos, a kitten blanket instead of earrings that felt too personal. Eventually words are needed, and Himuro confesses before Fuyutsuki goes abroad (vol. 9).

**Characters as arguments.** Himuro argues that feelings cannot be hidden forever. Fuyutsuki argues that calm is not coldness. Katori, who confesses childishly and often, learns that a serious confession is a different thing (anime ep. 5). Komori and Saejima show love growing from noticing what someone likes.

**Symbols and motifs.** Snow and ice as emotion; melting as vulnerability (Himuro shrinks when overheated); matching mugs and plates; cats; the seasons passing through a year of office life.

**Traditions it echoes.** The yuki-onna legend, usually a tale of a dangerous snow woman, turned gentle; the office romantic comedy (Anime Feminist compares Wotakoi); iyashikei healing stories.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Community and kindness are affirmed throughout. Tradition appears as warm custom (shrines, families). There is no argument against any view.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-5 (ch. 1-40; the anime): meeting, trips and holidays. Volumes 6-8: deeper friendship; the overseas posting. Volume 9: confession. Volumes 10-12: couplehood, jealousy, meeting families.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Himuro's feelings show up as snow. How do you show what you feel?
2. Fuyutsuki changed her gift because earrings felt too personal. How do you choose a gift for a friend?
3. Why did Himuro wait so long to confess? What helped him finally do it?
4. How do the coworkers help each other?
5. Fuyutsuki took a job overseas she had hoped for. How should couples handle each other's dreams?
