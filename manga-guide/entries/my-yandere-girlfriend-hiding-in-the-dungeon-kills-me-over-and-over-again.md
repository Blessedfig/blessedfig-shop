**Premise.** Kiska is a farmer persecuted in his village because his hair color matches that of a clan that once betrayed humanity. When the village chief's son kills the woman Kiska loves, Kiska is framed for the crime and sent through a teleport circle to the deepest level of the Katarph Dungeon, a death sentence for someone with no skills (web novel synopsis; MangaUpdates). About to be killed by monsters, he hears a mysterious voice grant him the skill "Save & Reset": each time he dies, time rewinds to a save point. The dungeon's strongest inhabitants are beautiful, dangerous women who kill him on sight again and again, among them Ageha, a possessive sealed girl "hero," and Judit (Yudeeto), a true-ancestor vampire, plus a sword spirit (Yen Press light-novel blurb via MangaUpdates; Kadokawa). Using hundreds of deaths to learn, Kiska wins their trust and affection; manga vol. 3 covers his apprenticeship to the vampire (Kadokawa). The web novel's chapter titles show him returning for revenge on his village, with torture and a "gruesome death" (ch. 46-49, web novel); the manga's coverage of that arc is not confirmed.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** If you could not die for good, could you endure anything? And what would you become? Kiska is given endless retries, but each one costs him a death.

**The answer the story gives.** From the summaries: perseverance turns a powerless man into a strong one, and the strength is used for love and for revenge. The web novel's tags promise a hero who becomes the strongest and makes his tormentors pay.

**Characters as arguments.** Kiska argues for not giving up and for learning from every failure. The women argue for a love that is possessive to the point of killing. The villagers are the case against prejudice and false witness, and their punishment is the genre's reward.

**Symbols and motifs.** The save point: a second chance that is also a curse. The dungeon depths as a pit of despair. The hair color that marks Kiska as an outcast.

**Traditions it echoes.** Time-loop stories (Re:Zero, Edge of Tomorrow; from general knowledge), the Count of Monte Cristo's wrongly imprisoned avenger, and Japanese web fiction's exile-and-revenge plots. Christian and Buddhist readers will set it beside teaching that revenge does not heal.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Might makes right and Pragmatism appear to win. Whether the story questions revenge or obsession is not known from sources opened.

**Shifts across the series.** Web novel arcs: the first deaths and skills (ch. 1-15), the vampire apprenticeship and the revenge on the village (ch. 16-50), alliances with Ageha and Judith, a kingdom at war and a decisive battle (ch. 51-99). Manga vol. 3 covers the apprenticeship (Kadokawa); later manga volumes are unconfirmed.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Kiska can retry after every death. Would that make you braver, or more afraid? Why?
2. The women who love Kiska also kill him. Is that love? What does the title want us to think?
3. Kiska's village hated him for his hair color. Where do you see people judged like that?
4. If someone framed you for a crime, what would justice look like? Is revenge the same thing?
5. What does Kiska learn from failing hundreds of times?
