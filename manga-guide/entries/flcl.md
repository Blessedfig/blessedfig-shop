**Premise.** Naota Nandaba is a sixth grader (12) in the factory town of Mabase, living above the family bakery with his father Kamon and grandfather. His older brother has gone to America to play baseball and left behind his girlfriend Mamimi, a lonely, bullied high-school girl (17 in the AniList and MangaDex synopses) who makes ambiguous, suggestive advances to Naota while still pining for his brother. Then Haruko, a self-described 19-year-old alien, runs Naota over with her Vespa and hits him on the head with her bass guitar; a horn grows from his forehead, robots begin to emerge from it, and Haruko moves in as the family maid. The manga keeps this setup but becomes a different, darker story than the anime: Naota is more aggressive and more fragile, someone is trying to kill him, a step-grandmother and her past with his grandfather are added, Haruko is crueler, and Naota kills his own father over Haruko, where in the anime he only strikes a robot copy (Japanese Wikipedia; FLCL wiki; TV Tropes; MAL review). The story ends with an assault on the Medical Mechanica factory and a different ending from the anime (TV Tropes).

## Life message

**Growing up is a messy, painful collision with adults who use you and desires you do not understand; the story offers no clean lesson, only that you have to find your own way through it.**

## Creator and publisher context

- Hajime Ueda began as a dōjinshi artist known for a quirky style; besides FLCL he created Q-Ko-chan: The Earth Invader Girl (2003) and illustrated Jagdtiger (2009); he also designs costumes and sculpts, and has animated openings and endings for Shaft's Monogatari series and worked on Kazuya Tsurumaki's productions such as Diebuster (Wikipedia, Hajime Ueda).
- Original creator credit: Gainax. The anime's director, Kazuya Tsurumaki, said he tried to "break the rules" of anime and that comprehension should not be an important factor (Wikipedia, about the anime).
- Japanese Wikipedia notes the manga differs from both the anime and its own magazine run.
- Publishers: Kodansha (Magazine Z); English: Tokyopop (2003), Dark Horse (2012 omnibus, 14+). The rights to FLCL passed from Gainax to Production I.G in 2015 (Japanese Wikipedia).
- No statement by Ueda on the manga's themes or its sexual content was found. **Status: Needs more info on the creators.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What does growing up feel like from the inside? For Naota it feels like being run over, hit on the head and having strange things burst out of you while older people want things from you that you do not understand.

**The answer the story gives.** No clean answer. The anime ends with Naota accepting that he is still a child (TV Tropes). The manga goes darker: he kills his father over the woman he wants and is left to chase her on a Vespa he has to fix himself (FLCL wiki; TV Tropes).

**Characters as arguments.** Haruko argues for desire without responsibility; she uses Naota and leaves. Mamimi argues for clinging to a lost love and finding a substitute. Kamon argues for staying a child forever, and loses everything. Naota argues, mostly silently, that a child needs adults who do not use him.

**Symbols and motifs.** The horn on Naota's forehead and the things that emerge from it stand for puberty (TV Tropes). The iron-shaped factory flattens thought. The bass guitar, the baseball bat Naota never swings, the Vespa.

**Traditions it echoes.** Coming-of-age stories, Gainax's Evangelion-era style (a reviewer compares the plot to Evangelion, MAL), and surrealism. For faith readers, the story is a sharp picture of what the fifth commandment and the duty to protect children are for, by showing their absence.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Absurdism and individualism are the story's own stance. Hedonism is shown in Haruko without a verdict. Existential self-making is forced on a child too young for it.

**Shifts across the series.** Volume 1 (2000) follows the anime's setup with added menace (someone wants Naota dead). Volume 2 (2001) diverges: the father's death, Haruko's cruelty toward Mamimi, the factory assault and a different ending (Japanese Wikipedia; TV Tropes; MAL review).

**Discussion questions.**
1. What do the adults in FLCL want from Naota? Do any of them think about what he needs?
2. If Mamimi were a 17-year-old boy and Naota a 12-year-old girl, how would the story feel?
3. Why does Naota carry a bat he never swings?
4. What is Medical Mechanica trying to "iron out," and why is that dangerous?
5. The manga ends darker than the anime. Which ending tells the truth about growing up?
