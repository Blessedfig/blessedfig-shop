**Premise.** Leo Cornelia is the kingdom's strongest knight, the "Undefeatable Holy Blade." Her father put a sword in her hand from birth, her body is covered in battle scars, and she has never been treated as anything but a warrior. Then Foolie Dent, an easygoing and seemingly foolish young mage, confesses his love to her. His plain, steady kindness (praising her, healing her wounds, listening to her talk about her workouts) leaves her flustered, and she slowly comes to rely on him (Wikipedia; ANN; Anime Corner). The supporting cast includes Foolie's younger sister Anri, who is jealous of Leo; Helga "Berserk Blade" Wagner, the second-strongest knight, who admires Leo and keeps a succubus familiar; the dark-elf fortune teller Sariela; and Leo's junior Fleur, who attacks the pair and falls for Foolie (AniList characters). Later volumes add a potion that turns Foolie into a woman (v4), a grand tournament in which a shaman takes over Leo's body (v5-6), a warning from Foolie's old teacher that his magic is wearing out his body (v6), and a final campaign against the Demon Lord ordered by Leo's father (v7) (Kodansha USA synopses; AniList).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What happens to a person who has only ever been valued for what she can do? Leo is the kingdom's sword, praised for her victories and never for herself. Foolie's confession asks whether she can be loved for who she is.

**The answer the story gives.** Patient, honest kindness can reach a guarded heart, and strength need not be given up to receive tenderness. Leo stays the strongest knight; what changes is that she lets herself be cared for (Wikipedia; Anime Corner).

**Characters as arguments.** Foolie argues that love is shown in small, steady acts: healing a cut, listening, praising without flattery. Leo argues that duty and strength are what make a person worth something, and slowly loses that argument. Fleur and Helga argue for rivalry and pride and end as allies (v7). Anri argues for possessive family love and learns to share her brother.

**Symbols and motifs.** Leo's scars, which she hides and Foolie treats as part of her. Healing magic set beside the sword. The bonus chapters imagine other lives: newlyweds (v3), high school (v5), a wedding night (v7).

**Traditions it echoes.** The tough-woman-and-gentle-man romantic comedy (compare the Uzaki-chan art style Anime Corner mentions) and the chivalric romance turned around: here the knight is the one who must be wooed. ANN notes the "defrosting the ice queen" plot has been done many times.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Traditionalism is explored rather than argued: courtship and marriage are the happy end, but the woman keeps her sword. Feminism appears as admiration for strong women and is turned on the series itself by ANN for the art. Communitarianism wins in the final volume as the women knights fight together.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-3 (ch. 1-57) are almost pure episodic romance comedy. Volume 4 (ch. 58-75) brings Fleur and the gender-swap potion. Volumes 5-6 (ch. 76-112) add the tournament, Leo's possessed body and Foolie's failing magic. Volume 7 (ch. 113-131) turns to the Demon Lord campaign and the ending, which reviewers found rushed.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Leo was praised her whole life for fighting. Why does Foolie's kindness confuse her at first?
2. Does Leo have to become "softer" to be loved, or does Foolie love her as she already is?
3. What small things does Foolie do that show care? Which of them could you do for someone?
4. Fleur and Helga start as rivals. What changes them into friends?
5. Some readers say the drawings show the women in ways the story itself does not need. What do you think?
