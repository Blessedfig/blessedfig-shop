**Premise.** Momo "Maki" Makimura and Midori Oonishi dated through all three years at a girls' high school, until Midori ended it on graduation day, saying they were now too old to be dating girls and that she looked forward to having a boyfriend (ANN; Wikipedia). Ten years later Maki, a graduate student who has never stopped loving her, meets Midori again at an optometry clinic (ANN places Maki at the clinic, Anime Feminist places Midori there; to verify). Midori seems to have the life she wanted: a fiancé, Tazune (spelled Tazane by ANN; Okazu gives the surname Tonoike), a nice apartment and a baby on the way. She kisses Maki and also invites her to the wedding. The story then shows the relationship from inside: Tazune belittles Midori, gaslights her, mocks her body, became engaged only because he refused a condom and she got pregnant, and at least once hits her (ANN; Anime UK News; Anime Feminist). Maki, pressed by her own mother and co-workers to marry, takes Midori to stay with Komari, a schoolgirl she knows online, on Shōdoshima island (vol. 2; Okazu; Kodansha). Midori asks Maki to run away with her; they ride a train line to its remote end, then return to Tokyo, where Midori refuses to marry Tazune or to give up who she is (vol. 3; Anime UK News; Anime Feminist). The main story ends with the two women trying on wedding dresses, and an extra chapter follows Midori's child, Kon (Anime Feminist).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Is a first love something to outgrow, or the truest thing about you? And what does it take to leave a life that looks right from outside?

**The answer the story gives.** Midori believed the script: girls' romances are a phase, and a man, a ring and a baby are the real life. The story shows that script hiding abuse and loneliness. With Maki's patient love and Komari's honesty, Midori chooses herself and her child (Anime Feminist; Okazu).

**Characters as arguments.** Maki argues for faithfulness: ten years of the same love. Midori argues, at first, for conformity, then for courage. Tazune argues that love is ownership; his own point-of-view chapter and backstory suggest how such men are made, without excusing him (ANN). Komari, a young person living honestly, is the argument that it can be done (Okazu).

**Symbols and motifs.** Running away (kakeochi, elopement) and the end of a train line: escape that must turn into return (Kodansha vol. 3). The island and the sea. Wedding dresses at the end.

**Traditions it echoes.** The "class S" girls' school romance and its usual ending, which the series rewrites (ANN); Shunji Iwai's A Bride for Rip Van Winkle (Manga-Passion).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism, feminism and progressivism are affirmed; traditionalism is the pressure the women escape. No religious lens appears.

**Shifts across the series.** Volume 1 (ch. 1-6): reunion, the kiss, the wedding invitation and the abuse revealed, ending in an act Okazu calls indefensible. Volume 2: the Shōdoshima trip, Komari's story, and Midori's plea to run away. Volume 3: the run, the return to Tokyo, the break with Tazune and the epilogues.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Midori called her high-school romance "fooling around." Why do people sometimes dismiss their own feelings?
2. What were the warning signs in Tazune's behavior, and what should a friend do on seeing them?
3. Was running away the right choice? What did it solve, and what did it not?
4. The story shows Tazune's past. Does knowing why someone is cruel change what we owe them?
5. How do different families in your community think about the ending, and why?
