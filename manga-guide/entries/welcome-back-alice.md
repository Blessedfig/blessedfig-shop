**Premise.** Yōhei Kamekawa, a shy boy, Kei Murota and Yui Mitani have been friends since kindergarten. In their first year of middle school Kei talks to Yōhei about masturbation, and Yōhei sees Yui confess to Kei and kiss him; soon after, Kei's family moves to Hokkaido (Japanese Wikipedia; Anime UK News, vol. 1). Three years later, at the start of high school, Kei returns alone, with long blond hair and a girls' uniform, saying he is done being a boy but does not want to be a girl. Kei pushes Yōhei into escalating sexual contact, and Yōhei is torn between Kei and Yui (vol. 1-2). Yui agrees to date Yōhei if he cuts off Kei (ch. 11), then pushes him into sex he regrets (vol. 4-6), while Kei withdraws and befriends Ren, an art-club student who also feels neither male nor female (vol. 4-5). Desperate to become "clean," Yōhei injures his own genitals and is hospitalized (vol. 6). In the last volume Yōhei asks Kei to leave their old lives behind together, Yui says a tearful goodbye, and Yōhei and Kei become a couple (ch. 37; Anime UK News, vol. 7; TV Tropes).

## Creator and publisher context

- Shūzō Oshimi (story and art), born March 19, 1981, in Gunma; debuted with Superfly (Monthly Shōnen Magazine); Tetsuya Chiba Award 2001. His works often draw on his own adolescence and explore puberty, perversion and gender; he has said he believes perversion is part of every person and wants readers to think about the stigma around it (Wikipedia).
- On-record intent: Oshimi says the series is about deconstructing male sexuality and grew out of his own discomfort with manhood after reading Masahiro Morioka's Confessions of a Frigid Man (Wikipedia). In the vol. 1 afterword he wrote that he hoped to "examine, confront, and deconstruct one's sex drive and masculinity," and that he had once thought he would be better off as a girl but saw that this ignored the struggles women face (Anime UK News, vol. 1). In the vol. 7 afterword he said he meant to write a romantic comedy that undermined itself from inside, but found the form too confining (Anime UK News, vol. 7, paraphrased).
- Publisher: Kodansha (Bessatsu Shōnen Magazine, a shōnen magazine). Kodansha USA publishes the English edition, rated 18+.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What if the problem is not whom you love but desire itself, and the roles of "man" and "woman" that come with it? Oshimi says he wrote the series to confront the male sex drive and masculinity from his own discomfort with being male (afterword; Wikipedia).

**The answer the story gives.** Ordinary romance fails everyone: Yui's dating becomes control, Yōhei's sex becomes shame, and Yōhei tries to cut desire out of his body (vol. 6). The story's resolution is to leave the roles behind: Kei has already stepped down from being a man, Yōhei follows, and the two begin again together (vol. 7). Reviewers found the answer abstract and unresolved, especially for Yui (Anime UK News).

**Characters as arguments.** Kei argues that gender is a costume one can take off, but also uses sex to pull Yōhei close, which Silverman reads as a harmful stereotype and Lemus as liberation. Yui argues for romance by the rules and for womanhood as fixed, and becomes controlling and cruel (vol. 3-6). Yōhei is the battleground: he wants Yui, responds to Kei, and hates his own wanting. Ren, who feels neither male nor female, offers the quietest version of the story's ideal: friendship without possession (vol. 4-5).

**Symbols and motifs.** Alice: Kei's blond hair and the fall into a world where the rules no longer hold (TV Tropes). The school uniform as a gender marker. The old elementary school, where Yōhei and Kei remember being children before puberty (vol. 5). Cutting: Yōhei's knife (vol. 6) and Yōhei cutting Kei's hair (ch. 37). Ren's nude portrait of Kei (vol. 5).

**Traditions it echoes.** Oshimi's own The Flowers of Evil and Inside Mari (ANN; Anime UK News); Masahiro Morioka's Confessions of a Frigid Man (Wikipedia); the old ascetic idea, found in several religions, that desire must be mastered or cut away, which the story literalizes and questions (from general knowledge; to verify).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism is affirmed: identity is chosen. Hedonism is critiqued: sex brings no happiness. Postmodern and feminist ideas about gender as learned run underneath. No religious view appears, so faith readers will bring their own teaching on the body to it.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-2 (about ch. 1-11): Kei's return and escalating advances; Yōhei and Yui begin dating (ch. 11). Vols. 3-4: the relationship sours, Kei and Yōhei grow close again, Ren appears, Yōhei and Yui sleep together. Vols. 5-6: Kei steps back, Yui grows abusive, Yōhei breaks and injures himself. Vol. 7 (about ch. 35-40): hospital, goodbyes and a new start for Yōhei and Kei (Anime UK News).
