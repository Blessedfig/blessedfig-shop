**Premise.** Kiyoshi Adachi, a shy, self-doubting salaryman, turns thirty without ever having had sex and becomes a "wizard": he can hear the thoughts of anyone he touches. He discovers that Yuichi Kurosawa, the handsome star of his office, is in love with him and often fantasizes about him. Hearing how sincerely Kurosawa values him, Adachi slowly returns his feelings. They become a couple and go on a disastrous first date (v4); Adachi confesses his power (v6) and loses it after the two sleep together for the first time (v6-7; around ch. 35); they get engaged during Adachi's transfer to Nagasaki (v7-8), come out to their families (v8-9) and hold a wedding (v10). Later volumes follow married life, work pressure and a client who pursues Adachi (v11-15), and the company president's discovery of the relationship (v16). A second couple, Adachi's friend Tsuge, a romance novelist who is also a "wizard," and Minato, a younger delivery worker and dancer, runs alongside (Wikipedia; Square Enix synopses; Anime UK News).

## Creator and publisher context

- Yuu Toyota (story and art) posted the first 4-page comic on Twitter and Pixiv in January 2018; an editor offered a deal within an hour (Wikipedia). She had imagined the story seven or eight years earlier from the legend that staying a virgin past thirty gives magic powers.
- Stated intent (Wikipedia): the theme is that even with magic powers, self-confidence is needed to move forward. Because readers included men and elementary school students, she added a note that "adult" developments might come later, with no plans to draw anything explicit. She has not specified a "top" or "bottom" role for the leads.
- For the 2020 drama she asked the production not to use derogatory language about the boys' love genre or about virgins (Wikipedia).
- In 2022 she donated part of the film rights fee to Marriage For All Japan, saying she hoped society could become a place where people can love each other in whatever form they wish (ANN).
- Publisher: Square Enix (Gangan Pixiv); English by Square Enix Manga & Books, rated Mature. No controversies were found.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What would it be like to know exactly what someone thinks of you? Adachi's power lets him hear the love he cannot believe he deserves; the series asks whether knowing is enough, or whether trust must be built in the open.

**The answer the story gives.** Honesty and courage, not magic, make love last. Adachi must tell Kurosawa his secret (v6), and he loses the power the morning after their first night together (v7), so the marriage that follows rests on ordinary trust. Toyota states the theme as needing self-confidence even when you have magic (Wikipedia).

**Characters as arguments.** Adachi argues that people who think little of themselves can grow. Kurosawa argues that even the "perfect" person is insecure inside, and learns to ask for help (v12). Tsuge, the romance novelist with no romance, argues that writing about love is not living it (v9-13). Tachibana shows love pursued without respect for commitments (v14-15).

**Symbols and motifs.** Touch as the doorway to the mind; letters (Adachi's handwritten letter, v6) as a way to speak sincerely without the power; rings and a white-suit wedding (v10); Udon the rescued cat who brings Tsuge and Minato together.

**Traditions it echoes.** The office romantic comedy, the boys' love genre (here adult and gentle), and the "what did you eat yesterday" style of domestic manga, which vol. 11 homages (Anime UK News). Its mind-reading premise echoes the old question of whether we would want to be fully known.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism, progressivism and rights-based liberalism are affirmed openly by vol. 10. Communitarian support from friends and coworkers is warmly shown. Religious frames appear only as the Nagasaki church wedding Adachi admires.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-5: comic courtship and Adachi's secret. Volumes 6-10: honesty, first night, engagement, coming out and wedding; the anime ends at ch. 35 (v7) and the drama at the wedding arc. Volumes 11-17: married life, Tsuge and Minato's story, workplace threats and the final arc.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Adachi could hear Kurosawa's thoughts before Kurosawa chose to share them. Was that fair? Why did telling the truth matter so much?
2. Why does Adachi think he does not deserve to be loved? What helps him change?
3. How do the characters handle a coworker or client who will not take no for an answer (v14-15)?
4. Different families and faiths think differently about same-sex marriage. What does our family believe, and how do we treat people who disagree?
