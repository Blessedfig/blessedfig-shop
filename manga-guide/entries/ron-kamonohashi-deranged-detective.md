**Premise.** Five years ago Ron Kamonohashi was the best student at BLUE, the world's top detective academy in England, until an incident called the Bloody Field Trip, which he cannot remember. It was found that once he solves a case and hears the killer confess, he falls into a trance in which he pressures and talks the culprit into killing themselves; his detective license was revoked and he was expelled (Wikipedia; Japanese Wikipedia). Living alone in Japan, starved of mysteries, he meets Totomaru "Toto" Isshiki, a kind but dim police detective in Tokyo's Metropolitan Police. They make a deal: Ron solves cases in secret, Toto takes the credit, and Toto must stop Ron before his "forbidden deduction" kills the culprit (Wikipedia; ANN). Ron turns out to be a descendant of both Sherlock Holmes (through his mother) and James Moriarty (through his father), and the Moriarty crime family, the "M Family" led by Milo Moriarty, wants him; a neurosurgeon, Mofu Usaki, makes medicine to suppress his culprit-killing syndrome (Wikipedia). Cases run from "The Case of the Metropolitan Serial Drownings" (ch. 1) through the Bloody Field Trip reveal (ch. 74-75), a 21-chapter cruise-ship murder arc (ch. 76-96), Ron's return to BLUE (ch. 97-100), the Tokyo Chimera serial murders (ch. 136-147), a final showdown with the youngest Moriarty, Alice (ch. 148-151), the Time Island murders (ch. 152-169) and a last chapter titled "A New Forbidden Deduction" (ch. 170) (Wikipedia chapter list).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What should a person do with a gift that can kill? Ron is the best detective of his generation, but his genius has a dark edge: once a killer confesses, something in him takes over and talks them toward their own death. The series asks whether truth-seeking can be kept humane, and whether a person is fated by his blood.

**The answer the story gives.** Truth needs a conscience beside it. Ron cannot fix himself alone; Toto, honest and not very bright, is the brake and the heart, and Mofu's medicine helps. The story never lets the culprits' guilt justify their deaths: they must be taken alive to face the law. Ron's double inheritance from Holmes and Moriarty is answered by his choices, not his genes.

**Characters as arguments.** Ron argues for pure reason and pays its price. Toto argues for goodness without brilliance and turns out to be indispensable. The Moriarty siblings argue that family and power come first: Milo shot his sister to rule, Alice survived and turned the family against him (Wikipedia). Winter, who breaks from Milo, shows a way out. The culprits, whose motives are explained in each case, show what happens when people make themselves judges.

**Symbols and motifs.** The "forbidden deduction" of the title: knowledge that becomes a weapon. Ron's eyes in his trance (MangaUpdates Eye Powers). The BLUE academy and the Bloody Field Trip, a lost memory that holds the key. The credit swap: the public sees Toto, the truth belongs to Ron.

**Traditions it echoes.** Sherlock Holmes and Moriarty, here fused in one hero; the Japanese honkaku mystery (Ayatsuji, Arisugawa) and its fair-play rules; the Detective Conan style of serialized cases with a hidden mastermind; the moral tradition that even the guilty may not be killed by a private hand.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Duty ethics wins: the rule to keep culprits alive is never broken by the story's approval. Virtue ethics shapes the partnership. Secular humanism and naturalism shape the explanations: crimes are solved by evidence, and Ron's condition is treated as neurological. Might makes right is embodied by the Moriarty family and rejected.

**Shifts across the series.** Early chapters (ch. 1-34) are case-of-the-week mysteries with heavy slapstick (the anime's range, roughly; not confirmed by chapter). The middle (ch. 35-75) brings recurring allies and the M Family and reveals the Bloody Field Trip (ch. 74-75). Part 1's later arcs (ch. 76-100) are long, the cruise-ship arc alone running 21 chapters, and return Ron to BLUE. Part 2 (from June 2023, ch. 101 or later; to verify) runs larger serial-murder arcs and the war with Alice Moriarty to the final chapter.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why does the story insist that even a murderer must be stopped from dying?
2. Ron has the blood of both Holmes and Moriarty. Does family decide who we become?
3. Toto takes credit for cases Ron solves. Is that a lie, a kindness, or a fair deal?
4. What does Toto have that Ron does not, and why does Ron need it?
5. If you had a talent that could hurt people, what would you want your friends to do?
