**Premise.** Ritsuka Uenoyama, a 16-year-old high-school guitarist, is bored with school, his basketball club and even music. He finds classmate Mafuyu Sato, also 16, sleeping in his napping spot holding a guitar with broken strings, and fixes it. When Mafuyu sings, Uenoyama is overwhelmed, and Mafuyu joins his band with two older musicians: bassist Haruki Nakayama (22, a graduate student) and drummer Akihiko Kaji (20, a music student) (ages per Wikipedia). The guitar belonged to Mafuyu's childhood friend and first boyfriend, Yuki Yoshida, who died by suicide before the story begins; the manga opens with Mafuyu finding him. The band's first live show, where Mafuyu sings his grief, is its turning point, and Uenoyama and Mafuyu begin dating. The band takes the name Given. Later arcs follow Haruki's love for Akihiko, who is bound up with his ex and roommate, violinist Ugetsu Murata; Yuki's bandmates Hiiragi Kashima and Shizusumi Yagi working through their own grief; and Mafuyu's future in music. The last volume jumps ten years ahead to the characters' adult music careers (SuBLime vol. 9 description).

## Life message

**A good life is lived by facing loss honestly and turning it into something you can give others; love and friendship are what carry a person through grief, and you cannot hold another person by force.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What do you do with a love that ended in death? Mafuyu's first boyfriend, Yuki, killed himself after an argument that ended with Mafuyu asking, "Would you die for me, then?" (TV Tropes). Mafuyu carries his guitar but cannot play it, and cannot say what happened. The series asks whether a person can grieve without being swallowed, and whether he is allowed to love again.

**The answer the story gives.** Grief has to be given a form before it can be carried. Mafuyu cannot talk about Yuki, but he can sing. At the band's first live show he sings the whole story, and the band keeps playing when his strings snap (fan wiki, codes 9-11). The song is a gift to the listeners and a release for him; the band's name, Given, echoes this. After the show Mafuyu and Uenoyama begin dating, and the story treats this second love as a continuation of healing, not a betrayal of the first. Ten years on, the epilogue shows the characters as working musicians (SuBLime vol. 9).

**Characters as arguments.** Mafuyu argues that pain expressed honestly can become something good. Uenoyama argues that passion lost to boredom can come back through another person. Akihiko argues, and then disproves, the idea that enough devotion can hold someone: his years of giving to Ugetsu leave both of them miserable (TV Tropes "All Take and No Give"), and when he forces himself on Haruki (vol. 4) the story makes him face the harm. Ugetsu argues that love should not be a cage. Haruki argues for patience, and is finally loved back. Yuki's friends Hiiragi and Yagi argue that grief is shared; Yuki was not only Mafuyu's loss.

**Symbols and motifs.** Yuki's guitar: a keepsake that must be restrung and played (TV Tropes "Tragic Keepsake"). Broken strings during the first show. Yuki's demo recording, which becomes a song in the late volumes (Wikipedia). Winter and snow in the characters' names (Mafuyu, "midwinter"; Yuki, "snow") (from general knowledge; to verify). Haruki's cut hair as renunciation.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Japanese boys'-love genre, which the series both uses (beautiful leads, older-younger pairings) and widens (a mainstream TV slot, grief as the core). Art as catharsis, an idea as old as Aristotle. Buddhist readers may see impermanence and the release of attachment; Christian readers may see lament, the Psalms' practice of singing grief to someone. These are readings, not the story's claims.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Existential and humanist meaning-making are affirmed. Progressive views of same-sex love are assumed, not argued. Hedonism (Akihiko's casual sex) is shown as a symptom of unhappiness. Religious and traditional views are not voiced.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-3 (likely): Mafuyu joins the band, the first live show, Mafuyu and Uenoyama become a couple. Vols. 4-5 (likely): the focus moves to Haruki, Akihiko and Ugetsu, including the forced advance and its fallout, and the band's festival tryout. Vols. 6-8 (likely): Yuki's friends Hiiragi and Yagi, Yuki's demo, and Mafuyu's future. Vol. 9: a ten-year time skip to adult careers. The tone moves from raw grief to warmth.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Mafuyu could not speak about Yuki but could sing about him. Why might art say what words cannot?
2. Mafuyu blames himself for one sentence he said. How does the story treat that guilt, and what would you tell a friend who felt the same?
3. Akihiko gives everything to Ugetsu and it does not work. What is the difference between love and dependence?
4. Akihiko forces himself on Haruki and they later become a couple. Does the story handle that well? What should happen after someone causes harm?
5. Your family's tradition has its own teaching about love, sex, grief and suicide. Where does this story agree with it, and where does it not?
