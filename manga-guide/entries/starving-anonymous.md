**Premise.** In a near-future Japan of record heat, shortages and falling birth rates, high schooler I'e, an aspiring painter with a photographic memory, and his friend Kazu are gassed on the bus home and wake on a truck of frozen bodies being sawn apart (vol. 1). They are inside a secret facility where people are fattened with a liquid that erases the self and fed to huge insect-like creatures. I'e will not abandon Kazu, who has been force-fed (ciatr). With Natsune, a boy grown in a lab to regenerate endlessly as food, and Yamabiki, a genius who grafted other creatures' genes into himself, they try to escape (vols. 1-2; Wikipedia; ciatr). They find the Breeders, women kept behind bars to bear child after child and men driven mad by aphrodisiacs (vol. 2); Natsune's revenge on the creatures puts the staff in danger (vol. 3); a scientist's human experiments and old feud with Yamabiki come to light, and the facility's director kills Natsune (vols. 4-5). Natsune regenerates; the creatures turn out to be aliens who once ate their own world bare, created humanity on Earth as food, and now escape into the world (vol. 6). To end them, Natsune agrees to be cloned endlessly as their food; I'e seals the door and escapes with Kazu and Ogura, a former reporter. Three years later the creatures are extinct, and I'e believes Natsune and Yamabiki still live (vol. 7; ciatr).

## Life message

**People are not livestock: even when the powerful treat human beings as food, refusing to abandon a friend and giving oneself for others are what keep people human.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What is a human being worth if someone else sees us as food? I'e wakes in a place where people are fattened, bred and eaten, run by people serving creatures. The series asks what keeps a person human when the world treats them as meat.

**The answer the story gives.** Loyalty and self-giving. I'e will not leave Kazu behind, even when Kazu has been drugged into a stupor (ciatr). Natsune, made in a lab as endless food, ends by choosing to be that food so the creatures can be destroyed (vol. 7). The story honors the choice even as it shows its horror.

**Characters as arguments.** I'e is the ordinary person who stays decent. Natsune begins as pure vengeance and learns friendship from I'e. Yamabiki is science without limits, dangerous but finally on humanity's side. The scientist who experiments on people and the facility's director are science and power without conscience. The creatures are consumption itself: having eaten their own world, they made a new food source.

**Symbols and motifs.** The fattening liquid that takes away the self; the Cradle where the queen lives; regeneration as a curse turned into a weapon; the prion disease that destroys those who eat their own kind; the message on a park bench in the last pages (vol. 7, ciatr). I'e's drawings from memory.

**Traditions it echoes.** Soylent Green and factory-farm horror (The Manga Critic), Attack on Titan and Dragon Head (Kodansha's pitch, via The Manga Critic), The Promised Neverland's farmed children (from general knowledge; to verify), and the Prometheus or substitute-sacrifice story in Natsune's end.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Might makes right and utilitarian sacrifice of the weak are condemned in the facility, yet the ending uses a form of utilitarian sacrifice for good. Transhumanism is horror and rescue at once. Naturalism supplies the cosmology and the cure.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-2 are the facility and the Breeders. Vols. 3-5 are Natsune's revenge, the scientist's experiments and Natsune's death. Vol. 6 reveals the creatures' origin and their escape. Vol. 7 is the sacrifice, the escape and a three-year epilogue.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why does I'e refuse to leave Kazu behind? What does that cost him?
2. The creatures made humans to eat them. Does making something give you the right to use it?
3. Natsune chooses to suffer forever so others can live. Was that right? Should anyone have asked it of him?
4. Does this story say anything about how we treat the animals we eat?
5. Which characters keep their humanity, and how?
