**Premise.** The villainous Secret Society Gekko has burned half the world and wants the rest; to stop it, Professor Big Gelato forms the Super Sentai team Freezing Sentai Gelato 5, led by Fudo Aikawa (Red Gelato), a muscle-training high-school student (Wikipedia). Gekko's newest fighter is the "Reaper Princess," Desumi Magahara, a third-year high-school girl from Hakata who loves cute things (Japanese Wikipedia). Fudo falls for her at first sight, confesses, and they start dating in secret while still fighting each other in public, holding hands in mid-battle and hiding the relationship from both organizations (ch. 1-6, v1; Wikipedia; ANN). Around them: Haru (Pink Gelato), who loves Fudo but steps back and supports the couple; Daigo (Green Gelato), Desumi's old karate senior, traumatized by her strength; Desumi's fellow "princesses" at Gekko and their monster bosses, including Culverin Bear, who starts dating the Steel Princess; Anna Hojo, a classmate and secret Gekko princess who is in love with Desumi; and Gekko's leader Bosslar, who sends Fudo flowers when he is hospitalized (Wikipedia; Japanese Wikipedia). Later arcs bring a beach outing (ch. 11), Gekko's "Ultimate Phantom" (ch. 22), a trip to Desumi's hometown of Fukuoka where Fudo meets her father under a false name (ch. 28-29, v5; Kodansha), a rich rival for Fudo, Hyoka Charlotte Rurimiya (v6; Kodansha), Fudo considering quitting Gelato 5 (ch. 34) and a farewell (ch. 36) before the final chapter, "Love After World Domination" (ch. 37) (Wikipedia chapter titles).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Are you the side you fight for? Fudo and Desumi are hero and villain by job, and the series asks what happens when two people refuse to let that decide who they love.

**The answer the story gives.** Love and character matter more than labels. Desumi is a "Reaper" in public and a girl who loves cute things in private; Fudo is a hero who holds his enemy's hand mid-battle. The story never makes either organization simply good or evil.

**Characters as arguments.** Fudo argues for sincerity: he confesses on sight and plans a workout-themed date (ANN vol. 1). Desumi argues that reputation is not identity. Haru argues for love that can let go. Bosslar, who sends flowers to a hospitalized enemy, argues that even villains have manners (Japanese Wikipedia). Daigo shows fear that has to be faced (ch. 25).

**Symbols and motifs.** Masks and transformations (henshin) as hidden selves; hand-holding disguised as grappling; Desumi's cat Hellko as her private world; the series title, suggesting a love that outlasts the war.

**Traditions it echoes.** Romeo and Juliet's lovers from rival houses, played for comedy; Super Sentai and Kamen Rider (ANN vol. 1); enemies-to-lovers romance. Some faith readers may hear "love your enemies" in comic form.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism and virtue win: the lovers' choice and their sincerity are rewarded. Collective loyalty is not condemned, only gently mocked. Feminist readers find a strong heroine in a revealing costume.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-2 (ch. 1-13) set up the secret romance, the beach and Haru's challenge; vols. 3-4 (ch. 14-25) widen to the princesses, Desumi's sister and Ultimate Phantom; v5 (ch. 26-31) brings a kiss and the trip to her family; v6 (ch. 32-37) adds a rival, Fudo's thought of quitting Gelato 5 and a farewell before the finale. The ending is not confirmed in sources opened.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Desumi is called the Reaper Princess but loves cute things. Which is the real her? Can it be both?
2. Fudo and Desumi keep their relationship secret. What do they gain, and what does it cost? Who could they have told?
3. Fudo uses a false name with Desumi's family. Was that right?
4. Haru loves Fudo but supports the couple. What does that say about her?
5. If the villains are decent people with jobs, what makes Gekko evil?
