**Premise.** Morio Kurokawa, 18, is "Mori the Black Panther," a feared delinquent with a knife scar under his left eye and a face full of piercings; he dropped out of high school, has no job, and lives with his grandmother, having no parents (Japanese Wikipedia; VIZ). While swaggering down the street with his crew he blocks the tactile paving, and Yukiko Akaza, 16, a low-vision student at a school for the blind, jabs him with her white cane. He falls for her on the spot (Japanese Wikipedia). She cannot see his scar and treats him as a person, and he learns guiding techniques, audio description and how the city works for a blind person (ANN; Review of Disability Studies). Around them: Yukiko's protective older sister Izumi (20), who has carried her sister's care alone; Morio's loyal follower Hachiko, a former child actor he once defended from bullies; Yukiko's classmates Sora (low vision, distrustful of sighted people) and Aono (blind from birth); and Shishio Kanazawa (26), the man who gave Morio his scar, now a video-shop employee who is gay and has told no one (Japanese Wikipedia). Volumes follow a first kiss at Christmas (v4), Yukiko's part-time job (v5), Morio's past (v6), a platform accident involving Aono (v7), and a beach trip and meeting Yukiko's father (v8) (VIZ).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What do we really see when we look at someone? The town sees a dangerous thug; Yukiko, who sees only colors and shapes, hears a kind young man. The town sees a blind girl to pity; Morio sees a sharp, funny person who knows exactly where her food is (v3). The series asks whether the labels we put on people are about them or about us.

**The answer the story gives.** Look again, learn, and make room. Morio's love is not pity: he learns how to guide, how to describe, how a city looks from the white cane's side. The story's answer is personal (respect) and practical (fix the bus stop, clear the paving), and the scholars note that it treats disability as part of "our" story rather than someone else's (Review of Disability Studies).

**Characters as arguments.** Morio argues that a scary face can hide a gentle heart. Yukiko argues that a disabled person wants a full, ordinary life, not admiration. Izumi argues that love can become a cage, and shows the loneliness of caregivers. Sora argues for resentment of the sighted world and slowly drops it. Shishio argues that the hardest label to carry is the one no one knows about. The man who shouts at Aono argues that public space belongs to the able-bodied, and the story answers him by putting him on crutches (v6).

**Symbols and motifs.** The white cane, which first strikes Morio and then becomes the thing he learns to respect. Tactile paving, which Morio blocks on day one and protects afterward. Colors: every character's name contains a color (Japanese Wikipedia), fitting a heroine who sees in colors. Morio's bright clothes, worn so Yukiko can find him (Mangapedia). The platform edge as "a bridge without railing" (v7).

**Traditions it echoes.** The redeemed-delinquent romance of Japanese manga; the biblical command not to put a stumbling block before the blind and the Qur'an's rebuke for turning from a blind man; and the modern social model of disability, which locates the barrier in the world rather than the body.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Progressivism and secular humanism lead: inclusion is right, and knowledge plus kindness can achieve it. Communitarianism appears in the many helpers. The fighter's code of strength is what Morio grows out of. No lens is attacked except prejudice itself.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-2 set up the couple, Hachiko and the sister. Volumes 3-4 bring Sora, the bully and the first kiss. Volumes 5-6 widen to Yukiko's job, coworkers and Morio's past. Volumes 7-8 turn to safety, adulthood and family approval, ending with plans for further schooling (Mangapedia; to verify).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Morio blocked the tactile paving without knowing it. What things in our town might block someone without our noticing?
2. Izumi loves her sister but holds her back. Where is the line between protecting and controlling?
3. Why does Yukiko get upset when people call her inspiring just for living her life?
