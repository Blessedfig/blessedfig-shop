**Premise.** Takahiro Sato, a 27-year-old office worker and gamer, wakes up as "Hiro" in the cockpit of his overpowered ship, the Krishna, in a universe like his favorite game, Stella Online (Wikipedia; web novel #003). He plans to earn money as a mercenary and buy a house on a pleasant planet. On his first space station he meets Serena Holz, an imperial officer and noble, and Elma, a space-elf mercenary who is 53 but looks young (web novel #005); he rescues Mimi, an orphan forced out of school by her dead parents' debts, from thugs in an alley and hires her as his operator (anime ep. 1-2; manga v1). He pays off Elma's crushing fine and takes her on too (v1-2), helps the Grakkan Empire beat back a Belbellum Federation invasion (v2, Seven Seas), fights bioweapon creatures in the Arein system and meets the doctor Shoko (v3-4, anime ep. 6-7), protects Chris, a young noble whose uncle murdered her parents (v5-7), adds Mei, a combat maid android (v8), and builds a mothership with twin "space dwarf" mechanics, Tina and Wiska (v9-10, Seven Seas). Mimi and Elma become his lovers early, and the crew grows into a harem (MangaUpdates; anime ep. 2-4).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** If you woke up powerful and free, what would you do with it? Hiro's answer is in the title: earn money, live freely and buy a house.

**The answer the story gives.** Use your skill to protect the people around you, refuse masters, and enjoy what you earn. Generosity is part of the answer: Hiro pays off Mimi's and Elma's debts and protects Chris without payment at first. So is pleasure: the crew of women who love him is presented as what a good man deserves.

**Characters as arguments.** Hiro argues for competent freedom. Serena argues for duty to the state and keeps losing the argument gracefully (anime ep. 5-7). Mimi argues for gratitude and devotion; Elma for pride that has to accept help. Pirates and Balthazar argue for taking by force and are destroyed by a stronger force, so the story never tests whether its hero's might is right.

**Symbols and motifs.** The Krishna (a Hindu god's name, used only as a ship name; from general knowledge); the dream house; cola, which this universe lacks; holding one's breath to slow time; the mothership as a growing household (v9).

**Traditions it echoes.** Isekai power fantasies and space opera; the harem genre. Faith readers will notice that rescue and debt release echo biblical themes (Luke 4:18; the shmita year), while the story's idea of reward runs the other way.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Hedonism, libertarianism and consumerism are celebrated. Strength rules is assumed rather than argued. Transhumanist technology is mostly a source of danger (bioweapons) and of comic gags.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-2: arrival, Mimi and Elma, the Federation invasion. Vols. 3-4: Arein system, Shoko, bioweapons, the pirate-hunting unit. Vols. 5-8: Chris, the resort worlds, Mei, the fight with Chris's uncle. Vols. 9-12: the mothership, the dwarf twins and new threats.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Hiro pays Mimi's debt. Does that change what she owes him? Should it?
2. Hiro turns down a military career to stay free. What does he gain, and what does he give up?
3. The story treats a crew of women who all love Hiro as a happy ending. How would the story look from Mimi's side?
4. Hiro kills pirates for bounty money without a second thought. Does it matter that they are pirates?
5. What is your "house on a nice planet," and what would you do to get it?
