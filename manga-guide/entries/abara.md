**Premise.** Far in the future, humans live in towering cities linked by a single highway and tubes. Some people mutate without warning into White Gaunas, armored monsters that grow weapons from bone-like material and devour people. Long ago, scientists built nuclear-powered time machines called mausoleums to send a few humans to a distant future, and used the plants' power to banish White Gaunas to another timeline; most mausoleums were later destroyed in fighting, and an Observation Bureau was set up to guard the secret. Centuries later the Bureau engineers Black Gaunas, human-Gauna hybrids with human minds, using children as test subjects: Kudou Denji and a girl, Nayuta, who is fitted with a control device. Denji escaped as a child, killing several people. Years later, a Bureau agent, Tadohomi, asks him to help kill a new White Gauna; Sakijima, an investigator for the Ministry, chases the Bureau's secrets. A giant White Gauna appears, Nayuta dies fighting it, and her telepathically linked twin Ayuta becomes a White Gauna. A missile carried by the Raven, a cyborg holding the memories of the ancient Eon Group, kills the giant. As the last reactor fails, Tadohomi and Sakijima escape to the far future in the time machine; the rest of humanity dies out, and Denji and Nayuta fight on against White Gaunas in a separate dimension (Wikipedia). The English edition adds Digimortal, in which a silent mercenary destroys an Inquisition base of a church that rules the world through salvaged technology (pixiv).

## Life message

**Humanity may survive only as a remnant, and only because a few beings, half-monstrous themselves, keep fighting the monsters; the powers that make such weapons by using children are not to be trusted.**

## Creator and publisher context

- Tsutomu Nihei (born 1971) worked in construction, studied at Parsons School of Design in New York, and assisted Tsutomu Takahashi before launching Blame! in 1997 (Wikipedia, Nihei). His influences include Akira, Nausicaa, Ghost in the Shell, H. R. Giger, Moebius, Blade Runner and Clive Barker (Wikipedia).
- Abara sits between Blame! and Knights of Sidonia (2015 Kodansha Manga Award), which reuses the name Gauna for its aliens (Wikipedia). Bleeding Cool calls it a transitional work linking to Knights of Sidonia and Aposimz.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** When humanity is turning into its own predator, what is worth saving, and who gets to decide? The Observation Bureau answers with secrecy and experiments on children; the ancient Eon Group answers by sending one pair of humans into a far future.

**The answer the story gives.** Survival comes at a terrible price, paid by those who are least human. Denji, made a weapon as a child, comes back to fight; Nayuta, controlled by an implant, dies and rises to fight her own twin; the Raven rides the missile. Two ordinary people walk into an overgrown future. The story does not say this is good, only that it is what remains.

**Characters as arguments.** Denji argues that a monster can choose to protect. Sakijima argues for truth against an authority that hides it, and kills to keep going. Tadohomi argues for loyalty to the one the Bureau used. The Bureau's boss argues that hybrids are monsters and cannot be trusted. Ayuta, turned into a White Gauna, shows what happens when the experiment fails.

**Symbols and motifs.** The mausoleums, ancient tombs that are actually time machines. The bone armor: the body made into a weapon. The single highway and tubes linking towers. The overgrown House at the end, and the sky the survivors look up at.

**Traditions it echoes.** Cyberpunk and body horror (Giger, Cronenberg, Bleeding Cool), the last-humans-as-Adam-and-Eve ending, and the monster-hunter who is part monster. Digimortal adds a dystopian church with an Inquisition, echoing older Western stories of corrupt religious power.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Transhumanism is explored without a verdict: the hybrids are both victims and saviors. Authoritarian secrecy is critiqued. Nihilism is close but not complete: Bleeding Cool finds a dark optimism in the survival of a remnant.

**Shifts across the series.** Volume 1 sets up the White Gauna attacks, Denji's return and Sakijima's investigation. Volume 2 brings the giant White Gauna, Nayuta's death and Ayuta's transformation, the missile, the reactor's failure and the escape to the future, followed by Digimortal.

**Discussion questions.**
1. The Bureau used children to make weapons. Can anything justify that? What does the story think?
2. Denji was hurt by the people he goes back to help. Why might he do that?
3. At the end, only two humans survive. Is the ending hopeful or sad, and why?
4. If people have to stop being fully human to survive, is that still a win?
