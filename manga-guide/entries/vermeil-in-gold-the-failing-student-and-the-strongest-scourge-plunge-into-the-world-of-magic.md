**Premise.** Alto Goldfield is a top student at the Royal Ortigia Magic Academy except in summoning: no familiar will answer him, and he will be held back a year unless he contracts one. A grimoire falls on his head in the library; its circle summons Vermeil, a nude, horned demon sealed for three centuries and known as the strongest "scourge" ever to threaten the world (ch. 0, vol. 1). His mana is so potent that ordinary spirits avoid him, but Vermeil loves it; she agrees to be his familiar on the condition that she can absorb his mana by kissing him. The two win duels, expose a teacher, Obsidian, who experiments on students with demon blood (vol. 1-2), and face the "Platinum Square" mage Iolite (vol. 3 onward). Vermeil's flirting hides trauma: humans killed her foster family over a plague they blamed on her, and in grief she destroyed her childhood town, before asking the mage Fatema to seal her away so she might someday find someone who would accept her (vol. 4-5). Alto accepts her, and they become a couple (fan wiki; Wikipedia; ANN).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can someone the whole world calls a monster be loved, and what happens when a weak boy holds the strongest power in the world? Alto summons a demon that once destroyed a city; everyone is taught to fear her kind.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes: Vermeil's destruction came from grief after humans murdered her foster family, and what she needed was someone who would accept her. Fatema sealed her away hoping for that person; Alto is that person (fan wiki, Fatema). Power bound by love is safe; power sought for itself (Obsidian, Iolite) is what is dangerous.

**Characters as arguments.** Alto argues for acceptance and steady effort. Vermeil argues for appetite and freedom, then reveals grief underneath. Lilia stands for conventional disapproval and for jealous devotion; she also voices the story's view that goodness is not about what kind of being you are. Obsidian is the teacher who uses students; Iolite is power for amusement, cloaked as "Heaven's Will."

**Symbols and motifs.** Gold: Alto's golden mana and the title (vermeil is gold-plated silver, fan wiki). The sealed book. The kiss as a contract. Vermeil's borrowed dresses. The demon form she hides.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Faust and succubus legends inverted (the demon is the one who needs love); Beauty and the Beast; magic-school stories; the shōnen "familiar" contract. Some faith readers will see an inversion of Christian demonology, where the demon is the victim of human cruelty.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Pagan/occult is the world's furniture and the heroine's nature. Hedonism is celebrated through Vermeil, and the story does not treat her disregard for Alto's consent as wrong (ANN; Anime Feminist). Might makes right is rejected in the villains.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1-2 (ch. 0-8): summoning, duels and Obsidian (the "Cliché Storm," TV Tropes). Vol. 3 (ch. 9-13): the Bronze exam and Iolite; the romance deepens. Vol. 4-5 (ch. 14-18): Vermeil's coma, her past and Fatema. Vol. 6-11 (ch. 19-41): not covered by sources opened (the fan wiki names later characters such as Noa, Gannet Granatus and a Dragonrider Regiment). The series stopped without an ending in 2026.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Alto says no to Vermeil several times. Does the story respect that? How would it feel if it happened to you?
2. Why did Vermeil destroy her hometown? Does knowing why change how you judge her?
3. What makes someone a "monster": what they are, or what they do?
4. Obsidian and Iolite want power. How is Alto's use of power different?
