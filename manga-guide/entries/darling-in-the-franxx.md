**Premise.** In a 22nd-century wasteland, humanity survives in mobile fortress cities called Plantations. The adults have become immortal at the cost of reproduction and no longer love or form families; they live in the inner city under APE and its chairman, Papa, who is worshipped almost as a god (Wikipedia). Children, artificially created and called "parasites," are raised in isolated "birdcages" and trained to pilot giant robots called Franxx in boy-girl pairs ("stamen" and "pistil") against monsters called klaxosaurs (Wikipedia). Hiro, a former prodigy who failed to sync with his partner Naomi, meets Zero Two, a horned girl with klaxosaur blood whose partners are said to die after three rides; he pilots with her, becomes her "darling," and joins Squad 13 of Plantation 13 (Wikipedia; fan wiki, Volume 1). The squad of ten (Hiro, Zero Two, Ichigo, Goro, Zorome, Miku, Kokoro, Futoshi, Mitsuru and Ikuno) slowly discovers friendship, love and the meaning of family. In the manga, they rebel against Papa when they learn that the elite 9's and all children judged weak are to be "disposed of" (v6), and Hiro and Zero Two recover a forgotten childhood bond (v8) (fan wiki). The ending differs from the anime: there is no space war; the klaxosaur princess destroys APE's headquarters, Papa is revealed as an alien and dies, Hiro and Zero Two defeat the princess, who gives her life force to restore the planet, and the two survive and face a new world with their friends (fan wiki, Chapter 60; Anime and Manga Differences).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What makes us human: living forever, or loving and being loved? The adults of the Plantations chose endless life and lost love, sex and children. The parasites were made to be tools, yet they turn out to be the only truly human people left.

**The answer the story gives.** Love chosen freely, and the community and children that come from it. The one-winged Jian bird that flies only as a pair is the image (ch. 60). The manga gives the pair a happy ending: Hiro and Zero Two survive and face a new world together, with their friends at their side.

**Characters as arguments.** Hiro argues that a person is more than his usefulness: once a failure, he finds purpose through Zero Two. Zero Two argues for the longing to be human and the guilt of having used others. Kokoro argues for curiosity about family and mercy toward enemies (v6). Mitsuru shows how pride and a broken promise turn to friendship. Papa argues the opposite case: freedom from the body as a cage (ch. 60). The princess argues for revenge on behalf of her lost people, and in death passes the world on.

**Symbols and motifs.** The birdcage. Flower names for male and female pilots. Codes replaced by names. The picture book of the beast and the prince. Horns that grow as Zero Two loses herself and shatter when she remembers (v8). A tree growing from a renewed earth.

**Traditions it echoes.** Genesis's "be fruitful and multiply" and the Babel story of a self-made heaven (for religious readers); dystopias such as Brave New World, where comfort replaces love; the coming-of-age mecha drama. Its sexual frankness about teens, heightened in the manga's art, sits uneasily with its pro-family message for many readers.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Transhumanism and authoritarian control are the villains. Expressive individualism and community are rewarded. Traditional goods (marriage, children) are affirmed. Existential choice gives Hiro his reason to live.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-3 follow the anime closely: Hiro and Zero Two meet and Squad 13 forms. From volume 4 the story diverges: the beach and abandoned-town arcs (v4-5), the 9's rescued from disposal and the rebellion (v6), the partner shuffle and Ikuno's confession (v7), Hiro's recovered memories and the battle at the Gran Crevasse (v8), and an ending without the anime's space war (fan wiki).

**Discussion questions.**
1. The adults chose to live forever and gave up having children. What did they lose?
2. Why does Hiro give the children names instead of code numbers?
3. Papa calls the body a cage. Why do the children refuse that idea?
4. Kokoro asks the squad to save the 9's, who had looked down on them. Why does that matter?
5. Many readers say the manga adds fan service of teenage characters that the story does not need. What do you think, and how should a reader respond?
