**Premise.** Kuroki, a quiet second-year high school boy with no friends and no dating experience, is seated next to Mai Chihara, whose "jirai-kei" (landmine) look of pale skin, red eye makeup and dark, frilly fashion has made her the subject of rumors that she is unstable and dangerous (Wikipedia; ANN; Seven Seas). Chihara decides she wants to be his friend, and he slowly learns she is sweet, candid, clumsy and a little odd, with a love of cats and gyoza dumplings. Across nine volumes the friendship becomes romance: a ruined weekend outing and a gift of cat sandals (vols. 1-2); jealousy when Kuroki's childhood friends Hibiya and Kazami appear, a summer camp where she sneaks into his tent, a pool trip where she kisses his forehead, and a school-festival gyoza stall (vols. 3-4); karaoke, hand-holding on a dog walk, and meeting her imposing father at his retro cafe (vols. 5-6); a joke that Kuroki is moving to Dubai that sends her running out of school, an overnight stay at her home making gyoza with her mother, an aquarium date, mutual confessions at a Christmas party, and a formal request to date (vols. 7-9) (ciatr). Her best friend Chinatsu Iwakura coaches her on clothes and makeup.

## Life message

**Don't judge someone by her looks or by rumor; patient, honest kindness lets two shy people become friends and then something more.**

| Pragmatic | Rumor fails a simple test: Kuroki talks to Chihara and finds the reputation false. Small concrete acts (a gift of sandals, a gyoza stall, showing up an hour early) build the relationship (ciatr). |

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can you know someone from what she wears? Chihara's classmates think so. The title asks the question the whole class is whispering, and the series spends nine volumes answering no.

**The answer the story gives.** Look again. Kuroki is the least likely person to take a social risk, and he does it anyway, a little at a time. What he finds is not a hidden tragedy but an ordinary sweet, clumsy girl who loves cats and gyoza (ANN; ciatr). The point is less that the stereotype hides depth than that it was simply wrong.

**Characters as arguments.** Chihara argues that self-expression and kindness can coexist; she never changes her style to be accepted. Kuroki argues for courage in small doses. Chinatsu shows what a loyal friend does: coach, encourage, cover. The classmates are the argument for first impressions, and they are never punished, only proved wrong.

**Symbols and motifs.** The landmine itself: a hidden danger that never goes off. Gyoza, folded by hand and shared at a festival stall and later with her mother. The cat sandals, a small gift that repairs a ruined day. The aquarium date where both arrive an hour early.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Japanese school rom-com of a scary-looking girl who is secretly gentle, the "don't judge a book by its cover" fable, and older teachings against gossip and rash judgment found in many faiths (Leviticus 19:16; Matthew 7:1; Qur'an 49:12; Buddhist right speech).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism is affirmed quietly: style is personal and harmless. Virtue is rewarded. Consumer culture (fashion, makeup, food) is part of the texture but not examined. Nothing in sources suggests a critique of anyone's beliefs.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-2 are fear turning to friendship; volumes 3-4 bring rivals and summer events; volumes 5-6 add families; volumes 7-9 move to confessions and dating (ciatr). The anime starts January 2027.

**Discussion questions.**
1. What did the class think Chihara was like, and where did that idea come from?
2. Kuroki was scared too. What made him keep talking to her?
3. Have you ever been wrong about someone because of how they dressed?
4. Is it fair to call any style "dangerous"? What does that do to the person who wears it?
5. Kuroki joked that he was moving away and it hurt Chihara. When does a joke stop being funny?
