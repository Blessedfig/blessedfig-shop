**Premise.** Deadpool, Marvel's regenerating, fourth-wall-breaking mercenary, is bribed by Iron Man to join the Avengers' new Tokyo team, the Samurai Squad. He is partnered with Sakura Spider (Hida Haruka), an earnest Japanese spider-hero who lives by "great power, great responsibility," and recruits Neiro Aratabi, a 16-year-old idol singer whose symbiote, Kage, eats people who threaten her (ch. 3-7). Loki, brainwashing people with Hydra collars, raises a clone of Thanos (ch. 8-10, beaten by a guest-starring All Might) and an army of villains on Mt. Fuji; with the Avengers' help and a villainous Deadpool from another universe who switches sides, Deadpool beats him (ch. 12-15). Loki's motive turns out to be that Thor has stopped fighting him because he is hooked on anime (ch. 15). Season 2 brings Deadpool back after Sakura Spider is reported killed (she is alive; the dead hero was a fake Spider-Man), against drug-powered gangsters and a multiverse of evil Deadpools (ch. 16-19). The finale has the variants stop fighting and Deadpool blow up Shueisha's building (ch. 26) (Marvel Database; ComicBook.com; VIZ).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can a character who mocks everything mean anything? Deadpool laughs at heroes, villains, fans and the comic he is in. The series asks, quietly, what lies under the jokes.

**The answer the story gives.** A little grief and a little loyalty. Captain America tells Sakura Spider that Deadpool's antics are how he copes with losing everyone he loved (ch. 11), and by the last volume VIZ's blurb calls him more decent than his reputation because of his bond with his coworkers. The story never lets that sincerity last for long.

**Characters as arguments.** Sakura Spider argues for responsibility and is mocked for it, yet she is the one who saves Deadpool by calling the Avengers (ch. 13). Neiro argues for devotion to fans above everything; she even forgives the man who planned to enslave her (ch. 6), which Deadpool cannot understand. Loki argues for destruction out of jealousy, a gag version of his myth. The evil Deadpool shows what Wade would be without friends, and is bought back for a better paycheck (ch. 14-15).

**Symbols and motifs.** Regeneration: Deadpool is torn apart and always returns, like the joke that never ends. The fourth wall: panels, polls and publishers as characters. Chimichangas he cannot cook (ch. 11): a clumsy gesture of friendship.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Deadpool comics' meta-humor, Gintama's parody style (But Why Tho), and the Jump crossover tradition. Faith traditions would recognize the scoffer of Psalm 1 and the fool who mocks, but also the prodigal who finds a home.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Postmodernism and absurdism are celebrated. Consumerism is mocked and practiced at once. Communitarianism wins in a small way: the team is the only thing Deadpool stops joking about.

**Shifts across the series.** Season 1 (vol. 1-2, ch. 1-15): Tokyo, Neiro, Thanos and Loki's army. Season 2 (vol. 3-4, ch. 16-26): darker opening with a reported death and drug-powered gangs, then a multiverse of Deadpools, ending in a finale about the series itself.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Neiro forgave the man who planned to hurt her, but Deadpool killed him. Who was right?
2. Captain America says Deadpool jokes because he is grieving. Do you think that is true?
3. Why do you think Sakura Spider stays on the team?
4. When is a joke about violence funny, and when is it not?
5. The series makes fun of the company that publishes it. Why would a publisher allow that?
