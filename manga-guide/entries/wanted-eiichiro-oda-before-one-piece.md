**Premise.** Five early short stories by the creator of One Piece. In **Wanted!**, Gill Bastar, a young gunman on the American frontier, kills a bounty hunter in self-defense; the dead man's ghost haunts and possesses him and pushes him into a duel with the hitman Sino Phoenix, which Gill wins. In **God's Gift for the Future**, God, angry at the pickpocket Branco, writes in his notebook that a meteor will kill him but miswrites the name of a department store, so he makes Branco his agent to clear the building; Branco, selfish at first, saves everyone. In **Ikki Yakō**, the cowardly young monk Guko, seeking his lost master, is asked by a village to stop a man-eating monster; a 300-year-old guardian spirit at the shrine helps, Guko learns the monster ate his master, and he cuts it in two with the deity's sword. In **Monsters**, the wandering samurai Ryuma exposes the famous swordsman Cyrano as the thief who destroyed the waitress Flare's village with a summoned dragon, slays the dragon and is revealed as "King," the world's best swordsman (later tied into One Piece). In **Romance Dawn** (version 2), Monkey D. Luffy rescues the girl Ann and her giant bird from the pirate-sorcerer Spiel; he wants to be a "Peace Main," a pirate who seeks adventure, not plunder (Wikipedia; TV Tropes).

## Life message

**Real heroes protect the helpless and stay humble, while those who prey on others, even famous ones, fall; and even a selfish thief or a frightened boy can choose to do the right thing.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What makes a hero? Each of the five stories puts a different answer on trial: the gunman who only defends himself, the thief who is forced to save people, the coward who must face a monster, the quiet swordsman who refuses fame, and the boy who wants to be a pirate without plundering.

**The answer the story gives.** A hero protects people who cannot protect themselves, and real greatness is humble. The famous swordsman is a fraud; the scruffy stranger is "King" (Monsters). The frightened monk finds courage when the people he loves are hurt (Ikki Yakō). Even the pickpocket, once he has to, saves everyone (God's Gift).

**Characters as arguments.** Gill argues that violence breeds more violence through bounties and revenge. Branco argues that people can change when given responsibility. God, here, is a parody of arbitrary judgment. Guko is fear turned to courage. Ryuma is humble strength; Cyrano is reputation without virtue. Luffy is freedom joined to kindness, the seed of One Piece.

**Symbols and motifs.** The wanted poster; God's notebook and pen; the guardian's oni mask and sword; the dragon-summoning horn; Luffy's straw hat and the "Peace Main" flag.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Western showdown, the Japanese ghost story (yūrei and yokai), the wandering-samurai tale, the folk tale of the hidden king, and the pirate romance. Christian readers will see a Western-looking God treated as a comic figure; Buddhist and Shinto readers will recognize a monk and a village kami.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Virtue and honor are affirmed in every story. Rule by strength is punished. Buddhist and folk-spirit ideas are setting and color. The Christian-looking God is satirized rather than argued with.

**Shifts across the series.** The stories run in the order written, 1992 to 1996, from a rough Western to a nearly finished One Piece; ANN's reviewers saw Oda's humor and character writing grow across them.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Cyrano was famous and Ryuma was not. Why did Flare trust the wrong one?
2. Branco only helped because God made him. Did he become a better person anyway?
3. Guko was a coward until the end. What changed him?
4. In one story God makes a mistake. How is that different from what your family believes about God?
5. Luffy wants to be a pirate who does not steal. Is that possible? What makes a "good" pirate?
