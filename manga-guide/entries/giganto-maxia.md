**Premise.** One hundred million years after the Great Destruction, the wasteland belongs to giant insects, demi-human tribes ("Myuu") and humans ("Hyuu"). The Empire of Olympus, a slave-trading human empire, uses colossal "Giganto" (titans) steered through an oracle to crush other peoples. Delos, a kind, heavily muscled former gladiator, walks the desert with Prome, a small, pale, not-quite-human girl who rides on his shoulders, analyzes everything aloud and calls him her "contractor." The Scarabaei, a beetle-riding demi-human clan, capture the pair and force Delos to fight their champion Ogun in the arena (ch. 1-2). Delos, a wrestler, wins without weapons and without killing, and wins the tribe's trust. When the Empire's army arrives with a titan, Prome merges with Delos to form the giant Gohra, who defeats the titan with wrestling moves and a finishing dive; Delos makes sure the defeated imperial soldiers can retreat alive. The pair walk on into the desert at the end (Wikipedia; Dark Horse; Otaku USA; fan wiki; TV Tropes).

## Life message

**Strength is for protecting and sparing, not destroying: a true warrior fights so that both sides can walk away, and stands with a threatened people against an empire that would wipe them out.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What is strength for? In a world where an empire turns living beings into weapons the size of mountains, Delos is the strongest man in the arena and refuses to use that strength to kill.

**The answer the story gives.** Strength exists to protect and to spare. Delos wins every fight by letting his opponent show his best and then pinning him, so both walk away (Manga Test Drive). He defends the beetle clan from genocide and then shields the beaten imperial soldiers from the clan's revenge (fan wiki). Publishers Weekly sums the theme as striving to do better through suffering.

**Characters as arguments.** Delos argues for mercy backed by power. Prome argues for clear-eyed reason; she calls his style foolish, and the story lets both be right. Ogun stands for a wronged people's pride and distrust: humans burned his city, so every human is the Empire's dog until Delos proves otherwise. The Empire of Olympus is power without restraint, a faceless machine of conquest.

**Symbols and motifs.** The titans (Giganto) as weapons of empire and Gohra as a titan of protection; the oasis that is really a monster's mouth (ch. 1), a picture of false hope; the beetle nest that makes the desert bloom; the road into the sunset at the end. The Greek myth of the giants' war is turned around: here the giant fights for the small peoples.

**Traditions it echoes.** Greek myth (the gigantomachia, Olympus, Icarus), Roman gladiator stories, Nausicaä's post-apocalyptic ecology, giant-monster and Attack on Titan battles, and professional wrestling's code of making the opponent look good (Otaku USA; Manga Test Drive). Christian readers may hear Romans 12:21, Buddhists the precept of non-harming.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Might makes right is rejected: the mightiest hero refuses to dominate. Classical virtue (courage joined to magnanimity) is the story's ideal. Pacifism is approached but not reached: Delos fights, but never to kill. The pagan names are decor. Reviewers' two criticisms sit outside the moral line: the sexualized gags about Prome and the way the demi-human tribe is drawn.

**Shifts across the series.** Chapters 1-2 are the capture and the arena; the middle chapters bring the Empire's attack; the climax is the titan fight and the bonus chapter rounds it off. Reviewers find the ending abrupt and wish for more (Binge Senpai; Manga Bookshelf).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Delos could kill Ogun but does not. Why does that win him more than a victory would?
2. Why did the beetle clan hate humans at first? Was their distrust fair?
3. Prome calls Delos's fighting style foolish. Is she right?
4. Some reviewers felt the jokes about Prome did not fit the story. What do you think a story owes its characters?
5. What would it mean to use your own strengths, at school or at home, the way Delos uses his?
