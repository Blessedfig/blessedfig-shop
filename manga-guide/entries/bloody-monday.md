**Premise.** Fujimaru Takagi, 16, is a lazy-seeming second-year in his high school's newspaper club. Secretly he is "Falcon," a genius hacker who breaks into the computers of corrupt people and exposes them, and who helps Third-i, a secret counter-terrorism section of Japan's Public Security Intelligence Agency where his father Ryunosuke is deputy chief. A Russian agent is found dead in Tokyo with a memory chip showing a virus outbreak in Russia that killed thousands. A doomsday religious cult, whose imprisoned leader Kamishima Shimon is on death row, plans to free him and to bring about "Bloody Monday." Maya Orihara, a mercenary who brought the "Bloody-X" virus from Russia, arrives at Fujimaru's school as the new biology teacher, replacing one whom Falcon had exposed for sexual harassment. Fujimaru's father is framed for murdering his boss; his sister Haruka, who needs dialysis, is kidnapped; a Third-i officer turns out to be a mole; a classmate is infected and killed; and the cult's real leader, "K," turns out to be a 15-year-old girl in the newspaper club, Mako Anzai, who plans to set off a stolen neutron bomb at Tokyo Tower so that destruction can bring rebirth (Wikipedia; Japanese Wikipedia; fan wiki).

**Where traditions disagree.** Almost every frame welcomes the hero's loyalty to family and his fight against terror. The divides are two. Second, religion: the only faith shown is a doomsday cult with biblical code names, which secular and Atheist frames read as a warning about certainty (A or B) and which some religious and Agnostic readers may find one-sided.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Whom can you trust when anyone might be the enemy? Fujimaru's father is framed, a trusted officer is a mole, a teacher is a terrorist, and the cult's leader sits beside him in the newspaper club.

**The answer the story gives.** Trust family and the friends who prove themselves, use your gifts for others, and expect betrayal. Fujimaru wins by wit and by refusing to give up on his sister and father; the cost is that some friends were never what they seemed.

**Characters as arguments.** K argues that the world must be destroyed to be reborn, and dies refusing to stop. Judas argues that the bomb's threat could bring revolution without killing, and K shoots him. Maya argues for nothing but money. J treats it all as a game and despises his father's faith. Ryunosuke argues for duty; Kujo Masamune for security at any cost. Fujimaru argues, mostly by action, that skill is for protecting people.

**Symbols and motifs.** Falcon, the hunter in the network; the memory chip; the virus as invisible betrayal; Tokyo Tower as the city's heart; biblical code names worn by terrorists; Haruka's dialysis machine, a reminder of fragile life.

**Traditions it echoes.** The hacker thriller and the ticking-bomb story (24, Die Hard), the Japanese school mystery of Kibayashi's Kindaichi Case Files, and the real fear of doomsday cults. Readers will notice the ticking-bomb argument for torture, which the story accepts without debate.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Pragmatism wins: cleverness beats force. Utilitarian methods are used by the state and go unquestioned. Nihilism, in the cult's creed, is the enemy. Patriotism is quiet service rather than ideology.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-4 set up Falcon, Maya and the framed father, and expose the mole (likely). Vols. 5-7 bring the school siege and the virus outbreak (likely). Vols. 8-11 cover the cult leader's escape and death, the hunt for the neutron bomb and K's unmasking at Tokyo Tower (vol. 11). The sequels jump ahead two and three years with new villains.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Fujimaru breaks into other people's computers to expose wrongdoing. When is that right, and when is it not?
2. Third-i tortures Maya to get information. Did that bother you? Should it have bothered the story?
3. K truly believed destruction would bring rebirth. Why is sincere belief not enough to make something right?
4. How did Fujimaru decide whom to trust?
5. Fujimaru still grieves K after everything she did. Why?
