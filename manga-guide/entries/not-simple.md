**Premise.** Jim, a freelance journalist, meets Ian, a young Australian drifter with an odd, open way about him, and resolves to turn his life into a novel called not simple. The book opens with the end: Ian, homeless and worn out, is stabbed at a train station on the orders of a jealous stepfather who mistakes him for his stepdaughter Irene's boyfriend, and Jim, arriving, stops Irene from touching his blood (prologue). Then it goes back. Ian's sister Kylie leaves prison; their father has divorced their alcoholic mother, who took Ian to London, where he runs errands for a butcher and she hands him over to a pimp. Kylie sends Ian home to Melbourne and promises to meet him when he beats a running record (early chapters). Years later Ian follows Kylie's trail to New York and London with Jim; Kylie dies of pneumonia in prison after trying to kill her boyfriend; Ian's mother tells him he was born of incest between Kylie and their father, and that she wants never to see him again; Ian learns that the boyfriend was the pimp who abused him as a child, and that he has been HIV positive since then (late chapters). The epilogue goes back to a night when Ian, traveling, persuades a young mother, Irene's mother, not to abandon her husband and baby, and promises to meet her again (Wikipedia; MangaUpdates; Ganriki).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What does a person need from his family, and what becomes of him when he never gets it? Ian is loved by one person, his sister Kylie, and failed by everyone else. The book asks whether that one love, and the love he keeps offering, is enough to make a life.

**The answer the story gives.** Not enough to save him, but enough to matter. Ian's last wish, Wikipedia records, is to feel the love of those who should be close to him. He does not get it. But in the epilogue, told last though it happened earlier, he talks a young mother out of abandoning her baby and her husband, so that the family he never had exists for someone else. The book refuses to say whether that redeems the rest.

**Characters as arguments.** Ian argues that you can be harmed without becoming hard; reviewers note his innocence and lack of resentment. Kylie argues for fierce love that turns to crime. Ian's mother argues that a child can be blamed for an adult's unhappiness, and the book condemns her by showing her plainly. Jim argues for witness: he watches, listens and writes, and the question of whether he should have done more hangs over the ending (MyAnimeList reader BlueKite).

**Symbols and motifs.** Chewing gum, a pimp's reward and later the sign of what was done (Wikipedia). Running: the record Ian must beat before he can see Kylie again. Trains and stations, where the story begins and ends. The title: a line in the book calls Ian hard to grasp, not simple (PopMatters), and Jim's novel takes that name.

**Traditions it echoes.** The tragic picaresque, the story told backwards from a death, and the novel about a novel. PopMatters places Ian alongside Zen and D.T. Suzuki's idea of eternal loneliness; Christian readers may hear Psalm 27:10 in a son forsaken by father and mother.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Existentialism frames Ian's acceptance of a life without reasons. Postmodernism shapes the telling. Communitarianism is affirmed by absence: family is what everyone needs and almost no one gives. Nihilism is a temptation the epilogue resists, if barely.

**Shifts across the book.** Prologue: Ian's death and the novel's publication. Early chapters: Kylie's release, London, the mother and the pimp, Ian sent home. Middle chapters: Jim's interviews, New York, Rick, Kylie's arrest. Late chapters: Kylie's death, the incest revelation, the pimp, HIV. Epilogue: the night with Irene's mother on the beach (Wikipedia).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Ian is hurt by almost every adult in his life but does not become cruel. Why not?
2. What could have been done for Ian when he was a child in London? Who should have done it?
3. Jim spends years listening to Ian. Should he have done more than write a book?
4. Why does the book show Ian's death first?
