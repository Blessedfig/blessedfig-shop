**Premise.** A hundred years ago, the hero Prophet Sid led humanity to victory in a war among five races (humans, demons, the "celestials" such as angels and elves, spirits, and magical beasts), and sealed the other four in black monoliths. Kai Sakura-Vento, a 17-year-old soldier who guards one of them, sees the world rewritten before his eyes: in the new history Sid never existed, humanity lost, and the survivors hide in shelters under the rule of other races. No one, not even his childhood friend Jeanne, remembers Kai. Inside the monolith he finds Sid's sword, Codeholder, and frees Rinne, a chained girl with angel and demon wings who is an outcast from every race and who also remembers the true world (anime ep. 1-2; Wikipedia). With Jeanne, who now leads the human resistance disguised as a man, Kai defeats the demon hero Vanessa, a succubus (ep. 3-4); the angel hero Alfreya, who has declared all other races unneeded (ep. 6-8); and allies with the spirit hero Kyoko against the mysterious "Last Riser," an embodiment of hatred that kills anyone who remembers Sid (ep. 9-12). Later volumes reveal that the world was rewritten by a darker Rinne from the altered history, who hates every race that rejected her kind and seeks a world where all life vanishes the moment it is born; Kai defeats her and reaches a third world in which the great war ended in a draw and all races, Rinne's included, live together under a pact. In that world no one remembers what Kai did; he sets out to find Rinne and is reunited with her (Japanese Wikipedia, character notes for Kai and Rinne; light-novel ending; manga ending to verify).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** If everyone forgot you, who would you be, and would you still do the right thing? Kai loses his whole place in the world in a moment. The series asks whether identity depends on others' memory, and whether goodness needs a witness.

**The answer the story gives.** Goodness does not need to be remembered. Kai becomes the hero himself (Wikipedia plot), and in the end saves every race and is remembered by none (Japanese Wikipedia). The real enemy was never a race: it was hatred, first named by the dying Alfreya (ep. 8) and embodied in Last Riser (ep. 11), born from a people rejected by everyone.

**Characters as arguments.** Kai argues for acceptance without conditions. Rinne, rejected by all five races, argues that the outcast deserves a home; her darker self argues what rejection can become. Alfreya's avatar argues for supremacy and is destroyed; the real Alfreya is fair-minded and helps (Japanese Wikipedia). Vanessa argues for strength, yet keeps Sid's secret (ep. 4). Jeanne argues for duty and becomes humanity's new prophet (ep. 10). Dante argues for rank and is humbled.

**Symbols and motifs.** The monolith (the "crypt"). Codeholder, a sword that cuts between worlds. Rinne's two wings, one angelic and one demonic. Chains (ep. 1, 12). Memory and forgetting. The spring in the primordial forest where Kai finds Rinne at the end (Japanese Wikipedia).

**Traditions it echoes.** The forgotten hero who acts without reward; parallel-world and rewritten-history fantasy; the "mixed blood" outcast who unites peoples. Its "hatred is never appeased by hatred" theme echoes the Dhammapada for Buddhist readers and "love your enemies" for Christian readers, though the story does not cite either.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Nationalism in its hateful form and Might makes right are critiqued; existential questions of identity are explored; the pact of coexistence (Liberalism) is the happy ending. Anti-natalism and nihilism appear only as the villain's goal and are defeated.

**Shifts across the series.** Anime ep. 1-4 (about manga vol. 1-3, to verify): the rewritten world and the demon arc. Ep. 5-8: the angels and elves, Alfreya's madness. Ep. 9-12 (to about ch. 48, vol. 11): the spirits, the goddess, Last Riser. Manga vol. 12-14 (ch. 49-65): Rath=IE, the beast hero, and the final war with the darker Rinne (Japanese Wikipedia; chapter placement to verify).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Kai keeps fighting even though no one remembers him. Would you?
2. Why is Rinne rejected by every race? What does the story say happens to people who are always rejected?
3. Alfreya thinks angels are better than everyone else. How does the story answer him?
4. Is it fair that Kai's deeds are never remembered?
5. What makes the final peace possible: strength, or something else?
