**Premise.** Taizou Kuzuki, a former "intellectual yakuza" who kept the books for a gang until he could no longer stay in Japan, checks into room 805 of the run-down Voynich Hotel on Blefuscu, a tiny Pacific island nation scarred by Spanish conquest, civil war and landmines (Japanese Wikipedia; Seven Seas). The hotel is run by a masked former Mexican wrestler who quietly funds war orphans, staffed by two maids, Elena and Berna, and a suicidal cook, Emilia, and full of guests who are hitmen, drug dealers, an undercover drug agent and a manga artist. In short, linked episodes the island's secrets come out: Elena is one of the "Three Mothers," ancient witches who fought a Spanish army; a serial killer named Snark, who sold her soul to a demon for three wishes, is murdering islanders; a children's detective club investigates; ghosts of the dead keep returning because of the witches' curse; and a new drug, Mercello, spreads through the island. Taizou grows close to Elena. When hired killers come for him, his new friends, above all the witches, defend him (Wikipedia; Japanese Wikipedia; AniList reviews).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can people who have done terrible things, or had terrible things done to them, find a place to belong? Everyone at the hotel is running from something: Taizou from the yakuza, Emilia from grief, the witches from centuries of war.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, but only among each other, and not without blood. The hotel becomes a family that defends Taizou (Wikipedia), and love grows in unlikely pairs (Taizou and Elena; Hakase and the dead witch Tenebrarum). The story also lets greed and killing-for-pay meet grim ends.

**Characters as arguments.** Snark argues that you can buy what you want; the demon pact costs her life and soul, though she apologizes to her sister before she goes (AniList review). Haraki argues for escape through drugs and then for recovery. The Mamiya sisters argue for killing without limits and lose. Elena argues for love that will do anything, including killing, which the story treats as heroic.

**Symbols and motifs.** The hotel on a sacred forest; artificial limbs and eyes (landmines; Elena's glass eye, a gift from Taizou); masks (the manager's wrestling mask, Alice's rabbit mask that lets her speak); the undeciphered manuscript of the title.

**Traditions it echoes.** Manga-News compared it to Kōji Kumeta and Tim Burton (Wikipedia); Seven Seas' "Hotel California" allusion; the Faust bargain in Snark's pact; Lewis Carroll (Snark, Alice) and Gulliver's Travels (Blefuscu) in its names (from general knowledge; to verify).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Absurdism is the governing mood. Pagan/occult elements are real and heroic. Critical theory appears as satire of war and colonial damage. Hedonism is enjoyed and then charged for (addiction, overdose).

**Shifts across the series.** Volume 1 (Days 1-26) introduces the hotel and guests; volume 2 (Days 27-50) the serial killer, the detective club and the witches' history; volume 3 (Days 51-67 and Last Days) the hitwomen sent for Taizou and the resolution (Wikipedia; placement of events partly inferred).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why do you think the author jokes about things like murder and suicide? Does it work?
2. Snark gets three wishes from a demon. What did they cost her?
3. Elena kills to protect Taizou. Does the story want us to cheer? Should we?
4. The story says Elena is centuries old, but she is drawn as a young girl. How should readers think about that?
5. What makes the hotel a family?
