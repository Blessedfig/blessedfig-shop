**Premise.** Kogarashi Fuyuzora, 16, has been plagued since birth by spirits that possess him; it left him in debt, in and out of orphanages and homeless, until a master of the Yatahagane line trained him to punch spirits away (Wikipedia; ANN). He rents a cheap room at the Yuragi Inn, a former hot-spring inn, planning to exorcise its ghost for free rent, but the ghost is Yuuna, a kind girl with no memory of her life who cannot pass on. When a monk tries to exorcise her by force, Kogarashi protects her and promises to help her find her unfinished business (ch. 1, fan wiki). The other residents are supernatural: the innkeeper Chitose, a zashiki-warashi who looks about 13; Sagiri, a demon-slaying ninja; Nonko, a hard-drinking oni manga artist; Yaya, host of a cat god; later a tanuki girl, a kitsune, a tengu heiress and a dragon-clan woman who all fall for him, plus his classmate Chisaki. Late arcs reveal that Yuuna is a clone of Mahoro Tenko, made by Mahoro's father Byakuei in a forbidden bid to save his daughter, and tied to the soul-devouring Garandou; Kogarashi dies protecting the inn and is brought back. When Yuuna regains her memories and is ready to pass on, Kogarashi asks her to stay and binds her to the world; they marry (Wikipedia; fan wiki; The Otaku Author).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What do you owe a ghost? Kogarashi arrives to exorcise the inn's spirit for free rent, a job he is trained for. Yuuna turns out to be a frightened, kind girl who does not know why she cannot leave.

**The answer the story gives.** Mercy first. Kogarashi refuses to punch her out of the world and promises to help her pass on happily (ch. 1). Over 24 volumes that promise turns into love: when Yuuna finally remembers who she is and is ready to go, he asks her to stay and binds her to the world, and they marry (The Otaku Author; Wikipedia).

**Characters as arguments.** Kogarashi argues for compassion backed by strength. The monk who exorcises without asking argues for force and is defeated (ch. 1). Byakuei argues that a father may do anything to defeat death; his clones and the soul-eating Garandou show where that leads (fan wiki). Sagiri and Oboro argue for duty and bloodline, and soften into love. Karura argues for taking what you want, and ends bitter (TV Tropes).

**Symbols and motifs.** The hot spring: warmth, cleansing and, in this genre, exposure. The ghost's triangular white headcloth (fan wiki). Yuuna's lost memories as the unfinished business every person carries. The bond that keeps her here.

**Traditions it echoes.** Jump harem comedy (To Love Ru, which reviewers compare it to; Wikipedia), Japanese ghost stories of spirits held by regret, and yokai folklore. Buddhist readers will see the idea of helping a spirit let go; the ending's choice to hold on is a romantic reversal of it.

**How each lens is portrayed.** The spirit world is the setting, treated with affection. Community and loyalty are celebrated. Hedonism is the medium, and the story never questions it. Immortality bought with souls is the series' clearest evil.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-5 (ch. 1-36, the anime) introduce the residents and episodic comedy. Middle volumes add new suitors and clan arcs (Karura, Oboro's dragon clan, Sagiri's engagement). Late volumes turn serious: Ouga's return, the truth about Yuuna as a clone tied to Garandou, Kogarashi's death and revival, and the ending in v24 (fan wiki; TV Tropes).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Kogarashi could have exorcised Yuuna and lived rent-free. Why didn't he?
2. The monk wanted to remove Yuuna without asking her. What is wrong with that?
3. Many jokes are about Kogarashi accidentally seeing or touching girls. Is it funny if he did not choose it? What about for the girls?
4. Byakuei made clones to save his daughter. Can love for a child become wrong?
5. At the end Kogarashi keeps Yuuna in the world. Is that loving her, or holding on?
