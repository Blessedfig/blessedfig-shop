**Premise.** In present-day Japan, a secret state agency, the Ninja Public Security Force (NPSF), removes threats to the peace. Its young ace Yodaka (alias Yu Nozuki), from Squad No. 9, is supremely skilled but has crippling social anxiety. He is ordered to guard Aoi Mukai, a cheerful, clumsy 15-year-old heiress (fan wiki) who is orphaned and targeted because her family's blood boosts ninja powers. Posing as a first-year student, he works alongside squadmates Hibari (posing as her best friend) and Umineko (assigned to become her boyfriend), and talks with his handler, Operator No. 9, through a bird-shaped earpiece. Aoi befriends him first, even defending him from bullies (ch. 1-7). Fugitive ninja attack at a mall, a school camp and a summer festival (ch. 8-51); rival squads compete to replace Squad 9 (ch. 35-43). Yodaka falls for Aoi (ch. 44-51), loses and regains his confidence, and with his squad raids the powerful Uzen clan to rescue a turned ally, Kasasagi (ch. 61-87). At the culture festival he defeats the fugitive Kawasemi and dances with Aoi (ch. 88-102), and is then led away by a senior ninja for an "execution" that turns out to be a ruse (ch. 103-106) (Wikipedia volume summaries).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Are other people a weight that slows you down, or the thing that makes you strong? Yodaka is the best ninja of his age and cannot hold a conversation. His enemies say attachments drag you down; his assignment forces him to make friends.

**The answer the story gives.** Bonds are strength. In ch. 11 Yodaka beats a renegade who believes the weak cling to the strong, by braiding the man's own threads together (scrmbl). Later he learns from Mimizuku to set feelings aside in a fight (ch. 35-43), but in the end he wins because he protects Aoi out of love, which even his rival Kawasemi recognizes (ch. 96-99).

**Characters as arguments.** Yodaka argues that courage can grow alongside fear. Aoi argues for plain kindness: she defends the boy who is secretly defending her (ch. 1-7). Hibari shows a fierce person behind a friendly mask. Kasasagi shows how debt to a clan can turn a friend into an enemy, and how friends can win her back (ch. 70-87). Yoichi, a child locked away by his father, shows the cost of a family that uses people; Yodaka sees his own loneliness in him (ch. 79-87).

**Symbols and motifs.** Bird code names (the nightjar, a bird of the night). The earpiece voice of Operator 9 as conscience and coach. Aoi's blood, coveted as a power source: a person treated as a resource. The dance where two clumsy people move freely (ch. 100-102).

**Traditions it echoes.** The ninja story (the series is compared to Naruto, Screen Rant via Wikipedia), the bodyguard romantic comedy, and Weekly Shonen Jump's friendship-and-effort tradition.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarianism wins plainly. Duty is respected but not enough on its own. The agency's cold hierarchy is questioned, though not overthrown so far.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-4 (ch. 1-34): school comedy with fights; Yodaka learns friendship. Vols. 5-6 (ch. 35-51): squad rivalry and the festival attack. Vols. 7-10 (ch. 52-87): Kasasagi and the Uzen clan, darker violence and clan politics. Ch. 88-106 (uncollected): the culture festival, the fight with Kawasemi, the dance and a cliffhanger.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Yodaka is great at fighting and terrible at talking. Which is harder for you?
2. Hachikuma thinks other people slow you down. Why does the story say he is wrong?
3. Is it right that Aoi's friends were assigned to be her friends? Can those friendships still be real?
4. Why does Yodaka want to save Yoichi, who was dangerous?
5. When should you follow orders, and when should you follow your heart?
