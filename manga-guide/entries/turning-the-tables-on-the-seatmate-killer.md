**Premise.** High schooler Yuuki Narito gets a new seatmate, the popular Yui Takatsuki, known as the "Seatmate Killer" because every boy who sits next to her falls for her and is turned down. Stoic Yuuki assumes her friendliness is a game and refuses to be caught, so the two trade romantic one-upmanship. The story reveals that Yui is misunderstood: her playful manner, learned through manzai comedy, is read as flirting, and she falls for Yuuki first (ANN Fall 2022 guide; Manga Bookshelf). Yuuki's mother died three years earlier, and he cares for his younger sister Mina, a shut-in with trauma from elementary school who says she will make a friend once he has a girlfriend, so Yui and Yuuki pretend to date (ANN vols. 2-5; Manga Bookshelf). Later volumes add Yui's friend Rio (the "seatmate breaker"), a former "victim" named Hanashiro, and Sayo, who blames Yui for her brother's sudden interest in girls (vol. 5). The manga ends without a confession (MyAnimeList reviews).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Is the girl everyone calls a heartbreaker really playing with people, or is everyone misreading her?

**The answer the story gives.** She is misread: her manzai-trained playfulness looks like flirting, and the boys' heartbreak was their own projection (ANN Fall 2022 guide). Sincerity, not winning, is what matters.

**Characters as arguments.** Yuuki argues for steady honesty and for duty to family; Yui argues that people are more than their reputations; Mina shows that grief can hide behind jokes; Sayo's blame of Yui for her brother's adolescence (vol. 5) shows how rumors grow from misunderstanding.

**Symbols and motifs.** The seat next to Yui; the fake couple; T-shirts with jokes on them.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Japanese teasing rom-com; for faith readers, teaching against gossip and rash judgment.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Little ideology: care for family is affirmed, and the fan service sits uneasily beside the serious grief theme (ANN).

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1 sets up the contest and Mina; vol. 2 the fake relationship and Rio; vols. 3-4 more comedy and rivals; vol. 5 Sayo's reveal and an open ending.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why did everyone believe Yui was the "Seatmate Killer"?
2. Was the fake relationship a kind thing or a lie? Could Yuuki have helped Mina another way?
3. Why do you think Mina stopped going out?
4. Some readers find the jokes about Mina's underwear uncomfortable. Why might that be?
5. Have you ever been judged by a rumor? What helped?
