**Premise.** Hibari, a serious university art student who wants to be a graphic designer, worries that her work is plain and that she is "ordinary." After a chaotic run-in at a ramen shop she meets Gaku, a free-spirited guitarist at her university whose band is Pantera Negra; he falls for her at once and pursues her openly (vol. 1). She plants a drunken kiss on him that he will not let her forget (vol. 2), and by vol. 3 they are officially dating, while Rihito, her childhood friend and a popular rival musician, wants more than friendship. Gaku pushes Hibari to enter the university art festival; her friend Airi confesses to Gaku's bandmate Takeru (vol. 4). At the festival Hibari makes her first sale, worries about Gaku going viral on social media, and gets a public shout-out from the stage (vol. 5). Hibari takes a part-time job at a design firm run by Matsunaga, a lead from the author's earlier series; the band plays for a record label with Rihito's last-minute help, and Gaku is uneasy about how they got there (vols. 5-6) (Seven Seas descriptions; reader reviews).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can a person who runs her life by plan let something unplanned in, and is the chaos good for her? Hibari fears she is ordinary; Gaku sees her as special from the first day.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, if the chaos comes with care. Gaku's confidence gets Hibari to show her work, enter the festival and take a real job (vols. 4-6), but the story also shows his carelessness hurting her and him apologizing (vol. 5). The best moments are when they talk instead of guessing (vol. 3, reader report).

**Characters as arguments.** Hibari argues for diligence and caution; Gaku for living by feeling. Rihito is the rival who cannot let go; a reader notes he keeps a kind of code by helping when it counts. Airi shows that courage in love is not only the heroine's.

**Symbols and motifs.** Color and design (Hibari's "plain" work); the stage as freedom and, later, as a trap for Gaku (vol. 6, reader report); the ramen shop where they meet.

**Traditions it echoes.** The opposites-attract romance; the artist's coming-of-age; the band story of selling out versus staying true.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism is affirmed but balanced: freedom without care is shown to hurt. Pragmatic compromise in the music business is questioned (vol. 6).

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-2: meeting and the first kiss. Vol. 3: dating, the rival and the near-sex scene. Vols. 4-5: the art festival, viral fame and a design job. Vol. 6: the record label and Hibari's new opportunity.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Hibari worries she is ordinary. What does "ordinary" mean, and is it bad?
2. Gaku pursues Hibari very openly. When is that romantic, and when is it too much?
3. Their first kiss happened when Hibari was drunk. How did that change things between them?
4. How does each of them help the other toward a dream?
5. The band gets a chance because of help from a rival. Should Gaku be uneasy about that?
