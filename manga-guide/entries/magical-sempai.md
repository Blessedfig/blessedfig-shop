**Premise.** An unmotivated first-year boy at a school where clubs are mandatory wanders into the room of the Magic Club, whose only member, a third-year girl known only as Sempai, is a skilled magician when alone but has crippling stage fright. With anyone watching, her tricks fail, often leaving her in embarrassing, revealing situations. She drafts him as her "Assistant" (ch. 1-9). Her older sister, his homeroom teacher, becomes club advisor (ch. 9); Madara, the science-minded Chemistry Club president, lets him join her club as cover (ch. 31, 53); the gyaru transfer student Saki and her brother Masashi join after Saki loses a breast-size contest with Sempai (ch. 33-35) (Wikipedia; fan wiki). Chapters are short gag strips (each chapter splits into several mini-stories) built around real magic tricks, many with a note on the trick's history (Tiro Finale). Sempai is 17 (fan wiki, as of ch. 47); by the end she is a college student (fan wiki infobox).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Why can someone do a thing perfectly alone and fail the moment anyone watches? Sempai's stage fright is the engine; the Assistant is the audience she must learn to perform for.

**The answer the story gives.** Keep showing up. Failure in front of a friend is survivable, and a patient audience of one becomes a club. The deeper answer is never spelled out because the comedy needs her to keep failing.

**Characters as arguments.** Sempai argues for passion over polish. The Assistant argues for patience, and his private attraction shows the series' other, less innocent, interest. Madara argues for science and modesty and is mocked for prudishness. Saki argues for unashamed showmanship.

**Symbols and motifs.** The top hat and cape; the box she cannot escape; doves that will not obey; the empty classroom where tricks succeed.

**Traditions it echoes.** Stage-magic history (each trick explained); the Japanese "teasing senpai" romantic comedy; slapstick.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Hedonism, in the form of sexual teasing, is endorsed as the point; naturalism is quietly present in the explained tricks; community grows as the cast does.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1 (ch. 1-15) introduces the pair; vols. 2-3 (ch. 16-46) add the sister and Madara and turn up the fan service (Tiro Finale; Japanese Wikipedia); vols. 3-5 (ch. 32-78) add Saki and Masashi and the summer chapters; vols. 6-8 (ch. 79-128) carry the club to graduation (fan wiki: Sempai later a college student).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why can Sempai do her tricks alone but not with someone watching? Have you felt that?
2. The jokes are often about Sempai being embarrassed. Is that fair to her?
3. What does the Assistant do that makes him a good friend?
4. Madara says magic tricks fool people into believing in the supernatural. Is she right?
