**Premise.** Tatsu Yunoki, 23, an office worker who loves long hair, wakes up in bed beside a woman he does not know: Kotoka Hatsushima, a 23-year-old writer who loves backs. Under the influence of alcohol, the night before, they agreed to date, because each has exactly what the other adores (vol. 1; Kodansha; MangaUpdates). Told mostly in four-panel gag strips, Part 1 (ch. 1-73, vols. 1-5) follows the couple living together, indulging and teasing each other's fetishes, meeting friends and family with fetishes of their own (Tatsu's sisters, a co-worker), and growing into a real relationship: in vol. 5 a younger friend, Mashizu, confesses to Tatsu while Kotoka has gone back to her parents' home, and the drama turns on whether they can make things right (Kodansha vol. 5; AniList review). A time skip shows them later with a daughter (AniList character list, marked as a spoiler; to verify). Part 2 (ch. 74-145, vols. 6-9) starts a new story about three second-year high-school students: Mugi, drawn to scents; Naoi, obsessed with hair; and Miori, who adores small and cute things, in a love triangle, with Tatsu and Kotoka in cameo roles (Kodansha vol. 6; AniList review).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can love begin with a fetish? Tatsu and Kotoka start where most romances end, in bed, and from the narrowest reason: each has the feature the other craves. The series asks whether that can grow into love for a person.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, but only by growing. According to the AniList reviewer, the final arc of Part 1 is the couple learning to see each other as more than hair and back, and it is the part readers found most rewarding. A rival's confession and a separation (vol. 5) test whether the bond is more than taste.

**Characters as arguments.** Kotoka argues for being unashamed of what you want. Tatsu argues for embarrassment turned into honesty. Mashizu, who confesses to Tatsu, argues that love is also choice and timing. Part 2's teenagers repeat the experiment at an earlier age.

**Symbols and motifs.** Long hair and a bare back: the fetishes that start everything. The shared apartment. The four-panel strip that breaks into a full page when a moment matters.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Japanese 4-koma romantic comedy; the old question of eros versus agape, desire versus self-giving love; and a common sitcom shape in which a couple moves from comic chaos to settled family.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism is affirmed: quirks are accepted, not cured. Hedonism is enjoyed and then shown to be insufficient. No religion or politics appears.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-2 are gags and introductions; volumes 3-4 deepen the fetish comedy and the cast; volume 5 turns to drama and resolution (Mashizu's confession, Kotoka's return). Volumes 6-9 begin again with high-school students.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Tatsu and Kotoka start dating because each likes one thing about the other. What did they have to learn to stay together?
2. Their relationship began after a night of drinking. What might have gone wrong?
3. Is it kind or unkind to love someone for one feature?
4. How is the high-school story different from the adult story, and should it be?
5. What does it mean to accept someone's quirks?
