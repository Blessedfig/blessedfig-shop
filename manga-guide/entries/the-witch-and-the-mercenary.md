**Premise.** On the Old Continent witches are feared and hunted, and the mercenary Zig Crane, who fights with a rare double-bladed weapon, joins a hunt for Siasha, the "Silent Witch," an earth-magic user over 200 years old (Seven Seas and Wikipedia call her Saoirse; AniList; fan wiki). Their duel ends in a stalemate, and she hires him instead to guard her: both are people forced to become killers by the world they live in (Wikipedia). They sail to the New Continent, where magic is common, adventurers have a guild and dangerous "demon beasts" roam, only to find that here it is mercenaries, not witches, who are distrusted (ch. 1-5, Wikipedia; fan wiki). Siasha climbs the adventurer ranks while trying not to draw notice (v2, ch. 6-13); Zig is attacked by an adventurer in an alley (v3) and meets Lyell, his old mercenary comrade and adoptive brother, who hates the witch, and the two fight (v4, ch. 21-28); Isana asks him to look into disappearances among the demi-human tribes (v4); Zig is suspected of a string of attacks on adventurers (v5, ch. 29-37). The light novels go on to a clash between humans and demi-humans, a drug trade fought with the help of a mafia family, and a religion, Claritism, that teaches demi-humans are subhuman, whose church Zig and Siasha storm (light-novel vols. 3-5, PRH blurbs); where these fall in the manga is not confirmed.

## Life message

**Two people the world treats as disposable earn a place to live by keeping their word to each other; people should be judged by what they do, not by what they are.**

| Meta lens | How it reads the series |
|---|---|
| Evolutionary biology | A survival partnership: a strong fighter and a powerful but hunted woman trade protection for pay and form a bond. Groups (witch-hunters, humans against demi-humans, mafia families) close ranks against outsiders, and the story shows how fear of the other turns to violence. |
| Pragmatic | Zig treats survival like a science: he gathers information, prepares for the worst and keeps strict neutrality (fan wiki). Contracts, credibility and competence are the currency; Silverman (ANN, light novel) calls him honorable but flexible about killing for money. |
| Religious | No real-world religion appears. Witches are hunted on the Old Continent; on the New Continent a fictional religion, Claritism, holds that demi-humans are subhuman, and its confessor Yaesar is an antagonist (light-novel vol. 5 blurb; manga placement to verify). Faith readers weigh a sympathetic witch with innate magic and a religious order shown as a source of prejudice against the story's defense of the persecuted. |
| Socio-cultural | A story about prejudice and its reversal: the witch who was feared becomes an honored adventurer, while the mercenary who was useful becomes the suspect. One MyAnimeList reviewer singles out its handling of racism and discrimination. The pairing of a stoic bodyguard and a sheltered, powerful charge is a familiar Japanese fantasy pattern. |

Outlook: Hopeful — the pair escape a land that hunted her, find work, allies and a home on the new continent, and each arc ends with them surviving and better trusted, though the world remains violent and prejudiced.
Hope source: P — the way through is protecting others: Zig's promise to guard Siasha is the spine of the story, and their widening network of allies (F) is the close second.

**Where traditions disagree.** Nearly every frame welcomes the story's defense of the persecuted, its rejection of mob fear and its loyalty between partners. The split is over the heroine and the hero's trade: Baptist, Pentecostal, Adventist, Jehovah's Witness, Orthodox Jewish and Muslim readings weigh a sympathetic "witch" and killing for pay heavily (C or D), Anabaptist readers object to violence as a livelihood, while Pagan, humanist, progressive and Atheist readers see a plain story about prejudice (A). The later religious villain (Claritism) is fictional and condemned for its contempt of others, not for faith as such, but families should know a church is stormed (light-novel vol. 5; manga placement to verify).

## Creator and publisher context

- Kaeru Chōhōkiteki (original story) posted the series on the user-run novel site Shōsetsuka ni Narō from June 2021; Micro Magazine picked it up for its GCN Bunko light-novel imprint in 2023 (Wikipedia). No interview with the author was found.
- Makoto Miyagi (manga art), in a K Manga blog interview: draws battle scenes with deliberately rough strokes, saying "I try to make sure that the lines aren't too perfect"; wants Zig to look as imposing as possible and Siasha both beautiful and terrifying when she uses her full power; found the role reversal between the two continents the story's most interesting idea; names Golden Kamuy, Space Brothers, Summer Time Rendering and Magi among favorite series.
- Bench Kanase illustrates the light novel and is credited with the character designs; he also illustrated Putting a Price on World Peace (Wikipedia).
- No statements on religion or politics by the creators were found. **Status: Needs more info on the creators.**
- Publisher: Kodansha (Magazine Pocket, Shōnen Magazine Comics), with English chapters on Kodansha's K Manga app. Seven Seas publishes the light novel (Airship imprint) and will publish the manga in print from April 2027, both rated Older Teen 15+.
- No controversies involving the creators were found.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What do you do when the world has decided you are disposable? Siasha is hunted because she is a witch; Zig is paid to hunt her because killing is the only trade he has. In the first chapter they recognize each other, and the series asks whether two people the world treats as tools can build a life worth living.

**The answer the story gives.** Keep your word, trust carefully, and go where you can live. Zig's answer is professional: a contract honored is a kind of dignity. Siasha's answer is a quiet life. The new continent tests both: here witches are welcome and mercenaries are suspect, so the prejudice that hunted her now falls on him (Miyagi calls this reversal the story's heart, K Manga).

**Characters as arguments.** Zig argues for prudence and neutrality: stay out of other people's wars, gather facts, keep promises. Siasha argues for the right of the feared to live in peace, and her growth is learning to live among people (MyAnimeList review). Lyell, Zig's adoptive brother, carries the old continent's view of the witch into the new land (v4). Isana and the demi-human tribes stand for those whom the city despises; Claritism and its confessor Yaesar argue that some people are not people at all, and the story sets itself firmly against them (light-novel vol. 5).

**Symbols and motifs.** Zig's double-bladed weapon, which can cut either way. The sea crossing as a second birth. The guild card and its rank as a public verdict on a person's worth. Earth and stone, Siasha's magic, as power that could crush or shelter.

**Traditions it echoes.** The ronin or sellsword who finds a cause, from Yojimbo to Berserk-style dark fantasy (from general knowledge; to verify); the witch hunt as a picture of mob fear; and the immigrant's experience of finding that the new land has its own prejudices.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Pragmatism wins most fights. Duty ethics shows in Zig's refusal to break a contract. Might makes right is explored and then limited: power protects, but persecution of the weak is condemned. Religious readers will notice that the only religion shown in later arcs is a fictional doctrine of contempt, presented as a problem to be fought.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-2 (ch. 1-13): the duel, the crossing and Siasha's first adventures. Volumes 3-5 (ch. 14-37): Zig's past arrives in Lyell, the tribes' missing people, and suspicion falls on Zig. Later volumes likely move into the human and demi-human conflict, the drug trade and Claritism, following light-novel vols. 3-5 (to verify).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Zig was paid to kill Siasha. Why did he change his mind, and was it only about money?
2. On the new continent the hated person is Zig, not Siasha. What does that show about prejudice?
3. Is staying neutral in other people's fights wise, or can it be a way of not caring?
4. What makes a promise worth keeping when it puts you in danger?
5. Claritism calls some people less than human. Where have people said that in real history, and what happened?
