**Premise.** Daiki Hondo, a kind high-school boy who cannot say no to anyone, tells his friends his type is a girl with long black hair who carries herself well. The next day Kei Shimizu, the blonde "yankee" (delinquent) who sits beside him and has the scariest reputation in school, comes in with her hair dyed black, blushing (ch. 1; Kadokawa vol. 1). Each time Daiki mentions what he likes, she quietly does it: she turns up for the cooking class she always skipped, practices his favorite dish at home, and shares one of the two lunches she brought when he forgets his wallet (vol. 1; The Outerhaven). Daiki does not remember that he once helped her (The Outerhaven). Her older sister Ai, the student council vice president, teases her and plays matchmaker; Daiki's younger sister Kino sends him on errands. In vol. 2 a chance meeting at a mall turns into a day that feels like a date, and Daiki's own feelings begin to change (Kadokawa vol. 2). In vol. 3 Ai recruits Daiki into the astronomy club, and his feelings for Kei grow (Kadokawa vol. 3). The novel goes on to a summer job, a club camp, a summer festival and a school-festival play starring the two (novel; manga coverage not confirmed). The Outerhaven notes that Kei never actually acts like a delinquent: her only rule-breaking is her hair.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can you know someone from their reputation? Everyone fears Kei; Daiki sits next to her every day and does not know she likes him.

**The answer the story gives.** No: Kei is shy, earnest and kind, and her "delinquent" look hid that. The story shows her in small, steady acts: a dyed head of hair, a class attended, a lunch shared.

**Characters as arguments.** Kei argues that love is shown in effort. Daiki argues for kindness to everyone, even at the cost of being used (his sister's errands). Ai argues for nudging people toward happiness.

**Symbols and motifs.** Black hair as a sign of change and devotion; the bento; the astronomy club's night sky (vol. 3).

**Traditions it echoes.** The Japanese "gap moe" romantic comedy (a tough exterior, a soft heart) and the classic story of the misjudged outsider.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Kindness and community are affirmed. The idea of changing yourself for someone is shown as sweet, not questioned.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1 (ch. 1-5): the hair, cooking class and lunch. Vol. 2 (ch. 6-10): a day like a date. Vol. 3 (ch. 11-15): the astronomy club. Vol. 4 (ch. 16-22): not confirmed; the novel's summer events and school-festival play may appear.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why did everyone think Kei was scary? Were they right?
2. Kei changes her hair and habits to match Daiki's type. Is that romantic, or is something lost?
3. Daiki cannot say no to anyone. Is that a strength or a weakness?
4. What small things show someone cares about you?
5. Have you ever been wrong about someone because of their reputation?
