**Premise.** Ryōta Sakamoto, 22, unemployed, shut in his room and violent toward his stepfather, is one of the world's top players of the online bomb-fighting game Btooom! (fan wiki). He wakes on a tropical island with a chip in his hand and a bag of bombs (BIMs): someone in his life "nominated" him to disappear (his mother), and he must collect eight chips from other players, which means killing them, to leave (Wikipedia; fan wiki). He meets Himiko, a high school girl (15 per the fan wiki) who fears all men after being betrayed and nearly assaulted; she turns out to be his in-game wife. With the older Kansai man Taira, they try to survive without killing more than they must, against a sadistic boy (Kōsuke Kira), a war veteran, a treacherous doctor (Date) and Ryōta's former best friend Oda. Outside, investigators uncover that the game's developer serves a secret foundation led by Himiko's own father, which tests the players for a plan to rule the world through the virtual realm. The final chapter has two versions: in the light ending Ryōta survives and plans to marry Himiko; in the dark ending he dies to save her and the others. In both, Himiko kills her father with a gas bomb and ends his organization (Wikipedia).

## Life message

**Trust and cooperation, not killing, are the way to survive a world built to make people kill each other, and even a shut-in can change by choosing to protect someone.**

Outlook: Bittersweet — most players die, Ryōta's mother and Taira take their own lives, and the dark ending kills Ryōta, but the light ending (which the sequel follows) gives Ryōta and Himiko a future together, and the foundation is destroyed in both.
Hope source: F — the way through is trust between allies: Ryōta survives by making and keeping alliances (Taira, Himiko, finally Kira), and the story rewards those who trust; love between Ryōta and Himiko (L) is the close second.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** When the rules reward killing, what kind of person will you be? Ryōta was a hero only in a game and a failure at home; on the island his gaming skill becomes real power, and he must decide whom to trust and whether to kill.

**The answer the story gives.** Trust, at real risk, is what lets anyone survive. Multiple survivors are possible only if players cooperate (ICv2); those who betray or prey (Akechi, Date) die, and Ryōta's alliances carry him to the end. He also learns to hold back: he refuses to take advantage of the frightened Himiko (fan wiki).

**Characters as arguments.** Ryōta argues that a person can change. Himiko argues that trust must be earned after betrayal. Taira is the warning that despair can undo a good man. Oda is the case for cold rational killing, and Ryōta's old wound. Kira, abused and violent, shows that a cruel boy may still be saved. The foundation argues that the strong should control the many.

**Symbols and motifs.** The chip in the hand as a price on each life; the in-game marriage that becomes real; the timer bomb Himiko holds as her last defense; the island as a world without law.

**Traditions it echoes.** Battle Royale, Lord of the Flies, The Hunger Games and .hack (ICv2); the prodigal son who comes home changed; the thought experiment of the state of nature.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Might makes right is the island's law and the story's target. Trust and community are the answer. Critical theory's suspicion of elites shapes the outer plot. Pacifism appears as Ryōta's restraint but never as a rule.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-9 (ch. 1-50, the anime) are island survival and the first alliances. Middle volumes open the outside investigation. The final volumes reveal the foundation and Himiko's father and end in two versions (ch. 121, v26).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Ryōta's mother nominated him to disappear. How did his own choices contribute, and how did he change?
2. Why do people who betray their allies keep losing in this story?
3. Himiko distrusts all men. What would it take to earn her trust?
4. Taira gave up. What might have helped him?
5. There are two endings. Which one do you think fits the story's message, and why?
