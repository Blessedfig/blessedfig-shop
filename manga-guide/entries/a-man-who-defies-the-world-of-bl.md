**Premise.** An ordinary, unnamed college student realizes he lives inside a boys'-love manga, where every handsome man around him is one chance meeting away from a romance with another man. He likes women and wants no part of it, so he studies BL manga to learn the clichés (the delinquent and the honor student, the umbrella in the rain, the drinking party) and dodges them, staying a background "mob" character while couples form around him (SuBLime; Wikipedia). Each short chapter takes a trope apart: two strangers bump into each other and fall in love (ch. 2), his friends pair off (ch. 3), classmates become couples (ch. 8). His younger brother Ayato, a high-school student, is courted by his best friend Toujou, the most "main character" of all, and their slow romance runs through the series (vol. 3 blurb). From vol. 5 a high-school boy, Hatano, confesses to the protagonist and keeps pursuing him (SuBLime; Wikipedia).

## Life message

**Love in every form is treated warmly as part of the world, while each person still gets to choose his own role in the story; knowing the clichés of a genre lets you laugh at them.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What would you do if you realized your life was running on someone else's script? The hero's world is a boys'-love manga, and its rules push every man toward a romance; he wants a different story for himself.

**The answer the story gives.** Learn the script well enough to step around it, and let others enjoy their own stories. The hero never stops the couples; he steers himself out of their way, and sometimes nudges them together (The BL Xpress, drama).

**Characters as arguments.** The hero argues for self-knowledge and choosing your own role. Toujou, the flawless lead, shows the genre's ideal. Ayato, his brother, shows that a cliché can still be sincere first love. Mayama, the fan and artist, stands for the readers. Hatano, who keeps confessing, tests whether the hero can stay outside forever (vol. 5).

**Symbols and motifs.** The umbrella (rain means romance). "Flags," the gamer word for a plot trigger. The roses around Toujou. The background "mob" face.

**Traditions it echoes.** Meta-comedy about genre (Saiki K., which a MangaUpdates reader compares it to) and the "genre-savvy" hero; BL's own self-parody.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Postmodern play is the engine. Same-sex romance is normal and warm; the hero's refusal is about roles, though one drama critic hears gay panic in it. Traditional teaching on sexuality is not addressed at all.

**Shifts across the series.** Early volumes are one-chapter trope parodies (vol. 1-2); Ayato and Toujou's romance grows (vol. 3); the hero relaxes into college life (vol. 4); Hatano and Koga arrive and the hero becomes a target himself (vol. 5).

**Discussion questions.**
1. What clichés do you notice in the stories you like?
2. Is the hero avoiding gay men, or avoiding a role? What in the story tells you?
3. How does the hero treat his brother when Ayato falls in love?
4. When is a joke about a group of people kind, and when is it unkind?
5. Have you ever felt that others expected you to play a certain role?
