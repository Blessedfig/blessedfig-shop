**Premise.** Taira Namito, an ordinary, mild high school boy, is seated next to Youko Furi, who looks every inch a gang delinquent and is followed by rumors of fights and skipped classes. In truth Furi is gentle, loves shonen manga and cats, and fell for Taira at first sight; every attempt she makes to seem friendly (her smile above all) terrifies him (Seven Seas; TV Tropes; Honey's Anime). Classmates Kaho Maeda and Taira's otaku friend Takuo notice her crush and help (TV Tropes). Summer brings a school camp, the pool, fireworks and a bikini that unsettles Taira (v2); Furi wins the field-day relay and classmates start to see her kindness, while other boys take an interest at the class festival (v3); Taira realizes his own feelings and imagines a Christmas Eve with her (v4); on Valentine's Day Taira confesses first and Furi answers, ending the series (v5) (Penguin Random House synopses). MangaUpdates notes a time skip in the final chapter or epilogue (one vote; to verify).

## Life message

**Don't judge a person by their face or their reputation: kindness and sincere feelings can hide behind an intimidating look, and they are found by paying attention and being honest.**

| Pragmatic | Misunderstandings come from poor communication on both sides (TV Tropes); they are resolved by spending time together and speaking plainly. |
| Religious | No religion appears in sources opened. Faith readers will find the "judge the heart, not the face" theme familiar; the things to weigh are a high-school dating story and light fan service (a PE scene and a bikini, v1-2). |
| Socio-cultural | A gender flip of the "scary delinquent with a soft heart" type, usually given to boys (Honey's Anime calls Furi a breath of fresh air). It gently mocks rumor and appearance-based judgment in school life. |

Outlook: Hopeful — the misunderstandings clear, classmates come to see Furi as she is (v3), and the two become a couple (v5).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** How well can you know someone from their face and their reputation? Taira is sure Furi is dangerous; everything she does to reach him confirms his fear.

**The answer the story gives.** Not well at all. Time, attention and honesty reveal the real person; by the field day (v3) the class sees Furi's kindness, and by Valentine's Day (v5) Taira can say what he feels.

**Characters as arguments.** Furi argues that appearances lie; Taira argues, without meaning to, how fear distorts what we see; Kaho and Takuo show what friends who see clearly can do; the rumor-spreading classmates are the quiet case against gossip.

**Symbols and motifs.** Furi's smile, which comes out as a frightening grimace (TV Tropes); the shared desk; an umbrella shared in the rain; the relay baton (v3).

**Traditions it echoes.** Japanese "scary-looking but kind" comedies, here with the roles reversed; reviewers compare it to Komi Can't Communicate and Horimiya (Honey's Anime; MyAnimeList reviews). Many readers will recognize the old teaching not to judge by appearances.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Virtue ethics is affirmed through small kindnesses; expressive individualism appears as the wish to be seen truly rather than a call to rebel.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1 sets up the misunderstanding; vol. 2 brings summer events; vol. 3 turns the class's opinion; vol. 4 makes Taira aware of his feelings; vol. 5 resolves the romance (Penguin Random House synopses).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why is Taira afraid of Furi at first?
2. How do the rumors about Furi spread, and what stops them?
3. What does Furi do that shows who she really is?
4. Have you ever been wrong about someone because of how they looked?
5. Why do you think Taira is the one to confess first?
