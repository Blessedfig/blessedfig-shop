**Premise.** Yukari Hayasaka, 18, a senior at an elite prep school, studies to please her mother and has no goals of her own (Wikipedia; Thompson; fan wiki). Scouted on the street by punk-styled Arashi, she wakes up in the basement studio of "Paradise Kiss" (ch. 1): fashion students George, Arashi, Miwako and Isabella, who want her to model their graduation-show dress. She skips cram school to help, falls into a volatile romance with George, a gifted, detached designer, tells her mother the truth, runs away and drops out, then finds modeling work through Miwako's sister's brand Happy Berry. After meeting George's neglectful mother she understands him better, goes home, and wins her mother's permission to model and finish school. ParaKiss takes second place at the show; the group disbands at graduation. George plans to study in Paris and invites her; she declines, and they part (Wikipedia). Volume 5 reveals through a conversation between Arashi and Hiroyuki that Arashi's relationship with Miwako began when he raped her (Anime Feminist; Amber Audra). Ten years later (ch. 48) Yukari has a career in entertainment in Japan and has married her classmate Hiroyuki; Arashi and Miwako are married with a daughter; George designs costumes for Broadway (Wikipedia; fan wiki).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Whose life are you living? Yukari has done everything her mother asked and does not know what she wants. ParaKiss offers her a different world, and George offers her a love that refuses to tell her who to be.

**The answer the story gives.** Your own, and it will cost you. Yukari chooses modeling and pays for it in conflict with her mother, hard work and finally the loss of George. The ending is neither a fairy tale nor a tragedy: she builds a career and a stable marriage, and remembers George with love (Wikipedia; Anime Feminist).

**Characters as arguments.** George argues that love should not cling; Yukari argues that love should be chosen; her mother argues for security; Isabella shows a self lived bravely and kindly; Hiroyuki stands for quiet steadiness. Arashi argues, without the story judging him, that possessive love is still love; critics say that is where the book fails (Anime Feminist).

**Symbols and motifs.** The dress beaded by hand; the basement studio as heaven (ch. 1); butterflies and screentones in Yazawa's art (Thompson); the runway.

**Traditions it echoes.** Coming-of-age fiction and the fashion romance; a Japanese josei realism about careers and love. Christian and Jewish readers may hear the prodigal's return in Yukari's homecoming; secular readers, a story of self-authorship.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism is affirmed but tempered by realism. Feminist readers praise Yukari's agency and criticize the handling of Miwako. Progressive acceptance of Isabella is warm. Traditional family values are opposed, then partly honored.

**Shifts across the series.** Early chapters (ch. 1 onward): recruitment and the dress. The middle: the romance, the break with her mother and running away (Vertical Part 2). Volume 5: the showcase, graduation, the Arashi and Miwako reveal, the breakup and the ten-years-later epilogue (ch. 48).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Yukari did everything her mother wanted, and was unhappy. What would you say to her at the start?
2. Running away worked out for Yukari. Would it work in real life? What did it cost?
3. George wanted Yukari to be independent. Was that love or a way to protect himself?
4. What did you think of how the story told Miwako's past? What was missing?
5. Why do you think Yukari married Hiroyuki instead of following George?
