**Premise.** Chihiro Hiyoshi is a good-natured, hard-working class representative who can hardly ever say no to teachers or classmates (Kodansha; Tokyopop calls her student council president). Her partner as rep, Haruka Kisaragi, is a loner surrounded by rumors who skips class and refuses anything that looks like teamwork (Kodansha; Tokyopop). They meet by chance when she trips and lands on him asleep in an archive room (MyAnimeList synopsis, which names him differently). Chihiro admires his carefree honesty, and he keeps getting pulled into her duties. In vol. 1 they organize a ball-game tournament; in the final, when the other boys want to lose on purpose, Kisaragi plays hard and scores point after point, which confuses her (Tokyopop). In vol. 2 he suddenly kisses her, throwing her feelings into turmoil; a day at the beach is supposed to reveal what he feels but complicates things further (Tokyopop). Reader reviews describe a short, sweet romance that ends with the two together (MyAnimeList user reviews).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Is a person what others say about him? Kisaragi is the boy everyone whispers about; Chihiro is the girl everyone relies on. The story asks what each looks like up close.

**The answer the story gives.** Reputation is not the person. Kisaragi shows real effort when it counts (the tournament final), and Chihiro's kindness draws out his softer side (Kodansha blurb). The romance is the proof.

**Characters as arguments.** Chihiro argues for kindness and duty but has to learn her own limits; Kisaragi argues for saying no and keeping to himself, and has to learn to let someone in. The classmates who want to throw the final stand for going along with the group.

**Symbols and motifs.** The archive room where they first meet (hidden things); the tournament (true character shown in action); the beach (feelings exposed).

**Traditions it echoes.** The shōjo romance of opposites; the teaching in many traditions against judging by rumor (Jewish lashon hara, Muslim warnings against suspicion and backbiting, Christian teaching on false witness).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Individual independence is admired; kindness and effort are rewarded. Questions of consent are not raised by the story itself, only by some readers.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1 (ch. 1-4 approx.): meeting, rep duties and the tournament. Vol. 2 (ch. 5-8 approx., plus a bonus story): the kiss, the beach and the resolution. Chapter split by volume not confirmed.

**Discussion questions.**
1. What rumors did people believe about Kisaragi? How did Chihiro find out who he really was?
2. Chihiro has trouble saying no. When is saying yes kind, and when is it a problem?
3. Kisaragi kisses Chihiro without asking. How should someone show their feelings respectfully?
4. Why did Kisaragi try his hardest in the tournament when others wanted to lose?
5. Have you ever changed your mind about someone after getting to know them?
