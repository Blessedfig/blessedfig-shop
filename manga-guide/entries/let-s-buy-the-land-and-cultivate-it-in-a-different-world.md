**Premise.** Norio Itonami, an overworked Japanese office worker, is summoned to a fantasy world to fight the demon army. His skill cannot be read, so he is judged useless and released; with the money meant for a hero's equipment he buys wild land on the frontier and takes the name Kidan (Mangapedia; AniList novel entry). His hidden gift, "Supreme Wielder," given by the god Hephaestus after Zeus summoned him (Mangapedia), lets him use any tool to its fullest, so he farms, fishes, builds and cooks like a master. He fishes up Plattie, a proud mermaid who declares that he must marry her and becomes his wife by her own choice; she turns out to be a mermaid princess, which pulls the mermaids, the demons and the humans into a dispute (vol. 2 blurb). He also wins over a young dragon, Veel, with his cooking, and an undead king (a lich) becomes his neighbor (vol. 1 blurb). The farm keeps growing: a defeated demon general, Astares; mermaid witches; elves; a self-centered human princess, Lettaslat, sent to live there after the war ends; orcs; angels; and the Witch of the Abyss (vol. 3-9 blurbs). In vol. 10 a demon prince is born, and Plattie, wanting a child, undergoes strange training from the goddess Amphitrite (Seven Seas vol. 10 blurb).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What if the hero refused to be a hero? Kidan is summoned to fight a war, is told his gift is useless, and asks for a plot of land instead. The series asks whether a life of work, food and welcome can do more good than a sword.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes. Every new arrival, from a proud mermaid to a defeated demon general, a spoiled princess and a sea witch, is won over by Kidan's cooking and patience, and by the end of the war the farm is the place where all peoples meet (vols. 6-9).

**Characters as arguments.** Kidan argues for diligence and gentleness. Plattie argues for pride softened by love; she chose him, and by vol. 10 she wants a child. Princess Lettaslat stands for entitlement, tested against the farm's plain work (vols. 6-7). The summoners who called Kidan useless stand for judging people only by their usefulness in war.

**Symbols and motifs.** The farm itself, growing with each volume. Home cooking as diplomacy. Tools brought to their full potential. The sea and the land joined in a marriage.

**Traditions it echoes.** The georgic tradition of praising farm work, Japanese slow-life fantasy, and Greek myth (Hephaestus the craftsman god; Amphitrite the sea queen). Readers from many faiths will recognize the old idea that feeding a stranger can turn an enemy into a friend.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Community and tradition are celebrated. Nonviolence is explored rather than held absolutely. Pagan gods are kind background figures. Pleasure in food is honest and warm; the fan service is the one element most parents will question.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-3 set up the farm, the marriage and the conflict over Plattie and Astares. Volumes 4-5 add helpers and the elves. Volumes 6-7 follow the end of the war and the princess. Volumes 8-10 turn to the next generation: Arowana's journey, the Witch of the Abyss and Plattie's wish for a child (blurbs).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Kidan was called "useless" because he could not fight. What makes a person useful?
2. Why does cooking work better than fighting on Kidan's farm?
3. How should the farm treat a selfish guest like Princess Lettaslat?
4. What does Plattie's wish for a child say about what the farm is for?
5. Would you rather be a hero or a farmer? Why?
