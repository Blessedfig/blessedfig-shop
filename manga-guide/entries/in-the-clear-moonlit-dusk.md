**Premise.** Yoi Takiguchi is a first-year high school student who is tall, deep-voiced and handsome; girls call her "Prince," and she has half given up on being seen any other way. Her family runs a curry restaurant where she works, and she once trained in karate (Japanese Wikipedia; AniList says ramen shop). In ch. 1 she steps in when a drunk harasses a convenience-store clerk, and second-year Kohaku Ichimura, the school's other "Prince" (from a rich family; his grandfather runs a company), joins in, then picks her up princess-style: the first time anyone has treated her as a girl. He says he is interested in her, and they agree to date on a trial basis (vols. 1-2). They become a real couple at a summer festival (anime ep. 7), quarrel out of jealousy, and face a rival: Takuto Ouji, another "prince" who works part-time at her family's shop and has feelings for Yoi (anime eps. 10-11). At the school festival Kohaku announces their relationship from the stage (vol. 8). Yoi glimpses Kohaku's difficult family situation and looks for ways to help (vol. 9); after happy days through the new year, he suddenly stops coming to school (vol. 10 blurb). How the last chapters resolve this was not found in sources opened.

## Creator and publisher context

- Mika Yamamori (story and art), creator of Daytime Shooting Star and Tsubaki-chou Lonely Planet (Wikipedia). In the Clear Moonlit Dusk is her first series in Dessert (Japanese Wikipedia).
- On-record intent (Da Vinci, 2021, as summarized on Japanese Wikipedia): she first planned a story full of boys but could not make it work; when her editor told her to draw what she really wanted, she thought of a boyish girl. ## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What happens to a person who is always seen through a label? Yoi has been called "Prince" so long she no longer argues. The question is whether anyone can see past it, and whether she can.

**The answer the story gives.** Being seen by one person can change how you see yourself. Kohaku treats Yoi as a girl from the first chapter, and her confidence grows from there. The answer is romantic rather than solitary: love is the mirror. Critics differ on what that means: ANN's Beatrix Kondo argues the series refuses to settle whether Yoi's style is her true self or armor, while Anime Feminist worries it treats her boyishness as something to grow out of.

**Characters as arguments.** Yoi argues that a person is more than how she looks. Kohaku argues for wholehearted, sometimes overbearing devotion; Yamamori herself describes him as just short of creepy. Ouji, another "prince," argues that boys and girls cannot simply be friends, and loses. Nobara, a boys'-love fan, enjoys the princes as fantasy and stands in for the watching crowd.

**Symbols and motifs.** Two princes, one of whom is a girl. Flowers drawn around Yoi when she performs for others (AIPT). The empty apartment that shows Kohaku's loneliness (anime ep. 11). The moonlit dusk of the title, an in-between hour.

**Traditions it echoes.** Shojo manga's long line of "prince" heroines (Rose of Versailles, Revolutionary Girl Utena; from general knowledge), and the Cinderella-style moment of being chosen. For faith readers, the idea that the heart matters more than appearance (1 Samuel 16:7).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism is affirmed: Yoi should be seen as herself. Feminism and traditionalism are both present and critics argue over which wins. No lens is mocked.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-4: meeting, the trial relationship and growing feelings. Vols. 5-7 (to about ch. 25, the end of the anime): becoming a couple, jealousy, Ouji and the apartment scene. Vol. 8: the public announcement. Vols. 9-12: Kohaku's family situation, his absence and the ending (not confirmed in sources opened).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Everyone calls Yoi "Prince." How do labels like that change how people see themselves?
2. What does Kohaku do that makes Yoi feel seen? Is there anything he does that you would not want a boyfriend or girlfriend to do?
3. Does Yoi need to change to be loved, or does she need people to see her differently?
4. Ouji thinks boys and girls cannot just be friends. Do you agree?
5. How can you tell when someone is comfortable or not, even if they do not say so?
