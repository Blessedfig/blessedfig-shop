**Premise.** Tetsu Misato, a short, frugal, easily frightened high school senior, wants to work after graduation; his father, who runs a housekeeping agency, wants him in college. They strike a deal, and Tetsu takes a job as housekeeper at the Karasawa mansion on the hill (Japanese Wikipedia). Tetsu is also secretly saving money for his mother, in a coma for eight years after a car accident (fan wiki). In the annex lives Shizu Karasawa, 19, hidden since she was six: her body is easily possessed by spirits of the dead whenever she is unconscious. Her parents took it for dissociative identity disorder; treatment failed because it was not, and her father shut her away (Japanese Wikipedia; fan wiki). The spirit of her great-grandfather Shinobu organized a pact of friendly spirits who take turns in her body to keep hostile ones out: cheerful bodybuilder Haru, fashionable Mirei, and later Kanato, a boy who died in hospital. Tetsu's first confession of love (ch. 1) goes, by mistake, to Haru. Over six volumes Tetsu draws Shizu out: to school (vol. 3), to a soccer match that frees Tetsu from his own past (vols. 3-4), and on the run when her father plans to commit her to a hospital (vol. 5). As her heart fills with people and love, there is less room for the spirits, and they must leave (vol. 6).

**Everyone needs to become a self of their own; patient love, friendship and family can wake a lonely person up, and both the living and the dead must learn to let go.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Who are you, if your body has never been only yours? Shizu has lived since childhood as a house shared with the dead, sleeping while others live her days. The series asks what it takes to wake up into a life of one's own, and what it costs the ones who kept watch.

**The answer the story gives.** Connection. As Tetsu, friends and family fill Shizu's heart, there is less room for the spirits, and when she falls in love they leave for good (fan wiki). The "curse" is broken not by a kiss but by a full heart, and the price is grief for the friends who raised her.

**Characters as arguments.** Tetsu argues that love is work: cooking, cleaning, showing up. Shinobu argues for protection and hiding, out of love, and is gently overruled. Takashi argues for reputation and control; the story explains him (a boy sold to become an heir, ch. 21) without excusing him. Mirei, revealed as Tetsu's own comatose mother watching over him, argues that love sometimes hides itself so as not to raise false hope. Haru, who drowned saving a child, argues for self-giving; Kanato, who died young in hospital, argues for the hunger to live.

**Symbols and motifs.** Sleep and waking; the annex on the hill; the notebook in which Shizu and the spirits talk (praised by the AniList reviewer); the meteor shower of the first confession (ch. 1); the soccer ball; the shared kanji "shi" that binds the Karasawa line (fan wiki).

**Traditions it echoes.** Sleeping Beauty and the Grimm "Little Briar-Rose"; Japanese ghost stories of spirits bound by regret, and the Buddhist idea that peace comes by letting go; shojo romance. Christian, Jewish and Muslim readers will notice that it treats talking with the dead as a gift; Buddhist and Shinto readers will see a familiar world.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarianism and the search for self are both affirmed and reconciled: Shizu becomes herself through others. The Buddhist picture of release is affirmed. Psychiatry is shown failing, because in this world the problem is not psychiatric.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-2 (ch. 1-8) introduce the house, the spirits and the fear; vols. 3-4 (ch. 9-16) open outward to school, soccer and Chihiro's secret; vol. 5 (ch. 17-21) is the family crisis and the runaway; vol. 6 (ch. 22-26) is the farewell.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Shizu's parents hid her because they were afraid. What could they have done instead?
2. Tetsu shows love by cooking and cleaning. What ordinary acts show love in your family?
3. Mirei kept her identity secret from her own son. Was that right?
4. Why do the spirits have to leave for Shizu to become herself?
5. What does it mean to be "yourself" when other people have shaped you so much?
