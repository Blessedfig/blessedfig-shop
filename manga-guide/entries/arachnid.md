**Premise.** Alice Fuji, 15 (fan wiki), has a condition the story calls Congenital Excessive Concentration: she fixes on one thing and loses track of everything else. She is bullied at school and abused by the uncle she has lived with since her mother's death. The morning her uncle tries to assault her, an assassin called Kumo ("spider," codename Arachnid) hangs him with a living thread-weapon and spares Alice (ch. 1). Kumo, who has the same condition, trains her for two weeks as a spider-style assassin, then forces her into a duel to the death as her graduation; she kills him at his request (ch. 4-5). Hunted by the Organization, a syndicate of insect-themed killers, she refuses to join it and keeps her own name as Kumo asked (ch. 6). She moves in with her former bully, Yoriko, as a bodyguard, and gains an unlikely ally in Oki Megumi (Gokiburi, the cockroach), a classmate assassin (ch. 8-11). Most of the series (ch. 16-71) is the "Arachnid Hunt" inside her high school, where assassins compete to kill her while the student council turns the students into a mind-controlled mob (ch. 25-58). Alice learns that Suzumebachi, the hornet who killed her mother, is her father (ch. 40, 66-67), and that Yoriko is the Organization's ageless Boss (ch. 68-71). The epilogue shows Japan in a zombie-like outbreak the Organization caused; Alice sets out alone to fight (ch. 72; Blattodea continues it).

## Life message

**In a world run by predators, a bullied girl survives by learning to kill, but she refuses to let anyone own her name or her mind; friends are the only thing worth protecting.**

| Meta lens | How it reads the series |

| Pragmatic | Alice wins by preparation: she spends a week laying traps through the school before the Hunt (ch. 57). Kumo teaches technique, not ethics. |
| Religious | No religion appears in sources opened. Faith readers will weigh killing as a way of life, revenge, a suicide (ch. 59) and pervasive sexual violence; there is no repentance or forgiveness arc beyond Alice sparing some enemies (ch. 6, 11). |
| Socio-cultural | Bullying, child abuse and an adult underworld that preys on teenagers; the Boss's plan is to rule Japan by mind control and cut its population (fan wiki, timeline). Fans call the later arcs a turn into constant sexual assault (TV Tropes, YMMV). |

Outlook: Bleak — Alice survives and refuses the Boss's offer to rule, but her mentor, her mother and her closest friend (Yoriko, who was the Boss) are dead, and Japan is overrun by an outbreak the Organization caused (ch. 71-72).
Hope source: S — the story's way through is staying true to yourself: Alice keeps her name and her own will against the Organization and against Yoriko's mind control (ch. 6, 70); protecting friends (P) is the close second.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What does a child do when every adult who should protect her is a predator? Alice's uncle abuses her, her mentor trains her to kill and then makes her kill him, her father is the man who murdered her mother, and her best friend turns out to be the Boss who ordered it all.

**The answer the story gives.** Survive, but stay yourself. Alice learns to kill, but she keeps her name, refuses to join the Organization, refuses to become its Boss and refuses to let anyone control her mind (ch. 6, 70). The cost is almost everything else.

**Characters as arguments.** Kumo argues that a killer can still love: he wants Alice free, even as he forces her to kill him (ch. 5). Yoriko argues that Alice would have become a murderer anyway and should help rule Japan (ch. 70); Alice's refusal is the story's answer. Kuramoto, abused as a child, argues for revenge on the whole world (ch. 59). Oki argues for loyalty, mixed with crude desire. Kabutomushi argues for keeping a promise to the dead (ch. 39, 71).

**Symbols and motifs.** The spider's thread (a weapon that can kill its user); names versus code names; a blank sheet of paper from Alice's mother (ch. 47); the school as a jar of insects (fan wiki).

**Traditions it echoes.** Battle-royale and assassin fiction (Léon: The Professional's mentor and orphan, from general knowledge; to verify); the bullied-girl-turned-avenger story; zombie apocalypse at the end.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Might makes right is the law of the world, not a value the story praises; authoritarian control is the final enemy; existential self-ownership is what Alice keeps; nihilism is the temptation she resists (ch. 66).

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-3 (ch. 1-15): abuse, training, Kumo's death and the first assassins. Vols. 4-6 (ch. 16-30): school assassins and the start of the Hunt; the student-council plot. Vols. 7-11 (ch. 31-54): the Hunt, Dinoponera, Sasori, and the sexual-mob plot. Vols. 12-14 (ch. 55-72): Kuramoto, Suzumebachi, the Boss and the outbreak.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Alice says she must kill before she is killed. Is she right? When is self-defense different from murder?
2. Kumo trained Alice and made her kill him. Was he a good father figure?
3. Why does it matter so much to Alice to keep her own name?
4. Yoriko says Alice would have become a killer anyway. Is she right?
5. Which adults in the story should have helped Alice, and what should they have done?
