**Premise.** On his way home from school, fifteen-year-old Touya Mochizuki is killed by a stray lightning bolt: God, a kindly, apologetic old man, made a mistake. To make amends he sends Touya to a fantasy world, boosts his body and magic, and lets him keep his smartphone, which still reaches Earth's internet and can be charged with magic (Wikipedia; fan wiki ch. 1). Touya forgives God at once. In the town of Reflet he rescues twin adventurers Elze and Linze from thugs and forms a party with them and the samurai girl Yae (vol. 1). He saves Sue, the ten-year-old daughter of a duke, from kidnappers and cures the King of Belfast of poison; the king's daughter Yumina, twelve, reads his character with her Mystic Eyes and asks to marry him; the king agrees and sets a two-year trial (vols. 2-3; The Outer Haven; fan wiki ages). Touya refuses a noble title, gets a mansion and staff (vol. 4), meets the fairy court mage Leen (vol. 5), fights crystal invaders called the Phrase, gains an ancient sky-fortress, Babylon, and its gynoid keepers, opens a café, and stops a coup in the Regulus Empire, where Princess Lu falls for him (vols. 8-10). He eventually rules his own small country, Brunhild, and gains nine fiancées: three princesses, a duke's daughter, a fairy, the twins, Yae and later others (Wikipedia; fan wiki). In the novels he marries all nine and later ascends to become a minor god (fan wiki, citing the web novel).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What would you do with a second life and every advantage? Touya dies by a god's error and is given strength, magic and a phone that can look anything up (Wikipedia). The series asks what a decent person does with near-limitless power, and answers: be kind, be practical, and enjoy it.

**The answer the story gives.** Goodness is easy and pleasant. Touya forgives God at once (ch. 1), rescues people, turns down honors (vol. 4), frees slaves (LN vol. 4) and builds a peaceful country. Love comes to him rather than being sought; the women's fathers approve; the problems are other people's malice, which he removes. Critics call this low on tension (ANN; Anime Feminist), and the story seems content with that.

**Characters as arguments.** Touya argues that kindness plus know-how is enough. God argues for a hands-off, gentle providence. Yumina argues that love can be judged in an instant by seeing a person's heart (vol. 3). The Ramissh cardinals argue for religion as control and are exposed (LN vol. 5). Sandora's king argues that people can be owned and is defeated.

**Symbols and motifs.** The smartphone, a link to the old world and a tool of mastery; the Gate spell that erases distance; Babylon, an ancient sky-fortress of forgotten knowledge (vol. 8); the growing household as a kingdom in miniature.

**Traditions it echoes.** The isekai power fantasy and the harem comedy; older tales of the good king and the wise counselor. For faith readers it echoes, and departs from, stories of divine favor: here God is a friend who errs, not a sovereign judge.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Feel-good faith (moralistic therapeutic deism) is close to the story's view of God: kind, distant, wanting people happy. Consumerism and hedonism are affirmed in the comforts Touya builds. Traditional hierarchy is assumed; polygamy and child engagements are treated as normal. Organized religion appears mainly as fraud. Critics (Anime Feminist) praise the capable women and worry about the hero's dominance over them.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-4: forming the party, rescues, Yumina's engagement, the mansion. Vols. 5-7: Leen, Mismede and travel. Vols. 8-10: the Phrase, Babylon and the Regulus coup. Vols. 11-13: Eashen's war, Renne, Sue and Sakura. Later volumes follow the novels toward the founding of Brunhild and wider wars (Wikipedia; fan wiki).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Touya forgives God right away for killing him. Would you? What makes forgiveness easy or hard?
2. Yumina is 12 when she asks to marry Touya, and her father agrees. Why does the story treat that as normal, and what do you think?
3. How does Touya use his power? Is there anything he should not do just because he can?
4. The story's God admits mistakes and rarely steps in. How is that like or unlike what your family believes about God?
5. Which of Touya's Earth inventions would make the biggest difference in a world without them, and why?
