**Premise.** In a world where people can suddenly turn into werewolves that eat human flesh, the tiny mountain village of Kasoka has lost six people this year (Japanese Wikipedia). Velou, a pink-haired orphan whose parents were killed by werewolves, idolizes the village chief, the hunter who avenged them and raised him. The chief spends his savings to hire the Hunters Guild, founded by the legendary first Red Hood; the hunter who comes is Grimm, a woman in a red hood cursed into a child's body, who can return to adult form for a few hours with magic tools (VIZ; ANN). Part 1, "Werewolf Hunt" (ch. 1-5): the werewolf turns out to be a villager disguised as a human; in a turn on the fairy tale, it swallows Grimm and Velou cuts it open with an axe (ch. 1-3). More werewolves attack, and the werewolf leader Lycaon's ally, the Ash Witch (Cinderella), burns the village (ch. 5). Part 2, "The Outside World" (ch. 6-18): Velou goes with Grimm to the Ironworks, a moving training fortress run by the muscular, heat-cursed hunter Debonair, and joins a cast of hopefuls for the Guild exam, a two-hour game of handcuff cops-and-robbers in which killing or badly injuring rivals is forbidden (ch. 8-14). Velou wants every trainee to pass, which means getting rivals to cooperate (VIZ vol. 3). In the rushed final chapters (ch. 15-18) the chief returns alive: he is the second Red Hood, Ludwig Geppetto, who faked his death, made Velou as a puppet using magic and the power of "Pages," and schemed to push Grimm out of the protagonist's role. He plans to destroy the world; Velou, who wants a world without a written script, defeats him with a magic gun from the first Red Hood (Japanese Wikipedia). Readers describe the ending as openly meta, with jokes about the series' own cancellation (Anime Corner; AniList and MyAnimeList user reviews).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Who decides what part you play? Velou thinks he is an orphan boy who wants to be a hunter like the man who raised him. The last chapters ask what happens if that whole life was arranged by someone else.

**The answer the story gives.** Protect people, work with others, and refuse a script that is not yours. In Kasoka, Velou learns that a hunter protects the weak (ch. 1-5). At the Ironworks he insists everyone can pass together (ch. 14). Faced with the man who made him and wrote his role, he chooses a world without a scenario (ch. 18; Japanese Wikipedia).

**Characters as arguments.** Velou argues for courage that belongs to you. Grimm argues for duty and mentorship. The werewolves, who hide inside the village to eat it, are the case against deception. Mosco Cobbs, who sold an eye to start a company and wants to take over the Guild with money and fists, is the case against buying power. The chief, a beloved father who is really a puppet-master, is the darkest argument: that a person can be loved and still used.

**Symbols and motifs.** The red hood passed from hunter to hunter. The wolf's belly cut open, straight from the Grimm tale (ch. 1-3). Geppetto and a puppet boy. The Ironworks, a fortress on rails. The "True Book" and its Pages.

**Traditions it echoes.** Little Red Riding Hood, Cinderella and Pinocchio; the Brothers Grimm; werewolf folklore; and Jump's own exam-arc tradition (Hunter x Hunter). The puppet who becomes free echoes Pinocchio; for some faith readers, the deceiver who scripts lives echoes the serpent's lie that we are not free.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Virtue ethics and communitarianism are straightforwardly endorsed. Existentialism wins the ending. Postmodern metafiction is the ending's method, which readers either loved or found confusing. Occult elements are fantasy furniture, mostly in villains' hands.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1 (ch. 1-7) is horror-tinged werewolf hunting and the move to the Ironworks. Vol. 2 (ch. 8-13) is the training and exam setup. Vol. 3 (ch. 14-18) runs the exam and then the sudden reveal and ending.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Velou wanted every trainee to pass. Why was that better than beating them?
2. The chief raised Velou and also used him. Can someone be both a good parent and a bad one?
3. What does it mean to "write your own story"? Are there limits?
4. The werewolves hid among the villagers. How do you tell who to trust?
5. The series ended early. How do you think the author felt, and how does the ending show it?
