**Premise.** The sequel to Ace of Diamond continues pitcher Eijun Sawamura's second year at Seido High, a Tokyo baseball powerhouse where he lives in the team dormitory far from his family. After playing at the spring national tournament (Senbatsu), where Seido loses 2-0 to Komadai in the third round and Sawamura barely pitches, he competes with the hard-throwing Satoru Furuya for the team's ace number while catcher and new captain Kazuya Miyuki leads the team (fan wiki). New first-years arrive, the batters work to match the previous year's lineup, and the team aims to win the West Tokyo summer tournament before the third-years graduate (Wikipedia). Seido wins its way through the summer tournament: a 2-1 semifinal over Ichidaisan (ch. 224-253), in which Miyuki is hurt and hides the injury, and a 2-1 final over its long-time rival Inashiro and its ace Mei Narumiya (ch. 264-305), sending Seido to the national summer tournament at Koshien (fan wiki). The last chapters close the story (ch. 306-308) (AniList; MangaUpdates; K Manga).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What makes someone "the ace"? Talent, effort, or the trust of the team? Act II follows Eijun Sawamura through his second year as he and the gifted Satoru Furuya compete for Seido's number-one jersey, under a catcher-captain, Kazuya Miyuki, who must hold the team together.

**The answer the story gives.** Effort and trust. Sawamura, frustrated after barely pitching at the spring nationals, takes soil from the bullpen where he stood all game instead of the mound (fan wiki), and keeps adding pitches and speed. Furuya learns that raw speed is not enough. The team wins the West Tokyo final 2-1 (ch. 264-305), and the win belongs to everyone.

**Characters as arguments.** Sawamura argues that hard work and cheerfulness outlast setbacks. Furuya argues for talent and the will to be the best, and is tested by fatigue (fan wiki). Miyuki argues for responsibility, sometimes to a fault: he hides an injury to keep catching, and his friend Kuramochi asks whether it is duty or ego (fan wiki). Coach Kataoka stands for authority that is strict but fair. Rival aces (Narumiya of Inashiro, Amahisa of Ichidaisan, Hongou of Komadai) show that opponents can be admired.

**Symbols and motifs.** The ace number (jersey #1). Koshien soil. The catcher's mitt that Sawamura wants to pitch to, the image the series opens with (K Manga blurb). The seniors' last summer.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Japanese school-sports story (Touch, Major) and the wider sports story of the underdog who works harder; Aristotle's idea that virtues grow by practice.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Community and team are affirmed. Classical virtues are built through repetition and failure. Confucian respect for coach and seniors is the frame. Stoic endurance is admired but also questioned when it hides an injury. Individual ambition is welcomed as long as it serves the team.

**Shifts across the series.** Early chapters: spring nationals and the new first-years. Middle: spring tournament, practice games, the rivalry for the ace number. About ch. 140-305: the West Tokyo summer tournament, ending with the semifinal (ch. 224-253) and final (ch. 264-305). Ch. 306-308: closing chapters (content not opened).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Sawamura took soil from the bullpen, not the mound. What was he telling himself?
2. Miyuki hid his injury. Was that brave, selfish, or both? What should Kuramochi have done?
3. Furuya and Sawamura want the same jersey. How can rivals also be teammates?
4. Why does the coach choose Miyuki as captain?
5. What have you gotten better at by practicing when you weren't the best?
