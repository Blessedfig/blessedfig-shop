**Premise.** In 1938, fifty years after Part 1, Jonathan's grandson Joseph Joestar lives in New York with his grandmother Erina. He is brash, funny and a natural Hamon user who fights by tricks, often predicting his enemy's next line. A Hamon-trained former ally, Straizo, uses a stone mask to become a vampire and attacks them; Joseph defeats him and Straizo dies by his own hand (Wikipedia). Learning that Nazi soldiers in Mexico hold the elderly Speedwagon, Joseph goes there and, with the help of the Nazi officer Rudol von Stroheim, defeats Santana, an ancient "Pillar Man" the Nazis had revived to study for Hitler's conquest (Wikipedia; jojowiki). The Pillar Men are an ancient race that fed on humans; humans once "regarded them as either gods to be worshiped or demons to be feared," and their leader Kars invented the stone mask (jojowiki, Pillar Men). In Rome, Joseph meets Caesar Zeppeli, grandson of Part 1's Will Zeppeli. Three more Pillar Men, Wamuu, Esidisi and Kars, wake under the Colosseum and plant poisoned rings in Joseph, giving him 33 days. Joseph and Caesar train under the Hamon master Lisa Lisa. Esidisi dies; Caesar dies fighting Wamuu and leaves Joseph his antidote; Joseph defeats Wamuu in a chariot duel. Kars gains the Red Stone of Aja and becomes an "ultimate being," but a volcanic eruption on Vulcano throws him into space, where he freezes and "eventually ceases thinking" (Wikipedia; jojowiki). Lisa Lisa is revealed as Joseph's mother. Joseph marries Lisa Lisa's assistant Suzi Q; Stroheim dies at Stalingrad in 1943; Joseph's street friend Smokey becomes "the first black mayor" of a Georgia city (jojowiki).

## Life message

**A good life is lived with wit, courage and loyalty to friends and family, carrying on what those before you gave. Cleverness and humor can beat raw power, and a friend's sacrifice is honored by finishing the fight.**

**Where traditions disagree.** On message, most frames see a familiar adventure moral: friends and family overcome a pride that would make itself a god. Three lines divide them. First, the Nazi officer: Jewish frames and progressive frames weigh most heavily a story that makes a Nazi likable and comic while leaving out the regime's crimes; others read Stroheim as a period-adventure foil. Second, the supernatural: beings once worshipped as gods, an Aztec prophecy and vampire masks weigh most for Pentecostal, Adventist and Witness families. Third, the treatment of ancient Mesoamerica and of ethnic characters weighs for Wiccan/Pagan, progressive and Reform frames, while traditionalist frames focus on the family-lineage theme.

## Creator and publisher context

- Hirohiko Araki has created JoJo's Bizarre Adventure since 1987; Battle Tendency is Part 2 (Wikipedia).
- On Joseph (Wikipedia): Araki made him a more confrontational, rule-bending hero than Jonathan, a "swindler," and moved toward more "cerebral" fights. He prefers characters whose weapons have flaws and who make up for them with strategy.
- On Caesar (Wikipedia): he was added to give Joseph a friendly rival, which suited a shonen magazine.
- On Lisa Lisa and women (Wikipedia): Araki designed her as a "sadistic" warrior, which he felt was fresh, since shonen heroines were usually written to fit "a man's ideal woman." He said supernatural combat lets women and children stand against strong men, an idea that later shaped the Stands of Part 3.
- On the Pillar Men (Wikipedia): designs drawn from Roman statues, Egyptian sphinxes and Japanese Nio guardian statues; Kars's turban marks his intelligence and kingship.
- On the Nazis and Stroheim: no statement by Araki was found in the pages opened. **Status: Needs more info on the creators** for this point.
- No public statement by Araki about his own religion or politics was found in the pages opened.
- Publisher: Shueisha. Viz Media publishes the English edition; Viz is owned by Shueisha and Shogakukan (from general knowledge; to verify). The 2012 anime is by David Production (Wikipedia).
- Controversies: none widely reported for Part 2 in the pages opened. A 2023 JTA article, "Does anime have a Nazi problem? Some Jewish fans think so," was found in search but not opened, so whether it names JoJo is unverified.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What beats overwhelming power? The Pillar Men are older, stronger and smarter than humans, and see them as food (jojowiki). Joseph has none of their strength. The question is whether wit, training and other people can make up the difference.

**The answer the story gives.** They can. Araki built Joseph as a "swindler" who wins by "cerebral" fights, and said he prefers heroes whose powers have flaws, so strategy must fill the gap (Wikipedia). Joseph traps, bluffs and taunts. But he does not win alone. He lives because Caesar leaves him the antidote as he dies (ch. 49), because Lisa Lisa trains him (ch. 27-32), and because Smokey, Speedwagon and even a Nazi officer help him. The Japanese subtitle names the theme: "His Proud Lineage" (Wikipedia).

**Characters as arguments.** Joseph argues for cleverness and humor over solemn virtue; he is the opposite of his grandfather (ANN via Wikipedia). Caesar argues for honor and legacy: he carries his grandfather's name into a fight he cannot win. Lisa Lisa argues for discipline, and, once revealed as Joseph's mother, for a love that hid itself to protect (ch. 61-62). Wamuu argues that an enemy can have honor; the heroes respect him. Kars argues for the will to perfect oneself at any cost, and the story answers by giving him a perfect body in a place where it is useless. Stroheim is harder to read: a Nazi officer who is brave, loyal to his allies and comic, while the regime he serves wants the Pillar Men for "Hitler's conquest" (Wikipedia). The story does not argue for Nazism; it largely leaves it out, which some readers see as softening (AIPT raises "questionable" ethnic depictions; no review opened here addresses Stroheim).

**Symbols and motifs.** Breath and sunlight again: Hamon is life made into a weapon. The poisoned rings and the 33-day countdown: life on borrowed time. Caesar's bubble and bandana, his last gift (from general knowledge; to verify). The Red Stone of Aja, a jewel that turns ambition into godhood. Kars drifting in space, unable to die, "ceases thinking" (jojowiki): immortality as punishment.

**Traditions it echoes.** The 1930s pulp and adventure film: Nazis chasing ancient relics in Latin America and Europe (my own framing; compare Raiders of the Lost Ark, 1981). The trickster of folklore, who beats giants by cunning (my own framing). The Faustian or Promethean overreach of a being who wants to be more than he is. Kung fu training films, with a demanding master. The ancient-gods-as-aliens idea, where beings once worshipped turn out to be predators (my own framing).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Virtue ethics, pragmatism and community are affirmed: honor, cleverness and friendship win. Might makes right and the drive to transcend limits are critiqued through Kars. Religion is mostly backdrop: beings once called gods, an Aztec prophecy, the Colosseum. Politics enters through the 1938 setting, but the Nazis are a plot engine rather than a subject.

**Shifts across the series.** Chapters 1-18 (Viz vol. 1): New York, Straizo, and the Nazi lab in Mexico with Santana. Chapters 19-36 (vol. 2): Rome, Caesar, the three Pillar Men, the poison rings, Lisa Lisa's training and Esidisi. Chapters 37-49 (vol. 3): Stroheim's return, Kars, and Caesar's death. Chapters 50-69 (vol. 4): Wamuu's chariot duel, the revelation of Lisa Lisa, Kars's transformation, Vulcano and the epilogue. The tone moves from comedy toward grief at Caesar's death and back to a light epilogue.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Joseph wins by lying, bluffing and tricks. Is that different from cheating? When is it fair?
2. Caesar dies to give Joseph a chance (ch. 49). What do we owe to people who sacrifice for us?
3. Stroheim is a Nazi officer who helps the heroes. What does the story leave out about the regime he served? Does that matter?
4. Kars gets the perfect body he wanted and ends unable to die (ch. 69). What is the story saying about wanting to be perfect?
5. The heroes respect Wamuu, an enemy. Can you respect someone you have to fight?
