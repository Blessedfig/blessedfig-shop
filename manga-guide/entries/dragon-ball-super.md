**Premise.** Some years after the defeat of Majin Buu, Goku's peaceful life is interrupted when Beerus, the cat-like God of Destruction of Universe 7, and his angel attendant Whis come to Earth looking for a "Super Saiyan God" seen in Beerus's prophetic dream. Goku and Vegeta begin training under Whis and learn that the multiverse has twelve universes, each with its own gods, ruled over by Zeno (the Omni-King, Zen'ō), a childlike supreme being who can erase anything by raising his hand. Arcs follow: a tournament against Universe 6; the "Future" Trunks arc against Goku Black and Zamasu, a god-in-training who wants to wipe out all mortals; the Tournament of Power, in which losing universes are erased; the manga-original Moro arc (Goku and Vegeta join the Galactic Patrol to stop a planet-draining sorcerer) and Granolah arc (a survivor of Frieza's genocide seeks revenge, and Goku learns about his father Bardock); and the Super Hero arc, in which a revived Red Ribbon Army kidnaps Gohan's daughter Pan and Gohan and Piccolo fight Cell Max.

## Life message

**A good life is joyful, unending striving: keep training past every limit, enjoy a worthy opponent, protect your family and friends, and give former enemies a seat at the table, even in a cosmos whose rulers can erase everything on a whim.**

Hope source: E — the way through every threat, from gods to Moro to Cell Max, is training past one's limits and growing stronger (Ultra Instinct, Vegeta's God of Destruction power, Gohan's awakening).

Compared with the original *Dragon Ball*, *Super* drops nearly all of the sexual gags, so depiction concerns ease for many frames, while its theology weighs more: a supreme being who erases universes, gods who can be outfought, "angels" who serve gods of destruction and a god as chief villain. **Where traditions disagree.** On Zeno and the gods: Muslim, Pentecostal, Jehovah's Witness, Adventist, Orthodox Jewish and many Baptist families weigh a pantheon with "angels" and an omnipotent erasing ruler most heavily; Catholic, Reformed, Lutheran and Anglican readers more often treat it as fantasy that can prompt discussion of what makes divine power good; Hindu, Buddhist, Shinto, Pagan and secular readers tend to read it as folklore or satire. On violence: Anabaptists and Jehovah's Witnesses object to violence as entertainment; Buddhist and Bahá'í non-harm sit near them; most other traditions accept protective force. On sexual content: the original's gags are largely absent from the manga summaries opened, so depiction concerns here mostly rest on violence and spiritual imagery rather than modesty. A difference from the anime that matters for this reading: in the manga the Omni-Kings meant to cut the number of universes from the start, while the anime frames it as a test of virtue (Kanzenshuu ch. 42).

## Winners, losers and sympathy

The story rewards those who train, cooperate and act selflessly, defeats those who want to destroy or own others, and leaves the cosmic rulers above all judgment.

| Side | Worldview it carries | Outcome | How the story treats it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Goku and Vegeta | Joyful striving, rivalry, mercy | Win or survive every arc; reach new forms (Ultra Instinct, God of Destruction power) | Endorsed, though Goku's carelessness is noted by critics |
| Android 17 | Selflessness, family | Wins the Tournament; restores all erased universes (ch. 42) | Strongly endorsed |
| Zamasu / Goku Black | A god's "justice" of wiping out mortals | Erased by Zeno (ch. 26) | Condemned |
| Zeno, Great Priest, Gods of Destruction | Absolute cosmic order, power without accountability | Unchallenged; Zeno befriends Goku | Comic and awe-inspiring; not judged |
| Moro, Gas, Cell Max | Consumption, revenge, weaponized power | Destroyed | Condemned |
| Granolah | Revenge for genocide | Learns the truth about the past, ends as an ally | Sympathetic |
| Frieza | Domination, cruelty | Revived for helping Universe 7; returns stronger (Granolah arc) | Mixed: villain, temporary ally, ongoing threat |

**Fan favorites versus the official moral.** In the first official worldwide character poll (results January 2026, cited in the *Dragon Ball* entry from ANN), Goku ranked first and Vegeta second, with Frieza tenth; the poll covered the whole franchise, not *Super* alone. A MyAnimeList reviewer calls the Granolah arc the best recent arc, and ComicBook.com's Kofi Outlaw says it goes "deeper than the usual fan-service gimmicks." The "Zeno button" is criticized by a MyAnimeList reviewer for removing tension. The story's moral favors humble, selfless effort (Android 17), while fans prize the rival pair and the stylish villain, as with the original.

## Creator and publisher context

- Akira Toriyama supplied the story outline and major plot points; Toyotarou storyboards and draws, and Toriyama corrected the storyboards (Wikipedia). Toyotarou: "Every month, I'll create a storyboard for him to review, and then he'll give me input and suggest changes," and Toriyama is "more particular about the gags and the comedic moments!" (ANN interview, October 2016).
- Toriyama on tone (Volume 1 "Tori-Toyo Free Talk," Kanzenshuu translation): he wanted the Universe 6 arc "relatively simple and cheerful" after the tense film arcs, and hoped the next story would be "simple enough for kids to enjoy… hopefully."
- Toyotarou on himself: "I just loved Dragon Ball"; his introduction was *Dr. Slump*; he draws inspiration from "action films with straightforward good-versus-evil heroes"; he feels "a lot of responsibility to create something that won't let them down"; he most wants readers to look at "the comedic gags" (ANN 2016). He wanted Moro to be truly evil, like King Piccolo, drawing on goat and shinigami (death god) motifs (Wikipedia). On the Super Hero prequel: "I loved Gohan's high school arc... and wanted to place Trunks and Goten into that world" (Wikipedia).
- No statements by either creator on religion, politics or the meaning of Zeno and the gods were found in the pages opened. Toyotarou is a pen name and he keeps his private life out of public view (from general knowledge; to verify). **Status: Needs more info on the creators.**
- Toriyama died March 1, 2024, of an acute subdural hematoma, aged 68; V Jump announced an indefinite hiatus that month, with no reason stated (ANN). A one-shot (ch. 104) adapting Toriyama's text appeared in February 2025 (Wikipedia).
- Publisher: Shueisha (V Jump). English: VIZ Media. A 2022 unauthorized-access incident at Toei delayed the *Super Hero* film (Wikipedia). No creator controversies found.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What is power for, when the universe itself is ruled by beings who can erase it? The original *Dragon Ball* asked whether the strongest should rule. *Dragon Ball Super* raises the ceiling. Goku and Vegeta now train with angels and spar with gods. Above them all sits Zeno, a being who can unmake whole universes with a gesture and who seems to have the temperament of a small child. The question becomes whether striving still means anything in such a cosmos.

**The answer the story gives.** It answers, cheerfully, yes. Goku's joy in getting stronger is unchanged by the knowledge that someone stronger can erase him. Power is still for testing oneself and protecting friends. The Tournament of Power, the series' center, turns on one choice: Android 17, the last fighter standing, wishes the erased universes back rather than asking for anything for himself (ch. 42). The story rewards selflessness inside an order it never challenges.

**Characters as arguments.** Goku argues for play: he befriends Zeno and treats gods as sparring partners. Vegeta argues for pride that matures into responsibility; in the Granolah arc he admits the Saiyans "committed many sins" (ch. 69). Bardock, Goku's father, hesitates during the raid on Cereal and spares the last survivors, telling them to keep surviving (ch. 77), which Vegeta reads as the source of Goku's "soft nature." Zamasu is the clearest ideological villain: a god-in-training who decides mortals are the flaw in creation and that justice means removing them (ch. 17-26; Kanzenshuu notes the manga states he aims to eradicate mortals "from all timelines"). He is defeated by an even greater power, not refuted. Zeno embodies power with no visible moral reasoning; in the manga he intended to reduce the universes from the start. Granolah argues the case of the survivor of genocide who wants revenge, and Gohan, in the Super Hero arc, argues that a scholar and father can be the strongest when someone he loves is threatened.

**Symbols and motifs.** The "Zeno button" is power handed to a mortal who uses it lightly; one reviewer says it drains tension. The erasure of a universe is drawn as a white void. The Super Dragon Balls echo the original's wish-granting orbs on a cosmic scale. Ultra Instinct, a state of acting without thinking, contrasts with Vegeta's God of Destruction path. Uub, named as the reincarnation of pure Buu, carries the original's rebirth motif forward (ch. 66).

**Traditions it echoes.** The pantheon of creators and destroyers in balance echoes Hindu and Buddhist cosmologies, and the rebirth of Buu echoes Dharmic ideas, but no creator statement opened ties these to doctrine. Toriyama said earlier that he did not think deeply about the mythology (see the *Dragon Ball* entry). Toyotarou cites action films "with straightforward good-versus-evil heroes," and named King Piccolo and the shinigami as models for Moro.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Virtue ethics is the spine: effort, loyalty and mercy carry each arc. Might makes right is both the villains' creed and the shape of the cosmos, since Zeno's power goes unquestioned. Polytheism is setting, not worship: gods eat, sulk, are slapped and are trained under. Pragmatism appears whenever a wish, a button or a fusion solves a problem. Communitarian moments (pooled energy against Moro, 17's wish) mark the endings.

**Shifts across the series.** The early chapters adapt the films *Battle of Gods* and *Resurrection 'F'* and were criticized as a "supercut of the anime" (ANN). The Future Trunks arc darkens with a god's genocide and an erased timeline. The Tournament of Power stakes whole universes. After the anime ended in 2018 the manga grew more independent: the Moro arc adds space-police procedure, and the Granolah arc, praised by reviewers, faces genocide, revenge and Saiyan guilt directly. The Super Hero arc returns to family and comedy before the hiatus following Toriyama's death.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Zeno can erase a universe on a whim. What makes power good or bad: who has it, or how it is used?
2. Goku summons Zeno to stop Zamasu, knowing Zeno may erase the whole timeline. Was that the right choice?
3. Android 17 could have wished for anything, and he chose to bring back universes that were not his own. Why do you think he did that?
4. Vegeta admits his people did terrible things. How should someone respond to wrongs done by their family or people in the past?
5. Granolah wants revenge for his people. What changes his mind, and is revenge ever justice?
