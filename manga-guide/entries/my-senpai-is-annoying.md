**Premise.** Futaba Igarashi, 23, a tiny new salesperson at Itomaki Trading who is often mistaken for a child, is paired with her huge, loud, kind senior Harumi Takeda, who teases her by ruffling her hair. She insists he annoys her; everyone else can see she is falling for him. Short full-color chapters follow sales calls and mistakes (Takeda quietly fixes her first error, ch. 1), after-work drinks, a drunken half-confession, holidays, beach trips and a New Year shrine visit, alongside side romances: the office beauty Touko Sakurai and the deadpan gamer Souta Kazama; Futaba's athletic friend Natsumi Kurobe and Touko's shy high school brother Yuto, who has a crush on her; and Futaba's overprotective grandfather, who raised her. In the final volume Futaba has told Takeda how she feels and must decide whether to follow him when he is transferred overseas; the after-story shows Sakurai and Kazama's wedding and Futaba and Takeda's household (Wikipedia; Penguin Random House; Anitrendz; fan wiki).

## Life message

**Good work and a good life grow from people who look out for each other; being seen as a capable adult, and loved as one, takes patience on both sides.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** How do you get someone to see you as you really are? Futaba is a capable adult whom everyone, from strangers to her grandfather to the man she likes, treats as a child. Can she earn respect at work, and can love grow from a relationship that began with head pats?

**The answer the story gives.** Slowly, by showing up and being kind. Takeda respects her work more than his teasing suggests, Futaba learns to admit her feelings, and the two end up sharing a life (Anitrendz; fan wiki). The story's answer is patience and mutual care.

**Characters as arguments.** Futaba argues for being taken seriously; her pride is both her strength and the comedy. Takeda argues for quiet, protective kindness; critics differ on whether he respects her or talks down to her (ANN Preview Guide). Kazama and Sakurai show love held back by shyness and respect. Kurobe, the teasing friend with a hidden school crush on Futaba, shows love that does not get its wish. The grandfather argues for protection, and learns to let go.

**Symbols and motifs.** Head pats: affection or belittling, depending on who reads it. The scarf Takeda gives Futaba, which she wears all winter (fan wiki). Matching New Year fortunes saying the one you wait for is beside you (anime ep. 11). Shifting color palettes that track Futaba's mood (ANN).

**Traditions it echoes.** The Japanese office romantic comedy (critics compare Wotakoi) and the senpai-kohai mentor story. Readers from many traditions will recognize the dignity of work and patient courtship; temperance traditions will notice the drinking.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarianism and virtue are affirmed: a good workplace is a community of people who cover for each other. Feminism is the contested lens: the story wants Futaba respected, but some critics think its humor undercuts her.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-4 (ch. 1-77, the anime's span) set up the office, the drunken half-confession, the grandfather and the side couples. Volumes 5-12 (ch. 78-231) develop the romances (Yuto asks Kurobe to fireworks, v9). Volume 13 (ch. 232-239 plus after-story) brings the confession, Takeda's overseas transfer, Sakurai and Kazama's wedding and the leads' household (Wikipedia; Penguin Random House; Anitrendz).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Takeda fixed Futaba's mistake without telling her. Was that kind, or did it treat her like a child?
2. Futaba hates being mistaken for a kid. How should people treat someone who looks younger than they are?
3. When is teasing a sign of friendship, and when does it go too far?
4. Futaba said what she really felt only when she was drunk. Why is it so hard to say it sober?
5. What makes a good senpai, at work or at school?
