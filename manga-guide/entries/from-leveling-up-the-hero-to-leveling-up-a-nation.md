**Premise.** Eiga (Aega in English), 27, has spent years training the members of a hero's party; once they outgrow him, the good-natured hero lets him go (MangaDex synopsis; Dengeki Online). Instead of severance pay he is given Tokumo, a remote far-eastern frontier of seven villages and about 2,500 people (MangaDex synopsis; BookWalker light novel vol. 1). Finding the land rich in resources and talented people, he decides that if he trains its people, his territory might become strong enough to defeat the Demon King (BookWalker vol. 1). Early chapters are titled "Severance," "Merchant," "Strong Nation," "Zaharbelt" and "Kimura and Nakamura" (Mechacomic). He borrows from the merchant Garcia to train a 150-soldier elite squad and must repay 10 million within a month (manga vol. 5); the thief Torudo steals Eiga's face to take the territory of Centreia from its ruler Tiana while Eiga attends an imperial council (vol. 9); Torudo and the Grim Reaper Yuuri scheme against Eiga's group as it seeks an S-class license (vol. 12); and as the battle with the Demon King nears, Eiga resolves to confess his feelings to Tiana while the hero Cross, manipulated by Yuuri, gives in to the darkness in his heart (vol. 14, the climax of the imperial-capital arc) (BookWalker).

## Life message

**Patiently developing other people's abilities builds lasting strength, for a party or a whole land; being let go is a new beginning, not an ending.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What is a teacher worth once the students outgrow him? Eiga's party no longer needs him; the series asks whether his gift was only for them.

**The answer the story gives.** The gift was never about one party. Turned on a whole land of ordinary people, training can build a nation strong enough to face the Demon King (BookWalker vol. 1).

**Characters as arguments.** Eiga argues for patient investment in others. Garcia argues that money has terms that must be kept (vol. 5). Torudo argues for deception, stealing a face to steal a land (vol. 9). Yuuri argues for manipulation, and Cross shows what happens to a hero who loses his footing after outgrowing his teacher (vol. 14).

**Symbols and motifs.** Severance paid in land; the remote territory with a hidden secret; the stolen face; the S-class license.

**Traditions it echoes.** The 'expelled from the hero's party' genre and the kingdom-building isekai; for faith readers, the parable of the talents and the teacher who raises many students.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Pragmatism and communitarianism win. Might-makes-right is present as the world's ranking system but softened by fair dealing. Hedonism is a genre add-on.

**Shifts across the series.** Early chapters set up the dismissal, the merchant and the capital ("Severance" to "Zaharbelt"); middle volumes build the squad and handle debt (vol. 5) and impersonation (vol. 9); volumes 12-14 move to the imperial capital, licenses, romance and the hero's fall (BookWalker). The secret of Tokumo is not revealed in any source opened.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Eiga's party let him go when they no longer needed him. Was that fair? How should he respond?
2. Why might training other people be a more lasting strength than being strong yourself?
3. Eiga borrows money with a hard deadline. What are the risks of debt?
4. The hero Cross is led into darkness by someone who manipulates him. How can you tell when someone is manipulating you?
5. What would you want to build if you were given a place of your own?
