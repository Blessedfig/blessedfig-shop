**Premise.** Hiro Momose was used as an errand girl by delinquents in junior high, so she enrolls at a high school with no delinquents, dreaming of a peaceful life (Japanese Wikipedia; Kodansha series page). Required to join a club, she ends up in the nearly empty Helpers Club ("Otasuke-bu") with her classmate Dan Ichikura, a quiet, black-haired boy in glasses whom everyone admires as a model student. After he saves her from a delinquent's harassment, she learns he was a notorious delinquent in junior high and is hiding it (Wikipedia). He insists he has changed, but he is still quick to pick fights (vol. 1). In volume 2 he has her date him, almost by force, to prove he is not like other delinquents; she comes to like him as he keeps his promise not to fight, until a hardcore delinquent from his past appears (Kodansha and Seven Seas vol. 2). Later volumes bring a transfer student who knows his past (vol. 3), the whole class learning his secret after the sports festival and a one-on-one fight between Momose and a girl from his past (vol. 4), a visit to his family (vol. 5), a red-haired delinquent, Yachiyo, who challenges Ichikura and confesses to Momose (vols. 6-7), Ichikura's shady methods to recover money a club member lost to a scam (vol. 8), a turf war among delinquents at another school in which Momose is reported kidnapped and Ichikura is taken (vols. 9-10), a hot-spring trip (vol. 11) and the start of their second year (vol. 12) (Kodansha and Seven Seas blurbs).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can a person really leave their past behind, and should others trust them when they say they have? Momose, who was mistreated by delinquents, discovers that the model student beside her is one, and must decide whether to believe he has changed.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, with help. Ichikura changes because he wants to and because Momose and his friends give him reasons to keep trying. The story is honest that he slips, and it tests the change against rivals, a scam and a turf war (vols. 6-10).

**Characters as arguments.** Ichikura argues that people can change but that change is a daily choice. Momose argues for trust earned slowly, and becomes braver in giving it. Rivals like Yachiyo argue for the old code of strength, and some are won over. The class, once his secret is out, decides what kind of community it is (vol. 4).

**Symbols and motifs.** The glasses (a disguise that becomes a real self). Momose's bleached hair (her own disguise). The Helpers Club. The one-on-one fight as a test of honor and of love.

**Traditions it echoes.** The yankee romance of Japanese manga, from bad-boy shojo heroes to delinquent action stories; the redemption arc found in every moral tradition; and the old comedy of the hidden identity.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Virtue ethics is affirmed through habit and promise-keeping. Expressive individualism is affirmed against judging by looks. The delinquent code of strength is critiqued but its honor-duel style is enjoyed. Nonviolence is a goal the hero aims at, not a rule the story keeps.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-2 (the original 2021 run): the secret and the start of dating. Volumes 3-5: secrets exposed and accepted, family. Volumes 6-8: rivals and the club's cases. Volumes 9-10: the turf war. Volumes 11-12 (Japanese only so far): a trip, White Day and the second year.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Momose was hurt by delinquents. Why does she give Ichikura a chance?
2. Ichikura makes Momose date him at first. Was that okay? How does the relationship change?
3. When the class learns his secret, they have to decide how to treat him. What would you have done?
4. Is it ever right to fight to protect someone? Did the story need the final battle?
5. Ichikura's methods to recover the money shocked Momose. Do good goals excuse any method?
