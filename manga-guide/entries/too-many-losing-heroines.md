**Premise.** Kazuhiko Nukumizu, a first-year at Tsuwabuki High School in Toyohashi, thinks of himself as a background character: no friends, a light-novel habit, a love of tap water. At a restaurant he overhears Anna Yanami, the popular girl in his class, being turned down by her childhood friend Sōsuke, who is dating Karen. Soon he is tangled up with two more "losing heroines": track star Lemon Yakishio, in love with her childhood friend Mitsuki, who is dating Chihaya, and shy literature-club member Chika Komari, who confesses to the club president Shintarō and is rejected because he loves Koto (ch. 1-11). Nukumizu listens, pays for Yanami's meals, keeps secrets and helps each girl face her loss; later chapters follow Lemon's struggle not to come between Mitsuki and Chihaya (ch. 16-21). Along the way the literature club becomes his first real circle of friends. (Wikipedia; anime episode summaries; AniList.)

## Life message

**Losing in love is not the end of your story: be honest about heartbreak, refuse to win by hurting others, let go with grace, and the friends who stay with you will matter more.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What happens to the girl who is not chosen? Romantic comedies end when the hero picks one heroine; the others vanish. This series begins at that moment and asks how a person lives well after losing at love.

**The answer the story gives.** With honesty and friends. Anna admits her jealousy and still wishes Sōsuke and Karen well; Lemon, tempted to come between Mitsuki and Chihaya, chooses not to (ch. 16-21); Chika confesses knowing she may lose (ch. 11). Nukumizu, who expected nothing from people, finds that listening is a kind of strength.

**Characters as arguments.** Anna argues that heartbreak can coexist with appetite and laughter. Lemon argues for fairness even when it hurts. Chika argues for courage despite fear. Nukumizu argues that a "background character" can matter. The "winners" (Karen, Chihaya, Koto) argue that the other side is not a villain.

**Symbols and motifs.** Food and the bill Nukumizu keeps paying; tap water, his odd hobby; the beach and fireworks of a summer that does not go as planned; the trams and stations of Toyohashi; manuscripts written in the literature club.

**Traditions it echoes.** The romantic comedy and its parodies; the Japanese aesthetic of mono no aware (the gentle sadness of things passing); Stoic and Buddhist teaching on accepting what you cannot have; the Christian call to "weep with those who weep" (Romans 12:15).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Community and friendship are affirmed; genre parody is the playful frame; acceptance of loss is the moral core. Feminist readers find the girls well drawn, while the fan service sits uneasily with that.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-2 (ch. 1-11) introduce the three heroines and their rejections. Vol. 3 (ch. 12-15) settles the summer and Nukumizu's friendship with Anna. Vols. 4-5 (ch. 16-22) follow Lemon and Chihaya, then move toward the light novel's third volume; vol. 6 (Japan only) continues. Chapter-level plot beyond the anime is not covered by sources opened.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Anna stays friends with the boy who turned her down. Is that brave, painful, or both?
2. Lemon could have tried to break up Mitsuki and Chihaya. Why doesn't she, and what does it cost her?
3. Nukumizu calls himself a background character. What changes his mind?
4. Chapter 19's title borrows words Jesus spoke about judging others. Why might the author use them for a story about being rejected?
5. Which matters more in this story, romance or friendship?
