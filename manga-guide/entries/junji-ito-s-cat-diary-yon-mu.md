**Premise.** Horror artist J-kun (Junji Ito's self-portrait) builds a new house and invites his fiancée, A-ko, to live with him. She brings her family's cat Yon, whose back markings look to J-kun like a cursed skull, and adopts a Norwegian Forest kitten, Mu. J-kun, a self-described dog person who also lives with his mother and her long-haired chihuahua (ANN), tries play, bribes and pleading to win the cats over, mostly in vain. Each of the ten chapters is a household episode: Mu's arrival (ch. 1), Yon's stress illness, after which J-kun accepts him as family (ch. 2), failed cat-wand games (ch. 3), bribing the cats to sleep on his bed (ch. 4), late-night deadline hallucinations of Yon as a slug, a tsuchinoko and an old man (ch. 5), Yon's escape and recovery from a storehouse (ch. 6-7), Mu's neutering with a brief medical scare (ch. 8), a visit from A-ko's parents and their timid cat Goro (ch. 9), and a fake-poop prank that backfires (ch. 10). Ito draws ordinary moments realistically and switches to his horror style for comic effect (Wikipedia; ANN). The English edition ends with Yon's death in 2011 and the couple's grief (Wikipedia).

## Life message

**Living with others, human or feline, means letting go of being in control: patience, humility and a lot of affection turn a reluctant newcomer into family.**

## Creator and publisher context

- Junji Ito (born 1963, Gifu Prefecture) worked as a dental technician before turning to manga full time; he is known for horror such as Tomie, Uzumaki and Gyo (Wikipedia, Junji Ito).
- Development (Wikipedia, Cat Diary): his editor proposed a cat manga after noticing that Ito had started drawing cats as cute rather than frightening. Ito drew on his own home life; his wife, picture-book artist Ayako Ishiguro, advised on storyboards and the cats' personalities, and disliked only that her stand-in was drawn with tiny pupils and striped pants.
- The English edition adds "Yon Went to Heaven" and Ishiguro's letter on Yon's death in February 2011, first published in a 2011 charity book supporting cat shelters after the Tohoku earthquake (Wikipedia).
- Critics read the book as a self-parody of Ito's horror methods (Austin Price in The Comics Journal, via Wikipedia). No statements by Ito on religion or politics were found in sources opened. **Status: Enough for this entry.**
- Publisher: Kodansha (Monthly Magazine Z, a seinen magazine); Kodansha USA rates the English edition 13+.
- No controversies involving the book were found.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What happens when someone who likes to be in control shares a home with creatures who will not be controlled? J-kun wants the cats to love him on his terms: to play when he waves the wand, to sleep on his bed when he offers treats. They refuse.

**The answer the story gives.** Affection cannot be bought or forced; it grows from patience and from accepting others as they are. J-kun's turning point comes when Yon falls ill and he stops seeing a curse and starts seeing a cat (ch. 2).

**Characters as arguments.** J-kun is eager, anxious love that looks monstrous from the outside (Otaku USA). A-ko is calm, devoted care, so devoted she looks a little eerie herself (ANN's reviewer jokes about this). Yon is independence and dignity ("King Yon," ch. 7). Mu is trust that can be earned. Goro, the timid visitor, shows the household's welcome at its best (ch. 9).

**Symbols and motifs.** Yon's skull-shaped markings, which J-kun reads as a curse. The cat wand that never works. The bed the cats will not share. The horror face, Ito's signature, turned into a picture of frustrated love.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Japanese essay-manga and the long tradition of cat comics; for Ito, a parody of his own horror (Price, via Wikipedia). Many faith traditions' teaching on kindness to animals finds an easy illustration here. My own framing.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarianism wins quietly: the household becomes a family. Absurdism is the joke engine. The occult appears only as J-kun's overheated imagination.

**Shifts across the series.** Chapters 1-4 cover settling in and the first battles for affection; chapters 5-8 cover stranger episodes (hallucinations, escapes, surgery); chapters 9-10 bring visitors and pranks. The English bonus pages change the tone: Yon's death in 2011 and the couple's grief.
