**Premise.** Eight-year-old Katarina Claes, a duke's spoiled daughter, hits her head on a rock and remembers her past life as a 17-year-old Japanese otaku girl. She realizes she is the villainess of the otome game Fortune Lover, and every route ends with her exiled or killed by one of the boys she meets (Wikipedia; fan wiki). Her plan: become a farmer so she can survive exile, learn the sword to survive a duel, and stop bullying anyone. Instead she pulls her lonely adopted brother Keith out of his room (ch. 2), praises shy Mary's garden (ch. 3-4), befriends the albino Sophia and her brother Nicol (ch. 5-6), and wins over her fiancé, Prince Geordo, and his twin Alan. At the Magic Academy (ch. 8-22) she befriends the game's heroine, Maria, a commoner with rare light magic, and survives a hidden route in which the student council president Sirius, whose real name is Raphael, uses dark magic to put her in a coma. Later arcs follow a kidnapping by the spy Sora (ch. 27-34), Keith's abduction (ch. 35-45), graduation (ch. 51-54) and work at the Ministry of Magic in a sequel game's world (ch. 55-79). Nearly everyone, boys and girls, falls in love with her; she notices none of it (Wikipedia; fan wiki).

## Life message

**Treating everyone with sincere kindness and honesty, instead of scheming against the fate you fear, turns would-be enemies into friends and rewrites your ending.**

**Where traditions disagree.** Almost every frame welcomes a heroine who wins by kindness, honesty and friendship across class. The divide is romance: the same-sex crushes of Mary, Sophia and Maria are drawn with the same sympathy as the boys' feelings, so many Christian, Jewish, Muslim, Bahá'í and Latter-day Saint families will want to discuss them (B or a Discuss weight), while progressive, UU and secular readers count them as a strength (S or A). Jehovah's Witness families weigh the magic setting most heavily (C). Reviewers of the anime also differ on whether Geordo's pursuit is charming or possessive.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** If you knew your life was a story with only bad endings, what would you do? Katarina's answer at eight is practical: grow vegetables for exile and practice the sword for a duel. The series asks whether the future is written, and what actually changes it.

**The answer the story gives.** Character changes fate. Every plan Katarina makes fails or misfires, but the way she treats people (pulling Keith out of his room, praising Mary's garden, eating Maria's sweets with joy) turns each would-be executioner into a friend. Gaffney sums up the lesson as treating people as human beings.

**Characters as arguments.** Katarina argues for open-hearted honesty. Geordo is the bored genius who finds life interesting only through her. Keith is the feared child who needed someone not to be afraid of him. Mary and Sophia show bullied girls finding confidence. Maria is the commoner who earns her place by work and gentleness. Raphael is the case of revenge as a curse placed on someone else's pain, and the story cures rather than punishes him.

**Symbols and motifs.** The vegetable field (preparing for the worst becomes a source of joy); the "flags" of a game; the Council of Katarinas in her head; light and dark magic; sweets as friendship.

**Traditions it echoes.** The villainess-isekai genre, Cinderella and the wicked stepsister turned inside out, and the Golden Rule. Christian readers may hear love of enemies; Buddhist readers, metta; secular readers, empathy as the source of goodness.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Classical virtue is affirmed without being preached. Communitarianism wins: the circle of friends is the rescue. Progressive readers find queer acceptance and a critique of class prejudice; traditional readers find courtesy and family healed but may question the same-sex romance. Dark magic stands for envy and grief turned into harm.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-2 (ch. 1-12): childhood, the friends and the Academy's start. Vols. 3-4 (ch. 13-22): the dark-magic arc and the final event. Vols. 5-8 (ch. 23-45): festival, kidnapping and Keith's abduction, with more romance and a kiss. Vols. 9-12 (ch. 46-79): rival, graduation and a sequel game at the Ministry of Magic, with new adult coworkers.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Katarina's plans to avoid doom mostly fail. What actually saves her?
2. Why do so many characters fall for Katarina, and why doesn't she notice?
3. Raphael did harmful things under a curse. Should he be forgiven? What does the story decide?
4. Geordo keeps pursuing Katarina even when she says she wants to end the engagement. Is that romantic or not okay?
5. Maria is bullied because she is a commoner. How do the friends respond, and what would you do?
