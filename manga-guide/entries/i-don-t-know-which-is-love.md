**Premise.** At her high school graduation, Mei Soraike tries to confess to the best friend she has loved for years, only to learn the friend has just gotten a boyfriend (Wikipedia). Determined to get over it, she starts university resolved to find a girlfriend, and within days five women are pursuing her, each with a heightened sense (ANN vol. 1): Riri, a bashful classmate fixated on touch; Maria, her academic advisor and a psychology professor, with a photographic memory for cute sights; Kaoru, her roommate, with a keen sense of smell; Minato, a princely woman with sensitive hearing who loves voices; and Karin Ajima, a "kiss-crazy" upperclassman (taste is the likely fifth sense; to verify). Volume 1 ends with Karin suggesting they date (Okazu vol. 2). In volume 2 Karin confesses, and Mei is roped into a play with Karin and Minato (Yen Press; Okazu). In volume 3 half the script is Mei kissing Karin; she has kissed both Karin and Kaoru, enjoyed both, and goes to Professor Maria for help (Yen Press; Okazu vols. 3-4). In volume 4 Karin formally asks her out, Maria gets drunk, Kaoru takes her to a bathhouse, Mei meets her high school crush again, decides to date Karin, then finds Karin kissing another girl; Riri invites her to a swimsuit photoshoot (Yen Press; Okazu). Volumes 5-6 are a beach trip Maria arranges for all five suitors, ending with Maria proposing that she and Mei stay one more night alone (Yen Press). The central question is in the title: whether attraction is the same as love.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Is attraction love? Mei enjoys being near Riri, Kaoru, Minato, Karin and Maria, and enjoys kissing more than one of them (Okazu vols. 3-4). The title admits she can't tell which feeling is the real thing.

**The answer the story gives.** So far, an open one. The comedy treats attraction as good and fun, and the plot keeps asking Mei to choose; when she does choose Karin (vol. 4), the choice is immediately unsettled. Volume 8 will decide whether love is a choice made against other attractions, or something else (ANN; Okazu).

**Characters as arguments.** Each suitor stands for a sense: touch (Riri), sight and memory (Maria), smell (Kaoru), hearing (Minato) and, likely, taste (Karin's kisses) (ANN vol. 1). Together they argue that attraction comes in through the body. Maria, the professor, argues by her presence that desire can cross a line of responsibility; the story plays it for laughs, which is the point readers most dispute (Okazu). The high school crush who reappears (vol. 4) stands for the love Mei lost before she knew how to look.

**Symbols and motifs.** The five senses; the play (vol. 2-4), where kissing is a role and Mei must decide what is acting; the beach (vol. 5-6), where all the suitors meet; the graduation confession that opens the story.

**Traditions it echoes.** The harem romantic comedy, reversed into an all-female cast (ANN); the old literary question of eros versus lasting love, from the Symposium to Shakespeare's comedies of mistaken affection (from general knowledge; to verify).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism and progressivism are affirmed without argument. Hedonism is explored: pleasure is the evidence Mei must interpret, not yet the answer. Faith traditions will find the question worth asking but the story's practice at odds with their teaching on sex and romance.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1: the five suitors appear. Vols. 2-3: Karin's confession and the kissing play. Vol. 4: Mei chooses, the old crush returns, and the choice wobbles. Vols. 5-6: the beach showdown and a night alone with Maria. Vols. 7-8 (Japan only): not covered by any source.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Mei enjoys kissing two different people. Does that mean she loves both? How would you tell attraction from love?
2. Maria is Mei's professor and academic advisor. Why do schools have rules about teachers dating students? Does the story take that seriously?
3. Early scenes have suitors push hard; later ones ask for consent first. What changes between the two, and why does it matter?
4. The rivals mostly stay friends. Is that realistic? What makes it possible?
5. What does your family believe about dating, and how does this story fit or differ?
