**Premise.** Hatsu Takagi, a high school girl, has a rare fictional illness (called Kokuhaku disease in AniList's and MyAnimeList's synopsis; MangaUpdates calls it "Tempocardy") that gives her heart a limited number of beats; a device on her chest counts how many remain. To save them she lives quietly and alone, avoiding anything exciting. When classmate Hato Iijima accidentally walks in on her changing and sees the device, she admits that what she really wants is to feel everything fully with the time she has. Hato vows to help her live to the fullest and make her heart race (AniList; MyAnimeList). Database tags add a school club and rock band, a summer setting, a first-person narrator, a time skip and the heroine's death (AniList; MangaUpdates; details to verify).

## Life message

**A short life lived fully, with music, friendship and love, is worth more than a longer one spent hiding from feeling.**

Outlook: Bittersweet — the heroine dies (database tags; to verify), but she gets the full life she wanted and the narrator carries it forward after a time skip.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** If you knew exactly how many heartbeats you had left, would you save them or spend them?

**The answer the story gives.** Spend them. Hatsu's careful, isolated life protects her heart and wastes it. With Hato she chooses music, friendship and love, knowing it costs her time (AniList; MyAnimeList).

**Characters as arguments.** Hatsu at first argues for caution; Hatsu afterward argues for life. Hato argues that love means helping someone live as they want, not keeping them safe.

**Symbols and motifs.** The counter on her chest; the racing heart (doki doki) as both danger and joy; summer and a rock band (database tags).

**Traditions it echoes.** Memento mori and Psalm 90:12 for Christian readers; impermanence for Buddhist readers; the Japanese tear-jerker romance (from general knowledge; to verify).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Existential choice is affirmed; withdrawn acceptance is set aside; pleasure is valued but held inside love.

**Shifts across the series.** A single chapter: setup, a summer of living fully, the ending and a time skip (MangaUpdates; to verify).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Hatsu saved her heartbeats by avoiding everything exciting. Was that wise?
2. What did Hato give her that no one else had?
3. If your time were counted, what would you change first?
4. Is there a difference between living fully and taking needless risks?
5. How do people we lose stay with us?
