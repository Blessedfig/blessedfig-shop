**Premise.** First-year high schooler Sakamoto is impossibly cool: handsome, brilliant and unflappable. Girls adore him, many boys resent him, and a steady stream of rivals and delinquents try to humiliate him with pranks and traps, which he turns aside with absurd grace (ch. 1-2). Under the poses he is kind: he gets Kubota, a classmate being bullied for money, a fast-food job and helps him stand up for himself (ch. 3); he protects insects and birds, rescues a delinquent from a fire (ch. 8), wins over an upperclass gang leader, Hayabusa (ch. 10-11), and helps a stalker become a photographer (ch. 13). Over one school year his enemies become admirers. At graduation the scheming veteran delinquent Fukase pushes Acchan, Sakamoto's first bully, to attack him with a metal bat during his speech; Sakamoto turns the attack into part of the speech, saves Acchan from a fall and from punishment, and helps the school be rid of Fukase without violence (ch. 22-24). He then leaves for America to train as an astronaut, sent off with a classroom pie fight (ch. 24) (Wikipedia episode summaries; fan wiki; Seven Seas).

## Life message

**Meeting hostility with composure, kindness and style can change the people around you; the coolest thing is to help others and refuse revenge.**

## Creator and publisher context

- Nami Sano (1987-2023), born in Nishinomiya, Hyogo; wanted to be a manga artist from elementary school; graduated from Kyoto Seika University's manga course and assisted Yoshiaki Sukeno (Twin Star Exorcists). Debuted in Fellows! in 2010; Sakamoto was her first series; Migi & Dali (2017-2021) her second. She died on August 5, 2023, aged 36 (Wikipedia, Nami Sano).
- On-record intent (Wikipedia, Nami Sano): her first pitch was a romantic comedy about a boy who can see five seconds into the future; her editor, Tatsuya Shiode, found the storyboards "not funny at all," and she turned it into a gag manga. She said her next series would also be comedy but focus more on feelings and a goal.

- Publisher: Enterbrain (Kadokawa), Fellows!/Harta, a seinen magazine known for varied, artful titles. English: Seven Seas (rated 13+ per ANN).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What does it mean to be cool? Everyone at the school wants status: looks, popularity, power over others. Sakamoto has all of it effortlessly, and the series asks what he does with it.

**The answer the story gives.** He spends it on others. His tricks are flashy, but their purpose is to help: a job for an extorted boy (ch. 3), a rescue from a fire (ch. 8), a festival saved (ch. 16), a falling attacker caught (ch. 24). Coolness that serves is the real thing; coolness that dominates (the delinquents' hierarchies) is a sham.

**Characters as arguments.** Acchan argues for envy: if I cannot be him, I will bring him down. He ends by vowing to surpass Sakamoto through study (ch. 24). Kubota argues that a weak person can stand up, with help. Hayabusa argues for honor among delinquents and learns that filling another's lack beats faking strength (ch. 21). Fukase is the one who will not change: a manipulator who makes others do harm, compared by himself to a deep ocean of hopelessness, which Sakamoto answers with hope (ch. 24).

**Symbols and motifs.** Sakamoto's glasses and poses; birds he feeds and protects; bubbles in place of cigarettes; a first name no one ever hears; the pie in the face at the end, which hides his only tears (fan wiki).

**Traditions it echoes.** The Japanese gag manga and the "perfect man" parody. Many religious traditions will recognize the soft answer that turns away wrath (Proverbs 15:1), repelling evil with good (Quran 41:34), and Buddhist equanimity.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Classical virtue ethics and Stoicism are celebrated in the hero. Absurdism is the comic method. Nonviolence is shown in his refusals, though not argued. Communitarianism grows as the class bonds.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-2 (ch. 1-11) are pure gags and rival-of-the-week stories. Volume 3 (ch. 12-17.5) adds warmer stories and introduces Fukase. Volume 4 (ch. 18-24) turns sentimental, with memories of the year and the farewell.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Sakamoto takes the blame for the delinquents' fire (ch. 1). Why would he do that, and was it right?
2. How did Sakamoto help Kubota without fighting his battles for him?
3. Acchan attacked Sakamoto, and Sakamoto protected him. What would you have done?
4. Is it okay to bend the truth to keep someone out of trouble?
5. What does it really mean to be "cool"?
