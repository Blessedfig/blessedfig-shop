**Premise.** Touru Suzuki, a 21-year-old college dropout and gamer (ANN; Seven Seas says "high school dropout"), rents a two-bedroom apartment in Tachikawa, Tokyo, for almost nothing, because its front door opens into a stone chamber in a dungeon in another world. He finds Ria, an 18-year-old knight poisoned and abandoned by her party, carries her home and cures her with cola. Because running water, snacks and his otaku belongings work like magic there, she takes him for a great sage, and he lets her believe it (ANN; Goodreads). His apartment becomes a rest stop for adventurers. A white slime that can take human form (Shizuku) and a lonely elf mage (Deet) move in; Touru becomes the first person from Earth to "level up" and plans safe ways to grow stronger (v2-3). The group takes revenge on the adventurers who abandoned Ria (v4), tangles with the Mercenary and Thieves' Guilds (v5), adds the Thieves' Guild leader Miri while his coworkers nearly discover his secret (v6), and ends by holding a Japanese-style summer festival in the dungeon (v7) (Penguin Random House blurbs).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What if the ordinary comforts of modern life (clean water, cola, a hot bath) were the real treasures, and an ordinary young man were the sage?

**The answer the story gives.** Ordinary things, used wisely and shared, make a home and a hero. Touru does not get a magic sword; he gets a plan (Goodreads; v3 blurb), and his door becomes a rest stop where lost adventurers find safety.

**Characters as arguments.** Touru argues for prudence and practical cleverness, but also for a convenient lie (letting Ria think he is a sage). Ria argues for honor and trust, and for the danger of trusting too easily (ANN finds her too soft for a warrior). Her old party argues the opposite of hospitality and is punished (v4). Miri shows a rival turned ally (v6).

**Symbols and motifs.** The front door as a border between worlds; the rest stop as a home; cola as a potion; the summer festival as a gift from one culture to another (v7).

**Traditions it echoes.** The isekai and dungeon-crawl genres, the harem comedy, and the "reverse isekai" fish-out-of-water joke. Its hospitality theme echoes old guest-and-host traditions in many religions.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Pragmatism is the hero's strength; consumerism is played as wonder; hedonism as comfort. The fan service and harem dynamics are part of the appeal and are not questioned by the story.

**Shifts across the series.** Volume 1 (ch. 1-5): the apartment and Ria. Volumes 2-3 (ch. 6-17): Shizuku, Deet, leveling up. Volume 4 (ch. 18-23): revenge on Ria's old party. Volumes 5-6 (ch. 24-35): guild intrigue, Miri, coworkers. Volume 7 (ch. 36-40): the dungeon festival and an open ending (PRH blurbs; MangaDex map).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Touru lets Ria believe he is a great sage. Was that harmless? When would you tell her the truth?
2. What ordinary things in your home would seem like magic to someone from another world?
3. Touru and his friends get payback on Ria's old party. Is that justice or revenge?
4. What makes Touru's apartment feel like a home for his guests?
5. How does the art show the women in the story? Do you think it changes how readers see them?
