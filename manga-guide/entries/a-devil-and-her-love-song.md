**Premise.** Maria Kawai, a beautiful girl with a striking singing voice, transfers to Totsuka High after being expelled from St. Katria, a Catholic girls' school, for hitting a teacher, a nun whose cross she still carries (Wikipedia; The Manga Critic). Her habit of saying exactly what she sees makes the class hate her; they call her "Devil Maria." Two boys are drawn in by her singing: cheerful Yusuke Kanda, who teaches her to put a "lovely spin" on things, and aloof Shin Meguro, a pianist who froze at his childhood debut (Wikipedia). The first arc (vols. 1-4) is bullying and a class choir contest: girls tear up her uniform and ruin her shoes, the homeroom teacher scapegoats her, pours ink on her concert dress and plots to stage her "reform" on television, and a sweet-seeming classmate, Hana, turns the class against her, until the truth comes out on camera (Wikipedia; TV Tropes; ANN vols. 3-4). The second arc (vols. 5-10) brings back Anna, a former friend who lost her voice and now tries to take Shin by telling him about Maria's buried past (VIZ vols. 5-6). Maria has blocked out that her mother died by suicide in her arms when she was a small child (TV Tropes); as memories return she has hallucinations (vol. 10), loses her own voice, and attempts suicide (TV Tropes). In vol. 11 she learns that her mother was abducted and raped at 14 by an American soldier, John Cross, who was released without conviction, and that she is his daughter; in Yokosuka she finds her mother's family and learns Cross is now a priest who runs a church orphanage where her younger schoolmate Shintaro Kurosu grew up (The Fandom Post). Maria reconciles with her father (vol. 12, VIZ; ANN). Shin goes abroad for surgery on his injured hand, and the series closes at graduation with Maria singing on and the couple's future left open (VIZ vol. 13; ANN vol. 13).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Is it better to tell the truth and be hated, or to fit in? Maria sees through people and says so; the class calls her a devil. Beneath that runs a second question: can a person who has done terrible harm be forgiven, and can the one harmed sing again?

**The answer the story gives.** Truth, joined with kindness and real friends, wins: Maria leads the choir, the teacher's lie is exposed, and the class comes around (vols. 1-4). Deeper healing comes through memory faced and forgiveness given: Maria learns the truth of her birth and reconciles with the father who wronged her mother (vols. 11-12). The Fandom Post reads this as earned redemption; ANN finds it too warm.

**Characters as arguments.** Maria argues for honesty; Yusuke for tact; Shin for quiet protection, which can become overprotection. The homeroom teacher argues that children are tools for an institution's image, and loses. Hana argues for manipulation behind a smile. Anna argues that love can be possessive and wounding. John Cross is the argument that repentance can remake a life, set against the reader's sense that his crime went unpunished.

**Symbols and motifs.** "Amazing Grace," a song about a wretch saved, sung by a girl called a devil. The nun's cross she keeps. The lost voice (Anna's, then Maria's). Ink poured on a white concert dress. The names: Maria, Cross, Kurosu.

**Traditions it echoes.** The shoujo outcast story and the bullying drama; for Christian readers, the parable of the prodigal and the call to forgive seventy times seven; for others, the question of whether forgiveness and justice can coexist.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Christian grace is affirmed and carries the ending. Expressive individualism is affirmed but tamed by kindness. Critical theory's concern with adult and state power appears in the teacher and the soldier, without becoming the message.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-4: bullying, the corrupt teacher, the choir. Vols. 5-7: Anna returns and Maria's buried past surfaces. Vols. 8-10 (ch. about 50-68): Kurosu, Shin's concert, hallucinations, the suicide attempt (to verify). Vols. 11-12: Yokosuka, the truth of her birth, forgiveness. Vol. 13: Shin abroad, graduation, an open ending.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Maria always tells the truth. When is honesty kind, and when is it cruel?
2. Why did the class change its mind about Maria? Was it fair that it took so long?
3. The teacher used Maria to make the school look good. What should students do when an adult abuses their power?
4. John Cross did something terrible and spent his life trying to make up for it. Can that be enough? Who gets to decide?
5. "Amazing Grace" keeps returning. Why that song, for this girl?
