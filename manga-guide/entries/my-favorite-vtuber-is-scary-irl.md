**Premise.** In a rural town, a short, introverted college student delivers food for the app Eatza (Siliconera). One regular customer frightens him: a very tall woman with piercings and chokers who stares in silence (Vice). After work he unwinds with streams by his favorite VTuber, Fuwari Hanae. The customer is in fact the woman behind Fuwari, a shut-in with severe social anxiety who was bullied for her height and figure and lost her voice-acting dream to gossip that she had used her body to get roles (Anime UK News). Wanting to change, she speaks to him, and later offers to pay him just to talk with her (publisher blurb). Neither character is named. In vol. 1 they become friends without his knowing who she is; in vol. 2 a rival delivery worker, Akari, joins, an outing to a crowded city tests her, and she reveals that she is Fuwari; in vol. 3 he decides to stay by her side, the would-be VTuber Mizu Melody arrives, and the two plan a "redo" of the youth they missed, starting with a train ride (Siliconera; The Outer Haven).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can someone who has been hurt for how she looks learn to face the world again, and what does real help look like? The "scary" customer is the most frightened person in the story; the timid delivery man is the one who can help her.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, slowly, with someone at your side who does not take over. Siliconera stresses that he consults her and lets her decide, and that he too is vulnerable: he once nearly dropped out of school and kept going because of her streams (vol. 3). Help runs both ways.

**Characters as arguments.** She argues that courage is built in small steps. He argues that kindness means patience and honesty, even when it would be easier to keep his distance (vol. 3). Akari shows hostility born of insecurity melting when people talk (vol. 2). Mizu Melody shows the loneliness that online fame can hide (vol. 3).

**Symbols and motifs.** The apartment door, first a barrier, then a meeting place. The VTuber avatar: a safe face for a frightened person. Height: hers made her a target, his made him one too. The train ride: the first item of their "redo" of youth (vol. 3).

**Traditions it echoes.** Komi Can't Communicate (Siliconera's comparison), the idol-and-fan romance and the healing slice of life; more broadly, every story in which someone who looks frightening is the one most in need of kindness.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Care and community win. The consumer world of streaming and gig apps is simply the setting, neither praised nor attacked. The feminist critique of body-shaming is in the plot, and reviewers turn it on the art itself (Anime UK News).

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1: meeting and friendship, with the secret kept. Vol. 2: Akari, an outing to the city, the reveal. Vol. 3: honesty, Mizu Melody, plans to redo their youth. Vol. 4 onward: not yet in English.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why was the delivery man afraid of her at first? What did he learn?
2. How does he help her without taking over?
3. Gossip cost her a job she loved. What could others have done?
4. Was it a good idea to accept money to talk with a customer? What makes a situation safe?
5. Some reviewers say the art focuses too much on her body. Do you agree, and does it matter?
