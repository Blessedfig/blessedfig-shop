**Premise.** Renako Amaori spent middle school as a friendless shut-in and reinvents herself to join the popular crowd at an elite girls' high school, where she becomes best friends with Mai Oduka, a teen supermodel (Seven Seas; Wikipedia). When Mai finds her on the roof and later confesses romantic love, Renako insists friendship is better; the two agree to a contest over whether they will be best friends or lovers, and settle on 'friends with Rena-fits' (Wikipedia; ANN). The rest of their popular five-girl group falls for Renako too: Satsuki fake-dates her to provoke Mai (manga vols. 3-4, ANN), Ajisai, who carries the weight of caring for her younger brothers, runs away with her and confesses (vols. 5-6, ANN), and Kaho, an old friend turned cosplayer, joins later. In the light novels Renako confesses to both Mai and Ajisai and the three begin dating with everyone's agreement, after which Satsuki asks to join (LN vol. 4; Wikipedia; Okazu); the anime film covering this starts from manga vol. 8, ch. 60 (MangaUpdates). The characters are high school first-years.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Is it safer to be someone's best friend than their lover? Renako, who spent middle school alone, believes friendship is the stable thing and romance the risk, so when Mai confesses she argues for friendship and they turn the question into a contest (Wikipedia; Anime Feminist).

**The answer the story gives.** Honesty beats strategy. Every scheme (the contest, Satsuki's fake dating, the masks the girls wear at school) gives way to people saying what they feel. The light novels end the romance by letting Renako love two girls openly, with their agreement (LN vol. 4). The story is weaker on its other answer: critics say it treats Renako's 'no' as part of the fun, though she does slap Mai when Mai goes too far (end of vol. 1).

**Characters as arguments.** Renako argues for caution and, later, for honesty. Mai argues that love should be declared and pursued, and her pursuit crosses lines the story partly excuses. Ajisai argues for care and respect, and ANN's reviewer finds her the better match. Satsuki argues from pride and rivalry. Mai's mother, in the novels, stands for image and control.

**Symbols and motifs.** The rooftop where Mai and Renako first connect; Mai's hair up or down as a signal of friend or girlfriend days (ANN); masks and makeup (Kaho's colored contacts, Mai's model persona); the group of five.

**Traditions it echoes.** The yuri romantic comedy and the harem comedy with a female lead (ANN calls it a GL harem); the school glow-up story; Japanese tatemae and honne (public face and true feeling).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism and progressivism are affirmed: being honest about who you love is the way to happiness, whatever form the relationship takes. Hedonism appears as fan service rather than as a philosophy. Tradition appears mainly as pressure from Mai's family, which the girls resist.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-2: Mai's confession, the contest and the forced advance and slap (end of vol. 1). Vols. 3-4: Satsuki's fake dating and her troubled home (ANN). Vols. 5-6: Ajisai's image pressure, running away and confession; the relationships tangle (ANN). Vols. 7-10 (not covered by any review opened): likely Kaho's arc and the three-way relationship, which the anime film adapts from vol. 8, ch. 60 (MangaUpdates).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Renako thinks friendship is safer than romance. Why might she think that, and do you agree?
2. Mai keeps pushing after Renako says no. How should a friend respond to a 'no'?
3. Why do Renako, Mai and Ajisai each feel they have to wear a mask at school?
4. What does Ajisai do differently from Mai that reviewers like?
5. The story ends with three people agreeing to date. What do you think of that ending, and what would your family think?
