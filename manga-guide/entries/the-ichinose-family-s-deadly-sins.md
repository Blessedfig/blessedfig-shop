**Premise.** On a trip to Fukui, the six Ichinoses crash and all wake in hospital with amnesia: middle schooler Tsubasa, his younger sister Shiori, their parents Kakeru and Minako, and grandparents Kozo and Sachie. They happily imagine the loving family they must have been, then go home to a filthy house, locks on every door, a family photo with faces scratched out, and Tsubasa's room covered with the word "die" (ch. 1; Anime Corner; Japanese Wikipedia). At school his "best friend" Nakajima pours leftover food on him; Tsubasa turns the bullying back on Nakajima before they reconcile (ch. 2-5). He learns that Shiori meets adult men she found on social media and stops one of them (ch. 6-9). On a company trip Kakeru drives the family off the road; they wake with amnesia again and the father is now a different man (ch. 10-11). Minako remembers her husband's affair, takes the other woman's child, Kenta, meaning to hurt him and does not, and is then erased from everyone's memory (ch. 12-15). Kozo reveals the family has looped about 2,000 times (ch. 16). The new "father," who is really Tsubasa's older brother Sota, tells Tsubasa the whole world is his dream: he has been in a coma for four years (ch. 17-25). Awake, Tsubasa finds Sota living in a makeshift "family" with Ayano and Kenta, which also falls apart (ch. 35-43), before the real Ichinoses meet again, still quarrelling but together (ch. 44-48) (ciatr; Japanese Wikipedia; AniList reviews).

## Life message

**No family is perfect; running from what you did into a comfortable dream only deepens the harm, and a family can go on only by facing its past, together and still quarrelling.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** If your whole family forgot everything, could you start over as the family you wish you had been? The Ichinoses try. The series asks whether forgetting is a mercy or a hiding place.

**The answer the story gives.** Forgetting is a hiding place. Each loop resets the family to the same false start; the dream world exists because Tsubasa, and then others, cannot face what happened (ch. 16-25). Only accepting the past lets him wake (Japanese Wikipedia). The ending refuses a neat cure: the family leaves the hospital tired and still arguing, but together (AniList review).

**Characters as arguments.** Tsubasa argues for honesty, even about himself: he discovers he may have been a bully as well as a victim (ch. 2-5). Kakeru argues for escape, and his deliberate crash is its end point. Minako argues for justice turned to revenge, and stops short of harming a child (ch. 15). Sota argues that a family that fails you can be replaced; his chosen family scatters (ch. 40). Kozo argues that a family can be engineered back together; his "trip" is explained at the end.

**Symbols and motifs.** Rooms as souls: each hidden "sin" is shown by a bedroom (Real Sound via Japanese Wikipedia); "die" on the wall; locks on every door; the scratched photo; the family meal table with an empty chair (Anime Corner); the hospital as both the start and the end.

**Traditions it echoes.** The amnesia mystery and the time loop; the Japanese home drama; Christian and Jewish ideas of confession and repentance; the Buddhist picture of waking from delusion. It shares Takopi's Original Sin's gap between cute art and cruel subject matter.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Existentialism wins: Tsubasa chooses reality. Communitarian loyalty is affirmed but not idealized. Confucian family hierarchy is critiqued for hiding harm. No religious frame is offered or mocked.

**Shifts across the series.** Volume 1 (ch. 1-7): the house, bullying, Shiori's secret. Volume 2 (ch. 8-16): the crash, the new father, the affair, the loops. Volume 3 (ch. 17-25): the dream revealed, Sachie's illness, Tsubasa wakes. Volumes 4-5 (ch. 26-43): Sota, the collapse, Sota's makeshift family. Volume 6 (ch. 44-48): the return and the ending.

**Discussion questions.**
1. If you could forget something you did wrong, would you want to? What would you lose?
2. Tsubasa was bullied and then became a bully. How do people get caught in that cycle, and how did he get out?
3. Shiori met adults she found online. What should she have done, and what did Tsubasa do right?
4. Sota tried to build a new family. Why did it fall apart?
5. The family is still arguing at the end. Is that a happy ending?
