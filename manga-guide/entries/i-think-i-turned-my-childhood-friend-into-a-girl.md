**Premise.** Kenshiro Mido, a tall, popular, athletic high schooler, has secretly loved cosmetics since his three older sisters let him practice on them. He asks his childhood friend Hiura Mihate, small, shy and plain, to be his practice model. The makeover turns Hiura into a strikingly pretty "girl"; Hiura finds he likes it, starts wearing the girls' uniform to school, and becomes popular and more confident, while Kenshiro keeps his hobby secret by letting people think Hiura does his own makeup (Seven Seas vol. 1-2; TV Tropes). Hiura, who loved Kenshiro before the makeover and partly dresses up hoping Kenshiro will notice him, resolves to confess at the summer festival (vol. 4). The two end up living under one roof for a time (vol. 5), Hiura makes friends and a "society debut" with a rich classmate (vol. 6), a makeup-loving transfer student, Miria, stirs jealousy (vol. 7), and Hiura stars in the class's Little Mermaid at the school festival with Kenshiro credited as makeup artist (vols. 8-9). By vol. 10 they are dating; vol. 11 brings their first date, holding hands at an aquarium, a Halloween arc for chapter 100, and a bonus chapter Seven Seas says "raises the heat" (Seven Seas). Hiura is a boy throughout; the author confirmed this after the first English printing presented him as a trans girl (Bounding Into Comics; TV Tropes; Seven Seas' revision notice).

## Creator and publisher context

- Azusa Banjo debuted with an otokonoko story and set out to write a romance about a shy, slightly clumsy feminine boy; she added makeup so someone other than Hiura would trigger the change (Wikipedia, Production).
- On-record intent (via Wikipedia): she designed Hiura from his dressed-up look first, wanted his plain look still cute, named him after Pyrrha, the name Achilles used while disguised as a girl, and wanted a positive picture of male friendship. ## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What happens when the person you have known all your life suddenly looks like someone new? Kenshiro meant only to practice makeup; he ends up seeing his oldest friend, and himself, differently. The series asks whether we love people for how they look or for who they have always been.

**The answer the story gives.** Both, and that is fine. Seven Seas' vol. 2 blurb has Kenshiro accept that Hiura is the same person in a new face, while Hiura's new look gives him the confidence to speak up, make friends and confess. Appearance is shown as a key that opens a door, not a mask.

**Characters as arguments.** Hiura argues that choosing how to present yourself can make you more yourself. Kenshiro argues that a passion others might call unmanly is worth owning; his arc is to step out from behind Hiura and be credited for his work (vol. 8). The sisters stand for a family that encourages both. Miria, a girl who shares Kenshiro's hobby, tests whether Hiura's place in Kenshiro's life rests on makeup or on love (vol. 7).

**Symbols and motifs.** The makeup brush as a tool of discovery; the school uniform, switched; the Little Mermaid (vols. 8-9), a story of changing one's form for love, staged with Hiura as the lead and Kenshiro credited by name; the umbrella of vol. 10's title, a shared space once they are a couple.

**Traditions it echoes.** The makeover romance (the plain friend made beautiful), turned so that both friends are boys; the Japanese otokonoko genre; and, by the author's choice of name, the myth of Achilles disguised as a girl.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism is the whole engine and is always rewarded. Progressivism appears as a world where nobody objects, rather than as a fight against objectors. Traditional views of gender are not argued against; they are simply absent.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-3: the makeover, school reactions, summer and the beach. Vols. 4-5: the festival confession and living together. Vols. 6-9: new friends, a rival, and the school play, with Kenshiro's secret going public. Vols. 10-12: dating, first dates and the 100th chapter.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why did Kenshiro keep his love of makeup a secret? Have you ever hidden something you enjoy?
2. Hiura says dressing up makes him braver. What makes you feel braver?
3. Kenshiro realizes Hiura is the same person with a different look. Do clothes change who someone is?
4. The first English version called Hiura a girl; the author said he is a boy. Why might that difference matter to readers?
5. What does your family believe about how boys and girls should dress, and why?
