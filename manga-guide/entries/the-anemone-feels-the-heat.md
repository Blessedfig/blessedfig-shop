**Premise.** Nagisa Ootsuki studied all through middle school for one elite high school, but on exam day she stopped to help a girl collapsing in the snow, missed the exam and failed. She enrolls at a nearby private school with a new look (dyed hair, contacts instead of glasses) and finds that the girl at the next desk, the frail, cheerful Mashiro Komiyama, is the same girl, who does not remember her (Wikipedia; Comic Natalie). Every look at Mashiro reminds Nagisa of her failure, so she resolves to change her feelings by learning to like her (Yen Press). Okazu's vol. 1 review traces how Mashiro, often in the infirmary, begins to take care of herself and seize chances because of Nagisa, and how a hug in the infirmary changes Nagisa's feelings. Chapter titles trace the romance: "I Like You" (ch. 12), "Girlfriend" (ch. 19), "Matching Rings" (ch. 25), Christmas and a New Year shrine visit (ch. 27-30), graduation of older students (ch. 39), new juniors (ch. 36, 42) and a final chapter titled "Love" (ch. 52) (Wikipedia; MangaDex). Side couples fill the school: the student council vice president Riri loves the president Mizuki, who in turn loves the school nurse, Akari Hayama (AniList character notes); Okazu calls later volumes a school full of couples.

## Life message

**Choosing warmth over resentment can turn a loss into love: kindness given and returned helps two girls grow, take care of themselves and build a life together.**

Outlook: Hopeful — a lost exam becomes a new life: Nagisa and Mashiro become girlfriends (ch. 19), exchange rings (ch. 25) and reach a final chapter titled "Love" (ch. 52); a side character's heartbreak (ch. 37) and quarrels (ch. 14, 44) are the shadows.
Hope source: L — the way through is love between two: Nagisa's choice to like Mashiro and their relationship carry the story; friendship (F) among classmates and the student council is the close second.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What do you do with someone who, through no fault of her own, cost you the future you planned? Nagisa missed her exam because she helped Mashiro, and Mashiro does not even know it.

**The answer the story gives.** Choose to like her. Nagisa's decision to turn resentment into warmth is the story's moral turn, and it pays back: the girl she resented becomes the person she loves, and the girl she helped learns to live more boldly (Comic Natalie; Okazu).

**Characters as arguments.** Nagisa argues that feelings can be chosen and trained. Mashiro argues that gratitude and cheerfulness can coexist with weakness. Mizuki, who loves someone she cannot have, argues from experience for honesty. Nagisa's little sisters represent the family love that a new relationship must make room for.

**Symbols and motifs.** The anemone (in flower lore, often linked to fragile hope or abandonment, from general knowledge; to verify) taking on heat; snow on exam day; the infirmary as the place where things change; matching rings.

**Traditions it echoes.** The good deed that costs the doer (the Good Samaritan, for Christian readers), the enemies-to-lovers romance, and the Japanese school yuri story.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism and progressivism are simply assumed: love between girls is normal in this school. Classical virtue (kindness, patience) carries the moral weight. No religious lens appears beyond a seasonal shrine visit.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-2 (ch. 1-12): resentment to confession. Vols. 3-5 (ch. 13-30): a couple, fights, the beach, culture festival, Christmas, New Year. Vols. 6-7 (ch. 31-41): side couples, the nurse thread, graduation of seniors and new juniors. Vols. 8-9 (ch. 42-52): misunderstandings, an argument and the final chapter, "Love."

**Discussion questions.**
1. Nagisa lost her chance at her dream school by helping a stranger. Would you have stopped?
2. Can you decide to like someone? What did Nagisa actually do to change her feelings?
3. How does Mashiro change because of Nagisa? How does Nagisa change because of Mashiro?
4. What does the story say about living with a body that is often sick?
5. What does a good friend do when someone has feelings that cannot be returned?
