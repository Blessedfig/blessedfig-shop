**Premise.** Tsuyoshi Mukouda ("Mukohda"), a 27-year-old salaryman who likes to cook, is pulled into a fantasy world by accident when the Kingdom of Reijseger summons three Japanese high school students as "Heroes" for its war. His stats are poor and his only unique skill is "Online Supermarket," which lets him buy goods from modern Japan with local money. Sensing that the royal family plans to use the Heroes (the kingdom, fans' wiki notes, really wants them to conquer its neighbors), he talks his way out with a little gold and leaves the country (manga vol. 1; Wikipedia; fan wiki). His cooking turns out to raise people's stats and charms Fel, a legendary Fenrir wolf, who forces a familiar contract on him in exchange for meals. A baby slime, Sui, a pixie dragon, Dora-chan, and later an ancient dragon join. The gods of the world notice too: Ninrir, goddess of wind, trades her blessing for sweets; the goddesses of fire, earth and water and the gods of war and smithing follow, wanting beer, beauty products, cakes and whiskey; the creator god, Demiurge, finally scolds them for extorting Mukohda (Wikipedia). Mukohda earns money as an adventurer (his familiars do the fighting) and as a supplier of soap and shampoo to the merchant Lambert, and the manga follows the party through Karelina, a dungeon city, the seaside town of Berléand and an undead-filled dungeon (J-Novel Club vols. 4-11).

## Life message

**A good life is a modest, comfortable one shared with companions over good food: avoid being used by the powerful, work honestly, and be generous with what you have.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What do you do when you are handed a world of adventure and a useless power? Mukohda's answer is to decline the adventure: he sees that the kingdom wants weapons, not heroes, and chooses a quiet life of cooking instead.

**The answer the story gives.** A good life is small and shared. The "absurd" skill turns out to be the most powerful one, not because it wins battles but because it feeds people, and fed people (and wolves, and gods) become friends. Caution and generosity beat ambition.

**Characters as arguments.** Mukohda argues for prudence and hospitality. Fel argues for strength and appetite, and is tamed by kindness and ginger pork. The gods argue, comically, that even divine power wants to be fed; their creator argues that greed must be checked. The Reijseger royals are the case against using people as tools.

**Symbols and motifs.** The online supermarket screen. The campfire. The offering box for the gods. The bath. The Item Box, where everything can be stored and nothing spoils.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Japanese isekai boom and its "slow life" branch; food manga (*Restaurant to Another World*, *Delicious in Dungeon*, named by Anime Feminist); the folk motif of feeding a dangerous beast to befriend it; and the old idea, shared by many religions, that hospitality to strangers can mean entertaining gods unawares.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Pragmatism and hedonism are affirmed, gently. Consumerism is the engine, explored but not questioned. Authoritarian power is avoided rather than fought. The gods are affectionate satire. Later novels turn more critical (a false church, the kingdom's abuse of the Heroes), but the manga has not reached them.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-4: escape, Fel, Sui, the first goddess, guild quests. Volumes 5-6: wealth, more goddesses, the soap business, Karelina. Volumes 7-9: Krehl, Dolan's dungeon, the war and smithing gods, the earth dragon. Volumes 10-11: the sea at Berléand, the kraken, a strange dragon and an undead dungeon (J-Novel Club).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Mukohda leaves the kingdom instead of becoming a Hero. Was that cowardly or wise?
2. Why does Fel stay with Mukohda? What does that say about friendship?
3. The gods want snacks, beer and shampoo in return for blessings. What does the story suggest about these gods, and how is that different from what your family believes?
4. Is it fair that Mukohda's modern food is always better than the local food?
5. What would you share around your own campfire?
