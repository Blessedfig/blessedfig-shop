**Premise.** Kyoko Mogami, 16, left school in Kyoto and works several jobs in Tokyo to support her childhood friend Sho Fuwa as he becomes a pop idol, until she overhears him calling her a plain, boring girl he keeps around as a free maid. Her "Pandora's box" of grudges bursts open (drawn as tiny comic demons) and she vows to beat him in show business. She fails the audition at the agency LME because she cannot love an audience, so the president puts her in a new "Love Me Section" doing odd jobs to earn points (ch. 1-8). Through jobs, a chicken-mascot role, the TV drama Dark Moon (ch. 51-78) and other parts, revenge gives way to a real love of acting and friendship with the rival actress Kanae. Her mentor, Japan's top actor Ren Tsuruga, 20, is secretly Kuon Hizuri, son of a Hollywood star, haunted by the death of his friend Rick in America; he was also "Corn," the boy Kyoko met as a child. In the long Cain and Setsuka Heel arc (ch. 151-215), the two play siblings off-set to help Ren hold his dark past at bay. Kyoko learns her mother never wanted her (ch. 216-233), and Ren finally confesses his love; they agree not to date yet and to keep working toward their goals (ch. 273-286) (Wikipedia; fan wiki).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Who are you when the person you lived for throws you away? Kyoko had built her whole life around Sho; when he calls her a free maid, she has nothing of her own. The series asks what she will build in its place.

**The answer the story gives.** A self made through work and relationships. Lory tells her an actor needs love, and Kyoko, who has "lost the will to love" (VIZ volume description, via MangaUpdates), recovers it through roles, friends and her mentor. Revenge gives the first push; craft carries her on.

**Characters as arguments.** Sho argues that people are tools for success, then finds he cannot let Kyoko go. Ren argues for discipline and professionalism, while hiding guilt over Rick's death (fan wiki). Kanae argues that ambition and friendship can coexist. Saena, Kyoko's mother, is the hardest argument: some parents do not love their children, and the child must decide what to do with that (Wikipedia).

**Symbols and motifs.** Pandora's box and its tiny grudge demons; the Love Me Section's shocking pink uniforms (from general knowledge; to verify); Corn, the stone from a childhood friend; Bo the chicken suit through which Kyoko and Ren talk honestly; roles (Mio, Natsu, Setsuka) that let Kyoko explore darker selves.

**Traditions it echoes.** Shojo backstage dramas such as Glass Mask, the Cinderella story turned on its head, and fairy tales Kyoko loves. Christian and Buddhist readers may hear the theme of letting go of a grudge.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Virtue ethics is affirmed in professionalism and perseverance. Expressive individualism is affirmed, but the self Kyoko finds is relational, not lonely. Feminist readings find a heroine who refuses to be a doormat, though the romance with an older man and Sho's forced kisses will divide readers.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-8 (ch. 1-48): revenge and early jobs. Vols. 9-17 (ch. 49-102): Dark Moon and Ren's first love. Vols. 18-25 (ch. 103-150): Kuu, Natsu and Valentine. Vols. 26-36 (ch. 151-218): Cain and Setsuka, Guam. Vols. 36-46 (ch. 216-286): Kyoko's mother, a new role, and Ren's confession. Vols. 47-53 (ch. 287 onward): the Route Japan project and an acting contest with an American team (fan wiki; VIZ vol. 52 description).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Kyoko gave up school and her own plans for Sho. Why do people sometimes live for someone else, and what does it cost?
2. Is revenge a good reason to start something? When does Kyoko's reason change?
3. Ren is 20 and Kyoko is 17. How does the story handle the age difference, and how would you?
4. Kyoko's mother says she has no children. What does Kyoko decide to do with that hurt?
5. Sho kisses Kyoko without asking. How does the story treat it, and what do you think?
