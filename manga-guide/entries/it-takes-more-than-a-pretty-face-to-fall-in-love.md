**Premise.** Sana Chiken, a first-year at Minowa General High School, loves beautiful faces and is the biggest fan of Kanato Ugo, a blue-haired senior so handsome that a photo of him went viral; she picked the school partly because he was rumored to attend (Japanese Wikipedia; MangaDex). When she finally meets him, Kanato is about to be expelled for skipping too many classes. The principal will cancel the expulsion if the school's official social-media account reaches 100,000 followers, so Sana secretly runs the account and photographs Kanato for it (Comikey; Asmik Ace). Kanato turns out to be reserved, awkward with technology and troubled by the attention his looks bring (AniList user reviews). Hugs and near-kisses follow, Sana's crush on a face grows into love for a person, and her classmate Ryo Doigaki discovers their secret and becomes part of a love triangle (Asmik Ace; AniList spoiler tag). The ending is not described in sources opened.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can you love someone you first loved for their face? Sana is a self-declared fan of beautiful faces, and Kanato is the most beautiful face she knows. The series asks what happens when the image becomes a person who needs help.

**The answer the story gives.** The title answers it: it takes more than a pretty face. Sana's work on the account brings her close enough to see Kanato's awkwardness, reserve and kindness, and her feelings change from fandom to love (AniList user reviews).

**Characters as arguments.** Sana argues for joyful admiration of beauty and learns its limits without giving it up. Kanato argues, by his silence and awkwardness, that a face is not a self. Doigaki offers a friendship-based alternative. The principal stands for using a person's image for gain.

**Symbols and motifs.** The camera and the follower count: the image of Kanato grows while Sana gets to know the person behind it. The goal of 100,000 followers as a measure that misses what matters.

**Traditions it echoes.** The shojo fan-meets-idol romance; the older lesson that beauty is fleeting and the heart matters (1 Samuel 16:7; Proverbs 31:30 for some readers).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Consumerist and influencer culture is the setting; whether the story critiques it or simply uses it could not be confirmed. Expressive individualism appears as being known for who you are. Virtue, in the sense of character over appearance, carries the message.

**Shifts across the series.** Not confirmed in sources opened. Early volumes set up the account and the secret; a love triangle with Doigaki appears later (AniList spoiler tag); the series ends at vol. 17 with an after-story booklet.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Sana loves Kanato's face before she knows him. Is that love?
2. What does the school get from Kanato's photos, and what does Kanato get?
3. Have you ever found out someone was different from their online image?
4. What would you want people to know about you that a photo cannot show?
