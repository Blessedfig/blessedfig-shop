**Premise.** Eiji Aono, an ordinary second-year high school student, has dated his childhood friend Miyuki Amada since they were children. At the end of summer she stands him up on his birthday, and he finds her cheating with Kondo, the ace of the soccer team. When he confronts them, a senior (his club president) sets him up, Miyuki turns on him, and the school brands him a boyfriend who beat his girlfriend; he is isolated and harassed (web novel synopsis; chapters 1-4). Eating alone on the roof, he finds Ai Ichijo, the younger student called the most beautiful girl in the school, about to jump, and talks her out of it; she attaches herself to him (web-novel synopsis; chapter 5 "Encounter"). The novel's next chapters bring his homeroom teacher Takayanagi and his mother to his defense, an investigation of the false accusation, the exposure of Kondo, and Miyuki's collapse and regret (web-novel chapter titles 7-33; reader reviews). ## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What happens to a person when everyone believes a lie about him, and what saves him?

**The answer the story gives.** Truth comes out, but not by itself: a younger girl's attachment, a mother's fierce defense and a teacher's investigation lift Eiji up, while the people who lied fall by their own actions.

**Characters as arguments.** Eiji argues for patience and decency under slander. Ai argues that one kind act (his on the roof) can be returned. Miyuki argues, against herself, that fear of being abandoned can make someone betray the person she loves (a reader's reading). Kondo is the case against status and appetite.

**Symbols and motifs.** The school roof as the edge of despair and the place of meeting; the missed birthday date; rumors that spread faster than truth.

**Traditions it echoes.** The falsely accused vindicated (Joseph, Susanna); Japanese web novels of betrayal and comeuppance.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Virtue is rewarded and vice punished, plainly; due process beats mob judgment; status-based bullying is defeated.

**Shifts across the series.** The manga (about 13 chapters) covers the betrayal, the false accusation and the rooftop meeting; the web novel continues for over 300 episodes with the investigation, the wrongdoers' fall and Miyuki's regret (Kakuyomu).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why did so many classmates believe the rumor about Eiji?
2. What could one person have done to stop it?
3. Eiji saved Ai, and then she helped save him. Why does that matter?
4. Is it right to enjoy seeing someone who hurt you punished?
5. Can Miyuki make up for what she did? How?
