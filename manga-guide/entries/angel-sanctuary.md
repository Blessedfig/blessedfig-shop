**Premise.** Tokyo, 1999. Setsuna Mudo, 16, is a hot-tempered delinquent from a broken home whose mother hates him; he is in love with his 15-year-old sister Sara, and she with him (Wikipedia; fan wiki). He learns he is the reincarnation of Alexiel, an "Organic Angel" who led a rebellion against Heaven after the angels slaughtered a race of demons, was branded a fallen angel, and was condemned to be reborn again and again into miserable lives. Her mad twin brother Rosiel, freed by his devoted servant Katan, wants her back; demons (Kurai and Arachne) want her awakened. Setsuna and Sara run away together and consummate their love; Sara is then killed protecting him, and his grief unleashes Alexiel's power (vol. 3; VIZ vol. 3 and 4 blurbs). Time is frozen on Earth, and Setsuna goes through Hell and then Heaven to find Sara's soul, which has awakened in the body of the archangel Jibril and is put on trial for incest. Heaven turns out to be a cruel caste society run by a false prime minister; God (YHWH) is revealed as an indifferent being who made the universe as an experiment and plans to erase it. With Lucifer's help, Setsuna defeats God, Rosiel dies reconciled with Alexiel, Sara breaks free of the possessing angel Sandalphon, and the siblings reunite on Earth (vol. 20; Wikipedia; TV Tropes).

## Life message

**Love, even a forbidden love, is worth defying Heaven for: a tyrannical God and his cruel "pure" angels are overthrown so that people can choose for themselves.**

| Socio-cultural | A 1990s goth-shojo vision: androgynous angels, gender fluidity, a transgender demon (Arachne), caste oppression in Heaven, environmental damage (Organic versus Inorganic angels; the Evils as victims of pollution, per Manga News via Wikipedia). Outlook: Bittersweet — Setsuna defeats God and is reunited with Sara on Earth, but Sara, Kira, Katan, Rosiel and many others die along the way, and Kurai notes the two are still siblings (TV Tropes; Wikipedia). Yuki chose a happy ending over a planned tragic one (Wikipedia).
Hope source: L — love between two: Setsuna's love for Sara carries him through Hell and Heaven; Yuki names forbidden love as the theme (Wikipedia). Protecting others (P) and friendship (F) also matter.

**Where traditions disagree.** Nearly every tradition objects to the central romance, an incestuous relationship between two minors that the story rewards. Most Christian, Jewish, Muslim and Latter-day Saint readings add a second objection, that God is the final villain and Lucifer an ally, and grade F. Progressive, secular and pagan readers value the critique of oppressive religious power, the treatment of victims and the gender-diverse cast and grade C; Satanist readers welcome the rebellion against God (B) but not the sexual content involving minors.

## Creator and publisher context

- Kaori Yuki (pen name), born December 18 in Tokyo; debuted in 1987 with "Ellie in Summer Clothes" in Bessatsu Hana to Yume; best known for gothic shojo works (Wikipedia, Kaori Yuki).
- Intent (Wikipedia): as a middle-school student she was fascinated by fallen angels, especially Lucifer; inspired by the fantasy novel Nocturne; first allowed only ten chapters; named "forbidden love" as the theme; planned a tragic ending but chose a happy one so as not to upset fans after twenty volumes. In an interview she pointed to the tension between science and nature, reflected in the Organic and Inorganic Angels. The fan wiki and Wikipedia say her portrayal of angels differs from her own thoughts about real angels.
- Yuki took part in casting and directing the drama CDs and in the OVA's design (Wikipedia).
- Publisher: Hakusensha (Hana to Yume, a shojo magazine); VIZ Media (English; Teen Plus on its current pages).
- No controversies involving the creator were found.

**Profile notes for parents:** The series runs on liberty and loyalty and almost wholly rejects authority and sanctity: God is the final enemy and the hero's love for his younger sister is presented as true love. Sexual content involving minors exists (vol. 3 and the Heaven arc), and there is sexual violence among adult characters and graphic gore. VIZ rates it Teen Plus; a librarian review recommends older teens or adults.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** If God and Heaven forbid a love, does that make the love wrong, or Heaven wrong? Setsuna is told he is the reincarnation of a rebel angel and that his love for his sister is sin.

**The answer the story gives.** Heaven is wrong. The angels are cruel, the prime minister is a fraud, and God himself treats creation as a failed experiment (TV Tropes). The lovers suffer (Thompson's "guilt, madness, and the punishment of heaven," via Wikipedia), but Setsuna defeats God and returns home with Sara.

**Characters as arguments.** Setsuna argues that love outranks every law. Alexiel argues for compassion and for rebellion against a God who uses her. Rosiel shows love turned to madness and possession. Sevothtarte, a rape victim branded "impure," turns her trauma into a regime of purity, an argument about how victim-blaming breeds tyranny. Lucifer argues against an assigned destiny. Katan and Kira argue for loyalty unto death.

**Symbols and motifs.** Wings counted as power; the branding of the fallen; the frozen Earth; the Tower of Etenamenki (Babylon's Etemenanki); Orpheus going into Hell for his beloved; Organic versus Inorganic twins (nature and science); the red toy ring Setsuna gave Sara as children (fan wiki).

**Traditions it echoes.** Milton's sympathetic Lucifer and Gnostic ideas of a flawed creator; Greek myth (Orpheus, Pandora, Prometheus); Kabbalah; the gothic novel; 1990s visual-kei and goth style (Wikipedia).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism wins outright. Authoritarian religion is the chief villain, and the Christian frame is used to critique itself. Feminist and progressive concerns (victim-blaming, gender identity) are taken seriously, while the central relationship is one most readers of every tradition consider harmful.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-3: Tokyo, the siblings' romance, Sara's death; vols. 4-7: Hell (Hades, Gehenna) and the search for Sara's soul; vols. 8-15: Heaven, Sara's trial and the exposure of Sevothtarte; vols. 16-20: the war at the Tower and the defeat of God (VIZ blurbs; Wikipedia; volume placement approximate).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why do you think the author made God the villain? Do you agree with her picture of God?
2. Setsuna and Sara say their love is true. Why do nearly all cultures forbid it?
3. Sevothtarte was hurt and then hurt others. How does the story explain that?
4. Lucifer was told he was born to be evil. Can someone refuse their "role"?
5. Is a happy ending fair to all the people who died along the way?
