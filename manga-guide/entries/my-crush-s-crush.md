**Premise.** Second-year Sora Akizuki confesses to first-year Miya Fuwa, the younger sister of his childhood friend Mahiru, and is turned down: she likes someone else. That someone is a girl, Asahi Suzukaze, the cheerful third-year president of the Clinical Trials Club, a club that takes students' requests to test and try things out (Hakusensha vol. 1; Da Vinci Web). Sora joins to size up his rival, and Asahi, who claims to be a romance expert, offers to coach him toward Miya. What Sora does not know: six years ago a sick girl in hospital refused a new medicine, a boy drank it in her place, and she recovered; she has loved her "Prince of Clinical Trials" ever since. That girl is Asahi and the boy is Sora, who does not remember; only Miya knows (Wikipedia; Da Vinci Web). So Sora likes Miya, Miya likes Asahi, and Asahi likes Sora. Over seven volumes Asahi learns who her prince is (vols. 2 and 5), an accident during a trial of a discipline-enforcing robot causes a "major breach of public morals" between Sora and Asahi (vol. 3), a training camp brings swimsuits and a test of courage (vol. 5), Asahi suspects Sora's real first love was Mahiru (vol. 6), Miya confesses to Asahi and is gently refused, and Asahi tells Sora the truth about a hidden first kiss and then kisses him of her own will (vol. 7) (Hakusensha blurbs).

## Creator and publisher context

- Sekina Aoi (story): light novelist, author of *Student Council's Discretion* and *Gamers!*; began this series when Hakusensha asked for a romantic comedy; wrote the title first, then chapter 1 backwards from the final panel, which is why Miya speaks only at the end of chapter 1 (Wikipedia; Web The Television, 2025).
- Ryō Tsuzura (art): accepted the editor's offer despite never having drawn another writer's story or a male-lead romantic comedy; enjoys drawing comic facial expressions and hopes to reach vol. 10 (Web The Television).
- On-record intent: Aoi calls it a lighthearted, everyday comedy meant to soothe, despite a title that sounds like dark drama, and promises a happy ending (Web The Television). No statements on religion, politics or the same-sex storyline were found. **Status: Needs more info on the creators.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What do you do when the person you love loves someone else, and that someone loves someone else again? The title states the trap: the person my crush likes.

**The answer the story gives.** Be honest, be kind, and keep showing up. The club keeps the three together even as each hides a feeling, and the volumes so far move toward honesty: Miya confesses (vol. 7), Asahi tells the truth about the kiss (vol. 7). The author promises a happy ending.

**Characters as arguments.** Sora argues for responsibility and kindness, rooted in his brother's death. Asahi argues for faithful love and for testing things rather than guessing. Miya argues for silent loyalty, until she speaks. Mahiru stands for the plain truth that others can see what you cannot (vol. 6).

**Symbols and motifs.** The medicine a boy drank for a sick girl: love as taking someone's place. The Clinical Trials Club: feelings treated as experiments that keep failing in useful ways. The discipline robot that sets off a kiss (vol. 3).

**Traditions it echoes.** The love-triangle comedy and the childhood-promise romance; the hidden-identity plot of a forgotten first meeting. Faith readers may hear in the medicine story an echo of substitutionary love; secular readers, an honoring of medicine and research.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism is gentle: feelings should be spoken, but kindly. Community is the club. Progressivism appears only in how plainly the same-sex crush is accepted. Secular humanism lives in the hospital backstory.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-2 set up the chain and Asahi's search for her prince. Vols. 3-4 add the accidental kiss, a new heroine and Sora's first meeting with Miya. Vol. 5 brings the camp and the identity reveal. Vols. 6-7 test Sora's first love and bring the first confessions. The series is ongoing.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Asahi never stopped looking for the boy who helped her. What makes someone remember a kindness for years?
2. Miya kept a secret to protect Asahi. Was that kind or unfair to Sora?
3. How should you tell someone you can't return their feelings?
4. Sora joined the club to spy on his rival and found friends instead. Has that ever happened to you?
