**Premise.** An unnamed salaryman marries Setsuna after meeting her at a drinking party arranged by a mutual friend and dating only briefly; only after the wedding does he learn she is a professional assassin. He accepts it and asks few questions. Setsuna is cool and expressionless at work but warms up at home, where she is also an excellent cook and housekeeper; she skewers ingredients for dinner, draws a knife on door-to-door salespeople and sleeps with one eye open (Wikipedia; Seven Seas vol. 1). The episodic chapters follow the newlyweds through the seasons: water parks and summer festivals (v2); a trip to her assassin family's ancestral home with her younger sister Haruka, since everyone in her family except her husband is in the business (v2-3); a child neighbor who plays at being Setsuna's "Boss" and has to be babysat, and Setsuna's flu (v4); a promotion at work that Setsuna turns down to stay with her husband (v5); movies, exercise, the Boss's school Parent Day, and an admirer who follows Setsuna everywhere (v6); a visit from Setsuna's mother, an amusement park and a family friend's wedding (v7); Christmas and a New Year's gathering of the whole assassin family (v8) (Seven Seas volume pages). Seven Seas describes the killing as happening offscreen (v7). The couple's neighbor, Otonari-san, is a recurring character (Wikipedia).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can love accept anything? A newlywed discovers that his wife kills people for a living, and decides that he loves her anyway. The series asks, gently and as a joke, what acceptance in marriage means.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, if both keep work and home apart. Setsuna's coldness melts as the marriage grows (Wikipedia); she turns down a promotion to protect their home (v5); he nurses her through the flu (v4). The story treats her job the way an office comedy treats overtime: a nuisance that intrudes at dinner.

**Characters as arguments.** The husband argues for unconditional acceptance, at the price of not knowing. Setsuna argues that a person is more than her job. Her assassin family argues that a family trade is simply a family trade. The lonely child next door, whose parents are too busy for her school's Parent Day (v6), quietly shows what a home is for.

**Symbols and motifs.** The kitchen knife that is also a weapon. Sleeping with one eye open. The changing seasons: water parks, festivals, autumn walks, Christmas and New Year, marking a marriage that is growing.

**Traditions it echoes.** The newlywed comedy and the assassin comedy (compare the kuudere heroine), and the old story of a dangerous bride softened by love. For many faith traditions, it also raises the old question of whether love can make peace with a wrong it refuses to look at.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Traditional marriage and family are affirmed. Strength and violence are background facts, never examined.

**Shifts across the series.** The first volumes set up the couple and the joke; later ones widen to Setsuna's family (v3, v7, v8), a child neighbor (v4, v6) and her workplace (v5). The tone stays the same in every Seven Seas description.

**Discussion questions.**
1. The husband decides not to ask about Setsuna's job. Is that love, or avoiding the truth?
2. Why does the story never show the people Setsuna kills? How would the story change if it did?
3. What does Setsuna gain from her marriage? What does her husband gain?
4. Setsuna turns down a promotion. What do you think matters most to her?
5. What would you want to know about someone before you married them?
