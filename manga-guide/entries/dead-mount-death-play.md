**Premise.** In a fantasy world, the knight Shagrua Lugrid of the Geldwood Church defeats the Corpse God, a feared necromancer. The Corpse God escapes through reincarnation magic into modern Shinjuku, into the body of Polka Shinoyama, a 16-year-old heir of the wealthy Shinoyama Group whose throat has just been cut by Misaki Sakimiya, a 17-year-old assassin who kills killers for fun (ch. 1-2; Wikipedia; fan wiki). He kills Misaki in self-defense, revives her as a kind of zombie, and, seeking only the quiet life he never had, takes Polka's name; the real Polka's soul rides along, first in a drone and then in a shark plush toy, and lets him use his identity (ch. 2-6). The broker Lisa Kuraki ("Clarissa"), who runs a bar and an illegal freelance agency, gives him a home in a building gangsters use to dispose of bodies. He rescues children from a burning unlicensed daycare (ch. 2), and is pulled into Shinjuku's underworld: the serial killer Lemmings, the arsonist "Fire-breathing Bug," a special police unit, the Agakura assassin clan, rival brokers, the Shinoyama family's succession fight over who ordered Polka's death, an organization called Atelier, and, from the hospital arc (vols. 12-14), a radio contact and an artifact linking Earth to his old world (fan wiki; Yen Press; The Outer Haven). His backstory: sold by his parents to an empire's army because of his Evil Eye, used as a weapon, he later raised five orphans until Geldwood Church soldiers killed them to "save" their souls (fan wiki, Corpse God).

## Life message

**Reputations lie: a feared "monster" can be the one who rescues children and keeps faith with friends, while those who call themselves righteous can be cruel, so judge people by what they do.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Is a man defined by his reputation? The Corpse God was the most feared necromancer of his world; reborn in Shinjuku, he wants nothing but a quiet life, and finds that his new world has its own killers, churches of a kind, and families that sell their children.

**The answer the story gives.** Judge by deeds. The feared necromancer saves children from a fire on his first night (ch. 2), revives the girl who tried to kill him, and keeps faith with his friends; the holy church of his old world killed children to save their souls (fan wiki). The story does not make him innocent: he kills freely when threatened, and calculates losses.

**Characters as arguments.** The Corpse God argues for peace through power held carefully. Misaki argues, at first, that killing is fun and that her own life is worth nothing; her revival gives her a reason to live. The real Polka, a timid boy turned shark plush, argues for kindness and forgiveness. Clarissa argues for pragmatic brokerage. Shagrua and the Geldwood Church argue for righteous force, and the story questions them.

**Symbols and motifs.** The Evil Eye that sees souls; skeletal hands that destroy and rescue; the shark plush that carries a soul; the Torture Building turned into a home; a radio signal from another world (vol. 15).

**Traditions it echoes.** Narita's ensemble crime fantasies (Baccano!, Durarara!!), the reverse isekai (Anime Feminist), the murder-game thriller (Anime Feminist cites Future Diary), and the old theme of the misjudged monster.

**How each lens is portrayed.** The occult is the hero's toolkit, presented without judgment. Pragmatism wins most fights. Might is everywhere but tested by how it is used. Authoritarian empire and church are critiqued through backstory. Found family offers the warmth.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-4 (ch. 1-34): arrival, Misaki, Lemmings, the police and the first Shinoyama plot. Vols. 5-10 (ch. 35-87): the Agakura clan, the Fire-breathing Bug, rival brokers and other-world sorcerers on Earth (the anime's end, ch. 81). Vols. 11-14 (ch. 88-119): the hospital arc and the Wyvern scale. Vols. 15-18 (ch. 120-157): Atelier, the Shinoyama family, the radio link to the old world.

**Discussion questions.**
1. The Corpse God's first act in our world is to save children from a fire. Does that change how you see the rest of what he does?
2. Misaki enjoys killing. Does the story want us to laugh, worry, or both?
3. The Geldwood Church killed children "to save their souls." How can people do cruel things for a good-sounding reason?
4. The real Polka forgives the girl who killed him. Could you?
5. Is it fair to judge someone by their reputation before you meet them?
