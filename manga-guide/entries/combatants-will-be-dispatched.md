**Premise.** The evil Kisaragi Corporation has nearly conquered Earth and sends Combat Agent Six, a crude veteran henchman, and Alice, a combat android built to look like a little girl, to a fantasy planet to prepare an invasion. Six earns "Evil Points" for dishonorable and lecherous acts, which buy him weapons and gear from Earth. Drafted into the Kingdom of Grace's army, he leads a misfit squad: Snow, a greedy, glory-hungry knight (17 per the fan wiki); Rose, a chimera in the form of a young girl who gains the powers of what she eats (Wikipedia; the fan wiki says about 14-15); and Grimm, a wheelchair-using archbishop of the undead goddess Zenarith who is desperate to marry and comes back to life when she dies (Wikipedia; fan wiki). Six wins by dirty tactics against the Demon Army, whose generals include Heine of the Flame and the chimera boy Russell. Kisaragi gradually takes over: more agents arrive, the Demon Kingdom surrenders, and its new Demon Lord, Viper, is sentenced to execution by Grace. In the last arc Six breaks her out; she seems to blow herself up with a grenade rather than be executed, but the execution was staged, and she rejoins Kisaragi in disguise as "Snake Woman" (ch. 63-66, fan wiki). Six is told to stay on the new world.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What if the hero were the villain's henchman? The series asks whether honor, heroism and virtue mean anything, or whether results are all that count.

**The answer the story gives.** Results count, and loyalty to your own. Six wins by cheating, earns points by lechery and mocks every noble ideal, yet he never abandons a comrade, and the story rewards him. Its only consistent ideal is the crew.

**Characters as arguments.** Six argues for cynical pragmatism with a soft heart for friends. Alice argues for science and cold calculation. Snow argues for honor and is shown to want only money. Grimm's goddess and Rose's creator are jokes about faith and obedience. Heine and Viper, honorable enemies, are humiliated or saved by trickery. Agent Ten is the one figure the story treats as genuinely creepy.

**Symbols and motifs.** Evil Points, a running ledger of misdeeds; the teleporter; Alice's self-destruct device; Grimm's wheelchair and resurrections; the crude password.

**Traditions it echoes.** Tokusatsu villain organizations; isekai parody (Akatsuki's KonoSuba); the trickster tradition. For faith readers, the story inverts the usual hero tale: the virtues it mocks are those most traditions teach.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Pragmatism, absurdism and hedonism win. Honor codes and classical virtue are mocked. Religion is satire. Communitarian loyalty is the single value kept sincere.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1-5 (ch. 1-27, the anime's span): arrival, the squad, the tower, the desert march and the ancient robot. Vol. 6-8 (ch. 28-44): Kisaragi's base and more agents, the Undead Festival, Toris. Vol. 9-12 (ch. 45-66): the Demon Kingdom's fall, Viper, and the staged execution.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Six earns points for bad acts. What does it say about a story when bad acts are the way to win?
2. Many jokes target women and girls who object. Are the jokes on Six or on them?
3. What is the one thing Six never does to his friends?
4. Snow claims honor but wants money. Is honor real or just a mask?
5. Is it ever right to win by any means?
