**Premise.** Fifteen years after the great ninja war, the Hidden Leaf village is at peace and Naruto is its leader, the Seventh Hokage. Sarada Uchiha, about to take her ninja-academy exams, has almost no memory of her father, Sasuke, who travels the world on a secret mission. When her mother Sakura cannot answer her questions, and an old photo shows Sasuke with a woman, Karin, who wears glasses like Sarada's, she begins to fear Sakura is not her birth mother. She tags along after Naruto, with her comic best friend Cho-Cho, to find her father. Meanwhile a boy with the Uchiha clan's eye power attacks them: he is one of many clones of Shin Uchiha, a former test subject of the scientist Orochimaru, who has covered his body with stolen Sharingan eyes and wants to revive the Akatsuki and start a new war, believing peace makes humanity weak. Sasuke and Naruto fight Shin, Sakura is kidnapped, a mistaken DNA test convinces Sarada that Karin is her mother, and Naruto tells her that love, not blood, makes family. In the end Sakura is confirmed as her mother, the family fights side by side, the clone children are taken to an orphanage, and Sarada decides she wants to become Hokage (Wikipedia; Narutopedia).

## Life message

**Family is made by love and shared feelings more than by blood, and even an absent parent's love can be real; caring bonds and peace are stronger than the creed that only the strongest should survive.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What makes someone your family? Sarada has a mother who loves her, a father she cannot remember, and a photograph that seems to lie. When she starts to doubt, she reaches for the most scientific answer she can find, a DNA test, and it gives her the wrong one.

**The answer the story gives.** Love and shared life make family, not blood alone (Naruto, via Wikipedia). The story still gives Sarada the biological answer in the end (Sakura is her birth mother), so it affirms both: the family is real by blood and by love. Kishimoto's phrase for it is that feelings are connected.

**Characters as arguments.** Shin is the counterargument: he calls his clones sons but uses one as a shield and takes his liver, and he believes conflict and survival of the fittest drive progress (Narutopedia). Sasuke is love shown by sacrifice rather than words. Sakura is love shown by waiting and by strength. Naruto, who grew up without parents, speaks for chosen bonds. Cho-Cho's comic doubts about her own (obviously real) parents echo Sarada's in a lighter key.

**Symbols and motifs.** The family photograph, first suspect, then restored (ch. 10). Glasses, which make Sarada resemble Karin. The forehead poke, Sasuke's gesture of affection. The Sharingan eye, which awakens in Sarada from strong feeling, and which Shin has stolen and implanted across his body.

**Traditions it echoes.** Adoption stories and the welcome of orphans, which Christian, Jewish and Muslim readers will recognize; the duty of parents and children in Confucian thought; the critique of social Darwinism.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarianism and family loyalty are affirmed. Might-makes-right is defeated in its plainest form. Transhumanist body modification is portrayed as monstrous. Village service (a mild patriotism) is honored, though it is also why Sasuke is absent.

**Shifts across the series.** The early chapters are comic and domestic (Sakura wrecks the house; Cho-Cho's antics). The middle turns to action and the Shin plot. The last chapters resolve the family question and set up Boruto.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Sarada thought a DNA test would tell her who her mother was. What did it miss?
2. Sasuke is away for years doing important work. How should a family handle a parent's long absence?
3. Shin calls his clones his sons but treats them as spare parts. What makes someone a real parent?
4. Why does Naruto care so much about Sarada's question?
5. Some readers did not like the jokes about Cho-Cho's weight. Why might those jokes hurt?
