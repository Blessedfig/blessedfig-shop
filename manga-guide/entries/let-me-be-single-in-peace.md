**Premise.** Hodoka Nanamura hoped high school would bring friends and romance; by graduation he has neither. On the day before graduation he dies pushing his classmate Sora Hanamitsuji, a model-pretty, popular girl, out of the path of a truck (ch. 1). Instead of dying, both wake up three years earlier, just before the first-year entrance ceremony; a Japanese reader summary says it happened because Hanamitsuji prayed at a shrine (Renote). Nanamura plans to spend his second high school life as a quiet, comfortable loner, but Hanamitsuji, who remembers everything, vows to drag him out of it. He is drawn into a class trip (vol. 3), a quarrel with Hanamitsuji over their different values and an honest talk that repairs it (vol. 3-4), rumors that the two are dating (vol. 4), and the culture-festival committee (vol. 5). Ruri Hoshigasaki, a popular gyaru who secretly loves light novels as he does, grows closer to him; the class president Shiramine schemes to involve him and later becomes isolated; friends' suspicions and misunderstandings strain the group (vol. 5-6). Summer brings the beach, study sessions and a festival with all three girls (vol. 7) (BookWalker; Maruzen Junkudō; Manga UP! Global).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Is it wrong to want to be alone? Nanamura has already lived high school once, friendless, and decided that solitude is comfortable. Hanamitsuji, alive because of him, believes he deserves better. The story asks who gets to decide what a good life is.

**The answer the story gives.** Mostly connection, but on his terms. The blurbs show Nanamura pulled into outings and committees, then a sharp quarrel in which the two say what they really think (vol. 3), after which they mend things (vol. 4). The story seems to say that friendship cannot be forced; it grows when people are honest and accept each other's differences (the ending is not yet written).

**Characters as arguments.** Nanamura argues for the quiet life and is shown as kind, not broken. Hanamitsuji argues for gratitude and engagement. Hoshigasaki shows the cost of hiding what you love to keep your social place. Shiramine shows how even a leader can end up alone (vol. 7).

**Symbols and motifs.** The truck: a death that becomes a beginning. The shrine prayer. The entrance ceremony repeated. Light novels: the shared hobby that connects a loner and a popular girl.

**Traditions it echoes.** The time-loop "second chance" story (from general knowledge: like Groundhog Day or ReLIFE) and the Japanese loner romantic comedy (from general knowledge: like My Teen Romantic Comedy SNAFU). Faith readers may hear Genesis 2:18 and Ecclesiastes 4:9-12 on not being alone, and John 15:13 on giving one's life.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarianism is affirmed; expressive individualism is taken seriously rather than dismissed. No religious lens is argued; the shrine is a device.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1-2: the rewind and Hanamitsuji's campaign. Vol. 3-4: the class trip, the quarrel and the dating rumors. Vol. 5-6: the culture-festival committee, Hoshigasaki's secret and friends' suspicions. Vol. 7: summer with three girls and new trouble on the committee. Chapter numbers per volume were not found.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Nanamura says he wants to be left alone. Should Hanamitsuji respect that?
2. Why do you think Hoshigasaki hid her love of light novels?
3. How did an honest argument make Nanamura and Hanamitsuji closer?
4. If you could relive a school year, what would you do differently?
5. What is the difference between being alone and being lonely?
