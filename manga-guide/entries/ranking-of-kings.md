**Premise.** Prince Bojji is the eldest son of King Bosse, a giant once called the strongest man in the world. Bojji is tiny, deaf and unable to speak, so the court and the townspeople mock him as unfit to rule. He befriends Kage, a shadow-like creature who is the last survivor of a massacred clan of assassins and the only one who understands him. When Bosse dies, his widow Queen Hiling calls a vote that sets aside Bosse's will and crowns Bojji's younger half-brother Daida. Bojji leaves on a journey; his swordmaster Domas, ordered by Daida, pushes him into the pit that leads to the Underworld (anime ep. 5-6; likely vol. 2-3). There Bojji trains under Despa, a prince of the Underworld, and learns a quick, precise sword style. At home, a magic mirror that has advised Daida since childhood turns out to hold the soul of Miranjo, a sorceress tied to Bosse's past; she has Bosse's soul take over Daida's body and frees six criminals of the Underworld, including the immortal swordsman Ouken, to seize the kingdom. Bojji returns, defeats his father (who is inhabiting Daida) in a duel, and is accepted as king; the truth comes out that Bosse made a pact with a wish-granting demon, paying with his own lifespan and with his unborn son's strength (vol. 11-12). Bojji gives the throne to Daida and sets off with Kage to see the world (end of Part 1, ch. 155). In Part 2 (vol. 13 onward) the two travel to the low-ranked Kuhya Kingdom, foil a coup backed by the Gesuran Kingdom (the realm that wiped out Kage's clan), meet Zaki, a god who kills with a death spell (Bojji is killed and revived), and later return home to help free the Underworld from Zaki (fan wiki; Japanese Wikipedia).

## Life message

**Real strength is kindness, courage and friendship, not size or power; people who are mocked as weak can grow into true leaders, and even those who did great wrong can be understood and given a second chance.**

Despa trains him partly for pay; Kage earns money by thieving at first. The story rewards skill matched to one's real body over imitation of a strong father. |
| Religious | The world has "Gods", a race of powerful beings who enslaved human kingdoms and are mostly cruel or capricious (Bosse's flashbacks; Zaki, Chabi and Satun), plus a wish-granting demon whose bargains always cost too much. The Ranking itself is run by a committee for the Gods; the prize sword in the divine treasure vault is implied to drive its owners to mass killing (Japanese Wikipedia). Faith readers find a strong moral core (humility, mercy, forgiveness, protecting the weak) alongside occult elements: soul transfer into a mirror, possession of Daida, resurrection by wish. |

**Where traditions disagree.** Nearly every tradition welcomes the core message: a mocked, disabled child shown to be worthy, kindness as strength, and forgiveness for those who repent. The split is over the supernatural engine of the plot. Traditions that treat demonic pacts, possession and souls living on in objects as real spiritual dangers (Pentecostal, Seventh-day Adventist, Jehovah's Witness, some Orthodox Jewish and Muslim readers) grade lower or flag them as significant, while secular, Buddhist, Hindu, Shinto and Pagan frames read them as fantasy. A second, smaller split is over forgiveness: most religious frames welcome Bojji's mercy, while the Satanist frame and some critics (ANN) question how easily Miranjo, who caused Bojji's mother's death, is forgiven.

## Winners, losers and sympathy

Bojji's kindness wins allies, the throne (which he then gives away) and a world to explore; those who sought strength above all (Bosse, Miranjo, Domas at first) pay heavily, but most are understood, forgiven or redeemed rather than destroyed.

## Creator and publisher context

- Sōsuke Tōka (十日草輔), born 1975. He wanted to be a manga artist in his twenties, gave up after rejections, won a prize for a picture book whose publisher then went bankrupt, and worked for years in office jobs (web coding, design, marketing), where he says he learned what mattered in making comics; he quit in October 2016 and began Ranking of Kings on a web platform in January 2017 (Japanese Wikipedia, citing manba and BookLive interviews).
- He names Akira Toriyama as his greatest influence and Weekly Shonen Jump series (Kinnikuman, Dragon Ball) as favorites; reading One Piece as a returning reader inspired the title idea (Japanese Wikipedia). He has worked both at exploitative and good companies (same source); no statement linking that to the story was found.
- No statements on religion or politics were found. The volume 13 afterword explains that Daida's name comes from the giant yokai Daidarabotchi (fan wiki). **Status: Needs more info on the creator's stated intent about disability and forgiveness.**
- Publisher: web serialization on Manga Hack (Echoes/Creative Entertainment); print by Enterbrain/Kadokawa under Beam Comix. English digital edition by BookLive, which apologized in 2023 for plagiarized translations in vol. 1-7 (Wikipedia).
- No controversies involving the creator were found.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What makes a king, or anyone, strong? Bojji is born into a family of giants with a body that cannot lift a sword and ears that cannot hear. Everyone, including his father's court, measures him against Bosse, the strongest man in the world. The series asks whether strength is size and force or something else.

**The answer the story gives.** Strength is kindness held steady under pressure, joined to courage and the help of friends. Bojji does not become a giant; he becomes precise, quick and brave, with a sword that does not need to kill (vol. 4-6; Part 2). He is accepted as king because of who he is, and then gives the throne away to explore the world (ch. 155). The revelation that Bosse paid for his own power with his son's strength (vol. 11-12) turns the question into a judgment on power-worship.

**Characters as arguments.** Bosse argues that being strongest is worth any price and learns what it cost. Hiling argues that a ruler must be physically capable and comes to love Bojji openly. Domas argues that loyalty belongs to the strong, betrays a child and repents by cutting off his own hand. Daida argues from envy, is possessed by his own father and comes out humbler. Miranjo, tortured as a child and then fiercely devoted to Bosse, argues that love justifies anything; the story both condemns her acts and forgives her. Kage, who lost everything to a massacre, argues for loyalty to the one person who saw him. In Part 2 Oyabu, a bandit who fakes a disability, is the dark mirror of Bojji: he pretends weakness to use people.

**Symbols and motifs.** The ranking itself: worth measured by numbers. The mirror: Miranjo, Daida's self-image, and the souls trapped behind glass. Bosse's axe hung over Daida's throne. Kage's shadow body, invisible in darkness and weak when seen. Flowers on a mother's grave, the gift that wins Hokuro's lifelong devotion.

**Traditions it echoes.** The fairy tale of the overlooked youngest son, the shonen training arc (Toka cites Toriyama and Dragon Ball), and the Christian paradox of power made perfect in weakness. Bojji's name joins the Japanese words for "lonely" and "prince" (Wikipedia). The prize sword that demands blood echoes cautionary tales of cursed treasure.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Classical virtue ethics is celebrated in Bojji. Strength rules is critiqued through Bosse and the Gods. Pacifism appears in Bojji's choice not to kill. The occult is shown as powerful and always costly. Loyalty ethics are honored and questioned. Disability is treated with dignity, and the anime used a deaf organization to supervise its sign language.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1-3: Bojji's mockery, Kage's friendship, the succession and the betrayal. Vol. 4-7: training in the Underworld and the plot at home. Vol. 8-12: Ouken's assault, Bosse's return, the backstories of Bosse and Miranjo, the duel of father and son, and the resolution (ch. 155). Vol. 13 onward: Bojji and Kage's journey to Kuhya, the Gesuran threat, Zaki and Bojji's death and revival, the poison forest, and the return home to rescue the Underworld; darker gods and more ambiguous allies.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why did so many people think Bojji could not be king? What changed their minds?
2. Bosse traded his son's strength for his own power. Was anything worth that price?
3. Domas tried to kill Bojji and later regretted it. Should Bojji have forgiven him? What made forgiveness possible?
4. Bojji learns to win without wounding people. Is that braver or weaker than fighting to kill?
5. Kage wants revenge on the kingdom that killed his family. What does Bojji's friendship do to that wish?
