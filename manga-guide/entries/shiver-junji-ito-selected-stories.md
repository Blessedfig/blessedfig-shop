**Premise.** Ten horror stories, each followed by Ito's short notes and early sketches (The Comics Journal; Women Write About Comics). (1) "Used Record" (about 1990): a record of a dead singer's voice so compelling that a girl kills her friend to get it, then dies trapped in the alley where she hid the body (TV Tropes; TCJ). (2) "Shiver" (about 1991): chills turn into holes opening across people's skin, spread by a cursed statuette; one young man dies (TV Tropes; TCJ). (3) "Fashion Model" (about 1992): a student film crew hires Fuchi, a gaunt, fanged model who destroys people she thinks disrespect her (TCJ; Cemetery Dance). (4) "Hanging Balloons" (about 1994): after a teen idol is found dead in an apparent suicide, giant balloons shaped like people's heads hunt down the matching person and hang them by the cords beneath; copycat deaths spread, and the narrator's father is dragged away (WWAC; TV Tropes; ANN). (5) "Marionette Mansion" (about 1994): a puppeteer's family comes under the control of unseen manipulators in the ceiling; a brother saves his sister, but his girlfriend is killed and the family become marionettes (TV Tropes; ANN). (6) "Painter" (about 1995): from the Tomie series; the immortal Tomie torments a successful painter and a sculptor obsessed with capturing her beauty; the painter kills her, meets a bloody end himself, and she grows back (TCJ; Anime Feminist). (7) "The Long Dream" (about 1997): a hospital patient's dreams last years each night until his body changes and he dies; crystals from his brain are used to treat a woman terrified of death, who then begins to dream long (TV Tropes). (8) "Honored Ancestors" (about 1997): a girl with repeated amnesia learns that her boyfriend's family carries the living brains of its ancestors grafted to the heir's scalp, and that he wants to marry her to continue the line (TV Tropes; TCJ). (9) "Greased" (about 2003): a girl lives above her family's grease-soaked barbecue restaurant, abused by her oil-drinking brother, until their father kills him; the story includes dismemberment and cannibalism (TCJ; ANN anime review). (10) "Fashion Model: Cursed Frame" (2015, new, about eight pages): Fuchi returns in a rough, draft-like story (TCJ; WWAC).

## Life message

**No model of a good life is offered: in a cold, uncaring universe, obsession, vanity, envy and fear consume ordinary people, and the stories warn against fixation without offering a way out.**

| Meta lens | How it reads the series |
|---|---|
| Evolutionary biology | Disgust and contagion drive the horror: holes in skin ("Shiver"), grease and pus ("Greased"), parasitic ancestors ("Honored Ancestors"). Ito's themes include predator and prey and the loss of humanity (Wikipedia). Families and couples are traps as often as shelters. |
| Pragmatic | Characters rarely solve anything; the one clear rescue is a brother saving his sister by destroying a puppet ("Marionette Mansion"). The Long Dream's doctor treats a patient with crystals from a dead man's brain, and it backfires. |
| Religious | No real-world faith appears. Curses, cursed objects, a demonic woman (Fuchi), an immortal one (Tomie) and the living dead ancestors carry the horror. ANN's Lynzee Loveridge reads "Honored Ancestors" as playing on Japan's veneration of ancestors. Faith readers will note that the stories punish obsession but give no hope beyond it. |
| Socio-cultural | Beauty standards and the fashion industry (Foreword Reviews on "Fashion Model"); idol suicide and copycat contagion ("Hanging Balloons"); family pressure to produce an heir and arranged marriage (The Smart Set; TCJ on "Honored Ancestors"); poverty and family resentment ("Greased," The Smart Set). |

Outlook: Bleak — almost every story ends in death, transformation or a curse passed on; the few survivors are marked, and Ito describes a universe in which punishment far outweighs the fault (Wikipedia).
Hope source: N — the stories find little hope; the only lights are a brother who saves his sister ("Marionette Mansion") and a girl who seems freed from the curse in "Shiver" (TV Tropes).

## Creator and publisher context

- Junji Ito, born July 31, 1963, in Sakashita (now Nakatsugawa), Gifu; trained and worked as a dental technician before turning full-time to manga; began reading horror through Kazuo Umezu; won an honorable mention in the Kazuo Umezu Prize in 1987 for the story that became Tomie (Wikipedia).
- Influences he has named: Kazuo Umezu, Shigeru Mizuki, Hideshi Hino, Edogawa Ranpo and H. P. Lovecraft (Wikipedia). His themes are body horror and cosmic horror, predator and prey, loss of humanity and obsession, in a universe that is cruel and capricious; he draws on his own fears of death, war, insects and being watched (Wikipedia).
- In this book, Ito's notes after each story tie them to his memories of drawing them and include preparatory sketches (TCJ; WWAC; Cemetery Dance). He calls "Greased" unpleasant himself (Cemetery Dance). No statements on religion or politics found. **Status: Enough for this entry; the notes themselves should be read in a full read.**
- Awards: Eisner Awards in 2019 (Frankenstein), 2021 (Remina; Venus in the Blind Spot; Best Writer/Artist) and 2022 (Lovesickness); Inkpot Award 2023 (Wikipedia).
- Publisher: VIZ Media in English (rated T+ per Library Journal's listing). No controversies involving the creator were found.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What happens when a person fixes on one thing (a voice on a record, a beautiful woman, a perfect painting, the family line) and cannot let go? Shiver puts that question to ten sets of ordinary people and answers it with bodies that open, hang, melt or grow wrong.

**The answer the story gives.** Fixation destroys, and the universe does not care. Ito's stories punish their characters far beyond any fault (Wikipedia). Nothing is redeemed; at most someone escapes, as the brother does in "Marionette Mansion." The book does not offer a cure for obsession; it shows obsession's cost.

**Characters as arguments.** The collector argues for possession and becomes a murderer. The painter argues for perfect art and kills his model. Fuchi stands for beauty as a weapon; Tomie for beauty that turns men violent (Anime Feminist). Shuichi stands for a family that values its line over the person. The father in "Greased" stands for poverty turned to rage.

**Symbols and motifs.** Holes in skin. A noose under a balloon with your own face. Grease that covers everything. Wires from the ceiling. A dream that lasts a lifetime. Brains stacked on a living head.

**Traditions it echoes.** Japanese horror manga (Umezu, Hino, Mizuki), Lovecraft's cosmic horror, Edogawa Ranpo's grotesque tales (Wikipedia), and the Western gothic. For Buddhist readers it pictures clinging and its suffering; for Christian readers, the misery of sin without a rescuer.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Nihilism is the atmosphere rather than an argument. Tradition is critiqued in one story, modern beauty culture in another. Occult elements are pure horror. No faith or philosophy is offered as an answer.

**Shifts across the series.** The stories run roughly in order of composition, about 1990 to 2003, plus a 2015 sketch-like coda. Critics agree the later stories are stronger and stranger (Price, ANN; TCJ); Ito's notes after each give a reader a break from the dread.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Every story is about someone who cannot let go of something. What is it in each story?
2. "Hanging Balloons" shows a fear spreading from person to person. How do real fears and behaviors spread?
3. In "Honored Ancestors," what does the family want from the heroine? What does the story say about that?
4. Why do you think Ito gives his characters punishments much bigger than anything they did?
5. Ito's notes say how he made each story. Does knowing that make the stories less scary?
