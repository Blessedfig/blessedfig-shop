**Premise.** In Sakai, Osaka, Risa Koizumi (172 cm) and Atsushi Ōtani (156 cm) are the class's comedy duo, nicknamed after a famous tall-and-short comedy pair. They team up to win their crushes, Suzuki and Chiharu, who fall for each other instead. Risa and Ōtani become close friends, and Risa realizes she loves him; her confessions fail until he takes her seriously (vol. 4), and after a jealous stretch with student teacher Maitake, Ōtani confesses and they become a couple (vol. 7). Rivals test them: Ōtani's neighbor Mimi (vol. 8), Risa's young coworker Kohori, which leads to a breakup and reconciliation (vol. 10), and Risa's grandfather, who hires a woman to seduce Ōtani (vol. 14). Friends Nobu and Nakao and Chiharu and Suzuki pair off, Ōtani passes his entrance exam, and the last volume follows Risa's hard start at fashion school, with side stories from middle school (vol. 16-17) (Wikipedia; ANN; Manga Bookshelf; TV Tropes).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Does love have to look the way other people expect? Risa and Ōtani are a joke to everyone, a comedy duo whose height difference is the punchline. The series asks whether the person you laugh with every day could be the one you love, and whether you are brave enough to say so.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, and courage matters more than fit. Risa's confessions fail again and again (vol. 4) before Ōtani hears them; then Ōtani must find his own courage (vol. 7). After that the story is about keeping love: through jealousy (Mimi, Kohori), a breakup (vol. 10), a grandfather's scheme (vol. 14) and the strain of growing up and apart (vol. 17). The answer is never stated as a lesson; it is shown in two people who keep choosing each other.

**Characters as arguments.** Risa argues for honest, impulsive feeling, with its mistakes; Anime Feminist defends her as a realistic, flawed teenager. Ōtani argues for pride that must learn humility. Nobu and Nakao show steady love that survives distance. Seiko argues for being who you are; the friends' acceptance is the series' answer, though some jokes undercut it. Maitake, the teasing adult, shows that a kind push can help; Risa's grandfather shows that control, even meant well, harms. Kohori and Mimi argue for wanting someone who has already chosen another, and learn to let go.

**Symbols and motifs.** Height: the obstacle that becomes beside the point. The comedy duo: love as a partnership of timing and trust. Umibōzu, the rapper both adore, who turns out to be a devoted family man (Wikipedia): a hint of what the leads may become. Basketball, where the short boy is the star.

**Traditions it echoes.** The shōjo romantic comedy and the Osaka manzai pair; the "friends to lovers" plot; for some faith readers, the biblical theme that God looks at the heart, not the appearance (1 Samuel 16:7).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism is affirmed: choose the person and the path that fit you. Communitarianism is affirmed through the friend group. Traditional authority, in the person of the grandfather, is shown as controlling and loses. Progressive readers welcome Seiko and fault the jokes; traditional readers note Seiko as a concern but welcome the chaste, loyal romance.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-4: friendship and the confession struggle. Volumes 5-7: the Maitake arc and the start of the relationship. Volumes 8-12: rivals and the breakup (Mimi, Kohori). Volumes 13-16: the grandfather, Hitomi and the darker vol. 14, exams and graduation. Volume 17: side stories from middle school and Risa's first months at fashion school.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why did everyone, including Risa, think she and Ōtani could never be a couple?
2. Risa confesses several times before Ōtani believes her. What made it hard for him to hear it?
3. How do the friends treat Seiko after they learn she is transgender? How do the jokes about her land with you?
4. Risa's grandfather thought he was protecting her. What was wrong with what he did?
5. Risa and Ōtani tease and hit each other constantly. When is teasing affection, and when does it hurt?
