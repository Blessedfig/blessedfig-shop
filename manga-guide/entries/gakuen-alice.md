**Premise.** Ten-year-old Mikan Sakura, raised in the countryside by her grandfather, runs away to Tokyo to follow her best friend Hotaru to Alice Academy, a boarding school for children with special powers called Alices; the school pays families for their children (Wikipedia, vol. 1 summary). Teacher Narumi enrolls her, and she turns out to have the rare Nullification Alice. At first bullied by the fire-wielding Natsume and his classmates, she wins friends, and the school's dark side emerges: a Dangerous Ability class of children sent on government missions, a masked teacher, Persona, whose touch kills, and the Elementary School Principal (Kuonji, the "ESP"), who keeps a child's body by magic and uses students as weapons (Wikipedia chapter list and character list). Mikan learns she also has the Stealing Alice, inherited from her mother Yuka, and a time-travel flashback (vols. 18-21) reveals her parents' story. In the finale the students rebel against the ESP, Mikan's mother is killed, Natsume dies and is saved by a time-travel sacrifice that erases Hotaru and her brother from the present, and Mikan's memories are taken; four years later she is restored and sets out with Natsume and Ruka to find Hotaru (vols. 22-30). Omake pages show Mikan and Natsume married as adults (Wikipedia).

## Life message

**Friendship, kindness and courage can stand up to adults and institutions that use children, and love is worth giving everything for.**

| Meta lens | How it reads the series |
|---|---|
| Evolutionary biology | Children separated from their parents form a tight group that protects its weakest; rival adults compete for children with rare powers. Parents who abandon or sell children (Yuka's parents, vol. 18) are the deepest wound. |
| Pragmatic | A star-rank system controls food, rooms and allowances; the children learn which adults to trust and use their powers cleverly to escape (vols. 6-8, 17, 22-28). |
| Religious | No religion appears; spirits and souls do (a soul-sucking Alice, an evil-spirit Alice, Nobara's spirit appearing to Mikan, vol. 26). Faith readers will find self-sacrifice, forgiveness of a repentant killer (Persona, vol. 27) and resistance to abusive authority, alongside sexual content involving minors in the flashback and comic gags. |
| Socio-cultural | A school as a prison where abuse is ritualized (Gaffney); government and corporate interest in gifted children; ranking and bullying; adults who fail children and a few who protect them. |

Outlook: Bittersweet — Mikan loses her mother, her father before birth and, for a time, Hotaru, and many characters die, but the principal falls, Natsume lives, Mikan regains her memories and sets out to find Hotaru, and the omake show happy marriages (Wikipedia).
Hope source: F — friendship: Wikipedia's summary says Mikan discovers how important the strength of friendship is; sacrificial love (P, protecting others) and love between two (L) are close behind.

## Creator and publisher context

- Tachibana Higuchi, born 1976 in Kyoto; studied for art-university entrance exams before debuting in 1996 (Wikipedia, citing a Tokyopop interview).
- In the Tokyopop interview she says her storytelling improved as she thought about how to hold readers' attention and include what they were looking for (Wikipedia). No statement on the series' themes or on the flashback romance was found. **Status: Needs more info on the creator.**
- Publisher: Hakusensha (Hana to Yume, a shojo magazine for girls). Tokyopop published 16 volumes in English, rated 13+; the license lapsed with Tokyopop's 2011 shutdown (Wikipedia).
- Spin-off: Kageki no Kuni no Alice, set a few months after the finale (Wikipedia).
- No controversies involving the creator were found.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What happens to children when the adults in charge see them as tools? Alice Academy looks like a privilege and works like a prison: children are bought from their parents, ranked, punished and, in the Dangerous Ability class, sent out as weapons.

**The answer the story gives.** Children save each other. Mikan's power cancels other powers; her character cancels cruelty. Friendship, loyalty and sacrifice carry her and her friends through the academy's worst, and the tyrant falls to a rebellion of students and teachers (vols. 26-28).

**Characters as arguments.** Mikan argues for kindness that does not quit. Natsume argues for protecting others at any cost to himself. Hotaru argues for love shown in deeds, ending in the biggest sacrifice of all. The principal argues for control and loses everything. Persona, a boy made into a killer, argues that even the worst can turn back (vol. 27). The flashback adults complicate the picture: Izumi is the kind teacher whom everyone loves, and the story presents his romance with his former student, still a minor, as the love that gave Mikan life.

**Symbols and motifs.** Alice stones, exchanged as a sign of friendship or love (vols. 7, 13, 25); the Nullification Alice; masks (Persona); the star ranks; time travel that lets Mikan see her parents and lets Hotaru rewrite a death.

**Traditions it echoes.** The magical-school story and the magical-girl genre (Cardcaptor Sakura is a reviewer's comparison, via Wikipedia); Kodomo no Omocha in art and humor (ANN). Its child-soldier and institutional-abuse themes run closer to dystopian fiction than to typical shojo.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarianism wins; authoritarian control is the villain and is destroyed. Religious readers find a strong theme of sacrificial love and repentance, but also romances and gags that their traditions' teaching on children and chastity would reject.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-10 (ch. 1-58) are mostly comedy with darker turns (the festival, the Z hideout). Volumes 11-17 (ch. 59-100) bring Persona, the Dangerous Ability class and Luna. Volumes 18-21 (ch. 100-124) are the flashback to Mikan's parents, with the heaviest sexual content. Volumes 22-30 (ch. 125-180) are the escape, the rebellion, the deaths and the ending. Only volumes 1-16 are in English.

**Discussion questions.**
1. What made Alice Academy a bad place for children, even though it looked like a privilege?
2. Why does Mikan forgive Persona after everything he did?
3. Natsume takes Mikan's underwear in the first chapter as a joke. Is it funny? What would you do if someone did that?
4. What should a teacher never do? Which teachers in the story keep that rule, and which break it?
5. Hotaru gives up everything for her friend. Is there anything you would give up for a friend?
