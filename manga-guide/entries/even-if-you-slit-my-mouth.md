**Premise.** Miroku is a kuchisake-onna, the slit-mouthed woman of a Japanese urban legend who asks strangers whether she is beautiful and cuts or kills those who answer wrongly (CBR). In the story's modern Japan, kaii (beings born of folklore and urban legend) draw their strength from being known and feared, and as people stop believing they weaken and some vanish (Mangapedia; CBR). To keep existing, Miroku must marry Koichi Sano, a 17-year-old high school student who is the current head of a family that has looked after legends and superstitions for generations (CBR; Mangapedia; MangaUpdates). Koichi is surprisingly eager, for reasons tied to a past meeting with Miroku and to secrets of his family that come out later (Mangapedia; MangaUpdates). Proud Miroku would rather survive on her own strength, so the two make a contract: for a one-year trial they live as though married, and if she can frighten him before his eighteenth birthday the engagement is off; if she falls for him instead, the wedding goes ahead (Wikipedia; MangaUpdates; Game Rant). Her scare attempts keep failing while his straightforward advances leave her flustered (Mangapedia). Supporting characters include Miroku's protective younger brother Mikoto and Uda (AniList; MangaUpdates tags Overprotective Sibling/s). How the story develops after the set-up and how it ends could not be confirmed from sources opened; AniList readers flag spoilers for Tragedy and a Time Skip.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What keeps a person alive: being feared, or being known? Miroku was once so famous that almost everyone knew her name (Mangapedia). Now people have stopped believing, and she is fading. The series asks whether there is a better way to exist than to frighten people.

**The answer the story gives.** As far as sources reach, its answer is love that is chosen, not forced. The arranged marriage is the old answer, survival through a family's duty. The contract Miroku insists on is a second answer, survival through her own power. The romance that grows between them is the third answer: to be seen and cherished by one person. Game Rant ranks it as a romance first, and the reader tags stress the couple.

**Characters as arguments.** Miroku argues for pride and self-reliance: she would rather scare her way free than be kept alive by a husband. Koichi argues for devotion and duty: he takes on a family office at 17 and seems to want the marriage for reasons rooted in his past (Mangapedia). Mikoto, the protective brother, stands for family loyalty and suspicion of the human side (AniList; MangaUpdates tags).

**Symbols and motifs.** The slit mouth and the legend's question about beauty, turned from a threat into a question about being loved. The contract and its deadline, Koichi's eighteenth birthday. Fading as a picture of being forgotten.

**Traditions it echoes.** Japanese yokai lore and the 'monster bride' tale (from general knowledge; to verify); Beauty and the Beast with roles reversed; the arranged-marriage comedy. Its idea that spirits live by human belief echoes Shinto ideas of reverence and memory and, for secular readers, the plain fact that legends live in minds.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Pagan/occult elements are folklore furniture handled with affection. Traditionalism is honored but softened by choice. Duty ethics shows in the contract that both keep. Expressive individualism appears in Miroku's wish to stand on her own, which the romance tests rather than mocks.

**Shifts across the series.** The opening chapters set up the contract as comedy. Later chapters reveal Koichi's past meeting with Miroku and the Sano family's secrets (Mangapedia). AniList's spoiler tags suggest tragic turns and a time skip near the end; these could not be checked.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Miroku would rather survive by her own strength than by marrying. Why does that matter to her?
2. Koichi agrees to marry someone he has to be scared by. What does he seem to want?
3. Is it fair to arrange a marriage for a 17-year-old? What changes when the two start choosing each other?
4. Legends fade when people stop believing in them. What old stories in your family are worth remembering?
5. Would you rather be feared or remembered? Why?
