**Premise.** Akihito Aoyagi, a quiet high-school student, keeps his distance when Charlotte Bennett, an elegant British exchange student, transfers into his class and becomes the center of attention; he feels she lives in a different world. Then he helps a lost little girl, Emma, who turns out to be Charlotte's younger sister, and learns the sisters live in the apartment next door. Emma grows attached to him, and the sisters start visiting his room almost daily for meals, games and outings; Akihito and Charlotte, both awkward, slowly grow closer (Wikipedia). The web novel's own blurb promises a sweet, slow-burn romance in which the two end up as a couple, with a happy ending (Naro).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Do you have to be special to belong in someone else's life? Akihito decides Charlotte lives in a different world and keeps away. A lost child changes that.

**The answer the story gives.** No: ordinary kindness is enough. By helping Emma, Akihito becomes part of the sisters' daily life, and the relationship grows from meals and games rather than grand gestures (Wikipedia). The author promises a sweet, happy ending (Naro).

**Characters as arguments.** Akihito argues that quiet goodness is worth more than popularity. Charlotte argues for grace and gentleness in a strange place. Emma, a small child, is the bridge: her trust shows who is safe.

**Symbols and motifs.** The lost child found; the shared wall between apartments; the dinner table as a family.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Good Samaritan and the welcome of the stranger; the "girl next door" romantic comedy; the Japanese light-novel subgenre of a foreign heroine who learns the hero's kindness (from general knowledge; to verify).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Community and simple virtues are affirmed; there is no argued philosophy or religion.

**Shifts across the series.** The manga's first volumes cover the meeting and the daily visits (Wikipedia; MangaDex chapter list, untitled). Later developments (the couple forming, the secret relationship) come from the web novel's tags and are not placed by chapter.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Akihito first kept away from Charlotte because she seemed too different. Have you ever done that?
2. Why does helping Emma change everything?
3. What makes the sisters feel at home in a new country?
4. Some families have rules about teens spending time alone together. What are your family's rules, and why?
5. If a relationship is kept secret, what might be the reasons? Which are good, and which are warning signs?
