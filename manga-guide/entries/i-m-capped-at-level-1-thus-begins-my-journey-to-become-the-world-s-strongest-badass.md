**Premise.** Tetsuya Takahashi, an overworked company employee (ComicWalker), is pulled into another world by a hero-summoning ritual while trying to save a girl from a gang of thugs (J-Novel Club). There, everyone has a fixed level cap; his is 1, the lowest possible. The king brands him defective and has him thrown into the Valley of Death, a ravine full of monsters, to die (AniList; Kadokawa). Near death, he discovers his unique skill, Corpse Absorb, which lets him take the stats and abilities of any dead body. He vows to survive, grow strong and pay back those who wronged him (AniList; Kadokawa). An elf, Mekh, saves him in the valley; he travels with an energetic cat-sith and a stuffed bear (J-Novel Club, v1). In v2 he joins a guild to earn money, learns more about his skill and gambles everything to save his friends. In v3 the humans invade the elves' domain, and Tetsuya defends the elf capital against a hero of level 91 (Kadokawa vol. 3). In v4 he fights alongside Mekh and her sister Sakh, is forced into a duel with a hero, and is offered great power by the Abyss, which reminds him of his old goal: revenge on the bullies from Earth who were summoned with him. In v5 he goes to Varfol, where a saint protects farmers exiled by the humans for their low levels, and stands with them against another hero; the saint seems familiar (J-Novel Club blurbs). The manga ends there, cut short.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Who decides what a person is worth? In Tetsuya's new world a single number, the level cap, decides, and his is 1. The king throws him away like broken equipment.

**The answer the story gives.** Not the number. Tetsuya's skill, absorbing the strengths of the dead, lets a level-1 man beat a level-91 hero (v3). The story's answer is still a power fantasy: the weak one turns out to be strongest. But what he does with strength matters: he repays the elf who saved him, defends her people and stands with farmers exiled for their low levels (v5).

**Characters as arguments.** Tetsuya argues that a label is not destiny, and wavers between protector and avenger: in v4 the Abyss offers great power and he remembers his vow against the bullies from Earth. The king and the summoned heroes argue that rank is worth. Mekh argues for mercy to a stranger. The saint of Varfol, who shelters exiles, is the story's picture of strength used rightly.

**Symbols and motifs.** The level cap: a fixed number as fate. The Valley of Death: being thrown away. Corpse Absorb: life drawn from death. The Abyss: power offered at a price. The stuffed bear and the cat-sith: unlikely allies for an unlikely hero.

**Traditions it echoes.** The isekai revenge-and-rise story (MyAnimeList reviewers compare it to many late-2010s titles), the underdog tale, and, for some faith readers, the rejected stone that becomes the cornerstone, or the temptation of power in the wilderness.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Strength rules is explored and partly turned on itself: the ranking system is unjust, but strength still settles every conflict. Virtue (courage, gratitude, loyalty) is affirmed. Revenge stays a live temptation, unresolved because the series was cancelled.

**Shifts across the series.** Volume 1 (ch. 1-6): summoning, condemnation, survival in the valley. Volume 2 (ch. 7-13): the guild, a ritual and the Abyss. Volume 3 (ch. 14-19): the human invasion of the elves. Volume 4 (ch. 20-26): the duel with a hero and the Abyss's offer. Volume 5 (ch. 27-33): Varfol, the saint and the exiled farmers; the manga stops there (chapter counts from J-Novel Club's volume lists).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why does the king think Tetsuya is worthless? Is he right?
2. What changes when Tetsuya fights to protect others instead of for revenge?
3. Is it right to take power from the dead?
4. Are there "level caps" in our world, labels that decide what people can become?
5. If you were thrown away like Tetsuya, would you want revenge? What would help you choose otherwise?
