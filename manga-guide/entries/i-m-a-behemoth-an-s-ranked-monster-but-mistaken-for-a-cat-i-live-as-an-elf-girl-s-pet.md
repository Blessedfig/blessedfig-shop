**Premise.** A knight killed by demons is reborn in a dungeon as a baby behemoth, one of the strongest monsters in the world, which happens to look exactly like a kitten. He eats monsters to take their skills. Aria, a busty elf adventurer who works alone because male adventurers keep pestering her, finds him injured, nurses him back to health and keeps him as her pet, Tama. He vows to protect her and secretly does so with his monstrous powers; everyone assumes he is a rare "elemental cat" (anime ep. 1). Aria dreams of becoming a warrior like Alisha the Sword Saint, who saved her village from demons when she was a child. Over the series she gathers a party: Vulcan, a tiger-eared blacksmith who looks like a girl; Stella, the earth dragon Tama killed, reborn as a dragonnewt who wants to mate with him; and the fairy Lily and the dryad Feri. They rise through the guild ranks, fight demons who want to free the demon king Mammon and revive a God of Darkness, and win titles as "Sacred Blade" and "Sacred Beast" (anime ep. 12). Throughout, Aria's attraction to her cat, including a secret wish to be taken by a beast (Japanese Wikipedia), and nearly constant bath scenes are played as comedy (Wikipedia; Japanese Wikipedia; ANN; GeekTyrant).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What does loyalty look like when you cannot say who you are? Tama was a proud knight; now he is a "kitten" who lives in the arms of the elf who saved him. He cannot tell her he is a behemoth, or a man, so he protects her in secret, again and again.

**The answer the story gives.** Loyalty is shown, not spoken: Tama fights an earth dragon he cannot beat and evolves to save Aria's life (ep. 3-4), and later reveals his monster form to protect the party (ep. 10). The reward is a family: by the end of the first anime season Aria calls the party her new family and they defend her home village together. The comedy, meanwhile, insists that everyone wants Tama, and treats that desire as harmless fun.

**Characters as arguments.** Tama argues for faithful service without recognition. Aria argues for growing strong instead of always being saved (ep. 4). Kasman and the Earl are arguments against men who think women owe them their bodies; both lose. Reiss argues for revenge and is answered by the woman he loved (ep. 7). Stella argues that strength is the only reason to love someone, and slowly joins the team instead.

**Symbols and motifs.** The kitten that is really a monster (and a man); evolution by eating; holy blades and Excalibur; the bath as the place of rest, talk and rivalry; the house the party buys together (ep. 8).

**Traditions it echoes.** The knight's vow and the loyal animal companion of Western romance; the "reincarnated as a monster" web-novel genre; the harem comedy. Critics at ANN place it in a line of fan-service fantasy aimed at male readers.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Hedonism is affirmed through the comedy. Honor and loyalty are affirmed through Tama. Strength is the world's currency but is shown best when used to protect. Spirits and holy beasts are good fantasy furniture.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-2 set up Aria, Tama, Vulcan and the first demon (anime ep. 1-4); volumes 3-6 add Stella, the Gladstone arc and the fairies (ep. 5-8); volumes 7-10 bring the empire, Alisha's training and the war for the Philosopher's Stone (ep. 9-12, ending about ch. 59). Later volumes travel to the domain of the demon lord Belphegor (Yen Press series page). Volume boundaries are estimated from the anime.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Tama protects Aria without ever telling her who he is. Why does he keep the secret?
2. Aria wants to stop being saved and become strong. How does she do it?
3. Kasman and the Earl both think they are owed something by women. How does the story answer them?
4. Reiss gives up his revenge because of Eliza. What changed his mind?
5. The story jokes a lot about bodies and desire. Do the jokes respect the characters? Who are they for?
