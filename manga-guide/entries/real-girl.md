**Premise.** Hikari Tsutsui, a high-school senior and anime fan mocked as a "creepy otaku" since childhood, has one friend, the cat-ear-wearing Yuto Ito. Punished for lateness, he is paired for pool cleaning with Iroha Igarashi, a beautiful girl with a reputation for going through boyfriends. She stands up for him, he stands up for her, and she asks him out; he refuses, then agrees, and learns she will move away in six months (ch. 1-2). Her reputation turns out to be gossip: she is a virgin (fan wiki). Through the relationship Hikari makes friends: Arisa Ishino, who leaves a boyfriend who used her for money; Sumie Ayado, a younger otaku girl with a crush on him; and Mitsuya Takanashi, a proud rival who frames Hikari as a pedophile by having his little sister scream, then apologizes and joins the group (Wikipedia; Japanese Wikipedia). Later arcs cover a school festival, Hikari's parents' marriage scare (ch. 28-31), Iroha's protective step-brother Chika, and a first overnight trip (ch. 36-39). Iroha reveals she has a brain tumor; surgery abroad saves her but erases her memories of Hikari. Seven years later they meet again, her memories return, and they marry and expect a child (ch. 44-47; fan wiki; TV Tropes).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can you love someone you have already decided to dislike? Hikari has learned from years of mockery to keep real people at a distance; Iroha has been judged by a reputation she never earned. Each must risk being seen as they are.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, if you look past labels and stay faithful. Hikari's growth comes from taking Iroha at her word, and the series tests that faith with the hardest case: a partner who forgets you entirely. Love returns her memories and ends in marriage.

**Characters as arguments.** Hikari argues for honesty over social skill. Iroha argues that reputation is not truth. Mitsuya argues for status, and loses until he apologizes. Ito is loyal friendship without jealousy. Arisa shows that being used is not love. Ezomichi, a cartoon in Hikari's head, argues that fiction can still teach real courage.

**Symbols and motifs.** The swimming pool where they meet; the matching keychains that bring Iroha's memories back (fan wiki); "2D" versus "3D" as fantasy versus reality; the six-month deadline.

**Traditions it echoes.** The shojo first-love story, told from the boy's side; illness-and-amnesia melodrama; for faith readers, marriage vows "in sickness and in health" and the teaching against gossip.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism and virtue ethics both win: be yourself, and be faithful. Feminist themes are mixed: the story defends Iroha, while Anime Feminist faults the hero's framing. Traditionalism wins at the end with marriage and a child.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1-3 (ch. 1-11): the couple forms against gossip. Vol. 4-9 (ch. 12-35): the friend group, the frame-up, the festival and family. Vol. 10-12 (ch. 36-47): the trip, the illness, memory loss, and the seven-year time skip to marriage.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Iroha had a reputation she did not deserve. How do reputations start, and how do they stick?
2. Hikari followed Iroha in secret early on. Was that caring or creepy?
3. Mitsuya framed Hikari and later apologized. What made his apology real?
4. How should a couple set limits in a teen relationship?
5. Would you wait seven years for someone? What makes love last?
