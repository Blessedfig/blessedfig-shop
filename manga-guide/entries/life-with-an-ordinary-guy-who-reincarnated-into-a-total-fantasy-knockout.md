**Premise.** Hinata Tachibana, a 32-year-old nerdy office worker, keeps failing at singles mixers, partly because his handsome childhood friend Tsukasa Jinguji, who has no interest in women, is always beside him (Seven Seas; Wikipedia). Walking home drunk, Tachibana wishes he were a pretty girl. A naked Goddess of Love and Beauty appears, grants the wish and sends both men to a fantasy world to defeat the Demon Lord (Seven Seas blurb). Jinguji gets superhuman strength and skills; Tachibana gets almost nothing but a peerless beauty that makes men fall for her, plus a "troublemaker" skill; when Jinguji refuses the quest, the goddess curses them. Each secretly realizes he is attracted to the other and hides it (Wikipedia; TV Tropes). Early arcs (anime eps. 1-12, about v1-5) bring bandits who fight over Tachibana, an elf chieftess named Telolilo, the self-styled hero Schwartz from Earth and the knight Lucius, a living suit of armor run by a demon officer, Vizzd, a squid god that demands human sacrifices, the assassin Shen, a princess, Ugraine, whom Tachibana talks out of suicide, and a rebel army steered by a demon general (Wikipedia). Later publisher blurbs show Jinguji fighting the Demon Lord's general (v10) and the goddess turning Tachibana into a handsome man and Jinguji into a little girl (v11) (Penguin Random House). TV Tropes reports the Demon King turns out to be a baby.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What is a friendship, and does it change when a body changes? Tachibana and Jinguji have been inseparable since childhood; now one of them is a beautiful woman, and both are confused.

**The answer the story gives.** The bond comes first. Each arc ends with the friends closer, and the first one ends with Jinguji listing everything he admires about Tachibana (ep. 12). The story keeps the romance ambiguous: the goddess's curse may or may not be the cause (TV Tropes), and later volumes swap the bodies again (v11).

**Characters as arguments.** Tachibana argues for warmth and openness; Jinguji for stoic strength and suspicion of women, which the story mocks and slowly softens. Schwartz argues that real men say what they feel. The goddess stands for desire that does not care about consequences.

**Symbols and motifs.** The wish made drunk; the crown that hides Tachibana's beauty; Jinguji's strength that fades when the two are apart; the paper bag over her head (ep. 4).

**Traditions it echoes.** The isekai adventure and its parody; Japanese gender-swap comedy; the buddy comedy. Critics connect it to trans wish-fulfillment fiction (Anime Feminist) and to "will they, won't they" romance.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism is affirmed: be honest about who you are and whom you love. Progressivism is explored, not argued, and critics disagree about whether the jokes undercut it. Postmodern parody keeps everything light. The gods are pagan furniture for jokes.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-5 (ch. 1-69, the anime) set up the world, the companions and the first big battle. Volumes 6-10 move toward the Demon Lord's generals (v10 blurb). Volume 11 swaps the bodies again (Tachibana male, Jinguji a little girl). Later volumes add a hero who chose to become male (ch. 103-104) and head toward the end in volume 20; contents not checked.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Jinguji thinks showing feelings is weak. Why do some people believe that?
2. The villagers worship Tachibana for her looks. What is wrong with being liked only for how you look?
3. If your best friend changed completely on the outside, what would stay the same?
4. Some jokes are about people being afraid of seeming gay. Who is the joke on?
5. Tachibana talks a princess out of ending her life. What did she say that helped?
