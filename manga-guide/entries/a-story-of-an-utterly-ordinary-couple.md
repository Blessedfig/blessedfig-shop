**Premise.** Haru Hanasaki and Aki (Akito) Hojo met as broke college students living next door to each other in a run-down apartment; in the prequel, money mysteriously appeared whenever they flirted or touched, and they pretended their cuddling was only for the money while hiding that they loved each other (KADOKAWA; MangaUpdates). This sequel follows them after they graduate, find jobs and marry, and the money gimmick is gone: it is, by design, "a very ordinary story" (publisher blurb). Haru loves the smell of her husband's sweat; Aki adores his wife (publisher blurb). Short chapters show them kissing while working from home, hugging after work (MyAnimeList synopsis), dressing up for Halloween, Christmas and Valentine's Day, exercising, and playing with costumes, including erotic cosplay and a bikini (MangaUpdates categories). There is no conflict and no outside cast to speak of (MangaPlaza and MyAnimeList reviewers).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What happens after "happily ever after"? The prequel asked whether two broke students who pretended to cuddle for the money really loved each other (KADOKAWA). The sequel takes the answer for granted and asks what married love looks like on an ordinary Tuesday.

**The answer the story gives.** It looks like touch, play and attention: kisses while working from home, hugs after work, costumes and holidays (MyAnimeList; MangaUpdates). The title is the thesis: an "utterly ordinary" marriage is worth a whole book.

**Characters as arguments.** Haru argues that a wife's desire is as natural and as funny as a husband's (her love of his sweat smell); Aki, that devotion is shown in small attentions. There are no counter-arguments: no rival, no parents, no crisis.

**Symbols and motifs.** The seasons in the couple's names (Haru, "spring"; Aki, "autumn," per AniList); holidays as markers of a year together; the end-of-chapter kiss.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Song of Songs and many traditions' teaching that marital love is good and joyful; the Japanese "married-life" romance genre. Readers from modest traditions will note that those same teachings keep such intimacy private.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Hedonism is affirmed but bounded by fidelity; traditional marriage is the frame. No religious, political or social argument is made.

**Shifts across the series.** The prequel (two volumes) had a supernatural money mystery and side characters; this sequel drops both for short, self-contained chapters (MyAnimeList review).

**Discussion questions.**
1. What small things do Haru and Aki do that keep their marriage happy?
2. The book is called "utterly ordinary." Why might an ordinary life be worth a story?
3. What parts of a marriage do you think should stay private, and why?
4. How is this marriage different from romances that end at the wedding?
