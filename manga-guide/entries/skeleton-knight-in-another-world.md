**Premise.** A gamer wakes up in the body of his online-game avatar, Arc, a two-meter knight in silver armor who is a skeleton underneath. He never removes his helmet in public. In his first hour he kills bandits who have murdered a noble's escort and are tearing the clothes of a young noblewoman and her maid (ch. 1), then becomes a mercenary. He heals a green fox, Ponta, whose trust marks him as good (ch. 4), and joins the dark-elf warrior Ariane in freeing elf children and captives from human slavers and from the Marquis of Diento, who dies at the elves' hands (ch. 5-8). The elves accept him, skeleton and all (ch. 9-10). With Ariane and the cat-girl ninja Chiyome of the Jinshin clan he raids slave markets that also hold beast people (ch. 13-14), revives an assassinated princess, Yuriarna (ch. 12), and is drawn into a succession struggle in the Rhoden Kingdom. Later arcs reveal that the human-supremacist Hilk Church, whose cardinals are secretly undead, and the slave-holding Holy East Revlon Empire stand behind much of the evil (manga ch. 41, "The Identity of the Cardinal"; Wikipedia; fan wiki).

**Where traditions disagree.** Every frame welcomes the anti-slavery heart of the story and the defense of peoples called inferior. Frames divide on two points. Second, the depiction of sexual violence: interrupted and condemned in the plot, but used as an opening hook that critics call exploitative, it weighs as a Significant depiction concern for most church frames and as a message concern for secular progressive readers. Faith readers will also note that the one organized religion is a supremacist fraud run by undead, which Atheist and Satanist frames welcome as exposure of manipulative authority and some church frames feel as a stock attack on religion.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What is power for? Arc wakes up nearly invincible in a world where humans buy and sell elves and beast people. He could live quietly; instead he keeps stepping in.

**The answer the story gives.** Power is for protecting those who cannot protect themselves. Arc frees captives, revives a murdered princess (ch. 12) and helps two peoples strike at the slave trade (ch. 13-14). His skeleton body is the joke and the point: what matters is what you do, not what you look like.

**Characters as arguments.** Arc argues for the courteous, practical protector who also likes money and food. Ariane argues for loyalty to one's own people and slowly learns to trust a human. Chiyome, whose parents died because of slavery, argues for patient, organized liberation. The Marquis of Diento and the slavers argue for treating persons as goods and lose. The Hilk Church argues for racial supremacy and turns out to be a front for the undead. The elf council argues for restraint over war (ch. 10).

**Symbols and motifs.** The helmet that never comes off; Ponta the spirit fox, whose trust vouches for Arc; the magic pool that briefly restores his flesh (light novel v4); collars that eat magic, worn by slaves (fan wiki).

**Traditions it echoes.** Chivalric romance (the wandering knight who rights wrongs), the isekai power fantasy, and abolition stories. Critics compare it to Overlord and Re:Zero as lighter popcorn fare (THEM via Wikipedia).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Virtue and liberation are affirmed; strength is admired but always attached to protecting; organized religion appears only as the villains' tool. Critics argue the story uses the victims' suffering, including sexual violence, as spectacle more than it examines it (Anime Feminist; Common Sense Media).

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1-2 (ch. 1-10): Arc's arrival, Diento and the elves. Vol. 3-6 (ch. 11-30): Olav, Yuriarna, the beast-people slave markets, the magic pool, Chiyome's clan. Vol. 7-11 (ch. 31-51): northern kingdoms, undead armies, the cardinal unmasked (ch. 41) and dragon lords. Vol. 12-15 (ch. 52-73): alliances between elves and humans ("Joint Struggle With Humans," "The Elves' Decision") (chapter titles; light-novel summaries).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Arc hides his face because people would fear him. Have you ever judged someone by how they look?
2. Arc kills slavers without a trial. When, if ever, is that right? Who should decide?
3. The elf council chose not to start a war (ch. 10). Was that wise or weak?
4. The Hilk Church teaches that some peoples are less than human. Where have people believed that in real history, and how did it end?
5. Some critics say the opening scene was unnecessary. Can a story show cruelty without using it to entertain?
