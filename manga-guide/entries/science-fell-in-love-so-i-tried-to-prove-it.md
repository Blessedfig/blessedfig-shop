**Premise.** Ayame Himuro and Shinya Yukimura are first-year graduate students in Professor Ikeda's information-science lab at Saitama University (written with different kanji in the story), both convinced that anything can be explained with math. When Himuro tells Yukimura she seems to be in love with him, he cannot answer until "love" is defined, so they set out to prove it: tracking heart rates through a hundred wall-pins (kabedon), dates with data sheets, a kiss experiment, saliva tests for oxytocin. Their labmates are Kotonoha Kanade, a fourth-year undergraduate and the story's common-sense narrator; Ena Ibarada, a game-loving second-year graduate student; and Kosuke Inukai, a fourth-year who brags of a girlfriend who turns out to be a dating-sim character (Wikipedia, episodes 1-3). Season 1 (to ch. 31) ends with a training camp in Okinawa, a fight and Yukimura realizing he loves her too; by season 2 he declares it (Wikipedia episode summaries). Season 2 adds a biology-lab couple, a high-school girl Yukimura tutors, a love triangle, and a dark turn: Naoya Shikijo, rejected by Kanade, abducts her with a stun gun and his friends try to rape her until Yukimura rescues her and they are arrested (v11). Later volumes, known only from publisher synopses, follow Kanade's feelings for Yukimura (v12), Kosuke and Ibarada's romance (v14-17), Himuro's mother trying to take her to America (v19) and the final experiment (v20-21) (Kirapo volume pages).

## Life message

**You can measure love, but you prove it by choosing it: reason and feeling are partners, not rivals, and people are fine just as they are.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can love be proven? Two people who trust only what can be measured fall for each other and refuse to say so until they have evidence. The series is a long joke on that premise, and a real question: what kind of knowledge is love?

**The answer the story gives.** Data helps but cannot decide. The couple measure heart rates, hormones and "mood values," and their experiments draw everyone closer, but Yukimura's breakthrough is that no single measure fits every couple; what is needed is one person's awareness of loving another (season 1 end, Wikipedia). Later volumes keep experimenting, but the experiments become ways to spend time together and to face real-life choices (Himuro's mother, v19; the final experiment, v20-21).

**Characters as arguments.** Yukimura is pure rationalism, kind underneath and willing to be disliked for the sake of clarity (Japanese Wikipedia). Himuro is reason that admits feeling. Kanade is common sense and the longing to be normal; her arc ends with being told she is fine as she is. Kosuke argues that love for a 2D character is still love, until he finds he loves a real person. Naoya is the counter-argument: a man who demands "normal" from others and turns violent when refused; he is arrested. Professor Kagurano shows how contempt makes a child fail, and changes.

**Symbols and motifs.** The lab coat; heart-rate monitors; equations of love (the season 2 title r=1-sinθ draws a heart-shaped cardioid); the roof at sunset chosen for the kiss experiment; prime numbers recited at parties.

**Traditions it echoes.** The battle-of-wits romantic comedy (reviewers compare Kaguya-sama), the educational manga, and the old question of reason versus the heart. For religious readers, the ending's humility about what can be measured leaves room for love as gift or calling; for secular readers, the method itself is the hero.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Naturalism is the working method and is tested rather than simply affirmed. Secular humanism is affirmed through education and kindness. Expressive individualism appears as self-acceptance. The lab as a community is the warm center.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-6 (season 1) are lab comedy, dates and the Okinawa camp. Volumes 6-11 (season 2) add other couples, a triangle and the dark abduction arc. Volumes 12-18 shift to the labmates' romances, overnight stays and a hot-spring trip. Volumes 19-21 bring Himuro's mother, the final experiment and Yukimura's brother; online chapters reach graduation (proof 108).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Yukimura won't say he loves Himuro until he can prove it. What kinds of things can't be proven but are still true?
2. Kanade wants to be "normal." Who decides what normal is? What does the story say?
3. Yukimura tells Haru's father that low expectations made her fail. Have you seen expectations change how someone does?
4. Naoya says he wants a normal romance, then hurts Kanade when she says no. What is wrong with how he sees her?
5. Some of the lab's experiments are done to people without asking first. When is that funny, and when is it not okay?
