**Premise.** Suzume Yosano, 15, a country girl, moves to Tokyo to live with her uncle Yukichi, who runs a café, when her parents move to Bangladesh for her father's job. Lost on the way, she is helped by a stranger who turns out to be her new homeroom teacher, Satsuki Shishio, a friend of her uncle, who then tutors her (Wikipedia; fan wiki). She befriends her seatmate Mamura, a boy who cannot handle being near girls, and Yuyuka, who at first fakes niceness to get close to Mamura (Wikipedia). Suzume falls for Shishio (v2), confesses while he pretends to be asleep (v3), and after weeks of mixed signals he confesses too (v5). They date in secret for a couple of months, including a trip together (v6-7); then Shishio ends it, telling her it was only admiration and that he does not love her, a lie meant to protect her (ch. 47; fan wiki). In their second year Suzume poses as Mamura's girlfriend to fend off his fans (v9); Mamura confesses for real and she chooses him (v10). Shishio declares his love again (v11), but in the final chapter Suzume tells him her feelings are with Mamura (ch. 78; TV Tropes). The side stories show Suzume and Mamura engaged and Shishio six years later (TV Tropes).

## Life message

**First love is not always lasting love: real love is honest and between equals, a relationship that has to be hidden is a warning sign, and growing up means choosing what is good for you over what dazzles you.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What is the difference between a first love and a lasting one? Suzume falls for the first adult in Tokyo who is kind to her, and he is her teacher. The series asks how a teenager can tell admiration from love, and what an adult owes a young person who looks up to him.

**The answer the story gives.** Real love is honest, equal and does not need to hide. Shishio's secret relationship brings confusion and pain; Mamura's honest confession (v10) brings steadiness. Silverman reads the ending as the series' rare argument that first love is not necessarily lasting love, and Suzume's realization that her relationship with Shishio was unequal as a step in the right direction (vol. 12 review).

**Characters as arguments.** Shishio argues that feelings justify crossing a line, then that ending it is the protective thing, then that he cannot let go; the story leaves him alone. Mamura argues for patience and plain honesty. Yuyuka moves from manipulation to loyal friendship. Yukichi, the guardian, argues by his actions that adults must protect the young.

**Symbols and motifs.** The shooting star seen in daytime, which Suzume chases from the school roof: a rare, dazzling thing that is not what it seems. The aquarium, revisited first with Shishio and later with Mamura. Shishio's nickname for her, "Chun-chun" (a sparrow's chirp; suzume means sparrow).

**Traditions it echoes.** The shōjo love triangle and the coming-of-age story; teacher-student romance is a familiar shōjo trope that this series finally turns against. Readers may hear echoes of wisdom literature on guarding the heart and of teaching on the responsibility of those in authority.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Virtue (honesty, courage) wins. Expressive individualism is present but corrected: feelings alone do not make a choice good. Duty ethics is affirmed by consequence: the rule Shishio broke was there for a reason.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-4: arrival in Tokyo, the crush, Mamura's cheek kiss (ch. 11), the confession and the aquarium. Volumes 5-7: Shishio's confession, secret dating, a trip, and the breakup (ch. 47). Volume 8: Suzume flees home and her friends bring her back. Volumes 9-12: fake dating, Mamura's confession, Shishio's return, Okinawa (ch. 73) and the final choice (ch. 78).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Suzume falls for her teacher. Why might a new student in a strange city feel that way about the first adult who helps her?
2. Shishio keeps their relationship secret. What does the need for secrecy tell you?
3. Shishio tells Suzume he does not love her, to protect her. Was that the right thing to do?
4. Why does Suzume finally choose Mamura? What makes his love different?
5. What should a teacher do if a student says she has a crush on him?
