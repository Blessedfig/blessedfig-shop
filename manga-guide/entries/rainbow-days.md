**Premise.** Four second-year boys at Seiryo High are an unlikely friend group: Natsuki Hashiba, a soft-hearted romantic; Tomoya "Mattsun" Matsunaga, a playboy dating several girls at once; Keiichi Katakura, a smiling athlete who carries a whip and jokes about his sadistic side; and Tsuyoshi "Tsuyopon" Naoe, a blunt otaku who draws doujinshi and has the only steady girlfriend, the cosplayer Yukiko. On Christmas Eve Natsuki is dumped by a girl who wanted his money; crying in the street, he is handed tissues by a girl in a Santa suit, Anna Kobayakawa, who turns out to go to his school (v1). Over two school years the boys help and tease each other through love: Natsuki's slow courtship of Anna (an impulsive kiss while feverish, v6-7; a confession at the school festival, v13), Tomoya's change from playboy to devoted boyfriend of the prickly Mari Tsutsui (v6, v12), Keiichi's crush on the school nurse (v4) and his slow turn toward Tomoya's younger sister Nozomi (v11-16), and Tsuyoshi's choice about college and his girlfriend (v14). The last volumes deal with graduation and the fear of friends drifting apart (v15-16) (Wikipedia; Japanese Wikipedia; VIZ blurbs; The Outerhaven).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What do friends owe each other while they are growing up? Four boys who would never have chosen each other end up as a family of sorts, and the story asks how friendship changes when love, rivals and the end of school arrive.

**The answer the story gives.** Friends tell you the truth even when it hurts (v1), stand with you when you are scared (v15) and let you go when it is time (v16). Love works the same way: Natsuki's patience and honesty win out over Tomoya's early games, and the playboy himself becomes faithful.

**Characters as arguments.** Natsuki argues for sincere, even naive love. Tomoya argues for love as a game, then shows what changes when he takes Mari seriously. Keiichi hides behind a cheerful sadism and is most afraid of being alone. Tsuyoshi, the odd one out, turns out to be the most mature (The Outerhaven, v14). Mari shows how being hurt turns into distrust, and how kindness undoes it.

**Symbols and motifs.** The scarf given to a stranger on Christmas Eve (v1); the rainbow of four different personalities; the ghost who cannot move on until he lets go (v5); graduation as the end of a season.

**Traditions it echoes.** The shojo romantic comedy, told from the boys' side; the school ghost story (the "seven wonders" of a school); the friendship tale in which very different people form one group.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarian friendship is celebrated throughout. Expressive individualism is welcomed but always inside the group. Hedonism (Tomoya's early dating) is gently corrected. Religious readers find no religion but a strong moral core of loyalty, honesty and faithful love, mixed with crude sexual jokes.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-5 are episodic comedy (the first meeting, the bullying senior, Comiket, the nurse, the amusement park, the ghost). Volumes 6-13 build the romances (the feverish kiss, the class trip, Mochizuki's and Mari's confessions, the festival confession). Volumes 14-16 turn to the future: college, Keiichi's fear of change and graduation.
