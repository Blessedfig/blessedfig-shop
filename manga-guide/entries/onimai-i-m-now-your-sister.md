**Premise.** Mahiro Oyama, a shut-in in his twenties who lives on video games and erotic games, wakes up in the body of a girl of about 13. His younger sister Mihari, a grade-skipping prodigy doing research at a university, drugged him as part of her plan to rehabilitate her big brother (Wikipedia; Kodansha). Mihari records his progress while teaching him to bathe, dress, wear a bra and cope with his first period (anime ep. 1-2). Her friend Kaede, a gyaru high schooler, and Kaede's tomboyish sister Momiji draw Mahiro outside (ep. 3-5); Mahiro is enrolled in middle school, where Momiji's friends Asahi and Miyo become his circle (manga ch. 21; ep. 6). When the drug starts to wear off on a hot-spring trip, Mahiro chooses to keep taking it and live as a girl (anime ep. 12; Japanese Wikipedia). The ongoing manga continues into second year, adds a new classmate, Nayuta, who is watching Mahiro for Mihari's advisor Chitose, whose motives are hidden (Wikipedia), and has their mother accept Mahiro as she is (Japanese Wikipedia).

## Creator and publisher context

- Nekotofu (story and art) is a pen name. Before Onimai the author was a fan of gender-swap manga and anime (Ranma ½, Kashimashi, Idol Pretender) and was moved to start after seeing Your Name and after a well-received gender-swap doujinshi; the series began on Pixiv in 2017 and moved to Niconico Seiga, then Comic Rex (2019). The author still self-publishes online and produces doujinshi with Ichijinsha's permission (Wikipedia).
- The author self-produced drama CDs from 2018, wanting to expand an original work within reach; the cast continued into the anime. The author attended every anime script meeting (Japanese Wikipedia).
- Anime staff: director Shingo Fujii (his first series; previously on Precure and Mushoku Tensei episodes) asked for a woman head writer because the story depicts women's hardships and sex differences; Michiko Yokote wrote it (Japanese Wikipedia).
- No public statements on religion, politics, gender identity or the fan-service criticism were found in sources opened. No controversies involving the creator found. **Status: Needs more info on the creator, especially any statement on the gender theme.**
- Publisher: Ichijinsha (Comic Rex). English: Kodansha USA, digital only, rated 16+.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can someone who has withdrawn from life be brought back, and what is it right to do to help them? Mihari's answer is a drug that turns her brother into a girl.

**The answer the story gives.** Connection heals. Mahiro is coaxed out by small things: a jog, a haircut, a friend's cooking, a class that accepts her. By the hot-spring trip she chooses this life (ep. 12). The story treats Mihari's method as justified by the result; ANN critics did not.

**Characters as arguments.** Mahiro argues that people can change and be happier for it. Mihari argues for loving intervention, and her methods raise the question of consent. Kaede and Momiji argue for practical, unpushy kindness. Chitose stands for science with a hidden purpose.

**Symbols and motifs.** The pills. The mirror and new clothes. The middle school uniform as a second childhood. The hot spring, where the drug wears off and Mahiro decides.

**Traditions it echoes.** Japanese gender-swap comedy (Ranma ½, Kashimashi) and iyashikei slice of life. Readers bring their own frames: trans and genderfluid self-discovery for some, and for religious traditions the teaching that the body and sex are given. Some Hindu and Buddhist stories feature sex changes that readers may compare.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism and communitarianism are affirmed together: self-acceptance happens among friends. Transhumanism is explored without much worry. Feminism and progressivism are explored but, critics argue, undercut by the fan service.

**Shifts across the series.** The early chapters (anime ep. 1-5) are at home: the change, bathing, periods, shopping. Middle school begins around ch. 21 (ep. 6), with friends, tests and holidays. The hot-spring decision closes the anime (ep. 12). Later volumes add summer at the beach (v6), second year, Nayuta and Chitose's plot, and Mahiro's mother.

4. Critics said the camera treats Mahiro's body in a way that is not okay. What do you think they meant?
5. What would you do to help a family member who never leaves their room?
