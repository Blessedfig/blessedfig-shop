**Premise.** Fuuko Izumo, 18, has lived alone for ten years since her power, Unluck, which brings disaster on anyone who touches her skin, killed her parents and more than 200 others in a plane explosion when she was eight. On the day her favorite shojo manga ends, she goes to a bridge to end her life. A man who cannot die stops her by stabbing himself with her knife and grabbing her face; Unluck drops him in front of a train, and he regenerates (ch. 1). She names him Andy. He wants her Unluck to give him a real death, and they join the Union, a secret group of "Negators" whose powers negate a rule of the world (Undead, Unchange, Unjustice and so on). The Union hunts UMAs, creatures embodying rules of nature, under quests set by a living book, Apocalypse, and aims to kill the God who keeps adding rules to torment humanity. That God, Sun, is a sadistic creator who gives each Negator a tragedy for entertainment; a second creator, Luna, believes people matter more than rules (fan wiki). In the Ragnarok arc (ch. 94-132) the world is destroyed; Fuuko loops back to a 101st world (ch. 133 on), where she uses what she knows to prevent each Negator's tragedy and gather an ultimate Union. They defeat Sun (ch. 236-237). The gods withdraw, Negator powers and UMAs end, Fuuko loses and regains her memories, and she and Andy marry (ch. 237-239) (fan wiki; Wikipedia).

## Life message

**Even a life marked by curse and loss is worth choosing; people who stand together can break the rules imposed on them, and love proves stronger than any of those rules.**

| Meta lens | How it reads the series |

Andy's early sexual pursuit of Fuuko is crude courtship played for laughs; it turns into a lasting pair bond and marriage (ch. 94, 239). |
| Pragmatic | Powers are puzzles: each Negator wins by using a rule cleverly (Andy firing parts of himself, Fuuko's "Bad Bullets"). In the 101st loop, Fuuko's foreknowledge is put to practical use, recruiting each ally by solving their problem first. |
| Religious | Religion is the frame of the plot. The creator-God, Sun, is cruel and enjoys human suffering; killing him is the Union's goal. A second creator, Luna, sides with humanity. At the end the gods withdraw and leave a world with no gods to people (ch. 237, fan wiki). Faith readers will see a myth of overthrowing a cruel false god rather than a portrait of their own God, but the story's happy ending is a godless world. |
| Socio-cultural | A Weekly Shonen Jump action series about outcasts whose gifts are curses, built on friendship, effort and victory. Reviewers of the first volume faulted Andy's groping of Fuuko played as comedy (ANN; CBR via Wikipedia; AIPT; Beneath the Tangles). |

Outlook: Hopeful — the first world ends in catastrophe and many friends die (ch. 94-132), but the second loop saves nearly everyone from their tragedies, the cruel god is defeated and the story closes on a wedding (ch. 239).
Hope source: F — the way through is people standing together: Fuuko's 101st loop is a mission to save and gather every Negator into an ultimate Union, and only all of them together beat Sun; love between Andy and Fuuko (L) is the close second, since the final weapon is the power of the Heart (ch. 236-237, fan wiki).

**Where traditions disagree.** Most frames welcome Fuuko's choice to live, the found family and the second-chance structure of the 101st loop. The divide is theological: the story's highest god is a sadist and its happy ending is a world without gods. Christian, Jewish, Muslim, Bahá'í and Latter-day Saint readings weigh that heavily (C to D), though many will read Sun as a false god rather than their own; secular, Satanist and Buddhist frames read the same plot as people freeing themselves from imposed suffering (A to B). Progressive and conservative readers agree in disliking the first volume's harassment jokes.

## Winners, losers and sympathy

The Negators who choose each other win: in the second loop nearly every outcast is saved from their tragedy and joins the Union, and the cruel god is defeated. Sun and those who serve his rules (the Regulators) lose, though the enemy Ruin survives and admits defeat (ch. 238).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** If the world were run by a god who enjoyed your suffering, would life still be worth living? Fuuko has been cursed so that touching anyone she loves hurts them. Andy cannot die. The series asks what to do with a life you did not choose.

**The answer the story gives.** Live, and live for others. Fuuko starts on a bridge ready to die (ch. 1) and ends on the same bridge with her memories and her future (ch. 238). Her second loop is a long act of rescue: she finds each Negator before their tragedy and changes it. The final weapon against the cruel god is love itself (the Heart, ch. 236-237).

**Characters as arguments.** Fuuko argues for choosing life and choosing people. Andy argues, at first, that a good death is the best one can hope for; Fuuko changes his mind. Juiz shows endurance across endless defeats. Victor shows love that would kill to end suffering, and is answered by a better way. Sun is the argument that rules matter more than people; Luna answers that people matter more. Ruin, who worships Sun's order, survives to admit he was beaten.

**Symbols and motifs.** The bridge where Fuuko meant to die. The manga within the manga, To You, From Me, which predicts the story. "Un-" powers as curses turned to gifts. The loop: a world destroyed and remade, with one person remembering. The wedding dress that the clothes-monster becomes before it fades (ch. 237).

**Traditions it echoes.** The Gnostic idea of a cruel lesser creator, the JRPG climax of killing God, the time-loop rescue story and the shonen found family. Some religious readers will read Sun as a false god; others will see a story whose happy ending simply leaves God out.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Existentialism and secular humanism win: meaning is made by people, and the reward is a world without gods. Nihilism and might-makes-right lose. Rebirth is used as story machinery more than belief.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-3 (ch. 1-25) set up the Union with the crude early humor reviewers objected to. Volumes 4-10 (ch. 26-88) are the season quests. Volumes 11-15 (ch. 94-132) are Ragnarok, in which the first world ends. Volumes 15-27 (ch. 133-239) are the 101st loop, a series of rescues, the final battle and the epilogue.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Fuuko almost ended her life in chapter 1 and returns to the same bridge at the end. What changed?
2. Andy wanted the best death. What did he want by the end, and why?
3. The story's god enjoys people suffering. How is that different from what your family believes about God?
4. In the second loop Fuuko saves people before their tragedies. If you could prevent one hurt for a friend, what would it be?
5. Reviewers disliked how Andy treated Fuuko at first. Why do jokes about touching someone without permission matter?
