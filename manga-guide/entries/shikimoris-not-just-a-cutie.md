**Premise.** Yuu Izumi, a second-year high school student, has had terrible luck all his life: falling objects, runaway balls, cars and animals find him, and he carries scars from it (Wikipedia; fan wiki). His girlfriend, classmate Shikimori (her first name, Miyako, is revealed only in ch. 175), is sweet, pretty and a little clumsy at cooking and singing, but when Izumi is in danger she turns "cool": her eyes sharpen, she becomes intimidating and athletic, and she saves him (Wikipedia; But Why Tho). The couple is already together on page one. Early chapters are short gags; later ones grow longer and more character-driven (TV Tropes). The publisher's volume blurbs give the arc: a first summer with a beach trip and fireworks (v3); the culture fair, where volleyball ace Ai Kamiya's crush on Izumi comes to light (v4); Shikimori and Kamiya becoming friends and the sports fair (v5-v6); Izumi telling Shikimori's mother about the relationship and a Christmas party (v7); the mothers meeting, a New Year's temple visit and a zoo date (v8); the Kyoto school trip (v9-v10); Izumi's "boldest of moves" on the way home, a cheek kiss in ch. 100 (v11); Shikimori helping Kamiya sort out her feelings on Valentine's Day (v12); third year with a dress-code rebel, Hayase, whom Shikimori once rescued (v13-v15); plans after graduation (v16-v17); entrance exams (v18-v19); and a first overnight trip, graduation and an epilogue (v20) (Kodansha USA blurbs via Penguin Random House, Apple Books and the fan wiki).

## Life message

**A good life is a partnership in which each person protects, supports and openly accepts the other as they are: love does not erase weakness or bad luck, but shares it, and frees both people from narrow ideas of what a boy or a girl should be.**

| Meta lens | How it reads the series |
|---|---|
| Evolutionary biology | Pair-bonding as mutual protection, with the usual roles swapped: the girl is the physical protector, the boy the nurturer who cooks, sews and cleans (TV Tropes; fan wiki). Rivalry for a mate appears once (Kamiya) and ends in friendship, "a wholesome commiseration" rather than a fight (Callum May, ANN ep. 8). The story closes on a committed couple planning a shared home (fan discussions of the vol. 20 epilogue). |
| Pragmatic | The couple solves problems by talking: reviewers single out their communication ("such a healthy relationship," Eisenbeis, ANN; "open and honest about their love," May, ANN ep. 12) and "resolving insecurity through conversation" (THEM). | Religious | Religion is incidental: a New Year's temple visit (v8 blurb; the anime's ep. 11 shrine visit, ANN) and Kyoto sightseeing (v9-v10). Faith readers will mainly weigh a chaste-seeming teen romance (a cheek kiss in ch. 100; hugs and hand-holding), mild swimsuit and lakeside scenes, the swap of gender roles, a first overnight trip as a couple at 18 shortly before graduation (vol. 20; fans report nothing sexual happens), and an epilogue in which the couple, now at university, plan to live together (TV Tropes; MyAnimeList discussion). |

Outlook: Hopeful — misfortune is a constant in Izumi's life, but the story treats it as bearable and even funny when shared, and it ends with graduation and the couple planning a shared future.

**Where traditions disagree.** No frame reports a sexual-content conflict, and the couple's conduct is widely praised. The first dividing line is **gender roles**: mainline, secular progressive and Unitarian readers welcome the reversal (Anime Feminist), while complementarian Baptist, Reformed and conservative families may treat it as a discussion point. The second is **teen dating** itself (Jehovah's Witness, Muslim, Orthodox Jewish, many Sikh and Bahá'í families). The third is the ending: a first overnight trip as a couple at 18 and an epilogue in which they plan to live together at university. Secular and progressive frames see a natural, happy close; traditions that teach chastity before marriage and against cohabitation (Catholic, Baptist, Latter-day Saint, Muslim, Jehovah's Witness, Bahá'í, conservative) will want to discuss how it is framed.

## Creator and publisher context

- Keigo Maki (真木蛍五) writes and draws the series. It began as a short Twitter manga on September 8, 2018, before Kodansha serialized it on Magazine Pocket (fan wiki; MyAnimeList news post; Kodansha Japan). ## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What does each person owe the one they love, and who gets to be strong? Izumi's life is a string of accidents he cannot prevent. Shikimori, sweet in every other moment, becomes formidable when he is in danger. The series asks whether a relationship can rest on one person needing protection, and whether a girl who is "cool" can also be "cute," and a boy who is "cute" can also be worth admiring.

**The answer the story gives.** Strength is shared, not owned. Izumi never stops being unlucky, and the story never treats that as shameful. He gives back what he can: cooking, sewing, care, praise, honesty, good grades (TV Tropes; fan wiki). Shikimori protects him, but she also wants to be seen as cute, and the story honors that wish. Reviewers note that she is "pretty feminine overall" (Moore, ANN) and that neither lead is pushed into the other gender's coding (Silverman, ANN). The answer is a partnership in which, as Eisenbeis puts it, the couple's communication makes "such a healthy relationship." By the last volume, fans quote Izumi turning the premise around: he no longer thinks of himself as unlucky, because he can make her "the luckiest person in the world" (MyAnimeList ch. 176 thread).

**Characters as arguments.** Shikimori argues that strength and softness can live in one person. Izumi argues that gentleness and needing help are compatible with dignity; Rebecca Silverman calls him "the damsel in distress to Shikimori's prince charming." Kamiya, who also likes Izumi, argues for competition, and the story answers by making her Shikimori's friend; ANN's Callum May reads the two girls as alike, both "perceived as being more masculine than they'd like," with Izumi the one boy who treats them as they want. Inuzuka argues that a male best friend can be kind, loyal and hard-working (THEM; fan wiki). Hayase, the dress-code rebel of the third year, argues that a troublemaker can be met with understanding rather than punishment (v14-v15 blurbs). Izumi's parents argue that adults can be warm and present (Silverman, ANN).

**Symbols and motifs.** Shikimori's changing eyes, drawn like a villainess's (Eisenbeis, ANN; But Why Tho), mark the shift from cute to cool. Izumi's bad luck stands for the misfortunes no one can fix, only share. Cooking stands for his way of giving. The stars, which Izumi knows well (fan wiki, ch. 25), return on the final overnight trip, where the couple talk about their future (MyAnimeList ch. 176 thread). The chapter 100 cheek kiss, kept off the page (Epicstream), stands for a private intimacy the story chooses not to show.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Japanese romcom of a dominant or teasing girl (*Nagatoro*, *Takagi-san*), and the "tomboy who wants to be feminine" story of *Tomo-chan Is a Girl!* (ANN ep. 11). More broadly it echoes the old ideal of love as mutual help in hardship, and modern arguments that strength and care belong to both sexes.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Feminist and progressive readers find the role reversal affirmed (Anime Feminist). Virtue and community lenses are quietly rewarded. Traditional readers find a faithful courtship and caring families, with gender roles and the ending's overnight trip and cohabitation plan as the points of difference. Faith traditions find little to object to in the main story beyond mild flirtation and teen dating.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1 (ch. 1-16) is short gags about accidents and rescues. Vols. 2-3 add the friend group and the first summer (beach, barbecue, fireworks). Vols. 4-6 bring the culture fair, Kamiya's crush (she first appears in ch. 39, per the fan wiki) and the sports fair; the anime ends around ch. 62 (MangaUpdates). Vols. 7-8 widen to families and winter (meeting Shikimori's mother, Christmas, a New Year's temple visit). Vols. 9-11 cover the Kyoto trip, the shy Saruogi joining the group, and the cheek kiss in ch. 100. Vol. 12 resolves Kamiya's feelings on Valentine's Day. Vols. 13-15 open the third year with Hayase's arc. Vols. 16-19 turn to life after graduation: Izumi's search for a dream, the last volleyball tournament, exams and Shikimori's "secret ambition." Vol. 20 (ch. 175-178 and three bonus stories) covers the overnight trip (ch. 175-176), a family chapter (ch. 177), graduation (ch. 178) and side stories for Kamiya, Inuzuka and, last, the couple at university planning to live together (MyAnimeList threads; TV Tropes). Some fans found the ending "a bit too safe" or "cute vanilla."

**Discussion questions.**
1. Shikimori protects Izumi, and Izumi cooks for Shikimori. Do roles in a relationship have to follow gender? What does your family believe?
2. Izumi's bad luck never goes away. How do people live well with problems that cannot be fixed?
3. Shikimori wants to be "cute" as well as "cool." Why might she want both?
4. How does Shikimori treat Kamiya, who likes Izumi too? What would you have done?
5. The story ends with an overnight trip and a plan to live together. What does your family think should come before those steps, and why?
