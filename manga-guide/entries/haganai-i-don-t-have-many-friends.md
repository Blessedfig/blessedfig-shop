**Premise.** Kodaka Hasegawa transfers to St. Chronica's Academy, a Catholic school, and cannot make friends: his patchy blond hair (from his late English mother) and sharp eyes make everyone think he is a delinquent. He finds the abrasive, brilliant Yozora Mikazuki talking to her imaginary friend "Tomo," and the two form the Neighbors Club, an after-school club meant to help people without friends learn to make them (Wikipedia). Joining are Sena Kashiwazaki, the beautiful, arrogant daughter of the school's chairman, who treats boys as servants and plays dating-sim games to make virtual girlfriends; Yukimura Kusunoki, a gentle first-year who wants to become manly and is tricked by Yozora into dressing as a maid (later revealed, even to Yukimura's surprise, to be a girl, ch. 38); Rika Shiguma, a genius scientist with a sexual mind; Kodaka's little sister Kobato, a junior high student who dresses as a vampire; and Maria Takayama, a foul-mouthed ten-year-old "nun" who serves as advisor (Wikipedia). They practice friendship through games, a pool trip, karaoke, the beach and a festival. Yozora turns out to be "Sora," Kodaka's only childhood friend, whom he took for a boy (ch. 28-29). Sena learns their fathers once betrothed them (ch. 52) and confesses; Kodaka runs away from the club, Rika confronts him about pretending not to hear, and Yozora breaks down (v13-14; Gaffney). Later volumes bring a hot-springs trip with the student council, Yozora's rivalry with her popular older sister Hinata, a painful Christmas party, Yukimura's demand that Kodaka choose (v19) and graduation (v20) (Seven Seas synopses).

## Life message

**You may already have the friends you think you lack; admitting it, and being honest about your feelings, is harder and better than hiding behind jokes.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Why is it so hard to say "we are friends"? The Neighbors Club exists to help people with no friends learn how to make them. Every activity is called practice. The longer they practice together, the more obviously they are already friends, and the less any of them can say so.

**The answer the story gives.** Honesty, at a cost. Kodaka hides behind not hearing; Rika finally tells him no one can be that oblivious (v10, v14). Sena says what she feels; Yozora admits who she was. Admitting friendship and love changes the group, and someone gets hurt, but pretending was worse.

**Characters as arguments.** Kodaka argues for keeping things as they are. Yozora argues for the past (their childhood friendship) and for distrust of everyone else. Sena argues for saying what you want. Rika argues for honesty disguised as jokes. Yukimura argues for wanting to become someone, and discovers who she already is. Kobato and Maria are children who mirror the older characters' loneliness.

**Symbols and motifs.** Tomo, the imaginary "air friend." The club name, Neighbors: close but not yet friends. Kodaka's hair, which makes people misjudge him. The film the club makes for the festival, edited by Rika until she collapses. Yozora's cut hair, which brings back "Sora."

**Traditions it echoes.** The harem romantic comedy and the school-club comedy (Haruhi Suzumiya is a frequent comparison, Gaffney); otaku self-parody; for religious readers, the long tradition on friendship (Proverbs, Aelred, Pirkei Avot, the Buddha's "admirable friendship"), set against the series' crude sexual comedy and its use of a Catholic school as a joke.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarianism and expressive individualism carry the heart of the story. Postmodern parody keeps it self-aware. Hedonism and fan service dominate the surface, and critics (Gaffney, the ALA panel) judge the genre's sexism harshly. Religion appears only as costume.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-7 (ch. 1-31) are club activities and the Sora reveal. Volumes 8-12 (ch. 32-55) add Kate, the amusement park and hot springs, Yukimura's reveal, Kobato's birthday and the festival film. Volumes 13-16 (ch. 56-74, about) are Sena's confession, Kodaka's retreat and Yozora's return. Volumes 17-20 (ch. 75-99, about) cover the hot-springs trip with the student council, Christmas, Yukimura's demand and graduation.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why did the club call everything "practice"? When did it stop being practice?
2. Kodaka keeps pretending not to hear. Who does that protect, and who does it hurt?
3. Yozora is cruel to people she likes. Why might someone act that way?
4. Many reviewers disliked the fan service. Does it change how you see the characters?
5. What makes it hard to say "you are my friend" out loud?
