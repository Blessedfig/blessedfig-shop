**Premise.** Two high-school students share a fascination with death and murder. Yoru Morino is a pale, silent girl who wears black, has a white scar on her wrist and is shunned as strange; the unnamed narrator, revealed late as Itsuki Kamiyama, is outwardly friendly and popular but hides a complete lack of feeling for others (Wikipedia; Experiments in Manga). Their small town draws serial killers, and the pair investigate the cases only to understand the killers, not to stop them, and do not go to the police (VIZ blurb; MangaUpdates). The manga adapts five of the novel's six stories in four cases: a "wrist cutter" who severs and keeps the hands of people and animals, whom the narrator hopes will take Morino's hands so he can have them (VIZ blurb); a killer's notebook that leads the pair to an undiscovered victim and makes Morino the next target (Publishers Weekly; novel facts from The Book Smugglers); a neighbor, Saeki, who buries people alive in coffins and has already killed a small boy (Wikipedia); and a final case that joins the novel's "Twins" and "Voice," revealing Morino's childhood with her twin sister Yu, who died, and putting Morino in the place of the novel's next victim. The "Dog" story is left out (Wikipedia). Kamiyama, who privately wonders how Morino might die, ends up protecting her each time (MangaUpdates; MyAnimeList reviews).

## Life message

**The story offers no model of a good life; it studies two teenagers drawn to death and asks what separates a person fascinated by darkness from one who acts on it, and its answer is unsettling: very little.**

| Pragmatic | Close observation and deduction solve every case; the police are never told. The pair act on their own interest, and killers often escape the law (MangaUpdates). Cleverness without conscience is shown working. |

| Socio-cultural | A honkaku (puzzle-first) mystery wrapped in horror; Otsuichi's dark "Black Otsuichi" mode (Wikipedia, Otsuichi). The title refers to a taste for the dark side of people, not to goth subculture; in the novel's afterword the author apologizes for any suggestion that goths are criminals (The Book Smugglers, novel). |

Outlook: Bleak — killers keep appearing, the law never catches them, and the narrator who saves Morino does so out of possession rather than love; the only light is that Morino is shown to feel deeply beneath her blankness.
Hope source: N — the story finds little hope: no one is redeemed and nothing changes the narrator; Morino's survival and her grief for her sister are the nearest thing to warmth.

## Creator and publisher context

- Otsuichi (Hirotaka Adachi), born October 21, 1978 in Fukuoka Prefecture; debuted at 17 with Summer, Fireworks and My Corpse (Jump Novel Grand Prix, 1996); member of the Mystery Writers of Japan and the Honkaku Mystery Writers Club. His work is often split into "Black Otsuichi" (horror) and "White Otsuichi" (sad, heartwarming stories); Goth, the best known of the dark side, won the 2003 Honkaku Mystery Award (Wikipedia, Otsuichi; VIZ).
- On-record intent: in the novel's afterword he apologizes that the title might seem to label goths as criminals (The Book Smugglers, novel). In the manga's postscript he tells readers the novel has more stories, some with different outcomes (Publishers Weekly, 2008). A MyAnimeList reviewer reports an afterword in which he admits the string of murders around the pair is contrived and compares Morino to Princess Peach (to verify).
- Kendi Oiwa (Kenji Ooiwa), artist; Goth was his first professional work (Experiments in Manga). No interview found. **Status: Needs more info on the artist.**
- Publishers: Kadokawa Shoten (Shonen Ace) in Japan; Tokyopop (2008) and VIZ Media (VIZ Select, 2015, rated Mature) in English. No controversies involving the creators were found.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What separates a person who is fascinated by death from one who kills? Morino and the narrator both love the dark side of people; the killers act on it. The story keeps asking which side the narrator is on.

**The answer the story gives.** A thin line. Morino, for all her coldness, turns out to feel deeply and to be on the victims' side; the narrator, for all his smiles, is the one without humanity (The Book Smugglers, reading the novel). The manga shows this more than it states it (MyAnimeList reviewers note the narrator's inner monologue is cut).

**Characters as arguments.** Kamiyama argues for observation without empathy: he understands killers perfectly and feels nothing. Morino argues that a fascination with death can sit beside grief and love. The killers each embody a single compulsion (hands, burial). The absent police stand for a world that does not see.

**Symbols and motifs.** Hands (Morino's pale hands, the collector's refrigerator); the wrist scar; the rope; black clothes and a dead girl's clothes; coffins and earth; the smile that is a mask (VIZ blurb: teach me how to smile).

**Traditions it echoes.** The honkaku puzzle mystery, the Japanese horror short story, and the Western serial-killer thriller (Publishers Weekly pitched it as Nancy Drew meets Dexter). Its narrator echoes the unreliable, cold narrators of crime fiction.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Nihilism is shown, not argued: nothing is set right. Existentialism appears in two teens refusing the masks others wear, though one of them wears the best mask of all. Naturalism appears in the predator-and-prey view the narrator holds. No faith lens appears.

**Shifts across the series.** One volume. The cases grow more personal: the first cases are about strangers, and the final story turns to Morino's own past and her twin (likely ch. 4-5), where she is most in danger and most human.

**Discussion questions.**
1. The narrator is friendly to everyone but says his smiles are lies. How can you tell when someone is wearing a mask?
2. Morino and the narrator both like dark things. What makes them different?
3. The pair never tell the police what they know. What should they have done, and why?
4. Why might a story like this be fascinating? Is there a cost to finding murder fascinating?
5. Morino has a scar and cannot sleep without a rope around her neck. If she were your classmate, what could you do?
