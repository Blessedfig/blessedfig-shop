**Premise.** Oni aliens offer Earth a game of tag for the planet. The computer picks Ataru Moroboshi, a lecherous, unlucky 17-year-old student from Tomobiki Town. He wins by snatching the alien princess Lum's bikini top so she must cover herself instead of her horns, then cheers that he can get married (meaning to his girlfriend Shinobu). Lum takes it as a proposal, moves into his house as his self-declared wife, and electrocutes him whenever he chases other women (ch. 1-3). Episodic chapters bring a crowd of aliens (Ten, Lum's fire-breathing toddler cousin; Benten; Oyuki; Ran; the crow-goblin princess Kurama), Japanese spirits and gods, the doom-saying Buddhist monk Cherry, his niece the Shinto priestess and school nurse Sakura, the rich rival Shutaro Mendo, and Ryunosuke, a girl raised as a boy by her father. Shinobu eventually leaves Ataru and finds love with Inaba. In the final arc, "Boy Meets Girl" (ch. 356-366), Ataru must again catch Lum to save the Earth (Wikipedia; chapter list; general knowledge for the finale's details; to verify).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What happens when the most faithful lover in the universe marries the least faithful boy on Earth? The series never fully answers; it plays the question for 366 chapters.

**The answer the story gives.** Faithfulness wins by outlasting. Lum never stops loving Ataru; Ataru never stops chasing other women; and yet he never leaves her. The final arc, "Boy Meets Girl," puts the original game of tag back on the table, and Ataru chooses Lum without saying the words she wants (from general knowledge; to verify).

**Characters as arguments.** Ataru argues for appetite; Lum for devotion; Shinobu for a normal, kind love, which she finally gets with Inaba. Mendo is wealth and pride brought low. Cherry is religious authority as a greedy prophet of doom. Ryunosuke is a person boxed in by a parent's wishes for her identity.

**Symbols and motifs.** Horns (grabbing an oni's horns grants your wish, Wikipedia), tiger stripes, electricity as love and punishment, the endless game of tag.

**Traditions it echoes.** Bewitched (the supernatural wife), Japanese folktales, rakugo-style punning, and the slapstick of Go Nagai (Comics Beat via Wikipedia). It became the template for the "geek gets girl" harem comedy (Jonathan Clements via Wikipedia).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Absurdism is the house style. Hedonism is the joke: always punished, never repented. Folk religion is affectionate set dressing; no faith is attacked, all are teased.

**Shifts across the series.** Early chapters (vol. 1-2) are harsher, with Shinobu as girlfriend and a meaner Lum (Suitable for Treatment); the middle volumes settle into the school-and-aliens sitcom with Mendo, Ryunosuke and Ran; late volumes add Inaba and Shinobu's happy ending; the final arc (ch. 356-366) brings the romance to a close.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Lum shocks Ataru whenever he flirts. Is that funny, fair or a problem?
2. Which jokes from 1978 would not be written the same way today?
3. Shinobu leaves Ataru for someone kinder. Was she right?
4. Ryunosuke's father decides who she should be. How would you feel in her place?
5. Why does Lum keep forgiving Ataru?
