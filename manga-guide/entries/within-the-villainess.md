**Premise.** Remilia Rosa Graupner, a noble girl who grows up without love, wakes from a fever to find the soul of Emi Kobayashi, a Japanese girl who died, living in her body; Remilia's own soul is trapped inside, watching. Emi knows this world as the otome (romance) game she played, in which Remilia is the villainess doomed to ruin. Out of compassion, Emi works hard and befriends everyone to give Remilia a happy ending, and Remilia, sharing Emi's memories, comes to love her (Wikipedia; Abrams vol. 2). At the academy, the heroine Pina Blanche, the "Star Maiden," is possessed by another reincarnated soul, Rina, who wants the game's original plot back. Rina frames Remilia for attempted murder; her fiancé, the second prince Williard, her childhood friend and her adopted brother abandon her, and she is exiled to an abandoned village (Abrams vol. 2; Wikipedia). Emi's soul falls into a deep sleep from grief, and Remilia takes control, vowing to clear Emi's name, take revenge on the lying heroine and all who betrayed her, and build a world where Emi can live happily. She builds up the village through trade, takes in orphans, and allies with Ángel, the Demon King, whose eyes see through lies; together they defeat the creator god, the source of the miasma that plagues the world (Wikipedia; MyAnimeList reviews). Most of the revenge falls in the last five chapters, using mind-reading magic (MyAnimeList review).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What do we owe the one person who loved us? Remilia grew up unloved; then a stranger's soul arrived in her body and treated her life as worth saving. When that stranger is destroyed by a lie, Remilia's answer is total: clear her name and make everyone who believed the lie pay.

**The answer the story gives.** Loyalty and truth justify revenge. The story never asks Remilia to forgive; it lets her win, step by step, with planning, power and magic, while also building something good (a village, a home for orphans, peace with the demons). Readers say the ending gives both souls closure but is not a typical happy ending (MyAnimeList; MangaUpdates reviews; details not confirmed).

**Characters as arguments.** Emi argues that kindness and effort can rewrite fate, and then is broken when they fail. Remilia argues that some wrongs must be repaid, and that love can make a cold person build rather than only destroy. Rina argues for the selfish use of a story's rules and is given little depth (reader reviews). Williard and the other men argue for trusting appearances, and are shamed. Ángel, who cannot be lied to, is the story's idea of a just ruler.

**Symbols and motifs.** The body with two souls. The otome game's script that both reincarnated girls know. The Star Maiden title held by a liar. The miasma and the creator god behind it. Eyes that see through lies.

**Traditions it echoes.** The revenge tragedy and The Count of Monte Cristo (a wronged person returning with power); the possession tale; the villainess-isekai genre's revision of the fairy-tale heroine. For religious readers, a creator who is the source of evil echoes Gnostic myth (from general knowledge; to verify), and the plot sets loyalty against the command to leave vengeance to God.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Might makes right is explored and largely endorsed, because the might serves the wronged. Utilitarian reasoning (any means for Emi's sake) is never seriously challenged. Communitarianism appears as the village and orphans. Feminism appears as women outthinking the men who failed them.

**Shifts across the series.** By the back-cover copy and reader reviews: vol. 1 is Remilia's childhood with Emi inside her; vol. 2 brings the framing, exile and Remilia's vow; the middle volumes (roughly v3-4) build the village and the alliance with Ángel, with major feats by ch. 11 (MyAnimeList review); the last five chapters (roughly v5-6) carry most of the revenge and the defeat of the creator god.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Emi tried to give Remilia a happy ending. Why did her kindness fail at first?
2. Remilia never forgives. Do you think she should? Why or why not?
3. Why did the prince and the others believe Rina instead of Emi?
4. What does it mean that the world's creator god turns out to be the enemy?
