**Premise.** Akira and Daichi have been friends since preschool and drifted into dating; now in their first year at different high schools, Daichi narrates their awkward early romance. Akira loves judo and martial arts and dresses in boyish clothes, and she is embarrassed by anything girly. The first chapter has Daichi visit her school festival and find her in a cat-eared maid costume (ch. 1). Each short chapter puts Akira in a new outfit or situation (casual clothes, sports-day wear, a work uniform, a gyaru look, a Halloween costume, overalls, a date outfit) while Daichi tells her she is cute and she squirms; they struggle to hold hands (ch. 4) and go on a first date (ch. 12-13). Her quiet best friend Reina nudges them along, and vol. 2 adds a bedroom movie night, judo practice and a secret admirer at Daichi's school (Wikipedia; Square Enix; The Outer Haven; Siliconera).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can someone love you as you are when you are not sure who that is? Akira likes boyish clothes and judo, but some part of her wants to be cute, and that embarrasses her.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes. Daichi likes her in every style, and the jokes come from Akira's surprise at being liked. Siliconera reads it as partners appreciating what makes each other comfortable; The Outer Haven finds the repetition wearing until Daichi reaffirms his feelings.

**Characters as arguments.** Akira argues that you can be more than one kind of person. Daichi argues that love notices and praises. Reina argues that friends help love along.

**Symbols and motifs.** Clothes as the outward self; each chapter title names an outfit. Holding hands as a big step.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Japanese "too cute" girlfriend web-manga genre and childhood-friend romances (from general knowledge; to verify).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Self-expression is affirmed; gender norms are bent gently, not argued.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1 (ch. 1-13 + bonus): outfits and first steps, ending with a first date. Vol. 2: Akira tries to be a "better girlfriend"; a secret admirer appears. Vols. 3-6: not described by any source opened.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why is Akira embarrassed when Daichi calls her cute?
2. Can a person like both boyish and girly things?
3. Daichi sometimes shows up when Akira asked him not to. Is that sweet or a problem?
4. What makes a friendship a good start for a relationship?
