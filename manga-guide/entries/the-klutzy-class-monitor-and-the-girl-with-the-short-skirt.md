**Premise.** Togo Sakuradaimon is the school's ultra-serious disciplinary-committee member: first to arrive, last to leave, and on patrol even on weekends. Every morning at the gate he scolds Poem Kohinata, a gyaru (fashion-forward girl) with bleached hair and a short skirt, who is so embarrassed by her unusual given name that she hides it. Stuck together in remedial classes, Poem finds that Togo is a hopeless klutz and poor student, especially in math, and that he lives just down the street (ch. 1, anime ep. 1). Togo has never had friends because of his stiffness; Poem has always been strong (she punched boys who mocked her name in middle school). Around them gathers an ensemble: Yu Izubuchi, a former delinquent turned health-committee member who carries injured students to the nurse; Seiichi Tsukishima, a library-committee member who turns handsome only around books; Poem's friends Motoko Akina (who has a crush on Yu) and Rui Tasaki (a gifted boys'-love manga artist in the manga club); and the student council president Nadeshiko Yamato. Togo, who always says what he thinks, tells Poem plainly that he likes her; after wondering whether it was a confession, she learns his feelings and they begin dating (Japanese Wikipedia; chapter not confirmed). Both families appear, including a culture-festival rap battle between the two fathers (anime ep. 10-11) (Wikipedia; Japanese Wikipedia).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Who are people under the roles they play? The rule-enforcer is a lonely klutz; the "problem" girl is kind and capable; the ex-delinquent is a carer; the library heartthrob is a grumbler.

**The answer the story gives.** Sincerity matters more than image. Togo's plain honesty, which made him "boring" to classmates, is what wins Poem (Japanese Wikipedia).

**Characters as arguments.** Togo argues for duty kept from the heart. Poem argues that style is not character. Yu argues that people change when someone helps them (he was inspired by a man in a white coat who helped him). Tasaki argues that hidden talents deserve respect. The fathers argue that very different men can share the same love for their children.

**Symbols and motifs.** The skirt length (the rule as first impression), the school gate, remedial class, Togo's patrols, Poem's hidden name.

**Traditions it echoes.** The opposites-attract school romcom (AniList reviewer compares School Rumble). Religious readers may hear "the Lord looks on the heart" (1 Samuel 16:7); Shinto readers, the value of makoto (sincerity).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism and communitarianism are celebrated together; duty ethics is honored in Togo but softened in practice.

**Shifts across the series.** The anime's span (ch. 1-35, v1-8) covers meeting, friends, families, summer and the culture festival. Later volumes include the start of the couple's relationship and new characters (sisters, the manga club) (Japanese Wikipedia; chapter numbers not confirmed).

**Discussion questions.**
1. What did Poem first think of Togo, and what changed her mind?
2. Is the school's skirt rule fair? Does the story say?
3. Togo always says what he thinks. When is that a strength, and when could it hurt?
4. Yu changed after someone helped him. Has that happened to you?
5. The two dads are very different. What do they find they share?
