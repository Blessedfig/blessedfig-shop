**Premise.** Prison City is a walled, snow-bound city where all criminals are sent and from which no one leaves alive; robot guards called Yes Men patrol the wall (Comics Beat). Seven-year-old Chloe and her baby brother Locke were born there; their parents are gone, and a neighbor, Ross Sandberg, "the Colonel," secretly brings them food (Wikipedia; Jiro Reviews). When Sandberg's group breaks out through a gap in the wall during a snowstorm, Chloe follows with Locke on her back; a guard shoots her and Sandberg, and the cloth holding Locke tears, so he is left behind (ch. 1; Jiro Reviews; Comics Beat). Sandberg trains her for years. About eleven years later, a skilled and reluctant killer, Chloe goes back in to find her brother, now 11, using Sandberg's old contacts (Seven Seas; Wikipedia). With an informant's help she slips into a district even the guards avoid (vol. 2 blurb), passes the lair of a cannibal gang (vol. 3, reader review), and finds Locke in a district ruled by a fanatical religious cult whose leader, a young-looking man, calls himself God (vol. 3 blurb; Jiro Reviews). Guilty for having left him, she hides who she is and protects him from the shadows while Locke and other children plan their own escape (vol. 3 blurb). Reader reviews report the cult leader's death, the Colonel's return with his group, and a short, bittersweet ending with hope (Goodreads; MyAnimeList; to verify).

## Life message

**Love for family is worth any danger: a promise to protect someone is kept even when it costs you your innocence, and hope can survive in the cruelest place.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What will you give up to keep a promise to someone you love? Chloe leaves her brother behind at seven, by accident, and spends the rest of the story paying that debt.

**The answer the story gives.** Everything, including innocence. Chloe returns, finds Locke and protects him, but she becomes a killer to do it, and she cannot tell him who she is because of her guilt (vol. 3 blurb). The story honors her love and counts its cost.

**Characters as arguments.** Chloe argues for fidelity at any price. Sandberg argues that the strong should train and shelter the weak. The cult leader argues that people will follow anyone who claims to be God, if he can make them afraid; his fall answers him. The children, planning their own escape, argue that hope is something you organize together.

**Symbols and motifs.** The wall and the cage of the title: a 'soloist' who must act alone. Snow covering everything. The cloth that tore and dropped a baby. Faceless robot guards called Yes Men. Children's skulls at a gang's door, a sign of what the city does to the weak.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Promised Neverland and Attack on Titan (walled worlds and child escapes, named by the publisher), Judge Dredd (Otaquest via Wikipedia), and, for Jewish and Christian readers, the Exodus and the parable of the lost sheep.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Might makes right and authoritarian rule are the enemies. Communitarianism and classical virtues (courage, fidelity) are affirmed. The cult borrows the outward forms of religion and is condemned, which secular readers will read as exposing false authority and church readers as a warning against false prophets.

**Shifts across the series.** Volume 1 covers the childhood escape and Chloe's return as an adult. Volume 2 takes her deeper into the city with an informant. Volume 3 brings the cannibal gang, the cult district, the reunion and the children's escape, in a finish many readers found rushed.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Chloe hides who she is from Locke. Why? Would you?
2. Chloe kills to survive, and it haunts her. What does that say about her?
3. Why do people in the cult follow a man who calls himself God?
4. The children plan their own escape. What do they have that the adults lack?
5. Is Prison City only a place, or also a picture of something real?
