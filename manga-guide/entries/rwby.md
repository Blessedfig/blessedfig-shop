**Premise.** In the world of Remnant, Huntsmen and Huntresses train at academies to fight the Grimm, soulless monsters drawn to negative feelings (from general knowledge; to verify). Four girls form Team RWBY at Beacon Academy. Ruby Rose visits her mother's grave and, encouraged by Weiss, beats the bully Cardin in a sparring match after remembering her uncle Qrow saving her as a child (ch. 1-2). Weiss Schnee, heiress of a powerful Dust company, defies her cold father's secretary and fights a giant armored suit to win the right to study at Beacon instead of staying home (ch. 3-4). Blake Belladonna, a Faunus (people with animal traits), helps her mentor Adam rob a Schnee train for the militant White Fang, then leaves the group when Adam is willing to kill the crew (ch. 5-6). Yang Xiao Long brawls through a nightclub to question an information broker about her missing mother (ch. 7-9). In the original arc, Teams RWBY and JNPR fight a fused, eight-headed Grimm controlled by a possessing Grimm that the criminal Roman Torchwick let loose while scouting a tunnel for an attack on the city (ch. 10-15) (fan wiki; Bubbleblabber).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Why become a hero? Each of the four girls has a different answer, and the book gives each a chapter or two to find it.

**The answer the story gives.** Because someone has to protect people, and because together you are stronger. Ruby remembers being saved (ch. 2), Weiss wants to be her own person (ch. 4), Blake wants justice without killing (ch. 6), Yang wants to protect her sister and find her mother (ch. 7-9).

**Characters as arguments.** Adam argues that an oppressed people may kill for freedom; Blake answers by leaving (ch. 6). Weiss's father, through his secretary, argues that family duty means staying home; Weiss answers by fighting her way out (ch. 4). Cardin argues that strength means pushing others around; Ruby answers by winning fairly (ch. 2).

**Symbols and motifs.** Ruby's red cloak, put on after her victory (ch. 2); Summer Rose's grave; Yang's golden hair as a memory of her sister (ch. 8); the eight-headed Grimm, likened to an old story (ch. 13).

**Traditions it echoes.** Western fairy tales and the knightly ideal of protecting the weak; the Japanese school-battle story.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Teamwork and courage are affirmed; individual choice against family control is affirmed; nonviolence is honored in Blake's choice but the heroes remain warriors.

**Shifts across the series.** Ch. 1-9: one character story per trailer. Ch. 10-15: an original team mission with JNPR, Torchwick and Penny.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Ruby stands up for Jaune against a bully. What would you do in her place?
2. Weiss wants to make her own choices, but her father wants her home. How should families handle that?
3. Blake agrees with the White Fang's cause but not its methods. Can a good cause be pursued the wrong way?
4. Why does the team win only when all eight work together?
5. Yang is searching for her missing mother. Why might that matter so much to her?
