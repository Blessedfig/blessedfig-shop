**Premise.** Amane Mizuno is a high school girl whose sanpaku eyes make her look like she is glaring at everyone, though she is shy and tender-hearted. She has a crush on her seatmate, Mitsuhide Katou, a gentle boy who is not put off by her looks, and each chapter is a small step: a morning greeting, a conversation, a sports festival (vol. 1). Her friends Miyo Takiya and Yui Kawamura and her siblings, who share her stern face, cheer her on, and her classmates quietly root for the pair (ANN; All the Anime). Volume 2 centers on a school culture festival where Amane stars in a chaotic retelling of the Momotaro folk tale, with bonus chapters about Miyo's and Yui's own romances. In volume 3 the class trip begins and Amane is put in Katou's group; the publisher teases that something between them is about to change. The week-long Kyoto trip changes their relationship (vol. 4, likely a confession; synopsis not seen), and volume 5 follows the new couple commuting together, going on first dates and perhaps spending Christmas together, with extra short stories (publisher synopses via booksellers).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Do people see who you are, or only how you look? Amane's eyes make her seem angry; she is in fact the gentlest person in the room. The series asks whether a shy person can be known, and what it takes to let someone know you.

**The answer the story gives.** Kindness sees through appearances, and courage grows in small steps. Katou never treats Amane as scary, the class cheers her on, and she moves from a greeting to a conversation to a relationship. No grand lesson is stated; the story trusts small acts.

**Characters as arguments.** Amane argues that the heart matters more than the face. Katou argues that kindness reads people better than first impressions. Miyo and Yui argue for the value of friends who push you gently. Even the siblings, who share Amane's stern look, show that the trait runs in a warm family. The Manga Critic's objection is that, with no one against them, the story has little to argue.

**Symbols and motifs.** The eyes themselves: a sign read wrongly by strangers and rightly by friends. The seat beside Katou. The Momotaro play (vol. 2), in which the shy girl is cast as the hero. The class trip as the threshold where things change (vol. 3-4).

**Traditions it echoes.** The Japanese healing (iyashikei) school romance, and the old idea, common to many faiths and to folk wisdom, that the heart matters more than outward looks (1 Samuel 16:7 for Christian and Jewish readers; a well-known hadith for Muslim readers).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Community is quietly celebrated: the class is a support network. Expressing one's feelings is the goal, but within kindness and modesty, not rebellion. No worldview is attacked.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-2: crush, greetings, a sports festival and a culture festival. Volume 3: the class trip begins. Volume 4: the Kyoto trip changes the relationship (synopsis not seen). Volume 5: the new couple's everyday dates and extra stories.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Have you ever judged someone by their face and been wrong? What changed your mind?
