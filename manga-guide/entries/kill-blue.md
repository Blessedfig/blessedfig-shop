**Premise.** Juzo Ogami, 39, is the Z.O.O. syndicate's legendary hitman, a man raised by the organization from childhood who never went past elementary school, and who is divorced from Eri, Z.O.O.'s head scientist, and estranged from their daughter Kozue. On a job against Mitsuoka Pharmaceuticals he is stung by a genetically engineered wasp and wakes up 13 years old. His boss sends him to Rikka Private Middle School to check it is safe for the boss's daughter, with his junior Kotatsu Nekota acting as guardian. Ogami discovers he loves learning and joins the Home Economics Club, where he meets Noren Mitsuoka, the heiress of the company behind the wasp, who hates men after being shown off to adult suitors since childhood and dreams of running her uncle's ramen shop. A fake engagement with Noren (ch. 5-9) turns him into a target for teenage assassins from rival groups (Yugang, JARDIN) and for the school's elite "Unicorn" students, whom he beats in duels, sports and fights. The final arc (ch. 107-115) reveals that Noren is herself a de-aged adult scientist, Noren Hosho, co-inventor of the wasp; Ogami rescues her, they part, and he transfers to another school (Wikipedia; fan wiki; ANN).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What did Juzo Ogami miss by becoming a killer as a child, and can he get it back? A man who has never had a youth is handed one by accident. The series asks whether school, friends and play can remake someone whose whole life has been killing.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, partly. Ogami weeps over algebra and negative numbers (ANN), joins a club for cooking, and cooks for the daughter he neglected (ch. 5). By ch. 28-30 he sees that growing up again could give him a future not forced on him by the syndicate (ep. 12). He keeps his skills but uses them to protect children, often with rubber bullets.

**Characters as arguments.** Ogami argues that competence means little without people to care for. Noren argues against being treated as a prize: she has been shown to adult suitors since elementary school and wants to run her uncle's ramen shop. Tenma argues for winning fairly; he refuses to use hypnosis to get the girl he likes (ep. 7). Kazuma argues the opposite, using hypnosis to win Noren for his brother, and loses. Kotatsu shows loyal friendship; Kyosuke ("Mama") shows that kindness can come from people others look down on.

**Symbols and motifs.** The wasp, a sting that gives a second youth. The fountain pen, the Mitsuoka family's token of engagement (ch. 9). Ramen as comfort and freedom. The glasses through which Kotatsu watches: an adult who still needs a guide. The title's "blue," the color of youth.

**Traditions it echoes.** The "adult in a child's body" story (Big, Case Closed), the retired-assassin comedy (SPY x FAMILY, Sakamoto Days (from general knowledge; to verify)), and shonen tournament arcs. For some faith readers it echoes the hope of being made new; it stops short of naming repentance.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Community and individual dreams are both affirmed. Honor codes and duels are played for comedy. Rejuvenation science is shown as dangerous in greedy hands. Might-makes-right rivals are beaten but usually spared and befriended.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-4 (ch. 1-34): school arrival, fake engagement, the Rindou brothers, the beach trip. Vols. 5-8 (ch. 35-70): rivals, JARDIN round two, the Mitsuoka dungeon and the cavalry battle. Vols. 9-10 (ch. 71-88): the culture festival and "The End of the Legendary Hit Man." Vols. 11-13 (ch. 89-115): meeting Noren's father, engagement conditions, the Student Council battle, and the final arc revealing Noren's identity and Ogami's farewell.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why does Ogami cry with joy in class? What do you take for granted at school?
2. Ogami refuses to treat Noren as a romantic partner. Why does that matter to the story?
3. Noren's parents wanted to choose her future. Was she right to want a ramen shop instead?
4. Ogami never kills women, children or ordinary people. Does a code make a killer good?
5. If you could start middle school over, what would you do differently?
