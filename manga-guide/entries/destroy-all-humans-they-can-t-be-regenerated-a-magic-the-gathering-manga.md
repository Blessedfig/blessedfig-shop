**Premise.** Tokyo, 1998. Hajime Kano, a brash otaku at Tokiwagi Middle School, plays Magic: The Gathering with friends at every break and always finishes second to the school's perfect honor student, Emi Sawatari, who scolds his group for bringing cards to school. At a new game shop he finds Emi there, secretly one of the strongest players in the store; she hides the hobby to protect her reputation, and their rivalry moves to the card table (vol. 1; Wikipedia; No Flying No Tights). Summer brings a pool outing and their first official tournament (vol. 2); the Urza's Saga set release and a mysterious girl on a school trip (vol. 3); talk of Nostradamus's prophecy that the world will end in 1999, a promise to Hajime's best friend Kurushima to meet at Grand Prix Kyoto, and a quarrel at karaoke (vol. 4); Grand Prix Kyoto and an unexpected romance (vol. 5); a White Day gift (vol. 6); a French transfer student, Lou, and Hajime's confession to Emi (vol. 7); a date with Yakumo, who has feelings for Hajime, and the regional championship (vol. 8); and Emi playing her last tournament before quitting to keep a promise to her strict mother (vol. 9) (VIZ blurbs; Goodreads readers). Later Japanese volumes and the ending were not covered in sources opened.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Who are you when no one from school is watching? Emi is the perfect student in class and a fierce card player in a back-street game shop; Hajime is the loud nerd who never hides what he loves.

**The answer the story gives.** Being known for who you really are is worth the risk. Emi's secret becomes the ground of a friendship and then a romance (vols. 1-7), while the pressure of her mother's expectations shows what hiding costs (vol. 9).

**Characters as arguments.** Hajime argues for open, unashamed passion. Emi argues, at first, that a reputation must be protected. Her mother stands for study over play. Yakumo shows the pain of loving someone who loves someone else (vol. 8).

**Symbols and motifs.** The title card, Wrath of God (destroy everything, no regeneration), beside the 1999 prophecy that the world would end; dropped dice and touching hands under the table (vol. 1, No Flying No Tights); fireworks (vol. 2).

**Traditions it echoes.** The rivals-to-lovers school romance, the hobby-manga tradition (card-game manga such as Yu-Gi-Oh!, per Commander's Herald), and nostalgia stories about the 1990s.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Community and authenticity are affirmed; consumer collecting is enjoyed without critique; duty to parents is a real pull, not a strawman, as far as sources show.

**Shifts across the series.** English vols. 1-3: rivalry, summer and first tournament (1998). Vols. 4-6: the doomsday prophecy, Grand Prix Kyoto and White Day (winter 1998-99). Vols. 7-9: confession, new rivals, Yakumo's feelings and the regional championship (1999). Japanese vols. 10-18 and the ending were not covered.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why did Emi hide that she plays Magic? Is it ever right to hide a hobby?
2. What makes Hajime and Emi good rivals rather than enemies?
3. Should Emi keep her promise to her mother to quit? What would you do?
4. People in 1999 worried the world would end. How do fears like that spread?
5. Some readers say the art treats Emi unfairly for a girl her age. What do you think?
