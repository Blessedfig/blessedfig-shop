**Premise.** Yusuke Tagami, an ordinary young man who loves video games, hears a mysterious voice asking what power he wants; he asks to be able to create and customize anything, and wakes in the world of Caltsio (also spelled Calzio) inside the shrine of the 'Evil God of Calamity' (AniList; TV Tropes). In Caltsio, hair color marks which of four elemental gods grants a person's 'divine arts' (red fire, blue water, yellow earth, green wind); white-haired people without powers are a despised underclass, and dyeing your hair is illegal. Black hair, which Yusuke has, marks the prophesied apostle of the Evil God (TV Tropes; MyAnimeList reviews). Taken in by Zeshald, an old former instructor of the kingdom living near the powerless people's village, Yusuke uses his 'Customize Create' power to reshape land, build towers, upgrade weapons and change the taste of food, and after trouble in the capital he becomes a recognized divinist of the kingdom of Folcnac (Mechacomic reader review; Alpha Manga). He is drawn into palace intrigue as a fiancé candidate of the loud, extravagant Crown Princess Violet, grows close to the shy Sun, and is pulled into a neighboring country's turmoil, border battles and a war with the nation of Nossentes, which invented the 'Four Gods Faith' and its caste system to justify its rule, trains orphaned girls as spies and assassins, and stages false-flag attacks (TV Tropes; Mechacomic). Japanese readers report that the manga ends abruptly (Mechacomic).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What do you do when a whole world decides who you are before you speak? Yusuke arrives with black hair, the mark of the Evil God's apostle, in a world that ranks everyone by the color of their hair.

**The answer the story gives.** Build. Yusuke answers fear with useful work: towers, fields, defenses and gifts. He wins allies one by one, protects the people the world despises, and helps bring down a nation that invented a religion to keep everyone in their place.

**Characters as arguments.** Yusuke argues that a label is not a self. Zeshald argues for sheltering the stranger. Violet shows royal power that can be generous. Sun shows that the shy and low-ranked can fight back. Nossentes is the case against rule by invented faith and manipulation. The murderous antagonists show force consuming itself.

**Symbols and motifs.** Hair color as destiny and caste. The shrine of the Evil God where Yusuke wakes. The tower raised in an instant. The ring that gives Sun the strength to defend herself.

**Traditions it echoes.** The isekai power fantasy, political fantasy in the line of court-intrigue novels, and stories of a prophesied evil one who turns out to be good. For religious readers it raises questions about prophecy, prejudice and how faith can be misused by rulers.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Liberal and progressive equality are affirmed; authoritarian theocracy and caste are condemned; pragmatism wins. Polyamory and prostitution are simply part of the world rather than argued.

**Shifts across the series.** Early chapters set up Yusuke's arrival, Zeshald and the capital (ch. 1-20 or so; MyAnimeList and Mechacomic reviewers); the middle turns to palace intrigue, a neighboring country's turmoil and border and fort battles; later chapters bring the war with Nossentes. Japanese readers say the ending is abrupt (Mechacomic). No volume-to-chapter map was found.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Caltsio judges people by their hair color. What do people in our world get judged by before they speak?
2. Yusuke could use his power to rule. Why does he build and protect instead?
3. Nossentes invented a religion to keep power. How can you tell when beliefs are being used to control people?
4. Sun stops her attacker. What does the story say about courage and help from friends?
5. In Caltsio, slavery and prostitution are legal and "normal." Does normal mean right?
