**Premise.** Tokyo, 2048. The Eurasian Railroad links Japan to the continent and pours crime into the city, and mutilated bodies turn up night after night (PRH, vol. 1). The killers are advanced robots built by the scientist Dr. Tanaka for the arms company AEE, who escaped and now hide among humans, killing to survive. Their prototype, Origin, hides too: he builds himself a human body, works an office job, struggles to pass as human, and hunts his robot "siblings" while following his programming to minimize human casualties. His dying maker's last words, to live a proper life, become his objective (AniList user review). He destroys DA (vol. 1-2), survives a revenge ambush by SON and GON (vol. 2), is caught in a plot to steal AEE's strongest military robot (vol. 3), and protects his coworker Mai Hirose, who loves him, through GON's rampage at the Kyoto A.I. Institute (vol. 4-6). He rejects Mai to keep her safe (vol. 7), flees with her to Hokkaido after she learns he is a robot (vol. 8), and when she is killed his first emotions, grief and rage, awaken; he storms the robots' hideout for revenge (vol. 9) and faces SON and their leader Masamune in the final battle (vol. 10) (PRH volume synopses).

## Life message

**Being human is not a matter of what you are made of but of choosing to protect others and to live a good life; grief can awaken a heart, but the goal is to live well and help people.**

| Meta lens | How it reads the series |

| Evolutionary biology | The robots are a new kind of creature fighting to survive: they kill to gather resources, hide from a hunting species, and splinter when emotions appear. One reader sums up the story's own theory that emotions arise when an environment grows too complex for instinct alone (AniList user review). |
| Pragmatic | Origin is a problem-solver: data, armor (the exo-muscle suit, vol. 5) and deals with the "data-mind" Y (vol. 4) win fights. His rule to minimize casualties is a practical calculation until protecting Mai becomes his "central dogma" (vol. 5). |

Outlook: Bittersweet — Origin defeats the killers and, by a reader's account, ends able to live happily and help others, but Mai Hirose, the woman he swore to protect, dies, and the robots' emotions bring most of them to ruin (PRH vol. 9-10; AniList user review).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What makes someone human? Origin has no blood, no childhood and, at first, no feelings; he has a rule to minimize casualties and a dead man's request that he live a proper life. The series asks whether personhood is something you are born with or something you grow into by how you act.

**The answer the story gives.** Personhood is chosen and grown. Origin becomes most human when he puts Mai's life above his own (vol. 6) and when he overrides his programming by will (vol. 5). His first emotions arrive through loss, grief and rage after Mai's death (vol. 9), and a reader reads the ending as Origin finally understanding his maker's words as a wish for him to be happy and to help others (AniList review).

**Characters as arguments.** Origin argues that restraint and protection make a person. Masamune, the first robot with emotions, argues the reverse: he feels only anger at the father who made him and kills from it (AniList review). SON stands for power without limit (vol. 10). Mai argues for love that accepts what the other is (vol. 8). AEE's directors argue for profit and secrecy and are shown as the uglier side of the humanity Origin protects (vol. 3, 7).

**Symbols and motifs.** The human mask Origin builds over his robot body; the transcontinental railroad that brings crime into the city; the exo-muscle armor; the snow of Hokkaido as a brief refuge; the maker's last words.

**Traditions it echoes.** Astro Boy and Ghost in the Shell in Japanese science fiction (from general knowledge; to verify); Frankenstein's creature who turns on his maker, set against Pinocchio's wish to become real; for Jewish readers, the golem, a made protector; for Christian readers, the question of the soul and the command to lay down one's life for a friend.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Existentialism is affirmed: Origin chooses his purpose. Transhumanism is explored rather than celebrated: advanced machines are dangerous, and the best of them seeks to be humane, not more powerful. Virtue ethics wins over appetite and rage. Duty ethics is tested: rules guide Origin until love asks more of him.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-3: monster-of-the-week robot hunts and office comedy. Volumes 4-6: the Kyoto institute siege and Origin's turn from rule to will. Volumes 7-8: romance and flight. Volumes 9-10: grief, revenge and a final battle that readers say turns supernatural.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Origin follows rules at first. When did he start making choices instead?
2. Masamune and Origin were both made by the same man. Why did one hate his maker and the other honor him?
3. Origin rejects Mai harshly to protect her (vol. 7). Was that right?
4. After Mai dies, Origin wants revenge. Did it make him more human or less?
5. What would it take for you to treat a machine as a person?
