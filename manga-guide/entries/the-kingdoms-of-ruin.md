**Premise.** Witches once lived in harmony with humans and gave them magic. When the Redia Empire's science (the "Gear Expansion") surpasses magic, the empire declares witches obsolete and hunts them to near extinction. Adonis, a starving street child taken in and raised by the Ice Witch Chloe Morgan as apprentice and son, watches her stripped, humiliated before a crowd and shot by a firing squad after she begs the emperor to spare him (ch. 1; Wikipedia; fan wiki). Imprisoned for ten years, he plans "the most precise way to slaughter a nation." Freed by Doroka, a captive witch whose disgraced power is "love magic" that bends men's wills, he massacres the citizens of the imperial capital (ch. 2-5) (Wikipedia). The surviving witches on the moon try to use him to resurrect Chloe; he revives Doroka instead (ch. 8-10), then lures soldiers who kill the witches as he escapes (ch. 11-16). The real ruler behind the witch hunt is revealed: the queen, the witch Dorothea Grethe from yoruhashi's earlier series, who controlled the emperor with love magic, orders him to kill himself and takes the throne to find a way to revive her own dead lover (ch. 15-21). On the road through the wastelands Adonis and Doroka meet the kind outlaw Punch, the ruined sex-doll city of Mamuta (ch. 24-26) and the cyborg assassin Shirousagi, who gouges out Doroka's eyes (ch. 28-32); Adonis gives her one of his own. Later volumes visit the medical nation of Markpoint, where his desire for revenge begins to fade (v8), a sealed witch forest (v12) and the nation of Suzure (v13) (Penguin Random House). The final chapter is described as "about as happy of an ending" as the hero could have hoped for (Comicbook.com).

## Life message

**Persecution breeds hatred, and hatred can consume a person and a world; love, loyalty and companionship can slowly turn a heart away from revenge, though the story lets the wronged fight back.**

| Meta lens | How it reads the series |
|---|---|
| Evolutionary biology | A war between two kinds of people over who survives: humans exterminate the witches they no longer need. Kin-loyalty dominates: Adonis says Chloe's life is worth more than all humanity (anime ep. 7). The Mamuta episode imagines a society that trades its women for sex robots and dies out (ch. 24-26). |
| Pragmatic | Adonis wins by planning: ten years of simulated slaughter, faked deaths, tracking chips turned against enemies (Wikipedia). The story shows cold calculation working and costing him his allies (the moon witches, ch. 15-16). |
| Religious | No real-world religion. The prologue says God created humans and witches (anime ep. 1, Wikipedia); witches are born from a sacred mito tree, and Chloe's spirit appears to Adonis (ch. 32). Chapter titles borrow Christmas imagery ("Joy to the World," ch. 11; "A Holy Night for Avengers," v10). Faith readers will weigh magic shown as a good gift and witches as innocent victims. |
| Socio-cultural | A witch-hunt and genocide story read by MangaUpdates taggers as about racism and discrimination; science and empire are the oppressors, magic and nature the victims. It also satirizes technology turned to vice (sex dolls, cyborgs). |

Outlook: Bittersweet: genocide, massacres and the deaths of most of the witches fill the story, but Adonis's hatred fades, he and Doroka stay together and the ending is described as happy for him (Comicbook.com; Penguin Random House v8).
Hope source: L (love between two): Doroka's steady love and loyalty, more than victory, is what turns Adonis from revenge (anime ep. 7, 12; Penguin Random House v8); forgiveness (R) is the close second.

## Creator and publisher context

- yoruhashi (story and art) is a Japanese manga artist; The Kingdoms of Ruin is a sequel-in-universe to The Kingdom of Caliburn, and its villain Dorothea is that series' heroine (fan wiki; Wikipedia).
- On craft, yoruhashi has said the key to natural pacing is to make stories and draw pictures as if you were the reader, to keep dialogue minimal and to let each character's personality decide what they say; Doroka's personality was harder to develop than Adonis's (fan wiki, citing creator comments).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** If the world murders the one you love, do you owe it anything? Adonis's answer, at first, is no: humanity deserves ruin, and he spends ten years planning it.

**The answer the story gives.** Revenge is understandable and the persecutors deserve judgment, but hatred eats the one who carries it. Doroka warns that if he goes on, his heart will shatter and he will become like the humans who took Chloe (anime ep. 7). By the medical-nation arc his desire for revenge begins to fade (Penguin Random House, v8), and the ending is happy for him (Comicbook.com).

**Characters as arguments.** Chloe argues for sacrificial, non-retaliating love: she will not fight humans so that Adonis can find peace (fan wiki). Adonis argues for revenge. Doroka argues for reconciliation, is blinded for it, and briefly doubts (anime ep. 11), but still chooses love. Punch argues that revenge is a person's own choice, and kindness the better one. Goethe argues that the obsolete may be erased. Dorothea is Adonis's dark mirror: a grieving lover who will burn worlds to bring someone back.

**Symbols and motifs.** The quill Adonis writes his spells with. Ice (Chloe's magic, which Adonis uses to end the Shirousagi fight, ch. 32). Eyes: Doroka's taken, Adonis's given (fan wiki). The mito tree and memory as life itself (anime ep. 4). Christmas songs as chapter titles over scenes of slaughter.

**Traditions it echoes.** The European witch hunts, the revenge tragedy, the science-versus-magic fantasy, and the dark shōnen anti-hero (comparisons to Berserk-style revenge stories are common; from general knowledge; to verify). Faith readers may hear Romans 12:17-21 and the Dhammapada's "hatred is never appeased by hatred."

**How each lens is portrayed.** Pagan and occult imagery is the good side; science and empire are the bad side; might-makes-right is shown failing on both sides. Pacifism is honored in Chloe and Doroka but not lived by the hero until late. No real-world religion is portrayed.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-3 (ch. 1-16): Chloe's death, the capital massacre, the moon witches. Volumes 4-7 (ch. 17-36): the wastelands, Mamuta, Shirousagi and Doroka's blinding; the anime ends here (ch. 33). Volumes 8-11 (ch. 37-52): Markpoint and the fading of revenge; Redia's intelligence director seeks the truth about magic. Volumes 12-15 (ch. 53-66): the witch forest, Suzure and the final confrontation; Adonis is asked whether Chloe still lives in his heart (v14).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Chloe begged for Adonis's life instead of fighting. What did she want for him?
2. Adonis killed people who had nothing to do with Chloe's death. Does the story think that was right?
3. Doroka has a power to control men, and refuses to use it. Why?
4. What do Mamuta's people lose when they trade their families for machines?
5. What turned Adonis away from revenge, and could anything have done it sooner?
