**Premise.** Hime Shiraki, a first-year high-school student, keeps up a flawless, sweet "princess" image while caring mainly about herself and dreaming of marrying rich (Wikipedia). After she falls on and injures Mai Koshiba, Mai guilts her into covering shifts at Cafe Liebe, a concept cafe where the all-female staff play students of a fictional girls' boarding school, pairing up as devoted "Schwestern" (sisters) for the customers (ch. 1). Her partner Mitsuki is loving on stage and cold backstage; she turns out to be Mitsuki Yano, the elementary-school friend who exposed Hime's two-faced act and left her friendless (ch. 5-7). They reconcile (ch. 10), but Mitsuki's feelings are romantic. Hime's best friend Kanoko, who secretly loves Hime to the point of obsession, joins the cafe; the senpai Sumika, burned by an earlier workplace romance engineered by the manipulative Youko, tries to keep love out of the cafe (vols. 3-4). Mitsuki kisses Hime and Hime quits, then realizes she does not want a romance with Mitsuki (vols. 6-7). Later volumes follow Sumika and Kanoko's relationship, Nene's past with Youko, and Youko's manipulation of Kanoko (vols. 9-13) (Okazu; Anime UK News; PRH).

## Creator and publisher context

- Miman (story and art), a yuri artist since a 2005 Comitia one-shot; this is her first serialization (Wikipedia). The series paused from May 2024 for her health and resumed in August 2025 (Wikipedia, citing ANN).
- On-record intent (Wikipedia, citing Febri and Animate Times interviews): she combined a classic girls'-school setting with a concept cafe because the gap between a character's real and outward personality would make an interesting story; she wanted it clear when a character is lying; her favorite scene is Hime and Mitsuki's reconciliation in ch. 10, which made her cry while storyboarding.
- Anime director Hijiri Sanpei said the manga moves from comedy into drama after the first volume (Wikipedia).
- No statement on the vol. 12 arc was found in sources opened. **Status: Needs more info on the creator.**
- Publisher: Ichijinsha (Comic Yuri Hime). Kodansha USA publishes it in English (Rated T, later T+).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Is a performed self a lie, a protection or a job? Hime survives by being liked by everyone; Cafe Liebe pays her to perform sisterly devotion. The series asks what happens to people when their masks meet someone who already knows the face underneath.

**The answer the story gives.** Masks are understandable, even useful at work, but only truth repairs a relationship: Hime and Mitsuki reconcile only when each learns why the other acted as she did (ch. 6-10). The story is less sure that romance follows: Hime does not return Mitsuki's love (vol. 7), and the later arcs show love used as a weapon (Youko) or as a cage (Kanoko).

**Characters as arguments.** Hime argues for pleasing others to survive, and slowly against it. Mitsuki argues for sincerity even when it is clumsy. Kanoko argues for silent, possessive devotion and suffers for it. Sumika argues for protecting the group from romance, and then falls in love. Youko is the argument that sex and affection can be tools of control; reviewers say her motive is barely explained (Okazu staff).

**Symbols and motifs.** The cafe as a theater; the Schwestern pairing; the piano Hime gave up to protect Mitsuki (ch. 6); a barrette thrown back (vol. 7); dark panel borders in vol. 12.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Class S schoolgirl romance and Maria-sama ga Miteru-style sister system (from general knowledge; to verify), stories of unmasking from many cultures, and the moral teaching shared by many faiths that truthfulness is the root of trust.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Authenticity and honesty win. Performance is treated as both art and danger. Progressive readers welcome its lesbian characters; some critics argue it treats intimacy as harmful (Okazu staff) or is "conservative yuri" (AniList user review).

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-5 (ch. 1-26): workplace comedy, the Yano reveal, reconciliation and the Blume election. Vols. 6-8 (ch. 27-41): Mitsuki's kiss, Hime's departure and return. Vols. 9-13 (ch. 42-66): Sumika, Kanoko, Nene and Youko, ending in the hotel assault and its aftermath. Vol. 14 onward: not yet reviewed in sources opened.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Hime learned to make people like her by acting. When is acting kind, and when is it a lie?
2. Why did Mitsuki expose Hime when they were children, and why did Hime quit the piano?
3. Kanoko loves Hime but never says so. Is that loyalty or something else?
4. Several characters kiss someone without asking. How does the story treat that?
5. What warning signs did Kanoko miss with Youko? What should she have done?
