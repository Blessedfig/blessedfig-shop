**Premise.** Yuzuru Shino, a bored third-year and former star of his high school's archery club, hears the rumor about first-year Touji Seryou: Seryou goes out with whoever asks him first on a Monday morning and ends it by the end of the week. On a whim, Shino asks him out at the school gate as a half-joke, and Seryou says yes. Seryou turns out to be a thoughtful, attentive boyfriend: texts, shopping, a movie (Shino falls asleep on his shoulder), a park at sunset. As the days pass Shino's feelings grow, he doubts Seryou means any of it, and he dreads Sunday. Seryou's history complicates things: his first crush, a capricious young woman whose name is also read "Shino," is his older brother's girlfriend. The two boys share kisses, each assuming the other does not feel the same, and by the end love wins: Seryou stops accepting Monday confessions (Wikipedia; The Fandom Post; Anime UK News; Tropedia).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can love be safe? Seryou has found a way to be loved without risk: say yes to anyone, be a perfect boyfriend, and end it before it matters. Shino asks him out as a joke, which is its own way of avoiding risk. The story asks what happens when two careful people stop being careful.

**The answer the story gives.** Love has to be risked to be real. As the week passes, Shino's feelings grow and he fears Sunday; Seryou, kind to everyone, has to decide whether Shino is different. Kisses come before either is sure of the other (The Fandom Post). The ending answers that commitment is worth the fear: Seryou stops accepting Monday confessions.

**Characters as arguments.** Seryou argues for kindness without commitment and learns its cost: Anime UK News notes that his blunt answer about his past girlfriends angers Shino. Shino argues for honesty, and his bluntness is what reaches Seryou (AVO Magazine). The other Shino, the brother's girlfriend who played with both brothers' feelings, is the reason Seryou learned to keep love on a timer.

**Symbols and motifs.** The calendar itself (each chapter a day); the archery club, where focus and release matter; the name "Shino" shared by Seryou's first hurt and his new love; Monday morning at the school gate.

**Traditions it echoes.** The BL school romance (Craft magazine, *Kono BL ga Yabai!*), the "pretend relationship that turns real" plot (MangaUpdates: Pretend Lovers), and the wider idea, shared by many faiths and philosophies, that love means binding yourself to one person rather than sampling many.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism and progressivism are affirmed quietly: no one in sources objects to two boys dating. Virtue ethics shows in the reward for honesty and constancy. Hedonism, in the form of dating for its own sake, is gently critiqued.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1 (Monday to Thursday): the joke, the dates, the first doubts. Vol. 2 (Friday to Sunday): jealousy over Seryou's past, the approaching end of the week, and the resolution, followed by the bonus story "Goodbye for Now" (Comic Book Bin).
