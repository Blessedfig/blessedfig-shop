**Premise.** Takuma Shinozaki, a timid new office worker, is mentored by his senior colleague (senpai) Shiori Katase, a very short, kind young woman who likes to praise and coddle him. He fell in love with her at first sight and daydreams about her so openly that his coworkers notice; she is slow to notice, then slowly falls for him too. Their manager Chihiro Akina enjoys imagining his staff in romantic scenarios, Shinozaki's otaku childhood friend Chinatsu Hayakawa quietly likes Akina, and Shinozaki's older sister Yutaka wants him to marry Katase (Wikipedia). In volume 8 both resolve to confess over a summer of sea trips and fireworks; volume 9 opens their dating arc as a couple (Dengeki Online). All main characters are adults.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What does it take for two shy people to say what they feel, when the safest thing is to stay "just coworkers"?

**The answer the story gives.** Kindness first, then courage. Katase's care makes Shinozaki feel at home; his devotion slowly makes her notice him. By volume 8 both decide to confess, and volume 9 begins their life as a couple (Dengeki Online). The answer is gentle: patience and encouragement win.

**Characters as arguments.** Katase argues that praise and care bring out the best in people. Shinozaki argues for faithful, if bashful, love. Akina, the matchmaking manager, and Yutaka, the sister who wants them married, show a community that wants people paired and happy. Hayakawa's unspoken crush on Akina mirrors the main couple.

**Symbols and motifs.** Height: Katase's smallness against her senior role. The office as a second home. Seasons: the summer sea and fireworks of volume 8 as the turning point.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Japanese "senpai" romantic comedy (My Senpai Is Annoying is a close neighbor, another office senior-junior romance; from general knowledge; to verify) and the web-manga format of short, sweet chapters.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communal warmth and the senior-junior bond are affirmed; self-expression is the small hurdle each lead must clear. Critics read the heroine through a feminist lens and find her drawn to a male ideal (Anime Feminist; ANN), a reading the story itself does not take up.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-3 (ch. 1-84, the anime's range) are mentoring gags and mutual crushes; volumes 4-7 deepen the unspoken romance; volume 8 is the summer of resolving to confess; volume 9 onward is the dating arc, toward a planned end in volume 14 (Wikipedia).

**Discussion questions.**
1. What does Katase do that makes her a good senior? Who does that for you?
2. Why do Shinozaki and Katase wait so long to say how they feel?
3. The art often focuses on Katase's body. How does that affect how you see her as a person?
4. What is the difference between teasing that builds someone up and teasing that hurts?
