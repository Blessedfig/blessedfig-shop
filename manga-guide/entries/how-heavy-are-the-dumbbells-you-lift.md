**Premise.** Hibiki Sakura, a second-year at Koyo Women's Academy, loves to eat and has gained weight; after her friend Ayaka points it out, she visits the Silverman Gym, where she finds her school's student-council president, Akemi Soryuin, signing up. Hibiki joins only after meeting the trainer Naruzo Machio, a polite, baby-faced young man whose muscles tear his clothes when he flexes, and she falls for him on sight (Wikipedia; Seven Seas blurb). Each chapter teaches a real exercise or nutrition point (bench press, squats, planks, protein, sleep, injury prevention), often with a reference page (Family Friendly Gaming). The cast grows: Ayaka, a boxer from a boxing family; Zina (Gina) Boyd, a sambo-practicing Russian who becomes Hibiki's arm-wrestling rival and moves into her house; Satomi Tachibana, a history teacher who is secretly a famous cosplayer; and other teachers and trainers, some related to Kengan Ashura characters (Wikipedia). Hibiki turns out to have a talent for combat sports. Later volumes (from blurbs) add a mountain trip (v6), Hibiki's growing feelings for Machio and a decision about her future (v7), a Kyoto school trip (v8-9), a fat-loss contest (v10), a school strength-training club (v12), a turf war with tough women (v13), time travel to a Sengoku battlefield to retrain Imagawa Yoshimoto's soldiers (v14, v20), body sculpting with yoga and pole dancing (v15), a VR fantasy world (v16), a women's amateur combat tournament (v17) and an arm-wrestling rematch (v19) (PRH blurbs).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can a person change her habits, and why would she want to? Hibiki starts for a vain reason (a waistline) and a romantic one (a handsome trainer), and the series asks whether the effort can become her own.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, through small, informed, repeated effort. Hibiki gains strength before she loses weight (Gaffney), finds a talent she did not know she had (combat sports, Wikipedia), and by v7 faces a decision about her future (PRH blurb). The story treats knowledge and practice, not willpower alone, as the key.

**Characters as arguments.** Machio is the case for patient, expert, kind teaching. Hibiki is appetite learning temperance. Akemi is admiration of strength turned into obsession, played for laughs. Zina is rivalry as motivation. Satomi shows a hidden life that needs a healthy body to sustain it.

**Symbols and motifs.** Machio's exploding shirts (strength hidden under ordinary clothes); the scale and the calorie count; each chapter's exercise name; Hibiki's snacks.

**Traditions it echoes.** The edutainment manga (learning through a story), the sports-training arc and the fan-service comedy. Faith readers may hear echoes of teaching on the body as a gift or temple; Buddhist and Hindu readers, of the middle way and moderation in food.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Pragmatism and virtue ethics win: method and habit work. Hedonism (Hibiki's appetite) is gently corrected rather than condemned. Feminist critics turn their lens on the series itself: strong women, framed for a male gaze (Anime Feminist; Gaffney calls it aimed at male readers).

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-6 (ch. 1-56) set up the gym and the cast and are what the anime adapts. Vols. 7-13 add school events, a fat-loss contest and turf fights. From v14 the series adds fantasy and time-travel arcs (a Sengoku battlefield, a VR world) and combat tournaments, while keeping one exercise per chapter.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Hibiki joins the gym because of a handsome trainer. Is that a good reason to start? What keeps her going?
2. What is the difference between being healthy and being thin? Does the series know the difference?
3. Machio never shames Hibiki. What makes him a good coach?
4. The series often shows the girls in revealing clothes. Why do you think the artist does this, and how does Hibiki react?
5. Which exercise from the series would you try, and how would you check the advice is safe?
