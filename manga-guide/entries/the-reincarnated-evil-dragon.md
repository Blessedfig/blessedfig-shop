**Premise.** A luckless salaryman whose catchphrase is "I wish I'd win the lottery" is killed in a car crash caused by a couple kissing while driving and is reborn as the strongest "evil dragon" of a fantasy world. He lives happily in a forest with slimes who adore him until the Demon King's subordinates slaughter them; he takes swift revenge, then leaves the forest so no one else dies on his account (vol. 1 blurb; ch. 1-2). Taking a human form, Ja becomes an adventurer, buys the half-elf girl Irene at a slave auction and helps her fight the Cyclops who wronged her (v1-2), joins a lord's daughter, Lucille, to slay a blue dragon (v2), and becomes the object of a romantic rivalry between Lucille, her bodyguard Rastera and Irene (v3). Later volumes bring a murder ring led by an inn maid (v4), an arrogant angel, Guriel, who with "Krishna," one of ten gods who once ruled the world, plots to revive his father "Zeus" through a Divine Dragon cult (v5-6), the hero Chloe (Kuroe) joining the party (v6), and a final war against the evil gods (v7) (Japanese publisher blurbs; MyAnimeList).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What would a nobody do with unlimited power? A salaryman who only ever hoped to win the lottery becomes the strongest creature in a world, and the series asks whether such power makes him a monster, a ruler or a protector.

**The answer the story gives.** A protector, on his own terms. The publisher calls Ja kind to the weak and easily moved to tears. When his slimes are slaughtered he takes revenge, then leaves the forest so no one else will die because of him (ch. 1-2). Each later arc is a defense: Irene against the Cyclops, people against a blue dragon, a town against a murder ring, humanity against gods who want to rule it again.

**Characters as arguments.** Ja argues that strength should serve those you love. Irene, bought at auction, argues for loyalty earned by kindness, though the story does not ask whether loyalty can be free while she is owned. The Demon King's minions and the murder ring argue for cruelty and lose. Guriel and the gods argue that the strong (themselves) have a right to rule the weak; Ja, the strongest of all, refuses that claim for himself and for them.

**Symbols and motifs.** The "evil" dragon who is not evil: a label versus a heart. The slimes, the weakest monsters, as his first family. The human form he takes to live among people.

**Traditions it echoes.** The dragon-guardian of East Asian lore, the revenge tale, and the 2010s web-novel isekai with its slave-companion and harem conventions. Its divine villains borrow names from Greek and Hindu religion (Zeus, Krishna), which some readers may find disrespectful.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Strength settles everything, but the story sides with strength used for others over strength used to dominate. Pleasure (money, admiring women) is the hero's reward. Religion appears only as a source of tyranny.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-3 (about ch. 1-18) are light adventure: the slimes, Irene, the blue dragon and the romantic rivalry. Reviewers say the tone darkens around ch. 15-16. Volumes 4-7 (about ch. 19-46) become a war against a murder ring, a cult and the gods, ending in a total war that one reviewer says closes "a bit suddenly."

**Discussion questions.**
1. Ja is called an "evil" dragon but grieves for slimes. What makes someone good or evil?
2. Ja buys Irene at a slave auction. Is he her rescuer, her owner or both? What would make it right?
3. Ja takes revenge for his slimes. Did it bring them back or make anyone safer?
4. The villains believe the strong should rule. How is Ja different, since he is the strongest of all?
5. Why might a story make gods its villains? How would your family's faith see that?
