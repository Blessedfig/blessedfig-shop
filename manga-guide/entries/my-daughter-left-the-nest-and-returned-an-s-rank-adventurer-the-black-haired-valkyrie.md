**Premise.** Belgrieve, a low-ranked adventurer, loses his right leg to a monster early in his career and goes home to the village of Turnera. Eight years later he finds a baby abandoned in the forest and raises her as his daughter, Angeline, teaching her the sword. At 12 she leaves for the city of Orphen; five years later she is the youngest living S-Rank adventurer, the "Black-Haired Valkyrie," with her party members Anessa (archer) and Miriam (a mage who hides her beast-folk ears). All she wants is to go home, but monster attacks, a revived demon lord (Ba'al), a cult of the ancient sorcerer Solomon, scheming nobles and a corrupt church keep cancelling her visits. When she does come home, Belgrieve is drawn back into the world: he duels, protects the Bordeaux sisters, helps a would-be avenger give up revenge, and begins to look for the party he lost long ago (Wikipedia, including anime episode summaries; AniList; J-Novel Club).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What makes a parent, and what makes strength? Belgrieve is a failed adventurer with one leg who never rose above a low rank; his daughter is the most famous adventurer of her generation. Yet she counts him the strongest man alive.

**The answer the story gives.** Strength is skill, humility and faithfulness, and a parent is the one who stays. Belgrieve beats his daughter in one move, wins duels by experience and patience, and turns enemies into friends with mercy (anime eps. 4, 6, 9). Angeline's fame matters to her mainly as a way home.

**Characters as arguments.** Belgrieve argues for the quiet virtues. Angeline argues for loyalty and duty, sometimes to the point of tantrums. Helvetica learns that leadership requires hard choices. Charlotte shows that revenge can be given up when someone offers a better path. Francois and Malta are rank and greed without merit. Kasim is despair turned back by an old friendship.

**Symbols and motifs.** The prosthetic leg; the letters Angeline sends home; the village of Turnera; the "Red Ogre" title Belgrieve does not want; the demon lords who appear as lost, miserable children seeking their master.

**Traditions it echoes.** The prodigal and the waiting father, the retired master swordsman, the found-family adventure; and the Goetic demons of Western occult lore (Solomon, Samigina) as villains.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarian, traditional and virtue-ethics lenses are affirmed. Occultism is critiqued. A Catholic-coded church is critiqued at the top but its charity is honored in a nun. Might makes right loses to character and skill.

**Shifts across the series.** Early volumes: cancelled vacations, the Ba'al fight and the homecoming. Middle volumes: the Bordeaux conflict, Charlotte and the cults, the elves. The volumes adapted at the end of the anime turn to Belgrieve's search for his old party (Kasim, Percival, Satie) (Wikipedia, anime summaries). Manga volume placement to verify.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why does Angeline think her father is the strongest, even though he lost his leg and never reached a high rank?
2. Belgrieve forbids Angeline to keep adventuring until she beats him. Was that fair?
3. Charlotte wanted revenge on the people who killed her father. What changed her?
4. The church in the story has a kind nun and a corrupt Pope. What does the story say about faith and its leaders?
5. Who in your life is a Belgrieve?
