**Premise.** Isaku Senagaki lost her parents in a car accident at five and was raised by her grandfather Tasuke, boss of the Senagaki yakuza clan, with the clan's young lieutenant Keiya Uto as her caretaker. Shunned at school because of her family, she enrolls in a distant high school (she is 15, turning 16) to have a normal life and to get over her crush on Keiya. Keiya, 26, fakes his age and enrolls with her (the grandfather arranged a back-door admission; only the principal knows) to keep "bad bugs" away. Classmates, two loyal friends, Isaku's delinquent relative Mikio Tanuki and Keiya's former partner Kaori, a hostess at a clan-run club, complicate things; rival gangsters and kidnappers threaten Isaku. Isaku confesses, Keiya admits he loves her, and by the end of the anime (ch. 26) they are a couple; Keiya's back tattoo is revealed as a vow to protect her for life. Volume 8 (Kodansha) has them planning their first birthday together as a couple when a rival gangster with a grudge against Keiya appears (Japanese Wikipedia; anime summaries; Kodansha).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can a girl raised in a world that frightens everyone else have an ordinary life and an ordinary love? Isaku wants friends, a school where no one knows her family, and a crush she can get over. The story answers by giving her friends and then giving her the very crush she meant to leave behind.

**The answer the story gives.** Devoted love is safety. Keiya's protection is played first as comedy (he blocks karaoke and beach trips, ANN) and then as the proof of his love (his tattoo vow, ep. 13). When he moves too fast physically, she says stop and he stops (ep. 12), which the story offers as his redemption.

**Characters as arguments.** Isaku argues for choosing your own path. Keiya argues that love means guarding. The grandfather argues for indulgence: he arranges Keiya's enrollment and approves the romance. Isaku's friends argue for ordinary teenage life; Mikio, the delinquent relative abandoned by his parents, is the mirror of what Isaku might have become without love.

**Symbols and motifs.** The school uniform on a 26-year-old; the guard dog; the tattoo on Keiya's back that Isaku is invited to cut (ep. 13); cigarettes as the mark of the adult world Isaku is crossing into.

**Traditions it echoes.** The shojo guardian romance (Daddy Long Legs, many teacher-student manga), the ninkyo yakuza film of honorable gangsters, and the bodyguard romance. Readers from many traditions will connect it to safeguarding rules about adults in authority over young people.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Honor codes and communal loyalty are warm and admired; individual choice belongs to Isaku; authority (the boss, Keiya) is benevolent. No lens is argued against; the main tension is between protection and freedom.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-3 (about ch. 1-15) are school comedy with Keiya's overprotection; volumes 4-5 (about ch. 16-26) bring danger, confession and the couple; volumes 6-9 continue them as a couple with new gang threats (Kodansha vol. 8). No source describes how volumes 6-9 handle the physical relationship.
