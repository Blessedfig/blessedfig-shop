**Premise.** At Kaimei High School, the three-member Sket-dan club takes any request from students or teachers, free of charge. Its leader Bossun (Yusuke Fujisaki) is goofy and socially awkward but can deduce anything when he puts on his goggles and concentrates; Himeko (Hime Onizuka), the muscle, a former Osaka delinquent called "Onihime," fights with a field hockey stick; Switch (Kazuyoshi Usui), an otaku and data gatherer, speaks only through speech software on his laptop. Most chapters are stand-alone comedy and parody: requests from a would-be samurai, an occult-loving girl, a voice-actress ex-delinquent (Momoka), a bickering student council led by Tsubaki. Between them come serious arcs: Himeko's betrayal by a friend and isolation (v7), Switch's younger brother murdered by a stalker and his silence afterward (ch. 42-44), Bossun's discovery that he is adopted and that Tsubaki is his twin (ch. 82-90), how the club was founded (ch. 241-251), and a last request to help a transgender boy who has shut himself away (ch. 280-285). At graduation Himeko and Bossun confess their feelings and Bossun leaves to help people around the world (ch. 286-288; Wikipedia; fan wiki).

## Life message

**Helping others with whatever they need, for nothing in return, is how you carry the love of those who came before you; friends pull each other out of grief and isolation, and no one should be left alone.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What do we owe the people around us? The Sket-dan's answer is: whatever they need. A boy who was told in his dead father's letters to help others, a girl betrayed by her only friend, and a boy whose words led to his brother's death form a club that takes any request.

**The answer the story gives.** Help is not a transaction. Bossun first rejects his parents' creed and declares he will live only for himself, then finds he cannot watch someone be bullied without stepping in (ch. 82-86). Each member was rescued by another, and each rescue becomes a debt repaid forward. The last image is Bossun abroad, still helping strangers (ch. 288).

**Characters as arguments.** Bossun argues for intuition and heart; Switch for data and reason; Himeko for courage and loyalty. Tsubaki and the student council argue for rules, and the series lets them be right sometimes before they become friends; Tsubaki turns out to be Bossun's twin (ch. 87-90). Momoka argues that a delinquent can remake herself. The stalker of the Switch Off arc is the one figure who wants to possess rather than help, and the story's only killer.

**Symbols and motifs.** Bossun's goggles (concentration); Himeko's named hockey sticks; Switch's synthesized voice, built by his dead brother, and the day he speaks with his own (ch. 287-288); the club room as a refuge; lollipops and gross candy as running gags.

**Traditions it echoes.** The school-club comedy, the detective trio, Gintama's mix of gag and drama, and the ethic of service found across religious and secular traditions: works of mercy, seva, chesed, the Good Samaritan.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarian belonging and classical virtues are affirmed; postmodern parody is the comic texture; the occult is a friendly joke; secular reason (Switch) and warm feeling (Bossun) are partners. The final arc adds acceptance of a transgender boy as part of the same ethic of not leaving anyone alone.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-4 (ch. 1-35) set up the cast and rivals; v5 (ch. 42-44) brings the first heavy backstory; v7-10 (ch. 59-90) give Himeko's and Bossun's origins; the middle volumes are mostly comedy with the council's handover (ch. 147-153); v27-28 (ch. 241-251) tell how the club was founded; v32 (ch. 280-288) closes with the last request and graduation.

**Discussion questions.**
1. The Sket-dan help anyone for free. Why do they do it?
2. Switch blamed himself for his brother's death. What helped him start speaking again?
3. Bossun learns he was adopted. What makes someone your family?
4. Is there someone at your school who might need a Sket-dan?
5. Which kind of helper are you: a Bossun, a Himeko or a Switch?
