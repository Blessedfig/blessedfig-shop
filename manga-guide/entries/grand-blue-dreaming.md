**Premise.** Iori Kitahara moves to a seaside town on the Izu Peninsula to start university, living above his uncle's diving shop, Grand Blue. He expects a normal campus life; instead he is swept into the diving club Peek a Boo, a crowd of muscular seniors (Tokita and Kotobuki) who spend more time drinking, partying and stripping than diving. He cannot swim, but his cousins Chisa (a classmate who lives for diving) and Nanaka (a dive instructor) slowly show him the wonder of the sea. His rival and best friend Kohei, an anime-obsessed otaku, and Aina, a shy student the boys defend from a bullying tennis-club captain (ch. 6-12), join him. Episodes cycle between drinking-party chaos, campus contests and real diving trips: Okinawa (ch. 16-21), an uninhabited island (ch. 41-45), Palau, where Chisa's estranged mother works (ch. 49-57), and a second Okinawa trip that Iori takes alone with Chisa (ch. 62-65). A fake relationship that Chisa invents to fend off suitors slowly turns real in later chapters (ch. 74-76 and on), though sources do not confirm that they become a couple (Wikipedia; TV Tropes; Moegirlpedia).

## Life message

**A good life is shared with loyal friends who laugh, drink and dive together; embarrassment passes, the sea is worth the effort, and you stand by your friends and family when it counts.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What makes young adulthood good: the freedom to be ridiculous with friends, or the discipline to master something real? Iori arrives wanting an ordinary college life and gets neither ordinary nor disciplined; he gets a club that drinks and strips, and also the sea.

**The answer the story gives.** Both, but mostly the friends. The series' heart is that embarrassment passes and loyalty lasts. The diving scenes (Iori's first open-water dives, Okinawa, Palau) are where the story turns sincere: the sea is beautiful, dangerous and worth learning properly (Screenwise; ANN, ep. 12). The drinking is never weighed against that; it is simply part of the package.

**Characters as arguments.** Iori argues that you can be swept along and still find your own love. Chisa argues for quiet devotion to a craft, and her slow thaw shows that devotion needs people too. Tokita and Kotobuki argue that you can be ridiculous and still be responsible when lives depend on it. Aina argues for a wholesome college life and keeps losing the argument for laughs. Kudo, the tennis captain, is the case against using people: he is humiliated (ch. 6-12). Shiori argues that family duty can be dodged by cleverness, until Iori protects her and she gives up her scheme (ch. 47-48).

**Symbols and motifs.** The sea (depth, calm, the buddy line); clothes coming off (the stripping of pretense, played as farce); the drink in the glass that is never really "oolong tea"; the diving license as a rite of passage.

**Traditions it echoes.** The college gag comedy, the Japanese "circle" story and the diving manga (Anime UK News hoped it would lean toward Amanchu!). Its loyal friends who insult each other echo old buddy comedies. For faith readers it raises the classic question of feast versus excess.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Hedonism is celebrated without cost; communitarian belonging is the deepest good. Virtue ethics shows up only in loyalty and courage; temperance is the missing virtue. Feminist and progressive readers would note the consent jokes; secular readers find little to object to beyond the risk.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-5 (ch. 1-21): joining the club, campus contests, Okinawa and the license. Volumes 6-11 (ch. 22-45): university life, Shiori, an uninhabited island. Volumes 12-16 (ch. 46-65): Palau and Chisa's mother, then a second Okinawa trip with Chisa alone. Volumes 17-27 (ch. 66-111): Sakurako's confession, hypnosis, a wedding, a campus pageant and the slow turn of the fake couple toward a real one.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Iori is pushed to drink on his first night. What could he have said? What would real friends have done?
2. The seniors joke constantly but take diving safety seriously. Why do they draw the line there?
3. What does the sea give Iori that the parties do not?
4. Is it ever funny when someone is made to do something they said no to?
5. Iori protects his sister even though she tricked him. What does that say about family?
