**Premise.** Iori Kitahara finishes high school at an all-boys' school. He moves to a seaside town on the Izu Peninsula for college. He lives above his uncle's dive shop, Grand Blue, with his cousins Chisa and Nanaka Kotegawa. He hopes for a glamorous new college life. Instead he is pulled into the university diving club, Peek-a-Boo. Its members spend more time drinking, partying and stripping naked than diving. Iori cannot swim at first. Over time he learns to dive and comes to love the ocean, while the club's binge-drinking comedy continues around him (Wikipedia; Pixiv Encyclopedia; Kodansha).

## Life message

**A good life is lived with loyal friends who laugh together, push each other past their comfort zones and share the sea; youth is for fun, and embarrassment fades.**

| Meta lens | How it reads the series |

| Evolutionary biology | Male coalition building through shared ordeals: drinking contests, nudity and hazing work as bonding rituals that sort insiders from outsiders. Status contests (pageants, rival clubs, mixers) and mate competition drive many plots. Romance is slow and mostly teased: Chisa and Iori fake-date, and a later reveal that they are not related by blood (ch. 29) opens the way to a possible pairing. No fighting beyond slapstick; no death. |
| Pragmatic | The club runs on costly gear, so events like the pageant and a tennis match with a rival club raise money (TV Tropes summary of the fan debate). Diving skills are taught with care: mask clearing and the buddy system (ScreenWise). Drinking has comic costs (vomiting, memory loss, hangovers) but no lasting ones. | Religious | Religion is almost absent. Read from faith traditions, the central issue is drunkenness and pressure to drink shown as harmless fun, plus frequent male nudity and crude sexual gags, set beside real loyalty and friendship. |
| Socio-cultural | Reflects Japanese university club culture: senpai (seniors) who pressure kohai (juniors) to drink, nomikai (drinking parties) and drinking games. In Japan, forced drinking at student parties is a recognized problem called "aruhara" (alcohol harassment); Business Insider Japan (2022) reports that people in their 20s are the largest group taken to hospital for acute alcohol poisoning. That article does not mention this series. Gender roles are comic: men strip and fight, women are often the "sane" characters who get embarrassed. |

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What is college for? Iori arrives expecting a stylish new life, with a girlfriend and a polished image. The diving club offers a different answer: shared foolishness, loyalty and the sea.

**The answer the story gives.** The story sides with the club. Each attempt to look cool, date well or stay sober ends in embarrassment, while the club's drinking games and pranks become the place where friendships form. The diving chapters add a second answer. In the water, the same characters are careful, skilled and calm. Tokita "takes diving dangers seriously" (Wikipedia). The ocean is the one thing nobody mocks. The story's view seems to be that youth should be loud on land and respectful at sea.

**Characters as arguments.** Iori is the argument that you become who you spend time with. He begins as a victim of the club's pressure and becomes one of its leaders in pranks. Kohei, the anime fan, argues for a fantasy life over real relationships, and is mocked and loved for it. Tokita and Kotobuki argue for brotherhood through excess. Chisa argues for restraint and seriousness. She is admired as a diver, but she is also the most frequent target of embarrassment, which critics such as Anime Feminist read as punishing the sober voice. Aina argues that dropping a defensive mask brings freedom, though her freedom is often shown through drinking. Azusa argues for comfort with one's body and desires and has become a fan favorite (TV Tropes).

**Symbols and motifs.** The sea stands for depth and wonder, set against the shallow party life on land. "Oolong tea" and "water" that turn out to be liquor stand for the trap of trusting the group. Nakedness stands for losing pretense, played as farce. The lighter test, used to check whether a drink is alcohol, is a small sign of distrust that becomes a running joke.

**Traditions it echoes.** College comedies such as Animal House, which Otaku USA cites, and American Pie, which GamesRadar cites. Earlier Japanese gag romance (Love Hina, per ANN's Rebecca Silverman). Japanese campus club culture. Inoue's own Baka and Test, with its school comedy of lovable idiots.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Hedonism is treated as the normal good life of youth and is rarely questioned. Community is affirmed: friends are always there. Expressive individualism appears in Aina's growth and in Azusa's ease with herself. Temperance, the classical virtue, is the main thing the comedy reverses. Feminist and safety critics read the jokes as relying on coercion. Supporters say the diving is taken seriously and that the club's events serve a real purpose (TV Tropes summary of the fan debate).

**Shifts across the series.** Volume 1 is almost all drinking and nudity, with no diving (Otaku USA). From the pageant and Okinawa arcs (ch. 5-21), diving and romance grow. Later arcs add the Palau trip and the Kotegawa sisters' mother (ch. 49.5-57), and the slow Chisa-Iori romance, from the reveal that they are not related by blood (ch. 29) to Chisa refusing to end their fake relationship (vol. 22). The drinking jokes continue throughout.

**Discussion questions.**
1. The club often makes people drink when they say no. Is it still funny? What would change if it happened to you?
2. Why do you think the characters who stay sober are often the ones embarrassed?
3. In the manga the freshmen are about 18. Why might the anime have aged them up to 20?
4. The friends are loyal and help each other (for example, trying to help Aina). Can a group be both loyal and pushy? What does your family's tradition say about friendship and pressure?
5. The characters are careful underwater and reckless on land. What does the sea teach them that the parties do not?
