**Premise.** A giant solar flare, the "Mercy Pulse," drained almost all color from the Earth, and humanity mutated into strange new forms in a slow decline. The few scraps of color left hold great power. Avidia, a gun-carrying, lone-wolf investigator and scientist of "chromadynamics" who works outside the law, hunts them while protecting Chie, a girl who looks like the humans of old (vols. 1-2). Against him stands the Iodopsy Order, a cult (教団, "religious order") that uses color for power and control and, per the Japanese blurb, seeks to unify the world (Comic Border); its leader appears to be the "Daisoujou-sama" (大僧正, a title like "archbishop") on AniList's character list. An Order assassin drags bystanders into the fight (vol. 3); Avidia survives a dangerous experiment to gain power as the Order works to awaken its "Divine Chroma" gods (vol. 4). A red, eldritch god is born through bloody sacrifice from the death of Avidia's best friend (vol. 5). Chie learns she is an android and searches the deserted city of Chuudoh for her maker while the Order makes a blue god (vol. 6). In the final volume the Order tries to set off a second Mercy Pulse, and the wounded Avidia wages all-out war to rescue Chie (vol. 7; Seven Seas blurbs).

## Life message

**Knowledge and loyal protection of the vulnerable, not fanatical faith that demands sacrifice, are the way to restore what a broken world has lost.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** In a world that has lost its color, what is worth saving, and who gets to decide how to save it?

**The answer the story gives.** Avidia studies the remnants of color and protects one girl; the Order wants to restore color through sacrifice and new gods. The story sides with knowledge and loyalty against fanatic power (Seven Seas blurbs).

**Characters as arguments.** Avidia argues for inquiry and personal loyalty; the Order and its Daisoujou argue that the world's restoration justifies any sacrifice; Chie, an android with the old human face, asks what it means to be human.

**Symbols and motifs.** Color as beauty and life; grey as decline; the two-tone printing that lets neon break through; gods of red and blue born from death; the second Mercy Pulse.

**Traditions it echoes.** Noir detective fiction, post-apocalyptic science fiction and the Japanese "evil cult" story; for faith readers, the prophets' attack on idols and on child sacrifice.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Authoritarian and utilitarian ends-justify-means thinking is condemned; secular reason and loyalty are affirmed; transhumanist themes (mutants, an android) are explored without a verdict in sources opened.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-3: noir investigations and the cult's rise; vols. 4-6: experiments, the red and blue gods and Chie's origin; vol. 7: all-out war against a second catastrophe.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why does the Order think its sacrifices are justified?
2. What does color stand for in this story?
3. Chie is an android. Does that make her less of a person?
4. Avidia works outside the law. When is that right, and when is it dangerous?
5. How can you tell a real hope from a false promise?
