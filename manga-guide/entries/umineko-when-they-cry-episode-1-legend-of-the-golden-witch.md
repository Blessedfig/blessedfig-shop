**Premise.** On October 4, 1986, the wealthy Ushiromiya family gathers on its private island, Rokkenjima, for an annual conference about the fortune of its dying head, Kinzo, who is obsessed with the legendary Golden Witch, Beatrice, said to have given him ten tons of gold. Eighteen people (relatives, a doctor and servants) are trapped by a typhoon. Kinzo's grandson Battler, back after six years, insists there are no witches; his nine-year-old cousin Maria insists Beatrice is real and delivers a letter in the witch's name. Murders follow the riddle-like epitaph beneath Beatrice's portrait: six bodies in a storehouse (ch. 8), a sealed-room double murder (ch. 15), a servant killed in the boiler room (ch. 16), and more until no one is left (final chapter). A message in a bottle is found later. Two epilogues (the Tea Party and a secret Tea Party, v4) step outside the story to set up the contest between Battler's reason and the witch's magic that the later episodes play out (Wikipedia; fan wiki).

## Creator and publisher context

- Ryukishi07 (story) is the pen name of the lead writer of 07th Expansion, born November 19, 1973, in Chiba Prefecture; he studied at an art vocational school, worked in retail and the civil service, then launched 07th Expansion in 2001 with Higurashi When They Cry (Wikipedia). He cites Agatha Christie's And Then There Were None as an influence and has said a story should be like a roller coaster, alternating hope and despair (Wikipedia).
- On-record intent: his Episode 1 afterwords say the question is not who did it but whether the witch exists, that he wanted to revive the older blur between occult tale and logical mystery, and that he would try to shake readers' certainty in Episode 2 (fan wiki, vols. 1 and 4; paraphrased).
- Kei Natsumi (art) said in the afterwords that she was a Higurashi fan and would go on to draw Episode 3 (fan wiki). Ryukishi07 confirmed in an APGNation interview that solutions shown in the manga adaptations are canon (Wikipedia; interview not opened).
- Publisher: Square Enix (Gangan Powered, then Gangan Joker, shonen magazines). Yen Press publishes it in English, rated OT.
- No controversies involving the creators were found.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Is this a fantasy or a mystery? Ryukishi07 frames Episode 1 as a contest: either a witch killed the Ushiromiya family, or a human did and dressed it as magic (afterwords). Under that puzzle sits a second question: what does a family become when money decides who matters?

**The answer the story gives.** Episode 1 refuses to answer. Its moral picture is clear (greed, envy and cruelty poisoned the family long before the first body), but who killed them and how is left to the reader, and the episode ends with everyone dead and the witch apparently triumphant.

**Characters as arguments.** Battler argues for reason and loyalty: he will not believe in magic, and he will not accuse family without proof (ch. 13). Maria argues for belief as a refuge: her devotion to Beatrice grows from a lonely, harshly punished childhood (ch. 4, 11). Eva argues for suspicion and self-reliance; Natsuhi for duty to the house; Kinzo for a fortune won at the cost of his soul. Kanon argues that a servant is not furniture and dies proving it (ch. 16).

**Symbols and motifs.** The witch's portrait and epitaph; the gold; the chessboard (Kyrie's chessboard thinking, ch. 6, and the tea party's game board); golden butterflies; the scorpion charms; the seagulls that stop crying (ch. 2); the message in a bottle.

**Traditions it echoes.** The closed-circle mystery, especially And Then There Were None (ANN; Wikipedia); the gothic family curse; Faust-style pacts for wealth. For Christian readers the coveting siblings echo Ecclesiastes and 1 Timothy 6:10; for Buddhist readers, craving.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Pagan/occult and naturalism are set against each other and left in balance; postmodernism is the frame (a story about how stories are told); greed and the old household order are what the story condemns.

**Shifts across the series.** Volume 1 (ch. 1-5): arrival, family quarrels, the witch's letter. Volume 2 (ch. 6-10): the first night, six bodies. Volume 3 (ch. 11-16): suspicion, a sealed-room murder, the boiler room. Volume 4 (ch. 17-final): the study, the last deaths, and two tea-party epilogues that move the contest outside the story.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Battler refuses to believe in witches even when everything points to one. Is that stubbornness or wisdom?
2. Why do you think Maria loves Beatrice so much? What does her home life have to do with it?
3. The siblings fight over money before anyone dies. Did the money cause the tragedy, or reveal it?
4. The servants call themselves furniture. What does that say about the house?
5. The episode ends without an answer. How would you start looking for the truth?
