**Premise.** Ayana Fujisawa, a third-year student (about 14-15) in class 3-3 at Shirosaki Middle School, is bullied by her whole class: extortion, beatings and sexual assault by classmates (Japanese Wikipedia; MyAnimeList and MangaUpdates readers). After someone in her school's uniform pushes her into traffic, she spends three weeks in hospital, decides she will be killed if she does nothing, and plans revenge on all of her classmates, bullies and bystanders alike, one "target" at a time. She recruits Ai Nomura, a quiet girl who was once bullied herself, and blackmails Shota Yoshinaga, a poor boy she photographed shoplifting. Her schemes expose secrets, frame, trap and injure; as they escalate, classmates die. Late in the story (likely ch. 33-45) the real ringleader is revealed as Mari Yuki, a former friend whose father killed himself after Ayana's father's company ruined his factory, and behind her the class president Yuya Yamase, Ayana's half-brother, who manipulated everyone. Ayana kills Mari in a fight; Ai is killed by Shota, who, released by Yuya, chases Ayana with a chainsaw until she falls and is left in a coma. Yuya goes unpunished (Japanese Wikipedia).

## Life message

**Revenge does not heal a bullied child: it spreads harm to friends and bystanders, turns the victim into someone worse, and leaves her broken, while the adults who should have protected children look away.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** If everyone around you has hurt you and no adult will help, is revenge justice? Fukushuu Kyoushitsu sets up a case that seems to say yes, and then follows the answer to its end.

**The answer the story gives.** No, though it takes its time. Ayana's first schemes punish people who earned it; then they reach Yuko and Emi, who had regretted the bullying and were trying to make amends, and both die (likely ch. 3-8). By the end she has killed, been disowned and lost her only friends, and lies in a coma while the real manipulator goes free.

**Characters as arguments.** Ayana argues for vengeance as survival. Mari is her mirror: her revenge for her father made Ayana's life hell, and Ayana's revenge ends hers. Ai shows what revenge does to a follower, Shota what it does to the coerced. Ren and Miho argue, mostly in secret, for protection and life. Yuya argues that people are predictable and nothing matters; the story lets him have the last word.

**Symbols and motifs.** The target list, crossed off by number (emaqi's chapter titles count the targets 1 to 24). Photographs and recordings as weapons. The traffic push that begins it and the fall that ends it. The chainsaw.

**Traditions it echoes.** Revenge tragedy, in which the avenger is destroyed with the guilty (from general knowledge; to verify); the Japanese school-death-game and ijime-revenge genre (emaqi compares it to Ousama Game and Doreiku).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Might makes right and nihilism are explored and, in the end, not refuted; existential choice is shown as a trap. Faith and humanist answers are absent, which is part of what the frames object to.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1-2 (ch. 1-13): methodical revenge with social punishments, then the first deaths. Vol. 3-4 (likely ch. 14-26): physical violence and murder; the class is broken up into other schools. Vol. 5-7 (likely ch. 27-45): torture and arson, the reveal of Mari and Yuya, and the bloody finale. The novel differs: Ayana takes revenge on everyone and dies (Japanese Wikipedia).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Ayana is pushed into traffic and no adult notices her bullying. Who should have helped, and how?
2. Yuko and Emi were sorry. Did they deserve what happened to them?
3. Mari and Ayana both take revenge for something done to them. How are they alike?
4. Ren tells Ayana to live. Why does that matter, and why doesn't she listen?
5. Yuya says nothing ever changes. Is he right?
