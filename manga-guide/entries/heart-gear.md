**Premise.** Two hundred years after a war wiped out humanity, only machines called gears remain. Roue, a girl in glasses who seems to be the last human, lives with Uncle Zett, the old gear who raised her. She accidentally activates Chrome, a powerful gear with no "base program" (a gear's built-in purpose, "essentially a robot's soul," VIZ), and she and Zett invite him into their family and encourage him to choose his own purpose (vol. 1). A rogue machine destroys Zett, so Roue and Chrome set out for Heaven Land, a gear utopia said to be able to revive him; an engineer gives Roue a device that hides that she is human (vol. 1; ciatr). When badly damaged, Chrome shifts into a frightening combat form (vol. 1-2). At Valhalla, a coliseum of warrior gears, General Wodan discovers Roue is human and uses her human biosignature to rewrite every gear's base program to obey him (vol. 3-4; VIZ); Chrome and the war gear Hild defeat him, and the camera gear Rock joins them. Hild urges Chrome to kill Roue, since a human could enslave every gear; he refuses (vol. 4; ciatr). Heaven Land's two administrators, D and R, disagree: one sends a team to welcome Roue, the other a team to eliminate her (vol. 4-5). In Heaven Land (vol. 7), D and R agree to revive Zett if Roue speaks with each of them; she learns the truth of the war 200 years ago and the two plans the last humans left them, and a major decision is placed in her hands (VIZ; Manga News). The last chapters are titled "Let's Become Family" and "Beautiful World" (Wikipedia).

## Life message

**What makes someone a person is the heart they choose, not the body or program they were given; a family can be made by anyone who chooses to care, and a world ruined by war can still be given a second chance.**

## Creator and publisher context

- Tsuyoshi Takaki, previously creator of Black Torch (Jump Square, 2016-2018). After Black Torch he wanted to write science fiction; with his editor he settled on a young girl and a robot as two viewpoints so the story would be easy to relate to; he modeled the robots on those in the 1982 film *Android* and built the setting from everyday objects and photos of ruins (Wikipedia, Production).
- The series paused twice for his health (2020-2022, 2022-2024) before ending in June 2024 (ANN). Manga News praises him for finishing despite health problems. No statements on religion or politics were found. **Status: Needs more info on the creator.**
- Publisher: Shueisha (Shonen Jump+). VIZ Media publishes it under VIZ Signature, rated T+.
- No controversies involving the creator were found.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What makes a person? Chrome wakes without a base program, the built-in purpose every gear has. Roue is the last human among machines. Each must work out what they are for.

**The answer the story gives.** A person is made by the heart they choose. Chrome chooses to protect Roue; Roue chooses to trust machines; Zett chose to raise a human child. The villain, Wodan, does the opposite: he uses Roue to rewrite every gear's will so that none can choose (vol. 3-4).

**Characters as arguments.** Hild argues that one human is a danger to all gears and should die; Chrome answers that he will bet on a better outcome (vol. 4). D and R, Heaven Land's administrators, argue opposite plans for the last human (vol. 4-5, 7). Rock shows that even a camera gear can take sides.

**Symbols and motifs.** The base program as soul; Roue's growling stomach, the one thing a machine cannot fake; the road to Heaven Land; the broken Zett waiting to be revived.

**Traditions it echoes.** Robot stories from Astro Boy to *Android* (1982), which Takaki names as his model; post-apocalyptic journey tales; Norse Valhalla as an arena of warriors. Religious readers may hear the question of the soul and of being knit together; secular readers hear a story of personhood made by choice.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Existential choice and chosen family are celebrated; authoritarian control is condemned; the utilitarian case for killing one to save many is heard and refused. Transhumanist questions (can a mind move to a new body?) are explored rather than answered.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1-2: home, loss and the road. Vol. 2-4: Valhalla and Wodan. Vol. 4-6: Heaven Land's teams, Rock and the sea crossing. Vol. 7: Heaven Land, the truth of the war and Roue's decision, with no combat (Manga News).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Chrome had no built-in purpose. How did he find one?
2. Hild said Roue should die to protect every gear. Why did Chrome refuse?
3. What did Wodan want, and why is it wrong to rewrite someone's will?
4. Can a robot have a soul? What would it need?
5. What makes Roue, Zett and Chrome a family?
