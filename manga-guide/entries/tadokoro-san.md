**Premise.** Kageko Tadokoro, a short, gloomy second-year high-school girl, spends class drawing manga in a hard-boiled style and is mocked by classmates for her drawings and looks. Sakurako Nikaidou, the class's beautiful, rich and popular girl, glimpses one drawing, secretly follows Tadokoro's artist account online and falls for the artist herself. When Tadokoro offers to draw her portrait, Nikaidou, whose head is full of lewd thoughts she tries to hold back, sees her chance (Lilyka; AniList; Wikipedia; AniList review). A near-kiss comes in ch. 6; they confess in ch. 14 and become a couple in ch. 15; Kirigaya, who bullied Tadokoro in middle school, returns, then apologizes in tears and is forgiven (ch. 25) (TV Tropes). The relationship turns physical: the couple's first sexual experience falls in the sleepover arc (ch. 30-39), and a later arc (ch. 120-125) is again sexual (TV Tropes; AniList review). In the Comic Valkyrie version, Tadokoro's manga hobby becomes known at school and she gains friends and rivals (Comic Valkyrie vol. 4 blurb).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What does it mean to be seen? Tadokoro is invisible or mocked until someone looks at her drawings and sees her.

**The answer the story gives.** Love that sees you heals. Nikaidou's adoration makes Tadokoro braver; her art becomes her way into a social world; the old bully apologizes (ch. 25). The story also argues that this love is rightly physical, and its later arcs are sexual (TV Tropes; AniList review).

**Characters as arguments.** Tadokoro argues for the quiet artist; Nikaidou for devoted desire, held back at first for Tadokoro's sake and then fulfilled; Kirigaya for the possibility of repentance; the male classmates are only obstacles.

**Symbols and motifs.** The sketchbook and the artist account; the portrait; heart eyes and blush marks the reviewer says fill the art; the nickname the bullies use.

**Traditions it echoes.** School-life yuri (from Class S stories to modern webcomics) and the opposites-attract romance; the mangaka-in-a-manga story.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism and hedonism are celebrated; progressivism is assumed rather than argued; traditional sexual ethics are absent.

**Shifts across the series.** Ch. 1-15: crush, near-kisses and confession; ch. 16-29: dating and the returning bully (Lilyka vols. 3-4 cover part of this); ch. 30-39: the sleepover arc, where the relationship becomes sexual; ch. 40-119: episodic school life and new friends; ch. 120-126: a final sexual arc and ending (TV Tropes; MangaDex chapter count).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why does Nikaidou notice Tadokoro when no one else does?
2. Kirigaya apologizes. Should Tadokoro forgive her, and why?
3. Nikaidou secretly follows Tadokoro's art account. Is that sweet or a problem?
4. How do the boys in the story treat the girls, and is that fair to boys?
5. What makes a relationship healthy at 16? (for parents to discuss)
