**Premise.** Yukiteru "Yuki" Amano, 14, is a loner who records everything around him in a phone diary and talks to an imaginary friend, Deus Ex Machina, god of time and space. Deus turns out to be real and dying: he turns Yuki's phone into a diary that predicts the future and enters him in a survival game with eleven other diary holders; the last one alive becomes the next god (Wikipedia). Yuki is protected by classmate Yuno Gasai, who loves him obsessively, stalks him and kills for him. Opponents include a serial-killer teacher (ch. 1), the terrorist Minene Uryū, who wants to destroy all religion after her parents died in a religious war and bombs Yuki's school (ch. 2-4), a child cult oracle (ch. 6-9), a small boy (ch. 10-13), a police chief who betrays Yuki to save his dying son (ch. 19-21), and the city's mayor, John Bacchus, who designed the diaries to "evolve" humanity (ch. 33-46). Yuki's parents die (v7-8) and he resolves to win and resurrect them; he shoots his own friends (likely ch. 46-48, v10). Yuno is revealed to be a Yuno from an earlier timeline who already won, found that a god cannot raise the dead, and went back in time, killing her other self (ch. 50-54). In a third world, Yuno kills herself so Yuki can live (ch. 58); Yuki becomes a grieving god and, ten thousand years later, is reunited with the third world's Yuno (ch. 59) (Wikipedia; fan wiki).

## Life message

**No power, not even a god's, can undo death or force a happy ending; obsession and the hunger to control the future destroy people, and love proves itself by letting the other one live.**

Outlook: Bittersweet — nearly everyone dies, Yuki spends ten thousand years alone as a god, and the reunion at the end comes only through another timeline; the ending rejects despair but at enormous cost.
Hope source: L — the only hope the story offers is love between two people: Yuno's final choice to let Yuki live (ch. 58) and their reunion (ch. 59). Power, godhood and winning the game all fail.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** If you could read the future, could you change it, and would you still be yourself? Future Diary gives twelve people a glimpse of what is coming and a reason to kill each other for the power to control everything. Underneath, it asks what love is when it becomes possession, and whether any power can undo loss.

**The answer the story gives.** No. The first Yuno won the game, became god and found that a god cannot bring back the dead; she spent that power undoing time instead (ch. 50-54). Yuki wins and spends ten thousand years mourning (ch. 59). The only real victory is Yuno's last choice to let Yuki live rather than keep him (ch. 58).

**Characters as arguments.** Yuno argues that love means total control and will kill to keep it; her final act reverses that. Yuki argues for survival, then for winning at any cost to bring his parents back, and becomes a killer of his own friends. Minene argues that religion is the enemy, and finds a reason to live in love. Aru argues for truth and dies for it. Kurusu argues that a father's love excuses anything, and cannot live with what he did.

**Symbols and motifs.** The phone diary: a window that becomes a cage. "Dead End" flags that can be changed. Stargazing, the promise Yuki and Yuno keep across timelines. The cage in Yuno's house, where abuse began and ended.

**Traditions it echoes.** The death-game story (*Battle Royale*), the bargain for godhood in Faust-like tales, and horror of the obsessed lover. Esuno cites Rumiko Takahashi's horror as an early influence (Wikipedia). Its claim that power cannot undo death echoes the tragic tradition; Buddhist readers may see the second noble truth acted out in Yuno.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Might makes right is put through a full test and fails. Nihilism dominates the middle but is answered, weakly, by love. Existentialist choice beats predicted fate in small ways. Religion appears mostly as fraud (the cult) or as a dying god, so faith frames find little to stand on.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-3 (ch. 1-13): the game, the school bombing, the cult arc with its sexual abuse backstory. Volumes 4-6 (ch. 14-26): friends, the police chief's betrayal, Yuno's secrets. Volumes 7-10 (ch. 27-50): Yuki's parents die, the mayor's plan, Yuki becomes a killer. Volumes 11-12 (ch. 51-59, no English edition): the timeline twist, the sexual relationship, the third world, Yuno's death and the ending.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Yuno says she loves Yuki. How is her love different from the kind of love that is good for someone?
2. The diaries tell the future, but people still change it. Do you think the future is fixed?
3. Yuki kills his friends because he thinks he can bring everyone back later. What does that show about using a good goal to excuse a bad act?
4. Yuki becomes god but cannot bring back the dead. What is the story saying about power?
5. Many fans love Yuno as a character. Why do you think a dangerous character can be so popular?
