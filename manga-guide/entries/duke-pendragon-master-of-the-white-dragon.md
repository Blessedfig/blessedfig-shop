**Premise.** Raven Valt, the second son of a minor noble house, is the lone survivor of a rebellion his family was caught up in (Kakao Webtoon); the novel says the treason charge against his house was false (novel ch. 1). To earn a pardon he serves ten years in a demon-fighting penal unit, the "Rakshasa" corps (악귀 부대), where most men die within a year. A month before his release he is assigned to guard Alan (also spelled Allen, Eren or Ellen) Pendragon, heir of a once-great duchy whose founders bonded with dragons; a conspiracy kills them both. Raven wakes seven years in the past inside Alan's body, the sickly heir of a declining house. He sets out to find the legendary white dragon, restore House Pendragon and uncover who betrayed them (Tapas; MangaUpdates; Kakao). At the family mausoleum he calls out the name of the former duke, Gordon Pendragon, and the white dragon Soldrake appears (novel ch. 26); later Soldrake travels with him in a woman's form (novel ch. 91), and he deals with nobles, a prince named Ian and Alan's sister Irene (novel ch. 50, 117). In the novel the duchy is eventually recognized as a kingdom (novel ch. 365); how far the 131-chapter webtoon gets is not confirmed.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** If you were wronged and given a second chance, what would you do with it? Raven spent ten years in a death unit for a crime he did not commit, then was murdered a month before freedom.

**The answer the story gives.** Use it to protect your people and set things right. Raven takes up the duties of a sickly heir, finds the dragon his house lost, and rebuilds the duchy (Tapas; novel ch. 26). The story treats justice for betrayers as part of restoration; whether it ever questions revenge could not be checked.

**Characters as arguments.** Raven argues that suffering can forge a leader. The penal commander Baltai argues for power used for pleasure and is drawn as a villain (novel ch. 1). Soldrake stands for a bond older than any one life. Alan's sister Irene is the family Raven has to protect though he is not her brother.

**Symbols and motifs.** The white dragon; the family mausoleum and the dragon statues (novel ch. 26); the borrowed body of a weak heir made strong; the seven-year return to the past.

**Traditions it echoes.** Korean regression and possession web novels; the wronged-soldier revenge tale (The Count of Monte Cristo is a common comparison; from general knowledge; to verify); European dragon-rider romance.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Traditional honor and lineage are affirmed; strength is admired but tied to protecting the house; there is no religious frame in sources opened.

**Shifts across the series.** Season 1 (ch. 1-43), season 2 (44-75), season 3 (76-119), season 4 (120-131) (MangaUpdates). No source opened summarizes what each season covers.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Raven was punished for something he didn't do. How should people respond to unfair punishment?
2. Raven lives in another man's body and takes his place. Is that fair to Alan and his family?
3. What is the difference between justice and revenge? Which is Raven after?
4. Ten years in the penal corps made Raven strong. Can suffering make someone better, or only harder?
5. What would you do differently if you could go back seven years?
