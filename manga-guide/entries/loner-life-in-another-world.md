**Premise.** Haruka, a bookish loner in his third year of high school, tries to dodge a summoning circle that takes his whole class to another world, and arrives last (Kaiten Books; Wikipedia). A God lets the students choose cheat skills first come, first served, so Haruka gets only the leftovers: Loner, NEET, Master of None, a wooden staff (ch. 1). Living alone in a forest cave, he combines his useless skills cleverly. His class splits into cliques: the Delinquents try to use mind-control skills to make the girls their harem, the Nerds who stopped them are driven out, and the girls are left on their own (likely ch. 6-10). Haruka, who secretly cares, ends up sheltering and training the girls led by his childhood friend, the Class Rep, Touka (likely ch. 10-20). A hooded student, the math genius Tanaka, murders classmates to steal their skills, including all four Delinquents; Haruka kills him in a duel (likely ch. 15-35). In a dungeon he frees a cursed skeleton knight, the Dungeon Emperor, who becomes Angelica, bound to him by a servitude skill; together they save the town of Omui from a monster stampede (likely ch. 36-60; anime eps. 7-12). Later volumes turn to politics: making Omui an independent trade hub against an exploiting lord (v6), a princess who wants to die (v10), a kingdom's power struggle and a Theocracy (v12) (Kaiten Books; ANN).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can someone who stands apart still be good for the group? Haruka wants nothing more than to be left alone, and the world gives him the skills of a loner. The series asks whether a person can belong without joining.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes: belonging is shown by protecting, not by signing up. Haruka keeps his "Loner" label but builds a house for the girls, trains them, kills the murderer among them and saves the town (likely ch. 10-60). The last anime episode, ending at ch. 60, has him resolve to conquer the world, still as a loner (Wikipedia).

**Characters as arguments.** Haruka argues for wit over gifts. The Class Rep argues for responsibility to everyone. The Delinquents argue for taking what you want, and are destroyed. Tanaka is Haruka's dark twin, a loner who decides to take everyone's power instead of building his own; Haruka tells him that is why he cannot win. Angelica, alone for ages, shows what isolation becomes if no one comes; Haruka comes.

**Symbols and motifs.** The leftover skills, especially "Loner" and "Master of None." The cave turned into a house. The wooden staff. The dungeon's 101st floor. Angelica's cursed armor. The maxed-out Luck stat.

**Traditions it echoes.** The isekai class-summoning story and its parodies (Silverman compares the self-aware humor with genre clichés); the knight freed from a curse; the outcast who saves the village. Tauber (ANN) likens the anime to an isekai version of a harem comedy.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Pragmatism is affirmed throughout; Might makes right is critiqued by the deaths of the Delinquents and Tanaka. Communitarianism grows as the class reunites. Expressive individualism is honored in Haruka's refusal to join, but softened by his care. Later volumes add a critique of exploiting rulers and a church.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-4 (likely ch. 1-35): survival, the class split, the murders and the duel with Tanaka. Vols. 5-7 (ch. 36-60): the dungeon, Angelica and the stampede (the end of the 2024 anime). Vols. 6-12 in English: Omui's independence, an exploiting lord, a despairing princess, a kingdom's succession fight and a Theocracy (Kaiten; ANN). Japanese vols. 13-29: not covered by any source opened.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Haruka got the leftover skills. How did he turn them into strengths?
2. Tanaka was also a loner. Why did he turn out so differently from Haruka?
3. The class drove the Nerds away for something they did not do. What made the Gals apologize?
4. Haruka kills Tanaka. Was there another way?
5. Can you be a loner and still belong somewhere?
