**Premise.** Mizuki Usami, a second-year at Tsukimori Middle School, is the only member of the art club who wants to make real art. The president, a third-year, does nothing but sleep on the club sofa; Colette, a wealthy, hyperactive first-year from abroad, is a troublemaker; and Subaru Uchimaki, a prize-level talent who can draw anyone's face from memory, spends all his effort on drawing his ideal "2D wife" and has no interest in real girls. Mizuki has a crush on Subaru that everyone notices except him. Later the club gains a new adviser, the earnest, clumsy teacher Yumeko Tachibana (from v2), and Maria Imari, a beautiful transfer student with an extreme case of chuunibyou (adolescent fantasy role-play) who shares Subaru's otaku interests (from v4) (Japanese Wikipedia). Each chapter is a short comic episode: a lost child, a love letter, an indirect kiss with a soda can, pool cleaning, a school festival with soda-can art (anime episode titles; Wikipedia).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What is art for, and what is love? Subaru can draw anything but chooses to draw only his perfect imaginary wife; Mizuki, who can see what he could be, cannot say what she feels.

**The answer the story gives.** Gently, that real people are better than perfect fantasies, and that gifts grow when they reach others: Subaru inspires a four-year-old to love drawing (Japanese Wikipedia), and the club's best moments are shared projects. The story never forces the lesson.

**Characters as arguments.** Mizuki argues for sincerity and effort. Subaru argues for the perfect ideal over messy reality. The president argues for comfort, and his crush on the teacher is mocked rather than admired. Maria argues that fantasy can be a way to make friends. Colette argues for play.

**Symbols and motifs.** The blank canvas; the 2D wife; the art-club room and its sofa; the soda-can art for the festival.

**Traditions it echoes.** The school comedy of misunderstandings; Pygmalion, the artist in love with his own creation; the parable of the talents for readers who know it.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism is affectionately questioned; friendship and diligence are affirmed. No religious or political lens appears.

**Shifts across the series.** Early volumes establish the four members; the adviser Yumeko joins in v2, Mizuki's friends in v3, Maria in v4 and the president's childhood friend Nonoka in v6 (Japanese Wikipedia). Later developments were not available in sources opened.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why do you think Subaru prefers drawing a fictional wife to talking with real people?
2. Mizuki never says how she feels. What makes honesty hard?
3. The president's crush on the teacher is played as a joke. Is it funny? Why or why not?
4. What is the best use of a talent?
5. What would you want your own club to be like?
