**Premise.** Ryou Hasegawa is a 25-year-old former NEET (not in work or training) who made money in stocks and then drifted (Mangapedia; MyAnimeList reviewers say a lottery win). One day his life turns into a "romance game." He is told to "conquer" a series of ranked female targets, with no guidance on what that means. Picking a target cannot be undone, and failing a route can kill him. He has a save-and-load power, can buy items, and plays detective: he tails targets and searches their homes for evidence. The targets hide secrets, and the higher a target's rank, the more dangerous she is. His first target nearly kills him (Mangapedia describes a serial-killer married woman). The A-rank target Yurea Kusora, a scarred, unstable heiress who secretly holds power behind the scenes in Japan, becomes a recurring partner and rival. The story moves through crime, mafia, assassins, drugs and terrorism.

**The story rewards survival through cunning, retrying and reading other people's hidden motives. Romance is framed as a dangerous contest, and trust is earned slowly, if at all.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What happens when romance becomes a game with deadly stakes? Dating sims reduce relationships to routes and flags that a player can learn and clear. This story asks what that model would mean if the player could die, and if the women were real people with secrets, histories and weapons of their own.

**The answer the story gives.** From summaries, the answer is that the game model fails as soon as it meets real people. Ryou is told to "conquer," but the women he targets are killers, power brokers and damaged survivors. He survives not by charm but by investigation: tailing, searching, saving and reloading. Mangapedia describes the work as a crime suspense story with superpowers; reviewers describe a detective story in a dating-sim costume. The story does not seem to reject the dating-sim frame outright, though. It keeps the ranked targets and sells fan service, so it critiques the frame and profits from it at once.

**Characters as arguments.** Ryou is the argument that ordinary persistence can beat a rigged system; reviewers note he is not overpowered and grows gradually. Yurea is the argument that the "prize" is a person: scarred, unstable, powerful, and the one who often holds the upper hand (a reviewer says she uses Ryou "as a toy"). The first target, a married woman who kills, is the argument that appearances deceive.

**Symbols and motifs.** The save point stands for second chances without memory loss: the hero keeps what he learned, others mostly do not (reviewers note exceptions as plot holes). The target rank stands for danger. Items for sale turn survival into a market.

**Traditions it echoes.** Visual-novel and dating-sim games; death-game thrillers; time-loop stories; Japanese crime fiction about hidden elites and organized crime.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Pragmatism is the story's working method and is rewarded. Might-makes-right lives in the criminals and the hidden elite. Hedonism sits in the fan service rather than the plot's verdict. Existential and absurd notes come from an unexplained game that gives orders but no reasons.

**Shifts across the series.** Reviewers describe a rocky opening (including the early assault and "clear deviancy," in one reviewer's words), a turn toward detective plotting and deeper heroines after the first target, and, in later chapters, slower progress, unresolved questions and a drop in art detail (MyAnimeList reviews, written at ch. 6 through ch. 87).

**Discussion questions.**
1. The game tells Ryou to "conquer" women. What changes when he starts treating them as people with secrets of their own?
2. If you could save and reload your life, would you act differently? What would you lose?
3. One reviewer says Ryou's early assault is later "redeemed." Can a story undo something like that, and should it try?
4. Ryou tails people and searches their homes to survive. Where is the line between investigation and stalking?
5. Why do readers like the most dangerous heroine best?
