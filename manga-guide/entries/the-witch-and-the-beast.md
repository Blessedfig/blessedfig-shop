**Premise.** In a steampunk world soaked in magic, the gentle, chain-smoking mage Ashaf and his feral, foul-mouthed partner Guideau work for the Order of Magical Resonance, which handles magical crimes, especially witches. Guideau is a powerful "beast" whose soul the witch Angela Anne Huell cursed into a young woman's body (one of Angela's own old bodies); the beast's true body rides in a coffin on Ashaf's back, and kissing any witch frees it for a short time (Wikipedia; fan wiki). Their cases: a beloved "heroic" witch who cuts off girls' limbs to unseal a city's hellfire as revenge for the persecution of her grandmother (ch. 1-2); twin boys who kill for a grimoire and are put to death by their adoptive mother, a police mage (ch. 3-5); an illegal necromancer (ch. 6-9); a young witch, Helga, framed by the Church's secret Executioners to steal a world-destroying demon sword (ch. 10-21); a mute witch, Falvell, hunted by Executioners who turn her village against her (ch. 22-27, flashback); a vampire world in the abyss called the Fall (ch. 28-41); and a coven led by Angela that steals forbidden instruments and hunts a living weapon, the Nameless Seed (ch. 42 onward) (fan wiki; Anime UK News).

## Life message

**Judge people by what they do, not by their blood or reputation: power, whether a witch's or a church's, corrupts when it is used on the helpless, and hatred has to be tempered by trust.**

Outlook: Bittersweet — each case ends with the innocent saved and the abuser stopped, often at a high cost (limbs lost, sons put to death, a family massacred in the past), and the larger war with Angela's coven is unresolved because the series is on hiatus.
Hope source: P — the way through is protecting others: Ashaf and Guideau save the framed and the hunted (Helga, Falvell, the Seed boy); their slowly growing trust (F, partnership) is the close second, and Ashaf has promised to teach Guideau what love is (ch. 27).

**Where traditions disagree.** Almost every frame welcomes the defense of the falsely accused and the condemnation of prejudice and mob violence. Two things divide them. ## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Who is the real monster? Every case turns on a reversal: the city's beloved heroine is a killer (ch. 1-2), the witch the army hunts is innocent and framed (ch. 10-21), the mute girl the village fears is the victim of the men stirring the village (ch. 22-27).

**The answer the story gives.** Judge by deeds, not by blood or reputation. Witches can be saints or murderers; a church can hold an honest paladin and a secret assassin; a vampire queen can be both abuser and abused. Power used on the helpless is the mark of evil, whoever holds it.

**Characters as arguments.** Guideau argues for vengeance and brute strength; the story lets her win fights but keeps asking what hatred costs her. Ashaf argues for patience, reason and care, and he is usually right. Matt argues for mercy, and Ashaf warns it may be his undoing. Ione argues that persecution justifies revenge; the story grants the grievance and condemns the revenge. Helga argues that duty and compassion can tame even a demon.

**Symbols and motifs.** The coffin on Ashaf's back (the true self carried by another); the kiss that breaks a curse for a moment (a fairy tale turned into a weapon); crows; the Fall, a seventeen-level abyss of hidden worlds; forbidden instruments that run on blood, power always paid for by someone.

**Traditions it echoes.** European fairy tales (the cursed beast, true love's kiss), witch-hunt history, the gothic novel, and the detective procedural; the publisher compares it to Fullmetal Alchemist and Cowboy Bebop. For religious readers it raises old questions about persecution in God's name and about power sought through forbidden means.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Might makes right and authoritarianism are critiqued through the villains. Occult elements are the world's fabric rather than a creed. Duty and virtue are affirmed in Helga and in Guideau's slow growth. Critical readings of prejudice are built into the plot.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-2 (ch. 1-9) are self-contained cases. Volumes 2-4 (ch. 10-21) bring the Church and the demon sword. Volumes 4-5 (ch. 22-27) give the partners' origin. Volumes 5-8 (ch. 28-41) go into the Fall. Volumes 9-10 (ch. 42 onward) begin the war with Angela's coven, where the story stopped for the hiatus.

**Discussion questions.**
1. In the first case, the town loved the witch who was the killer. Why are people so easily fooled about who is good?
2. Guideau wants revenge. Ashaf wants to teach her love. Which do you think will win, and why?
3. Matt spares a traitor, and Ashaf calls it a weakness. Is mercy a strength or a weakness?
4. The Executioners say they protect the Church. Can a good institution do evil to protect itself?
5. Guideau breaks the curse by kissing witches who do not agree to it. Is that fair to them?
