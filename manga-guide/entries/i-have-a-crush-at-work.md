**Premise.** Masugu Tateishi (accounting) and Yui Mitsuya (product planning) joined Tsuda Confectionery, a sweets maker, in the same year and are known around the office as sworn enemies. In truth they have been drawn to each other for years, and after a work party he confesses (Wikipedia; Da Vinci Web says six years after joining). Embarrassed at the thought of office gossip, Yui asks that they keep it secret, so at work she is colder than ever while at home they are openly affectionate, with weekend stays at each other's apartments (Da Vinci Web). Short chapters follow near misses (a junior, Somei, finds out, anime ep. 2), a first trip (ch. 17-19), anniversaries, a year-end party (ch. 43-44), Valentine's Day, camping, an internal transfer that moves Yui to public relations (ch. 74-78), going public (ch. 95), life plans (ch. 96-98), meeting the parents (ch. 107-108), living together (ch. 113), a proposal and marriage, and finally a daughter, Tsumugi (Wikipedia; fan wiki). A side romance grows between their boss Shizuno Hayakawa (32) and Somei (24) (Japanese Wikipedia).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can you love someone and still go to work with them every day? The series asks what a relationship looks like between the big moments: on Monday mornings, in the elevator, at the year-end party, in a storage room where someone might walk in.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, through small daily care and honest talk. The secret matters less as the series goes on (AniList review); what matters is how the two support each other, quarrel and make up, and plan a life. The ending in marriage and a child is presented as the natural fruit of that care.

**Characters as arguments.** Tateishi argues for steady kindness; Yui for courage and effort, even after failure (the director's reading, Animage Plus). Shizuno, burned by an office romance, argues for caution and is proved wrong by her own happiness with Somei. Kiribayashi, a brilliant director who keeps failing at arranged meetings, shows that success at work does not guarantee love.

**Symbols and motifs.** Sweets (the company's products and the couple's gifts); the "mortal enemies" act; masks and staggered commutes (Da Vinci Web); anniversaries; the shared apartment.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Japanese office romance and "secret relationship" comedy; the slice-of-life seinen of working adults. For faith readers it echoes the virtues of 1 Corinthians 13 or the householder ideal, while departing from teaching on premarital intimacy.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Traditionalism (marriage and family) and communitarianism (a decent workplace) are affirmed together; there are no villains to argue the other side.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-4 (ch. 1-37): secrecy and early dating; vols. 5-8 (ch. 38-77): anniversaries, parties and the transfer (the anime ends here); vols. 9-12 (ch. 78-117): going public, life plans, parents, living together; vols. 13-15: proposal, marriage announcement and family (fan wiki; Wikipedia).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why did Yui want to keep the relationship secret? Was it a good idea?
2. What small things do Tateishi and Yui do for each other that show love?
3. How do they make up after a disagreement?
4. Shizuno had a bad experience with office romance. How does that shape her advice?
5. What makes someone a good coworker as well as a good partner?
