**Premise.** Noir Stardia, third son of a baronet, the lowest and poorest rank of noble, loses a promised librarian job on his first day when a better-connected family takes it (Wikipedia; Japanese Wikipedia). He has a rare skill, Great Sage, that can answer any question but leaves him with crippling headaches; his childhood friend Emma Brightness learns from an old text that kissing relieves them, and the two begin kissing (ch. 1; anime ep. 1). Great Sage leads him to a hidden dungeon where the legendary adventurer Olivia Servant has been chained for 200 years. She gives him skills to create, bestow and edit abilities, paid for with Life Points (LP) that he earns from joyful experiences such as good food, achievement and, above all, romantic and sexual excitement; if LP runs out he dies (Wikipedia; Japanese Wikipedia). He enters the Hero Academy, where nobles snub him (vol. 1), and earns money as a guild adventurer with the receptionist Lola (vols. 1-2). He saves his classmate Maria, a duke's daughter cursed to die at sixteen, by editing the cleric Luna's curse-lifting skill so it costs money instead of her life (ch. 7-14, vols. 1-2). A harem forms: Emma, Lola, Luna, Maria, the teacher Elna, the exchange student Leila, and his younger sister Alice, who has a brother complex (Wikipedia; fan wiki). Later arcs include a phantom thief (vol. 4), a hot-spring training camp (vol. 5), school tournaments (vols. 8-10), a serial killer who targets women and a rival, Meledy, who wants Noir (vol. 11), and the final battle with a copy of Olivia made by her curse, which frees her (vol. 12) (Wikipedia; Seven Seas).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What should a poor young man do with sudden power? Noir loses a modest job to someone with better connections and gains, in a hidden dungeon, the power to rewrite skills, his own and others'.

**The answer the story gives.** Use it to save people and build an honest life: he saves Maria from a curse, spares Luna's life, earns his tuition, opens a family shop and frees his master (vols. 1-12). But the story also says that the power runs on pleasure, and it chooses sexual excitement as the main source, so most of his growth is tied to fan-service scenes (Wikipedia; Japanese Wikipedia).

**Characters as arguments.** Noir argues that merit and kindness should beat rank. Maria, a duke's daughter, argues the same from the top. The snobbish nobles and the guild receptionist Sarah, who burns out her adventurers for rankings, argue for using people (anime ep. 7). Olivia, haunted by a student she failed, argues that a teacher owes her student protection. Elna, the teacher whose "rewards" are sexual, is the story's comic contradiction of that ideal.

**Symbols and motifs.** The hidden dungeon as a secret place of growth; the Death Chains binding a mentor; the LP meter as a literal measure of pleasure; Luna's curse-lifting that costs life until it is edited to cost money; the family shop as a picture of honest prosperity.

**Traditions it echoes.** The poor-boy-makes-good tale; the apprentice and the imprisoned master; the Japanese harem comedy; and, in its LP system, a playful version of the old idea that joy strengthens and despair weakens.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Hedonism is built into the rules and affirmed. Virtue ethics shows in Noir's character, and pragmatism in his clever edits. Critique of privilege is clear but light. Readers of any tradition who value consent will see the gags with teenagers and the teacher as the story's weakest point.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-3: the academy, Maria's curse, Luna, the guild and the dungeon's dryad. Vols. 4-6: the phantom thief, the hot-spring camp and a journey ("The City of Honest"). Vols. 7-10: a great invasion, school tournaments and study abroad. Vols. 11-12: a serial killer, a rival for Noir, and the battle that frees Olivia (Wikipedia chapter titles; Seven Seas).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Noir lost his job because another family had better connections. Is that fair? What would make it fair?
2. In this story, Noir gets stronger when he feels excited or happy. What really makes a person stronger?
3. Noir refused to join the peeping plot. Why does the story treat the other boys as wrong?
4. Would a real teacher be allowed to give "rewards" like Elna's? Why not?
5. Noir changes Luna's skill so curing curses costs money instead of her life. What does that say about how he values people?
