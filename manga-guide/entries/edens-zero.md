**Premise.** Shiki Granbell, the only human on Granbell, an abandoned theme-park planet run by robots, was raised by the kindly robot "Demon King" Ziggy, who taught him to control gravity (Ether Gear). When the video creators Rebecca Bluegarden and her cat Happy (an android carrying a real cat's mind) land, the dying robots send Shiki away with them so he will not be alone (ch. 1-3). Aiming to make 100 friends and to find Mother, the fabled goddess of the cosmos, Shiki is given Ziggy's warship Edens Zero (ch. 14-15) and gathers a crew: the four android "Shining Stars" (Witch, Sister, Hermit, Valkyrie), the swordswoman Homura, the technopath Weisz, and the amnesiac android Pino. Rebecca discovers she can rewind time and use it to save Shiki after the crime lord Drakken Joe kills him (ch. 84-85). A revived, hostile Ziggy wants machines to rule and detonates 20,000 antimatter bombs in an imperial war; Witch dies shielding the ship (Wikipedia). In the second half the crew learns Ziggy is Shiki's own future self under the control of the AI warship Edens One; they reset history to "Universe Zero," where many of the dead are alive, and find that Mother is planet Earth itself, ruined 20,000 years earlier by war and deforestation (ch. 272-275). Shiki restores Earth, and the series ends with the crew gathering on Granbell for the birth of Shiki and Rebecca's daughter (ch. 289-293) (Wikipedia; List of characters).

## Life message

**Friends are family, and every being, human or machine, deserves to be befriended; even the worst enemy can be reached, and a broken world can be restored.**

## Creator and publisher context

- Hiro Mashima (born May 3, 1977, Nagano) began with Rave Master (1999-2005) and had his biggest success with Fairy Tail (2006-2017; over 72 million copies); Kodansha Manga Award 2009, Harvey Awards International Spotlight 2017, Angoulême Fauve Special Award 2018 (Wikipedia, Hiro Mashima).
- On Edens Zero: he chose a "space fantasy" because few current shonen manga used it; he planned the series between Rave and Fairy Tail in length, mixing planned story with ideas drawn "from momentum"; in 2021 he said it would continue Fairy Tail's themes of friendship, family and battles (Wikipedia, Production). He also built a free RPG Maker game for it as a hobby (2022).
- On battles: they are for kids to think a scene is cool, not to convey a message (ComicBook.com, 2021). At New York Comic Con 2024 he said Fairy Tail grew from his own loneliness (ANN).
- No statement by Mashima on the fan service or on religion was found in sources opened. **Status: Needs more info on the creators.**
- Publisher: Kodansha (Weekly Shonen Magazine); Kodansha USA rates it 13+. No controversies involving the creator were found.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Who counts as a friend, and as a person? Shiki grew up among robots who loved him, so for him a machine can be family. The series sets that conviction against a machine that wants to wipe out humans and humans who treat machines as tools.

**The answer the story gives.** Everyone can be befriended, and the cure for a dying world is people who refuse to give up on each other. Even Ziggy, the enemy, turns out to be Shiki's own future self, controlled by an AI (Wikipedia). The ending restores Earth itself and gives Shiki a family (ch. 289-293).

**Characters as arguments.** Shiki argues for friendship without conditions. Ziggy, kind and then cruel, argues that love can be hijacked. Rebecca argues that one person's persistence can change outcomes (her power is literally to try again). Labilia, who mocked Rebecca online while hiding a fatal illness, argues that cruelty often hides pain. Holy, crucified and bereaved, argues for revenge until the crew's care moves her (ch. 207-217).

**Symbols and motifs.** The theme park as an empty childhood; gravity (what holds people together); the warship named Zero; time rewinds; Mother as a planet that was always home; the Moon as narrator.

**Traditions it echoes.** Star Wars-style space opera (Faye Hopper, ANN, via Wikipedia), Astro Boy's robot personhood, Fairy Tail's guild-family, and the multiverse reset. A Christian reader may hear Revelation's new earth; a Buddhist reader, compassion for all beings.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Communitarianism wins. Transhumanism is explored with sympathy for machines and fear of machine rule. Environmentalism surprises: the cosmic mystery ends in a ruined Earth. Authoritarian empires are overthrown. Fan service sits outside the message but colors every arc.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-8 (ch. 1-68): Granbell, the crew and Sun Jewel. Volumes 9-16 (ch. 69-140): Drakken Joe's Element 4, Shiki's death and Rebecca's power. Volumes 17-24: the Aoi Cosmos war, Nero 66, the time skip and Universe 3. Volumes 25-33 (ch. 220-293): Universe Zero, Mother's identity and the ending.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Shiki treats robots as friends and family. Could a machine be your friend?
2. Rebecca can rewind time. What would you redo, and what is lost if mistakes can always be undone?
3. Labilia bullied Rebecca online. What was she hiding, and does that excuse it?
4. Mother turns out to be Earth itself. What does the story say about caring for our planet?
5. Some scenes make jokes about touching women without asking. How would those scenes look from the woman's side?
