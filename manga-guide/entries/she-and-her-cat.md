**Premise.** On a rainy spring day, a young woman named Miyu, who lives alone, brings home a white cat, Chobi. Chobi tells the story: through the four seasons of a year he watches Miyu manage the freedom and the loneliness of adult life, learning about the world outside through her, until the harsh realities of her life catch up with her (Kodansha blurb via MangaUpdates). The AniList character list adds Miyu's mother, a friend named Tomoka and a cat named Mimi. The AniList reviewer describes a job that pays the bills but does not fulfill Miyu, growing anguish about the future, a cat who reads her moods through smell, and an ending that stops short rather than resolving. In Shinkai's 1999 film, Miyu is an office worker and Chobi stays with her through ups and downs, including nights she drinks and brings men home (Wikipedia); how much of this the manga keeps is not confirmed.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What does it take to get through a lonely season of adult life? Miyu has her freedom, an apartment and a job that pays, and still the year wears her down (AniList review). The cat who loves her cannot fix it, only stay.

**The answer the story gives.** Stay, and take one more step. The reviewer reads the ending as a reminder that a small step can be enough, and that it is right to ask for help. The story does not promise more than that.

**Characters as arguments.** Chobi argues for faithful, wordless love; he understands Miyu's moods through scent though not her words (AniList review). Miyu argues the case of modern independence and its cost. Her mother and Tomoka, by the character list, stand for the ties that remain (to verify).

**Symbols and motifs.** Rain at the first meeting; the four seasons as chapters; the small apartment; scent as the language of feeling.

**Traditions it echoes.** Natsume Soseki's I Am a Cat for the cat narrator (from general knowledge; to verify); Shinkai's films of loneliness and distance; Japanese seasonal poetry.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Existential loneliness is explored without despairing conclusions; companionship is affirmed; no religious or political lens is argued.

**Shifts across the series.** One volume, one year: spring (meeting), summer, autumn and winter, with the strain growing toward winter (Wikipedia chapter titles; Kodansha blurb).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Chobi cannot talk to Miyu. How does he still help her?
2. Miyu has freedom but feels alone. Can both be true?
3. When have you needed to ask someone for help? Was it hard?
4. Why might the author tell this story through a cat?
5. The ending stops short. What do you think Miyu's next year looks like?
