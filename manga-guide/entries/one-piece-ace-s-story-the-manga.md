**Premise.** A prequel to One Piece told by Masked Deuce, a would-be writer who becomes the first mate of Portgas D. Ace, Luffy's older sworn brother. Stranded together on the island of Sixis, Ace and Deuce build a boat, quarrel when the starving Deuce wrongly accuses Ace of hoarding food, make up, and split a Devil Fruit that turns out to be the Flame-Flame Fruit; Ace gains power over fire and the two found the Spade Pirates (ch. 1). Ace, who is the hidden son of the executed pirate king Gol D. Roger, sails into the New World set on beating the great captain Whitebeard to prove himself; after a five-day fight with Jinbe he offers himself to Whitebeard so his crew can go free, and is taken aboard Whitebeard's ship (ch. 2). Over three months he tries to kill Whitebeard again and again, comes to respect him and his crew, and after his hundredth defeat accepts him as captain and father in a sake-cup ceremony, burning the Spade Pirates' flag (ch. 3-4). A closing flashforward recalls Ace's later death in the war at Marineford, and Deuce leaves the book he wrote about Ace at Ace's grave (ch. 4). The volumes add Boichi's redraws of Zoro's duel with Mihawk and Nami's fight with the government agent Kalifa (One Piece wiki; VIZ).

## Life message

**You are not your father's name: a young man scorned as a devil's child earns his own place through courage and loyalty, and finds a father worth following in the man he set out to defeat.**

| Meta lens | How it reads the series |

| Evolutionary biology | Status contests between males decide belonging: Ace challenges the strongest captain, loses a hundred times, and is adopted into his "family" crew (ch. 3-4). The crew is a band that shares food, risk and loyalty. |
| Pragmatic | Ace learns from defeat: he drops sneak attacks for head-on fights, learns Haki from Thatch, and uses Observation Haki to plan his 99th attempt (ch. 3). Deuce supplies the boat-building and planning Ace lacks (ch. 1). |
| Religious | No religion appears. The sake-cup (sakazuki) ceremony that makes Whitebeard Ace's "father" is a Japanese ritual of sworn kinship (ch. 4). The chapter title "The Devil's Child" (Oni no Ko) names the stigma of being Roger's son. |
| Socio-cultural | A story about inherited stigma: Deuce is prejudiced against the idea of Roger having a son until he knows Ace (One Piece wiki, Masked Deuce); a fish-man blames Ace for the crimes of pirates Roger inspired (ch. 2). Reviewers read Ace's goal as a reaction against his absent father (Brady; MrAJCosplay, ANN). |

Outlook: Bittersweet — the main story ends happily, with Ace adopted into Whitebeard's crew and his own crew still following him, but it is told after his death: the opening color spread shows Luffy holding the dying Ace, and the last pages recall Marineford and Deuce at his grave (ch. 1, 4; One Piece wiki).
Hope source: F — the way through is family: a crew that will not leave its captain and a captain who calls his crew his sons; Ace accepts Whitebeard as father and captain (ch. 4). Effort and growing stronger (E) is close behind.

## Creator and publisher context

- Boichi (Mu-jik Park, born 1973, South Korea) studied physics and image technology before turning to manga; he moved into the Japanese industry in 2004. His work ranges from adult-market one-shots early in his career to the seinen action series Sun-Ken Rock and the shōnen hit Dr. Stone (art), and he won awards in 2019 for Dr. Stone and his science-fiction series Origin (Wikipedia, Boichi).
- Public, work-related charity: he donated the royalties of a Sun-Ken Rock volume to a children's charity in Vietnam and organized artists' support for 2011 tsunami relief (Wikipedia).
- Volume notes (One Piece wiki translation): Oda said he would like to see Zoro, then Ace in full combat, and then "a sexy Nami" drawn by Boichi; Boichi expresses gratitude to Oda. Storyboarder Ryo Ishiyama says One Piece made him want to be a manga artist and that Oda revised the storyboards and dialogue.
- Original story: Sho Hinata (novel vol. 1) and Tatsuya Hamazaki (novel vol. 2), Shueisha 2018; concept by Eiichiro Oda.
- Publisher: Shueisha (One Piece Magazine; Jump Comics). English: VIZ Media, Shonen Jump imprint, rated Teen.
- No controversies involving this work were found.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can you be more than your father's name? Ace is the hidden son of the most hated pirate in the world. The chapter titled "The Devil's Child" names how others see him, and he answers by setting out to beat the strongest man alive.

**The answer the story gives.** You earn your own name, but not alone. Ace's courage and generosity win Deuce (ch. 1) and a crew that will not leave him (ch. 2). Then the story turns: the man he came to kill becomes the father he chooses. After a hundred defeats, Ace accepts Whitebeard as captain and father (ch. 4). Reviewers read the arc as Ace answering his absent father, Roger (Brady; MrAJCosplay, ANN).

**Characters as arguments.** Ace argues for pride turned into loyalty. Deuce, who once suspected Ace and was even ready to kill him for food, argues for repentance and faithful memory: he writes Ace's story and leaves it at his grave (ch. 4). Whitebeard argues that a captain is a father; he keeps fighting Ace despite failing health so as not to cut short the young man's journey (ch. 3). Hammond argues that the son should pay for the father's crimes, and is knocked out. Teach, glimpsed in the flashforward, is betrayal.

**Symbols and motifs.** Fire: Ace's power, both warmth and danger. The Spade Pirates' flag, burned at the adoption (ch. 4). The sake cup. The book Deuce writes. The opening image of Ace dying in Luffy's arms, which frames every happy scene with grief.

**Traditions it echoes.** The prodigal-son and adoption stories, the wandering samurai who finds a lord worth serving, Japanese sworn-kinship rituals, and the shōnen pattern of growth through defeat.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Chosen family is the clearest value. The honor code is admired: open combat, self-sacrifice, keeping one's word. Individual self-making is explored and then tempered by belonging. Strength-rules thinking is the world's logic, but Whitebeard's strength is shown as protective.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1 (ch. 1-2): castaways, the Spade Pirates, the New World and the defeat by Whitebeard; bonus Zoro duel. Vol. 2 (ch. 3-4): life aboard the Moby Dick, the hundred duels, the sakazuki and the flashforward; bonus Nami fight.

**Discussion questions.**
1. People judged Ace by his father. Have you ever been judged by your family's reputation?
2. Ace forgave Deuce before Deuce apologized. Why does that matter for everything that follows?
3. Why does Ace stop sneak-attacking Whitebeard and start fighting him face to face?
4. What makes Whitebeard a good father to his crew?
5. Deuce writes Ace's story after he dies. How do we keep the memory of people we lose?
