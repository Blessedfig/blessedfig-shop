**Premise.** Erika Sakura is the most popular girl at her high school, admired by everyone and proud of it. For no reason she can explain, she cannot stop thinking about Kuzumi, a short, plain, silent boy who sits in the corner, never seems to read the mood, and whose voice almost no one has heard except his only friend, Haruna Chiba, an intimidating-looking boy who is kind at heart (AniList characters; Yen Press). Kuzumi loves birds, wins over children and is oddly good at games (Yen Press column). Most chapters are Erika's comic inner monologue as she overthinks signs, primps for him and tries, tsundere-style, not to show her feelings, while her friends Yuuko (who quietly likes Chiba) and Juri watch, and rivals appear: Wakana, who also likes Kuzumi, and Tooru Misaki, the school's top boy, who likes Erika and cannot beat Kuzumi at anything (AniList characters). After Valentine's Day a casual "I like you" changes things; in their third year Kuzumi makes a surprise move before the culture festival (vol. 7 synopsis), and by vol. 8 they are a couple, followed through the end of high school and beyond (vol. 8 synopsis). One reader review says Kuzumi chooses a different university without telling Erika, and that the pair barely talk until about ch. 41 (AniList review; to verify).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Why do we like who we like? Erika has every reason, by the rules of her school, to choose Misaki. She keeps choosing Kuzumi, who seems to break every rule of mood and status.

**The answer the story gives.** Love notices what others overlook: Kuzumi's kindness to children, his love of birds, his loyalty to Chiba. The comedy is in Erika's pride, which keeps her from saying so.

**Characters as arguments.** Erika argues for feeling over status, while acting as if status still matters. Kuzumi argues, without words, that sincerity is enough. Misaki argues that being best should win; it does not. Chiba shows that looks deceive.

**Symbols and motifs.** The air (kūki) that Kuzumi cannot or will not read; birds; Erika's hair and lucky signs.

**Traditions it echoes.** The tsundere school comedy (Yen Press compares it to Monthly Girls' Nozaki-kun) and the old story of the overlooked one who turns out to be worthy.

**How each lens is portrayed.** No lens is argued at length. A reader's critique, that love built by one person is not healthy, is a fair question for families even though the story itself does not raise it.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-5: one-sided crush comedy. Volumes 6-7: Valentine's Day, a casual confession, third year and the culture festival. Volume 8: a couple through the end of high school and beyond, with an epilogue and extras (B&N synopses; AniList).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why does Erika like Kuzumi when everyone expects her to like Misaki?
2. Kuzumi almost never talks. How can you show someone you care without words, and when do you need words?
3. Is it ever wrong to not read the room?
4. What makes a relationship fair to both people?
