**Premise.** Four early short stories. **"Mermaid Rhapsody"** (2014): Toshihide, whose late mother was a mermaid, plays an underwater piano; the mermaid Shiju saves him from drowning and he teaches her to play, while a legend says mermaids eat people; in a blood frenzy she bites off his ear (TV Tropes). **"Woke-Up-as-a-Girl Syndrome"** (2017): a different Toshihide, a high-school boy, wakes up as a girl with an incurable condition; classmates harass him and some boys proposition him; his girlfriend Rie pressures him, and her brother Akira is kind; the ending stays open (Wikipedia; TV Tropes; AIPT). **"Nayuta of the Prophecy"** (2015): Kenji cares for his little sister Nayuta, a horned child with no human heart who was prophesied to end the world; frightened people turn on the family, Nayuta strikes back with a sky of swords, and Kenji scolds her and vows to keep protecting her (Wikipedia; ANN anime review; ending in the manga to verify). **"Sisters"** (2018): art student Mitsuko is mocked when her younger sister's prize-winning nude portrait of her hangs at the school entrance for a year; she paints a nude of her sister in revenge, and ends by realizing how much her sister admires her (Wikipedia; AIPT; ANN).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What do you do when someone you love is, or becomes, the thing everyone fears? A boy befriends a creature said to eat people; a boy wakes up in a body his classmates mock; a brother raises a sister who may end the world; a girl is humiliated by the sister who adores her.

**The answer the story gives.** Stay. Kenji's care for Nayuta is the clearest picture: he is afraid of her and does not leave. Mitsuko's revenge turns into recognition that her sister's painting was admiration, not mockery (AIPT). The answer is not sentimental: the mermaid still bites, Nayuta still kills, and Toshihide's story ends unresolved.

**Characters as arguments.** Kenji argues for love that does not need the loved one to be safe. The townspeople argue for fear, and the story sides against them. Rie argues for love on her own terms, and the story lets that go unexamined, which is where critics push back (Bolts). Mitsuko argues for pride and envy, and loses the argument to her own better self.

**Symbols and motifs.** The underwater piano: music where breath runs out. The prophecy: a label that decides a child's fate before birth. The nude portrait: the same image read as mockery by a crowd and as love by the artist. Horns and teeth: Fujimoto's women are both vulnerable and dangerous (Real Sound).

**Traditions it echoes.** The Little Mermaid and Japanese mermaid legends; prophecy tales in which fear of a child brings the doom it predicts; Look Back's portrait of artistic rivalry (AIPT).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Existentialism and expressive individualism are explored rather than preached: identity is something the characters must choose under pressure. Progressivism's gender theme is sympathetic but, for some critics, clumsy. Communities are almost always shown as mobs.

**Shifts across the series.** The book runs from a gentle romance (2014) through a loud comedy (2017) and a dark fable (2015) to a quiet realistic drama (2018); critics see the art improving and the last two stories as strongest (ANN; AIPT).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why does Kenji keep protecting Nayuta when he is afraid of her?
2. The town fears Nayuta because of a prophecy. Can fear make a prophecy come true?
