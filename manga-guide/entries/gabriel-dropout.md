**Premise.** Angels who pass their school in Heaven are sent to Earth to live among humans, attend high school and guide people toward happiness. Gabriel White Tenma graduated first in her class, but on Earth she discovers online games and becomes a slob: messy apartment, tracksuit, skipped homework, money spent on in-game purchases, and a part-time job at a coffee shop to pay for more (Wikipedia; Japanese Wikipedia). Her neighbor and best friend Vignette ("Vigne") is a demon who is kind, tidy and responsible; Satanichia ("Satanya") is a would-be "great demon" whose evil schemes always backfire; Raphiel, the second-ranked angel, smiles sweetly while teasing Satanya for fun. Later arrivals include Tapris, a younger angel who idolized Gabriel (ch. 41 onward introduces her demon friend Kurona per the fan wiki), Gabriel's strict older sister Zelel, Raphiel's housekeeper Martiel (ch. 75, vol. 10) and, in the third year (from about ch. 94), Kira Michael Abbey Road, a former classmate who wants to become an idol (Japanese Wikipedia; fan wiki). The series is episodic gag comedy across the girls' three years of high school; Heaven and Hell cut or raise their allowances depending on behavior. How the final chapter (2026) resolves the story was not found in sources opened.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What makes someone good: what they are, or what they do? Gabriel is an angel and acts like a slob; Vignette is a demon and acts like a saint; Satanya wants to be evil and cannot manage it. The series turns the question into a running joke.

**The answer the story gives.** What you do matters more than what you are called, and friends help you do it. Gabriel is lazy, but she visits Vigne when she is sick and, in the anime's second OVA, breaks Heaven's rules to heal a blind girl and does not regret it (Japanese Wikipedia). Vigne's goodness is never in doubt.

**Characters as arguments.** Gabriel argues for comfort and play over duty. Vigne argues for care and responsibility. Satanya argues for pride and grand gestures that always fail. Raphiel argues for mischief as entertainment, then softens (Wikipedia). Zelel argues for discipline and is scared off by a dog. Tapris argues for idealism, and keeps faith in Gabriel.

**Symbols and motifs.** Gabriel's halo, which turns dark with her lifestyle and is wiped clean before visits home (Japanese Wikipedia). The trumpet of the end of the world, kept in a messy room. Satanya's melon bread and the dog that steals it. The allowance from Heaven or Hell: goodness measured in pocket money.

**Traditions it echoes.** The fallen-angel story played as comedy (ANN's Austin Price notes the Lucifer-fall echo); the cute-girls gag manga; and the role-reversal comedy. For Christian, Jewish and Muslim readers the angel names carry weight the series does not intend.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Hedonism is laughed at, not praised. Communitarian care wins. Moralistic therapeutic deism describes Heaven itself: a lenient, happiness-minded system easily fooled. Consumerism is a gentle joke. Religious imagery is decoration, never doctrine.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-5 (ch. 1-39) are the anime's material: the cast, the coffee shop, the beach, Christmas and New Year, the visit home. Later volumes add Kurona (ch. 41), Martiel (ch. 75) and a third school year with Michael (from about ch. 94). Raphiel grows kinder over time (Wikipedia). The ending (2026) was not found in sources opened.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Gabriel was the best student in angel school. What happened to her on Earth?
2. Why is Vigne, a demon, the most responsible person in the story?
3. Is Raphiel's teasing of Satanya funny or mean? Where is the line?
4. Gabriel breaks Heaven's rules to help a girl. Was she right?
5. How much gaming is too much? How would you know?
