**Premise.** Ninon "Nino" Okamura, a first-year high school student who is part French through her mother, has withdrawn into herself since elementary school, when a boy she stood up to hit her with a plastic bat and left a scar on her forehead (Mangapedia). She talks only to her parents and her pet bird Sensei, a cockatiel who understands people and speaks in Kansai dialect (English Wikipedia calls him a parrot). Her parents tell her that her popular next-door neighbor and classmate, Yuiji Kira, has a heart disease and probably a year to live; Sensei reports that Kira cries alone, saying he does not want to die (Mangapedia). Nino vows to stay by his side for that year and urges him to give up his playboy, "lewd" lifestyle and fill his days with happiness (English Wikipedia). They fall in love knowing they may have no future. Along the way: Kira's brief involvement with the school nurse, Mio Shitara, who is grieving her fiancé (English Wikipedia; Mangapedia says she rejects his feelings); Kira's friend Kazuhiro Yabe, who once led the bullying of Nino, humbles himself and falls for her; and Rei, who poses as Kira's ex to test Nino and later becomes her friend and Yabe's girlfriend. Kira's father, a widower, works in drag as "Tiara" at a bar to pay for his son's treatment. Kira's heart stops (vol. 9, Kodansha); treatment overseas saves him but leaves the family in debt and moves them to Osaka. Sensei develops a tumor and dies, leaving a video telling Nino to lift her head and live. An epilogue shows Nino and Kira married, with a son (English Wikipedia).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** If someone you barely know may have only a year to live, what do you owe them, and what do you owe yourself? Nino has hidden from everyone since she was hurt; Kira has hidden his fear behind jokes. A deadline forces both out.

**The answer the story gives.** Stay, speak up and love fully. Nino's promise to stand by Kira changes both of them; friends who once hurt her become allies. The story then rewards that courage with survival and a family (English Wikipedia).

**Characters as arguments.** Nino argues that a frightened person can still keep a promise. Kira argues that a cheerful mask hides real fear. Yabe and Rei argue that cruelty can be repented of. Sensei, the protective, jealous bird, argues for letting go: his last message tells Nino to lift her head and live. The nurse is the cautionary figure of an adult who leans on a teenager in her grief.

**Symbols and motifs.** Tokyo Tower, where Kira retreats and where the epilogue family visits; Nino's hidden scar and her bangs; Kira's camera and photos of Nino (Mangapedia); the talking bird and his farewell video.

**Traditions it echoes.** The Japanese illness romance (a teen with little time left), shōjo comedy, and the guardian-animal story. Religious readers may hear echoes of visiting the sick and of grieving with hope.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Existentialism appears as the choice of how to live a limited time; communitarian friendship and secular hope (medicine) carry the ending. No religious lens is present.

**Shifts across the series.** Early volumes set up Nino's vow and Kira's secret; the middle deals with rivals and the nurse (likely vols. 3-6); the last volumes bring Kira's collapse, treatment, the move to Osaka and Sensei's death (vol. 9 and likely 8) (Kodansha; English Wikipedia).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Nino was hurt by bullies and stopped talking to people. What helped her open up?
2. Kira jokes all the time but cries alone. Why do people hide their fear?
3. Yabe once bullied Nino and later became her friend. What did it take for him to change?
4. The school nurse turned to a student for comfort. Why is that not okay for an adult?
5. Sensei's last message tells Nino to keep living. How can we remember someone we lose?
