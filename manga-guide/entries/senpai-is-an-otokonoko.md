**Premise.** Makoto Hanaoka, a second-year high school boy, loves cute things. At a school whose operator (a friend of his father) lets him dress as he likes, he wears the girls' sailor uniform and a long wig, though everyone knows he is a boy; at home his mother wants a strong, manly son, so he hides it from her. First-year Saki Aoi, bright, lonely and raised mostly by her grandmother, confesses to him believing he is a girl; when he tells her he is a boy, she is unbothered and still wants to be his first love. His childhood friend Ryuji Taiga, who has loved Makoto since they were small, joins them, and a gentle three-way story of friendship and unspoken love unfolds (Wikipedia; Japanese Wikipedia; ANN). The roots of the mother's fear lie in her own father, who also dressed in women's clothes and whom she cut off (Japanese Wikipedia).

## Creator and publisher context

- Pom, a female manga artist who debuted through LINE Manga's frontier program for independent creators; the series was signed for weekly serialization within four months (Wikipedia).
- Origin (Wikipedia; Japanese Wikipedia, citing Oricon): Pom found boys hard to draw and first considered a romance between two girls; an acquaintance suggested a boy who dresses as a girl. She chose a serious tone because she likes reading serious stories, and kept the cast to three main characters.
- Stated themes (Wikipedia): human relationships, love regardless of gender, respecting diversity, a boy being able to like cute things, and taking care of oneself. She drew Makoto as male on the inside who dresses femininely because he likes the look, and did not write specifically to fight prejudice. She called Makoto's father her ideal of a father (Japanese Wikipedia).
- She planned only 16 chapters and let the story grow from the characters; she ended at chapter 100 to finish on a high point (Wikipedia).
- Publishers: LINE Digital Frontier and Ichijinsha in Japan; Webtoon and Kodansha USA in English. Kodansha's age rating not confirmed.
- No controversies involving the creator were found.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can you be loved for exactly who you are? Makoto likes cute things and wears the girls' uniform, but hides it from the one person whose love matters most, his mother. Saki loves him without caring what he is; Ryuji loves him and hides it. The series asks what it costs to hide, and what it costs to be seen.

**The answer the story gives.** Honesty is worth the risk. Each turn of the story comes when someone stops hiding: Makoto's reveal to Saki (ch. 1), Ryuji's feelings (ch. 22-26), Makoto's coming clean to his mother (likely ch. 44-48), Hayase's apology (ch. 65-68). Pom framed it as a story about how a person works through a problem (Japanese Wikipedia).

**Characters as arguments.** Makoto argues that liking cute things does not make a boy less a boy. Saki argues for loving the person, not the category. Ryuji argues for loyal love that does not demand. The mother argues, from fear, that a son must be manly; her hurt over her own father explains her, and her change shows that fear can be unlearned. The father argues for quiet, practical support.

**Symbols and motifs.** The wig and the sailor uniform; whales (Saki's father studies them; ch. 28); rain and sunshine (the film's title, and the last chapter, "Sunny Days"); nail polish (ch. 70).

**Traditions it echoes.** Japanese otokonoko and josei romance, coming-of-age school stories, and family dramas of reconciliation. Religious readers may hear echoes of the prodigal-son pattern in the mother's return to her son and in her estranged father.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism and progressive acceptance are affirmed. Traditional gender norms are critiqued, but through a mother who is loved and who changes, not a villain. Communitarian care (friends, family) is how the individual finds freedom.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-3 (ch. 1-26): the confession, the three-way friendship, Ryuji's feelings. Volumes 4-6 (ch. 27-60): school festival, family confrontations at Christmas and New Year. Volumes 7-8 (ch. 61-80): the school trip and reconciliation with Hayase; Makoto's grandfather. Volumes 9-10 (ch. 81-100): choices, parents, and the end of the triangle.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why did Makoto hide his clothes from his mother but not from his friends?
2. What made his mother so afraid, and what helped her change?
3. Saki says Makoto's gender does not matter to her. What does she love about him?
4. Ryuji kept his feelings secret for years. Was that kind, or did it hurt him?
5. How can families who disagree still keep loving each other?
