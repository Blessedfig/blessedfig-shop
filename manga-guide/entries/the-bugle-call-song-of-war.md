**Premise.** In Arboral Year 1294, nine Spires, towers holding relics of an advanced ancient civilization, dominate a Europe-like continent; the two greatest are held by the Papal States (the Holy Realm), led by the Pontiff of the Spirism faith, and the Erin Empire, which wage the War of Spires (Wikipedia; VIZ). People born with branch-like growths, the Branched (or Branch-hexed), gain powers at 14 that work only near a Spire. Luca, a 14-year-old bugler raised by mercenaries who dreams of becoming a maestro, gains the power to turn the sound of his horn into light in battle; his adoptive father Gerhart, dying, asks the Pontiff to teach him music (fan wiki). The Pontiff, always helmeted, makes Luca captain of his Branched squad, promising him music lessons and his freedom once they kill the Garland, a 13-year-old Branched girl of the Empire who calls down meteors that destroy whole countries. The squad includes Zoe, 27 but with the mind of a 9-year-old, who believes the Pontiff is her mother; Miura, sole survivor of a nation the Garland destroyed; Oskar, devoted to the Pontiff for raising his sister from the dead; and Demi, bought from a farm where her brother was worked to death (Wikipedia; fan wiki). Campaigns follow in Gallia, the Land of Mirrors (v3-5), and in Normandy, the Land of Thralls, ruled by a Branched who makes slave-soldier copies of himself (v5-9). Luca learns the Pontiff impersonates the dead, had Zoe conditioned as a weapon and has an ally secretly killed to harvest his power; later volumes reveal the Pontiff is not human and that an alien entity, the Druid, brought the Branched powers (fan wiki; VIZ v10-11).

## Life message

**Even a child pulled into war can keep his conscience and his dream; power that uses people, however kindly it speaks, must be questioned, and comrades must be treated as people, not weapons.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Can you stay yourself when someone kind is using you? Luca is given everything he asked for, music, a home, a title, by a ruler who needs him to kill.

**The answer the story gives.** Only by watching what the kind voice does. Luca keeps asking the Pontiff for the truth and holds him to his promises; he protects Zoe and Miura rather than letting them be used up. The answer is not yet complete: the war goes on, and Luca himself becomes a battlefield commander.

**Characters as arguments.** The Pontiff argues that any act is justified to protect the Spire, and he argues it politely. Oskar argues for gratitude so deep it becomes blindness; Zahid argues for telling a friend the truth. Zoe is the cost of using people: a woman with a child's mind who kills without seeing enemies as people. Demi, who watched her brother die in serfdom, argues for hard realism. The Doppelganger and the Garland, enemies, are shown as lonely children of their own system.

**Symbols and motifs.** The bugle that turns sound into light: art used to guide killing. The helmet: a face no one sees, and faces borrowed from the dead. Branches: power that grows from the body like a tree. The Spires: holy places that are really machines.

**Traditions it echoes.** Critics compare it to Berserk and Vinland Saga (Wikipedia; Walt's Comic Shop); the Children's-Crusade image of the young sent to holy war; Mont-Saint-Michel and Hundred Years' War settings (from general knowledge; to verify).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Authoritarian control is the main target; utilitarian ends-justify-means reasoning belongs to the villain. Expressive individualism (the dream of music) and conscience are affirmed. Religion is shown as a tool of rule in this world; whether any faith is true is not addressed.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-2: recruitment and the first siege. Volumes 3-5: Gallia, the Looking Glass and Zoe's past. Volumes 5-9: Normandy, the Land of Thralls and Mont Tombe; Luca's "flowering." Volumes 9-11: the truth about the Spires, a hidden town, the alien Druid (VIZ). Later volumes: the final battle (Wikipedia).

**Discussion questions.**
1. The Pontiff gives Luca what he wants. Why doesn't Luca fully trust him?
2. Zoe believes the Pontiff is her mother. Is it ever right to deceive someone for their own good?
3. Oskar serves the Pontiff because he brought back his sister. What would you do in Oskar's place?
4. Luca uses music to win battles. Does that make his music less beautiful?
5. The story's church is fictional. What does it say about how power can use religion, and what does it not say?
