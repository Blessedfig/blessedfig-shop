**Premise.** Okuto Nakamura, a shy 16-year-old who is gay and loves octopuses, is now friends with his classmate and crush Aiki Hirose (end of vol. 1), but being close to him is its own torture. Short comic chapters follow him through a summer dream that ends with his pet octopus stuck to his face (ch. 1); the occult club choosing him as a 'sacrifice' for a summer séance until a student filmmaker drafts him into a night-time horror mockumentary (ch. 2); a strict new gym teacher, Nio (ch. 3); jealousy when classmate Takeuchi acts like Hirose's boyfriend to get a girl's attention (ch. 4); a trip to a beauty salon (ch. 6); Hirose's middle-school friend Matsumura, a rival who is also infatuated with Hirose (ch. 7); and a muscle-building scheme after Nakamura decides Hirose likes strong men (ch. 8). Running through the volume: Hirose's one-sided admiration and crush on their homeroom teacher, Otogiri (25), which Nakamura watches in alarm, and a short relationship between Hirose and a girl from another class that leaves Nakamura heartbroken until it ends and Hirose confides in him (Wikipedia; TV Tropes; Kotaku; M.J. Lyons; Boys Love Library). The volume ends without a confession, close to where it began (MyAnimeList review; Boys Love Library).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What do you do when you are finally friends with the person you love, and that is not enough? Nakamura has his friendship; now he has to watch Hirose with other people.

**The answer the story gives.** Stay kind and patient. Nakamura's jealousy keeps making him ridiculous, but when Hirose is dumped, Nakamura puts Hirose's feelings first and Hirose confides in him (M.J. Lyons). The story does not resolve the romance; it shows that loving someone includes wanting them to be happy.

**Characters as arguments.** Nakamura argues for steady, unselfish love. Hirose argues for openness: he tries dating a girl and is unsure what he wants. Takeuchi shows that affection can be a game (ch. 4). Matsumura is Nakamura's mirror, a devotion that turns possessive (ch. 7). Otogiri is the adult every student looks up to, and the story's weakest point: Hirose's crush on him is played for laughs, and reviewers and the creator later agreed it needed clearer boundaries.

**Symbols and motifs.** The octopus stuck to his face as the dream ends (ch. 1). The horror mockumentary in the dark school (ch. 2). Protein shakes and muscles, Nakamura trying to become someone else (ch. 8). The haircut (ch. 6).

**Traditions it echoes.** 1980s-90s Japanese romantic comedy (reviewers of the first volume named Ranma 1/2), BL's school-romance conventions, including the teacher crush that newer readers question. For religious readers it raises questions about friendship, jealousy and boundaries.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism and progressivism are assumed rather than argued. Virtue is shown in loyalty. The occult is only a joke.

**Shifts across the series.** The volume is episodic: summer (ch. 1-2), new teachers and jealousy (ch. 3-5), self-improvement and rivals (ch. 6-8), plus a newly drawn extra. The anime reorders these chapters among the first volume's (MangaUpdates).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Nakamura is jealous of Takeuchi and Matsumura. What does jealousy make him do, and what does he do instead when Hirose is hurt?
2. Hirose has a crush on his teacher. Why do teachers have to keep clear boundaries with students, even when a student admires them?
3. Is it okay for Nakamura to keep his feelings secret? What would change if he told Hirose?
4. Matsumura and Nakamura both "collect" things about Hirose. When does admiring someone become too much?
5. What makes a friend worth waiting for?
