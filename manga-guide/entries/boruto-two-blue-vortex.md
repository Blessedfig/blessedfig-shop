**Premise.** At the end of Part I, the cyborg Eida ("Ada" in VIZ's translation) used her power, Omnipotence, to rewrite the memories of the whole world: Boruto Uzumaki, son of the Seventh Hokage Naruto, is now remembered as the one who attacked his family, while his adopted brother Kawaki, who actually sealed Naruto and Hinata away in another dimension, is remembered as their true son. Only Sarada Uchiha and Sumire know the truth (Wikipedia). Three years later, Boruto, now about sixteen (from general knowledge; to verify), returns to Konoha after training with Sasuke and the mysterious Koji Kashin, and drives off the villain Code and his monsters (ch. 1-4). The Ten-Tails has become self-aware "Human God Trees" (Jura, Hidari, Matsuri, Ryū, Mamushi), each born from a person the Claw Grimes devoured and turned into a tree, including Sasuke (ch. 5, 12). Acting Hokage Shikamaru learns the truth and works with Boruto in secret while the village still hunts him (Wikipedia; ch. 6). Boruto's sister Himawari gains the Nine-Tails' chakra and healing power (ch. 10-11); Sarada awakens her Mangekyō Sharingan in the Land of Wind (ch. 21); Kawaki keeps Naruto and Hinata sealed and vows to kill Boruto (ch. 38) (fan wiki chapter summaries).

**Doing right and protecting the people you love matters even when no one believes you; truth and loyalty outlast a lie the whole world has accepted.**

**Where traditions disagree.** Nearly every frame honors the core story: a falsely accused son who keeps protecting his family and village, and friends who hold on to the truth. ## Creator and publisher context

- Mikio Ikemoto (story and art) worked as Masashi Kishimoto's assistant on *Naruto*; Kishimoto recommended him when Shueisha asked for a sequel and urged him to develop his own art style (Wikipedia).
- Masashi Kishimoto created *Naruto* (1999-2014; from general knowledge). He supervised *Boruto*, wrote Part I from November 2020, and in August 2024 said Ikemoto is fully in charge of the story and art (Wikipedia).
- On-record intent (Wikipedia): Ikemoto modeled Boruto partly on Neo from *The Matrix*, a possible savior of his people; he planned for the Boruto-Kawaki relationship to become the story's center; he hoped to finish the whole *Boruto* manga in about 30 volumes; monthly chapters often exceed 40 pages.
- No statements on religion or politics were found. Part I writer Ukyō Kodachi linked the setting's peace-after-war mood to the end of the Cold War (Wikipedia).
- Publisher: Shueisha (*V Jump*); VIZ Media (Shonen Jump imprint) in English, rated Teen. No controversies involving the creators were found in sources opened.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What do you do when everyone you love believes a lie about you? Boruto has lost his name, his home and his parents to a power that rewrote the world's memory. The series asks whether goodness needs to be seen to be real, and whether truth matters if almost no one remembers it.

**The answer the story gives.** Keep protecting people anyway, and let the truth come back one person at a time. Boruto returns not to clear his name but to stop Code (ch. 1-4); Sarada, Sumire and then Shikamaru stand with him; by ch. 25 the devoured are being freed. The story treats loyalty without reward as the real test of a hero.

**Characters as arguments.** Boruto argues for faithfulness without recognition. Kawaki is the dark mirror: he loves Naruto so much that he imprisons him and plans to kill Boruto, and the story treats that love as genuine and wrong (ch. 38). Sarada argues for holding to the truth against everyone. Koji argues that foresight justifies losses, and Boruto pushes back (ch. 21). The God Trees argue that humans are food; Jura's tears over a novel (ch. 26) hint that even they can change.

**Symbols and motifs.** Trees that grow from the devoured, and "Thornsoul" bulbs that can bring them back. Boruto's lost eye and borrowed sword (from Sasuke). The Rasengan reworked as the planet's own spin (ch. 3). Memory itself, as something that can be stolen.

**Traditions it echoes.** The shōnen hero's journey and the wrongly accused exile (The Count of Monte Cristo, Joseph in Genesis). Ikemoto names Neo from The Matrix as an influence: a savior who sees through a false world (Wikipedia).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Virtue and community win. Might-makes-right is the villains' creed and is defeated by planning and teamwork. Transhumanism is ambiguous: cyborgs are both victims and threats. Truth-as-consensus (the Omnipotence premise) is clearly rejected.

**Shifts across the series.** Ch. 1-15 (Boruto's Return): Boruto comes back, the God Trees appear and Himawari gains power. Ch. 16-25 (Matsuri and Ryū): the Land of Wind mission, Yodo's sacrifice and Sarada's awakening; the devoured are freed. Ch. 26-34 (Mamushi): Inojin joins Koji, Ada chooses friendship, Boruto's bargain with Momoshiki. Ch. 35-38: Kawaki captures Koji and vows to kill Boruto.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Boruto keeps protecting people who think he is a criminal. Why does he do it?
2. Sarada believes Boruto even though everyone's memory says otherwise. How do you decide what is true when everyone disagrees with you?
3. Kawaki says he imprisons Naruto to protect him. Is that love? Where did it go wrong?
4. Boruto makes a deal with Momoshiki, the being inside him. Is that wise or dangerous?
5. Yodo dies to help her friends. How should we honor sacrifices like that?
