**Premise.** Ao Kurose is a timid first-year on the health committee whom others push around; the other members keep leaving the cleaning to him, so he is often in the nurse's office (Japanese Wikipedia). There he meets second-year Mio Haimiya, who has a gray wolf cut, piercings, gyaru styling and a blunt Kansai dialect, and whom the whole school fears as a delinquent who looks down on people. To Kurose alone she is kind and gentle. They become friends while helping the school nurse, Ms. Aya; Haimiya gets jealous when Kurose befriends his classmate Tanzaki, a basketball player, something he does not notice. Kurose gradually realizes he has fallen for her, and she has feelings for him too (English Wikipedia). Later arcs widen the circle: Shiu Koyuki, a classmate obsessed with cute things, blackmails Haimiya into covering a daycare shift but turns out to want her friendship and had been leaving her encouraging notes about her frequent absences; her little brother Mashiro, who struggles with family and school, opens up when Haimiya tells him how she got past her own skipping of classes. Kurose, who quit music under high expectations, later joins a school band (Wikipedia; AniList characters).

## Life message

**People are more than their reputations, and quiet kindness can draw a lonely person out; friendship grows when someone chooses to see you.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** Are people who they look like? Everyone is sure Haimiya is scary; she is not. Kurose is sure he has no friends; that changes.

**The answer the story gives.** No: kindness is often hidden, and it takes someone patient to see it. Haimiya's gentleness to Kurose is the first crack in her reputation; Kurose's steady service in the infirmary is what earns her trust.

**Characters as arguments.** Haimiya argues that a rough exterior can hide a kind heart. Kurose argues that quiet, unnoticed work is worth doing. Koyuki shows that even scheming can come from loneliness, and Mashiro that a troubled child can be reached by someone who has been there.

**Symbols and motifs.** The nurse's office as a refuge; the carp Haimiya feeds; her contacts and glasses; music Kurose gave up and returns to; sticky notes of encouragement (Wikipedia; Japanese Wikipedia).

**Traditions it echoes.** The "gap moe" romantic comedy (a scary-looking girl who is sweet) and the senpai romance; for faith readers, the heart over appearance (1 Samuel 16:7) and visiting the sick (Matthew 25:36).

**How each lens is portrayed.** Community and individuality both win: Haimiya keeps her style and gains friends. No lens is critiqued.

**Shifts across the series.** Early chapters are short comic vignettes in the infirmary; later arcs add friends (Tanzaki, Koyuki, Mashiro) and a school band. The series is ongoing at ch. 49.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why do people at school think Haimiya is scary? Were they right?
2. Kurose always gets stuck with the cleaning. Was that unfair, or did it turn into something good?
3. Koyuki tricked Haimiya into helping her. Why did Haimiya forgive her?
4. Is jealousy ever cute? When does it become a problem?
5. Who is someone you misjudged at first?
