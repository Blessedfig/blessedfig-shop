**Premise.** Shiki Ichinose, a foul-mouthed teenager expelled from high school for fighting, argues with his adoptive father Tsuyoshi just before an assassin from the Momotarou Agency attacks. Shiki learns he has oni blood, and that Tsuyoshi was a Momotarou, a hunter of oni, who left the agency rather than kill the infant Shiki. Tsuyoshi dies holding off the attacker; Shiki's blood power awakens in a berserk rage (ch. 1-4). The stern teacher Naito Mudano takes him to Rasetsu Academy, a school that trains oni to fight the Momotarou, and Shiki vows revenge. The series inverts the Momotaro folk tale: the peach boy's heirs are a secret agency that hunts oni as vermin. Arcs follow the class into battles in Kyoto, Nerima, the Kegon Falls research facility, Koenji, Matsumoto and Toyosu (fan wiki). Along the way Shiki befriends Mikado, a kindly Momotarou who begins to doubt the war (ch. 37-76, 221-227); the existence of oni is made public and an "Oni Extermination Priority Act" lets Momotarou kill oni openly (ch. 228-272). Shiki is one of eight children of the Kishin, a demon-god bloodline, and the latest arcs search for hidden archives about the oni's past (ch. 273-on).

## Life message

**Treating a whole people as vermin is the true monstrosity: stand by the family that takes you in and fight back against those who hunt you, but do not hate every member of the other side.**

**Where traditions disagree.** Most traditions welcome the adoptive father's sacrifice and the condemnation of persecution. The divide is over two things: the heroes' "demon" bloodline and spirit guidance, which Pentecostal, Baptist, Adventist and Jehovah's Witness readings weigh heavily (D or F) while secular, Pagan and Shinto readings treat as folklore; and revenge, which Buddhist, Jewish and peace-church readings see as the story's fuel (C or D), while Satanist and some secular readings accept it as resistance. Jewish, Black Protestant and progressive readings give extra weight to the legalized-persecution arcs.

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What if the hero of the fairy tale was the villain? Every Japanese child knows Momotaro, who sails to Onigashima and defeats the oni. Tougen Anki asks what that story looks like to the oni, and whether a people can be monsters by birth.

**The answer the story gives.** No one is a monster by blood. The Momotarou who treat oni as pests are the cruelest characters, and as the story widens, a state law and blood tests make persecution systematic (ch. 206-272). But the story does not flip the hierarchy: Shiki refuses to judge all Momotarou, protects his friend Mikado (ch. 37-76), and is shaken by killing (ch. 163-205), while oni extremists who want every Momotarou dead are shown as a danger too (ch. 89-162).

**Characters as arguments.** Tsuyoshi argues that love outranks duty to a cause: he quit the agency rather than kill a baby. Mudano argues for discipline as survival. Jin, whose father tried to kill him, argues for revenge; Shiki argues for protecting the innocent first. Mikado is the conscience of the other side. Tsubakiri, made cruel by grief, shows how loss can harden into hatred; Momoiwa shows ambition without conscience; Ukyo uses a boy's love for his dead sister as a leash.

**Symbols and motifs.** Blood: a weapon, a test of identity and a family tie. Peaches and oni horns (Shiki learns to hide his, ch. 14-36). Fire: Shiki's inherited element, blue flames that he learns to control (ch. 228-279). Archives hidden by the ancients, which may reveal the true origin of the war (ch. 273-on).

**Traditions it echoes.** The Momotaro folktale and Setsubun oni lore; supernatural-school shonen such as Blue Exorcist and Soul Eater (Yatta-Tachi); Tokyo Ghoul's hunted species and the agency that hunts them (Anime UK News). Its anti-persecution arcs echo many histories of hunted minorities without naming one.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Critical theory's focus on dehumanization is affirmed; the Momotarou Agency is authoritarian and critiqued. Communitarian found family carries the hope. Pacifism is a minor but growing voice (Mikado). Folklore and spirits are the setting, not a creed.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-5 (ch. 1-36): origin, school and the Kyoto battle. Volumes 5-10 (ch. 37-88): Nerima, Mikado and training. Volumes 11-19 (ch. 89-162): the Kegon Falls raid and the Kikoku Unit. Volumes 19-25 (ch. 163-220): betrayal and blood tests. Volumes 25-31 (ch. 221-272): Mikado's doubts and the Extermination Act. From ch. 273: the search for the ancient archives.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Tsuyoshi was trained to kill oni, yet he raised one. What changed his mind?
2. How does calling a group "pests" make it easier to hurt them? Where else has that happened?
3. Shiki wants revenge but refuses to hate every Momotarou. Can both be true?
4. The Kikoku Unit wants to kill all Momotarou. How are they like the people they fight?
5. Why do you think Mikado started to doubt what he was taught?
