**Premise.** Wako Machida, a tall, plain-dressing manga editor, and Meguru Soma, a slight, beautiful young man who works in a used-clothing shop and models in the "genderless" fashion subculture with a large social-media following, have been a couple since high school and now live together. Meguru cooks, keeps house and does his makeup to be beautiful for Wako; strangers keep taking him for a woman or the couple for lesbians or a gay pair. Volume 2 adds Sasame, an androgynous model who wants to be seen as manly, and the two form the idol unit "Unicorn Boys"; volume 3 brings a sleepover at the cool model Kira's house and a mysterious delivery man; volume 4 the unit's big break and Wako's fangirling; volume 5 their first TV drama, the end of the unit, Meguru's move into a makeup career and a marriage proposal (Wikipedia; Seven Seas blurbs).

## Life message

**Love means supporting your partner as they are: how a man dresses or wears makeup says nothing about his love or his worth, and a couple can make its own kind of happiness.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What does it mean to be a man, or a good partner, when the man you love wears makeup? Meguru's look makes strangers misread him; Wako sees only how he loves her.

**The answer the story gives.** Appearance and love are separate questions. Meguru is beautiful by choice and devoted by character; Wako, who cares little for her own looks, loves beauty in others and loves him for his care. The story ends with a proposal, uniting a nontraditional look with a traditional commitment.

**Characters as arguments.** Meguru argues that gender expression is personal. Sasame argues the other side from inside the same world: he wants to be seen as manly despite his cute image (Wikipedia). Wako argues that work and love matter more than appearance. The misgendering strangers argue nothing; they are simply mistaken.

**Symbols and motifs.** Makeup as a gift to the beloved; mirrors and photo shoots; social-media posts curated by Wako; the shared apartment; the proposal.

**Traditions it echoes.** Josei office romance, bishonen aesthetics and the shojo tradition of beautiful boys; the "gender-bender" genre that The Beat says it improves on.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism is affirmed throughout; progressive views of gender are assumed rather than argued. Consumer culture is the background, celebrated. Traditional marriage reappears at the end as the natural next step.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1: daily life of the couple. Vols. 2-4: the Unicorn Boys idol unit, friends and fandom. Vol. 5: TV drama, careers, and the proposal (Seven Seas blurbs).

**Discussion questions.**
1. People keep assuming things about Meguru from his clothes. Why do we do that?
2. What does Meguru do that shows love? What does Wako do?
3. Meguru is asked to hide his relationship for his career. Should he?
4. Why do you think the story ends with a proposal?
5. Sasame wants to look manly. Is that any less valid than Meguru's choice?
