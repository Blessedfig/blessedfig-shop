**Premise.** Minoru Soramoto, a high school student, is abandoned by his parents, who vanish and leave him their debts; debt collectors are about to have his organs sold when his neighbor, a beautiful young woman he knows only as "Onee-san," pays off everything and takes him into her apartment (vol. 1; Wikipedia). She dotes on him without limit, pays for everything and wants to know everything about him, but will not reveal her own name or job (vol. 2). Her first name, Shiori, comes out in vol. 3 (TV Tropes: ch. 59). A letter from his parents threatens to end the arrangement (vol. 4); by vol. 5 Minoru stays by choice and wants to confess his feelings properly. His cousin Hana arrives claiming the right to look after him (vol. 7); the two kiss (vols. 8-9); after a night in a hotel they become a couple (vol. 11); Shiori's job and a bigger secret are revealed (vol. 12); and in the last volume the couple, both inexperienced, move toward a sexual relationship, Minoru graduates, and a time skip leads to the finale (vol. 13) (Kodansha blurbs). Database tags point to marriage and a pregnancy in the ending (AniList; MangaUpdates; to verify).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What happens when someone saves you and asks nothing in return? Minoru is abandoned by his parents and rescued by a stranger who pays his debts and wants only to take care of him. The series asks what he owes her and what kind of love can grow from that start.

**The answer the story gives.** It turns into romance. Minoru chooses to stay (vol. 5), the two grow closer through a kiss (vols. 8-9), a hotel night (vol. 11) and a sexual relationship (vols. 12-13), and the tags point to marriage and a child. The story never asks whether an adult who houses and pays for a teenager should become his partner; it treats the question as already answered by their feelings.

**Characters as arguments.** Shiori argues for total, generous devotion, but also for knowing everything about him while hiding herself (vol. 2). Minoru argues for gratitude turning into love. Hana, the cousin, argues that family should care for him (vol. 7) and is cast as a rival. The parents are the case of care abandoned.

**Symbols and motifs.** The debt paid off. The apartment next door. Her hidden name and job, revealed bit by bit (vols. 3, 8, 12). Graduation as the threshold before the time skip (vol. 13).

**Traditions it echoes.** The onee-san romantic comedy and "sugar" provider stories with roles reversed (from general knowledge; to verify); Cinderella with the genders flipped.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Hedonism and individual feeling are affirmed. Consumerism appears as money equal to care. No lens challenges the relationship.

**Shifts across the series.** Vols. 1-4: rescue, mystery and comedy. Vols. 5-8: Minoru stays by choice; rivals and secrets. Vols. 9-11: first kiss, the cousin's confession, becoming a couple. Vols. 12-13: intimacy, her secret, graduation, time skip and ending (Kodansha blurbs).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Minoru's parents left him with their debts. What should have happened to him?
2. Shiori wants to know everything about Minoru but tells him nothing about herself. Is that fair?
3. Does Minoru owe Shiori anything for paying his debt? What?
4. Would this story feel different if a man had taken in a high school girl? Why?
5. What makes a relationship between two people equal?
