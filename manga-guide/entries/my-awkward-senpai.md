**Premise.** Azusa Kannawa, 27, works in the publicity department of a video game company in Tokyo, where coworkers fear her for her strict manner and sharp gaze. In truth she is shy, clumsy and awkward, and her harshness is a front; under stress her Oita (Kyushu) dialect slips out with people she trusts. When she is made mentor to the new hire Yu Kamegawa, 22, a friendly and sociable young man, she struggles to invite him to lunch, overhears gossip about herself and cries in front of him, and slowly falls for him while denying it. He comes to admire and then love her. Short, mostly self-contained chapters follow office projects, a drinking party, an Okinawa business trip, a typhoon day, a fireworks festival, her mother's visit and his sick day (anime eps. 1-10). He blurts out his feelings at an office birthday party, and she answers by demanding a three-month trial relationship (anime ep. 12, about ch. 45). The manga continues to chapter 108 plus extras (Wikipedia; Japanese Wikipedia; MangaUpdates).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What happens when the person everyone fears is actually the most frightened person in the room? Azusa Kannawa's strictness is a defense; the series asks whether someone so practiced at hiding can let another person see her.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, slowly, through someone patient and kind. Yu defends her when coworkers gossip (anime ep. 1), notices her dialect and her fear of thunder, and keeps showing up. She learns to say what she means, first in small things (an invitation to lunch, a birthday gift) and finally in love, on her own awkward terms (a three-month trial, ep. 12).

**Characters as arguments.** Azusa stands for the person who copes by armoring up. Yu stands for openness, which the story shows is also a kind of strength. Hotta and Kankaiji represent friends who see what the two cannot. Azusa's mother shows the pressure of family expectations and also its love: she sees through the fake boyfriend and tells Yu that Azusa's dialect means she trusts him (ep. 7).

**Symbols and motifs.** The Kyushu dialect slipping out as a sign of trust. The cat mug Yu buys after Azusa breaks one (ep. 3) and keeps (ep. 10). Lunch invitations as tests of courage. The shared umbrella in the typhoon (ep. 5).

**Traditions it echoes.** The gap-moe romantic comedy, the Japanese office comedy, and the older story of the stern figure softened by kindness. Faith readers may hear the teaching that people look on outward appearance while the heart is what matters.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism is affirmed gently: being yourself is the goal, but within a caring community. Confucian ethics (the duties of senior to junior) is respected, not questioned. Feminist readers find a competent woman whose story is mostly about romance.

**Shifts across the series.** Volumes 1-3 (about ch. 1-45, the anime): mentoring, office events and the slow realization of feelings. Volumes 4-11 (to ch. 108 plus extras): the trial relationship and after; sources opened do not describe these chapters, and one fan guide says it ends in marriage and family (to verify).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Why does Azusa act so strict at work? Have you ever hidden how you really feel?
2. What does Yu do that helps Azusa open up?
3. The coworkers gossip about Azusa in the first chapter. How does that change later?
4. Yu pretends to be Azusa's boyfriend for her mother. Was that a good idea?
5. Azusa supervises Yu at work. Does that make their relationship harder? Why?
