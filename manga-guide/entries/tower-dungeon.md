**Premise.** A necromancer assassinates the king and carries Princess Ignelia to the top of the Dragon Tower, a colossal stone pillar floating above the mountains. The Royal Guard under Captain Minsabelle (the king's younger sister, part dracomorph and over a century old; AniList) cannot break through, so every village must send one conscript. Yuva, a quiet farm boy with enormous strength, arrives with an upturned pot for a helmet and a stick of firewood, and is mocked until he lifts boulders (ANN). In vol. 1 the Guard frees the fire mage Lilicen from a shapeshifting slime; then an envoy of the Royal Parliament orders the Guard home so that Minsabelle can take the throne, and only the archer Eriquo, Lilicen and Yuva press on as the 7th squad (Anime UK News). Their aim is to reach floor 100 and trade a magical gem for the princess (ANN). They trade salt to a strange, intelligent tower species for a way to level 80 (vol. 2), are ambushed by grieving former guardsmen who think they carry a secret weapon (vol. 2-3; it is the Dragonbane, an arrowhead the captain took without permission), defeat a basilisk on level 59, and learn that Yuva is of royal blood; the princess appears in his dreams and asks him to stop the necromancer from raising a dragon's remains (vol. 3). From vol. 4 a second party joins the climb: the rat-folk princess Churafee and her knight Gesh, the one-winged dracomorph scholar Iscale and his mute valet, the old pyromancer Sargan on a crow-goat, hunting the dracomorph Carache who killed his mother, and the armored knight Aridellia, whose past is told in vol. 5 (Anime UK News; Kodansha synopses). A K Manga chapter title calls her a necromancer (ch. 18(2)).

## Life message

**Plain strength, steadiness and loyalty to the people beside you can carry an ordinary person where titles, armies and schemes fail; fair dealing, even with strange creatures, opens doors that force cannot.**

| Meta lens | How it reads the series |

| Evolutionary biology | A survival climb: a small band shares food, water and salt, guards each other and grows close (the Mindlink rings, vol. 3). Kin ties matter (Yuva's sister Funapa, vol. 2; the royal bloodline, vol. 3). The deepest threats are predators that eat people: Carache and the dracomorphs who devour enemies (vol. 2, 4). |
| Pragmatic | Practical choices win: Yuva carries salt instead of water, and the salt buys passage from the tower's creatures (vol. 2). Experience and supplies count for more than rank. Reviewers note how little is explained (ANN; Anime UK News): characters learn the tower by trying. |
| Religious | No religion shapes the plot in sources opened. Raising the dead is the villain's power and is shown as horror (undead soldiers, reanimated knights, vol. 1, 4). A later chapter title names an "exorcist god from a distant land" (ch. 23(1)); its content is unknown. Faith readers will weigh the magic (heroes use fire magic; a party member is called a necromancer, ch. 18(2)) against a story that rewards humility, courage and fidelity. |
| Socio-cultural | A dungeon crawl built on role-playing-game ideas (ANN) over court politics: commoners conscripted, a Parliament that recalls the rescue to put a new ruler on the throne (vol. 1), contested heirs and "dragon blood" (Otaku USA), and soldiers turned against each other by grief and suspicion (vol. 2-3). |

Outlook: Bittersweet — the trio holds together and climbs ever higher, but the tower kills soldiers and raises them as undead, an army is turned to stone (vol. 4), and the series is unfinished.

## Creator and publisher context

- Tsutomu Nihei worked in construction, then studied at Parsons School of Design in New York; his background shows in the vast structures of his work. Influences listed by Wikipedia include Akira, Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind, Ghost in the Shell, H. R. Giger, Moebius and Blade Runner. Works: Blame!, NOiSE, Abara, Biomega, Knights of Sidonia (Kodansha Manga Award 2015, the first of his works adapted for TV), Aposimz, and as writer Kaina of the Great Snow Sea. He was a special guest of San Diego Comic-Con in 2016 and received an Inkpot Award (Wikipedia).
- Tower Dungeon is his first fantasy work: stone and organic architecture instead of metal and machines (ANN; Otaku USA; Kodansha). The Nihei article calls it a "hack-and-slash fantasy" (Wikipedia).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What does it take to climb something no one understands? The Dragon Tower is too vast to explain: its floors twist, its dead walk, and its builders are unknown (ANN). Knights with training and titles fail at its gates. The series asks what an ordinary person brings that they do not.

**The answer the story gives.** Steadiness, loyalty and plain usefulness. Yuva does not give speeches; he lifts what needs lifting, carries salt when others carry water, and keeps climbing when the Guard is recalled (vol. 1-2). The story rewards fair dealing as much as fighting: the salt trade with the tower's creatures opens the way to level 80 (vol. 2).

**Characters as arguments.** Yuva argues for humble fidelity. Lilicen, proud and skilled, learns to trust him. Eriquo argues for duty to the princess over political orders. The Parliament and its envoy argue, by their actions, that the throne matters more than the captive. The grieving guardsmen show loyalty curdled into suspicion (vol. 2-3). Carache argues the oldest argument of all: the strong eat the weak. Sargan's revenge quest (vol. 4) is left open.

**Symbols and motifs.** The tower itself, a vertical maze that, in Nihei's style, dwarfs every person in it. The pot-helmet and the wooden shield of a farm boy. Salt as currency and as a sign of practical wisdom. The Mindlink rings (vol. 3). The undead as former comrades: the dead used against the living.

**Traditions it echoes.** The fairy-tale rescue of a princess from a tower; the underdog hero (David with his sling, Sam Gamgee with his pans); the dungeon-crawl games of the 1980s and 1990s; and Nihei's own Blame!, where a lone figure crosses an endless structure. The Royal Parliament's politics add a note of court intrigue.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Classical virtue ethics and communitarian loyalty win. Might makes right is the enemy, in the dracomorphs who devour their foes. The knightly code is honored and tested. Magic is a tool in heroes' hands and a horror in the villain's, which faith readers will weigh differently.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1 sets up the Guard and the conscripts; vol. 2-3 narrow to the trio and reveal Yuva's bloodline; vol. 4-5 add a second party and Carache's threat, and tell Aridellia's past; vol. 6-7 were not traced in sources opened.

**Discussion questions.**
1. The knights laughed at Yuva's pot helmet. Why did he do better than many of them?
2. Yuva carried salt instead of water. What does that teach about planning and about trading fairly?
3. The former guardsmen attacked the trio because they were grieving. Does grief excuse what they did?
4. Sargan wants revenge for his mother. What would you want the story to say about that?
5. Why might an author choose never to explain where the tower came from?
