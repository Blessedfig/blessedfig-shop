**Premise.** At a ball, Crown Prince Cedric publicly breaks off his engagement to Aileen d'Autriche, a duke's daughter whose hard work made her look snobbish to the other students, in favor of the sweet commoner Lilia. In that moment Aileen remembers her past life as a terminally ill Japanese teenager who loved an otome (dating-sim) game, and realizes she is its villainess, fated to die within months when the Demon King, Claude, becomes the final boss. Claude is Cedric's elder half-brother, disinherited for his half-demon magic, who rules the demons from a crumbling castle. Aileen proposes marriage to him (ch. 1-3). Ordered by her father to recoup her losses, she renovates the castle with old allies (the cautious Isaac, the journalist Jasper, a doctor and a botanist) and starts a cosmetics business (vol. 2). Threatening letters forged in her name are sent to Lilia; Lilia disappears, Aileen is accused, and Claude arrives at the ball to back her while Isaac proves the forgery; she signs the annulment and dances with Claude (vol. 2). Lilia turns out to be another reincarnated player who wants Claude for herself. In vol. 3, Aileen uncovers that Claude's aide Keith has been blackmailed into selling demons; Cedric and Lilia kidnap Aileen in a plot to kill Claude and force her into marriage, and Cedric attempts to assault her before she fights him off; Claude loses control and becomes a dragon; Aileen reveals she too has Holy Sword Maiden blood, confesses her love, and her tears bring him back. Cedric loses his right to the throne, Claude becomes crown prince again and Aileen his fiancée (Wikipedia, anime ep. 1-4; Anime UK News; ANN; Yen Press).

## Life message

**Courage, planning and honest love can rewrite a fate others have scripted for you, and people cast out as monsters deserve fair treatment.**

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** If the story of your life were already written, and you were cast as the villain, could you change it? Aileen wakes up inside a dating game she knows by heart, three months from a death scene. The series asks whether roles are fate.

**The answer the story gives.** No role is fate. Aileen survives by refusing the script: she proposes to the "final boss," fixes his castle, proves a forgery, and frees the demons the court despises. Her love, not only her plans, saves Claude from the monster he was written to become (vol. 3).

**Characters as arguments.** Aileen argues for effort and honest love; she bluffs constantly but tells the truth when it counts. Claude argues that the outcast can be the rightful ruler. Lilia, the other reincarnated player, argues that people are pieces in a game, the dark mirror of Aileen; ANN's reviewer found her unsettling. Cedric argues for entitlement, and his plan to take Aileen by force is the story's clearest wrong. Keith shows loyalty bent by blackmail and given a second chance.

**Symbols and motifs.** The broken engagement at a ball and the second ball that reverses it; the game's script; the Holy Sword that both women can wield; Claude's weather and flowers that change with his mood (ANN); the dragon; the crow Almond named for a cookie.

**Traditions it echoes.** Cinderella reversed (the wicked stepsister as heroine); Beauty and the Beast (love restoring a cursed prince); Japanese villainess-isekai. Its claim that the despised may be righteous echoes, for some readers, the biblical theme of the outcast exalted.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism and feminism are celebrated through Aileen. Progressivism shows in the demons' cause. Religious imagery is inverted (holy maiden as villain), which is where religious families differ most. Strength as a right to take is shown as the villain's creed.

**Shifts across the series.** Vol. 1 (ch. 1-3) is the setup and proposal. Vol. 2 is castle-building, business and the forgery trial at the ball. Vol. 3 is the trafficking plot, kidnapping, assault attempt, dragon and resolution, with bonus stories (Claude as a child, Aileen's fashion, a romantic moment). Vol. 4 (English 2024) is all bonus stories. The novels and anime continue far beyond the manga.

**Discussion questions.**
1. Aileen was called a villain because of a role in a game. Has anyone ever cast you in a role you didn't choose?
2. Is it fair that the "holy" heroine is the antagonist? What makes someone good or bad in this story?
3. Aileen bluffs and schemes a lot. When is that clever, and when would it be wrong?
4. Cedric tries to force Aileen. How does the story show that this is wrong, and what happens to him?
5. What does Aileen do for the demons, and why does it matter to her?
