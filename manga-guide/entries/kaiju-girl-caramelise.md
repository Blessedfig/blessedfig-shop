**Premise.** Kuroe Akaishi, a high school student her classmates call "Psycho-tan," has a rare condition that makes her body sprout claws, spikes and a tail when she feels strong emotion, above all romantic feeling. When Arata Minami, the most popular boy in class, takes an interest in her, revealing that he was once bullied for being overweight, Kuroe falls for him and turns into a giant kaiju that the city names Harugon. Her mother Rinko, a former biologist, admits that she found a kaiju egg on a remote island and raised the hatchling as her human daughter. Kuroe dates Arata while hiding the truth, and makes friends with Manatsu Tomosato, a rich girl obsessed with the kaiju, and Rairi Kouno, a fashionable girl who hides, under makeup, a face she has been mocked for. In the anime's last arc (likely about vols. 5-6), a second, flying kaiju from a second egg attacks Tokyo, Arata learns that Kuroe is Harugon and declares he loves her anyway, and they kiss (Wikipedia, episodes 10-12). In vol. 7 Kuroe wakes on the island where she was born, now forbidden to leave, among masked strangers; in vol. 8 the world knows she is a kaiju, her friends stand by her, and she helps a boy named Drago reach Kinara, another kaiju stuck in monster form (Yen Press).

## Philosophy essay

**The central question.** What if the thing you are most ashamed of were literally visible, and huge? Kuroe's feelings turn her body into something that terrifies the city. The series asks whether she can be loved without hiding, and whether she can love herself.

**The answer the story gives.** Yes, but only by being seen. Every time Kuroe hides she suffers; every time someone sees her fully (Arata at the theme park, ep. 3; Arata holding Harugon, ep. 12; her friends in vol. 8) she is freed. ANN's Silverman sums up the theme as "that we're all kind of monstrous, so what's the point in being ashamed?"

**Characters as arguments.** Kuroe argues that a monster cannot be loved, and is proven wrong. Arata, once bullied for his weight, argues that appearance is not the person. Rairi is the mirror: she hides under makeup as Kuroe hides in the river, and finds that showing herself does not ruin her (ep. 6; ANN vol. 3). Manatsu loves the monster without knowing it is her friend. Rinko loves Kuroe completely but cannot tell her the truth. Daichi, a child in an adult-sized body, shows that others also do not fit their bodies.

**Symbols and motifs.** The river where Kuroe cools down and turns back. The egg, and a second egg. The cave painting showing kaiju were known long ago (ep. 8). Fireworks as a moment of togetherness. Makeup and wigs as masks. Caramelizing: sugar transformed by heat, as the title suggests (from general knowledge; to verify).

**Traditions it echoes.** Godzilla, Mothra and King Kong, reversed so the girl is the monster (Encyclopedia of Science Fiction via Wikipedia); Beauty and the Beast; the shojo romance of an outcast and a popular boy. Religious readers may hear 1 Samuel 16:7 or the Buddhist teaching that the self is not the body.

**How each lens is portrayed.** Expressive individualism is affirmed in its gentlest form: be yourself and be accepted, not do as you please. Community wins: friends are how Kuroe survives exposure. Critics' feminist, queer and disability readings fit the story, which never names them. Authority is cautious and sometimes threatening.

**Shifts across the series.** Early volumes (anime eps. 1-7) are school romance and hiding. Middle volumes (eps. 8-12) bring the kaiju island, a second kaiju, and Arata learning the truth. Vol. 7 onward moves to Kuroe's birth island, the world learning her secret, and other kaiju (Kinara) who also hide (Yen Press).

**Discussion questions.**
1. Kuroe turns into a monster when she has strong feelings. What feelings make you feel out of control?
2. Arata was bullied for his weight. How does that change the way he treats Kuroe?
3. Rairi hides her face with makeup. Is that wrong, and what changes when she shows it?
4. Was Kuroe's mother right to keep the truth from her for so long?
5. Who at your school might feel like a monster, and what could you do?
