is a Japanese manga series written and illustrated by Keitaro Takahashi. It was serialized in Shogakukan's  manga magazine Monthly Sunday Gene-X from April 2006 to January 2012, with its chapters collected in eleven  volumes.

An anime television series produced by White Fox was broadcast between April and June 2012. A second season titled Jormungand: Perfect Order aired from October to December 2012.

The manga was licensed for English language release in North America by Viz Media. Both seasons have been licensed by Funimation in North America and Manga Entertainment in the United Kingdom.


== Plot ==

The series follows Koko Hekmatyar, a young arms dealer who sells weapons under HCLI, an international shipping corporation and illegal smuggling operation. As one of the company's unofficial weapon dealers, she sells weapons in a variety of countries while avoiding both local and international authorities. Traveling with her is a team of bodyguards, mostly composed of former soldiers. The newest addition to her crew is Jonah, an inexpressive and deadly child soldier who hates arms dealers.


== Reception ==
===Manga===
Scott Green of Ain't It Cool News described the series as enjoyable but underachieving. Matthew Warner considered the fourth volume's focus on the weapons trade a pleasant change of pace, but was disappointed by the absence of the violence and dark, twisted plot points found in earlier volumes. Erica Friedman, organizer of Yuricon, noted the yuri potential of Valmet but described the manga as just a dumb manga that she really liked. Karen Maeda enjoyed the action in the sixth volume, found the panels easy to follow, and hoped that Jormungand would be adapted into an anime.

Carlo Santos of Anime News Network identified challenging ideas about war, peace, and world affairs, combined with military action.

In Japan, the ninth volume sold 41,712 copies in one week for a total of 42,337, ranking 27th out of 30. The eleventh volume ranked 10th with 70,593 copies sold. It later ranked 20th out of 30, selling 34,697 copies weekly for a total of 105,290.
